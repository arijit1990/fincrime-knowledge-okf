## 4. Intermediary agent PSPs

- **descriptive / expectation**: The Wolfsberg Payment Transparency Standards state that an intermediary agent PSP is responsible for ensuring compliance with its own local laws and regulations, including (if applicable) the rulebook of the PMI through which it intends to facilitate settlement of the payment, as well as FATF Recommendation 16 to the degree permissible if requirements and recommendations differ. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ensuring compliance with its own local laws and regulations, including (if applicable) the rulebook for the PMI"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: When applicable under a correspondent relationship, an intermediary agent PSP is responsible for conducting full CDD on the PSP customer whose payments it processes, in line with correspondent banking due diligence principles and other PSP customer-related requirements, as the relationship-owning PSP. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducting full CDD on the PSP customer whose payments it processes, in line with correspondent banking due diligence principles"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The CDD an intermediary agent PSP conducts on its PSP customer should include, on a risk-based approach, confirming that the payment model proposed by the PSP customer is executed correctly in practice. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confirming that the payment model as proposed by the PSP customer is executed correctly in practice"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The CDD an intermediary agent PSP conducts on its PSP customer should include, on a risk-based approach, confirming that the use of distinct payment approaches or permitted exceptions (e.g. bundled payments, collection agent models, or "goods or services" provisions) is in line with the policies the intermediary agent PSP established and agreed at onboarding. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are in line with the policies established by the intermediary agent PSP as agreed at onboarding"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is responsible for passing on complete information received within payment messages to the next PSP in the payment chain, to the fullest extent permitted by the relevant PMI. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Passing on complete information that is received within payment messages, to the fullest extent permitted by the relevant PMI"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is responsible for monitoring its PSP customer's payment activity, based on information provided in the payment message, to identify potentially suspicious activity or to enforce sanctions compliance, unless monitoring is not required by applicable regulations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unless monitoring is not required by applicable regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is responsible for retaining a record of all the information received from the debtor agent PSP or, if applicable, from the previous intermediary agent PSP now instructing the payment through it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Retaining a record of all the information received from the debtor agent PSP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Standards note that an intermediary agent PSP may be responsible under applicable law for ensuring the required fields have been completed for both the debtor and the creditor, but it is not responsible for the items listed next. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the intermediary agent PSP may be responsible under applicable law for ensuring that the required fields have been completed"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is not responsible for conducting CDD on debtor PSPs or other intermediary agent PSPs with which it does not hold a correspondent or similar relationship. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Conducting CDD on debtor or intermediary agent PSPs with whom the intermediary agent PSP does not hold"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is not responsible for conducting CDD on any creditor agent PSP with which it does not hold a business relationship. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Conducting CDD on any creditor agent PSP with whom the intermediary agent PSP does not hold a business relationship"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is not responsible for identifying, or otherwise conducting CDD on, the underlying customers of the debtor agent PSP, any other intermediary agent PSP or the creditor agent PSP. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identifying, or otherwise conducting CDD on, the underlying customers of the debtor agent PSP"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is not responsible for identifying whether payments it processes are bundled, nor for seeking to unbundle payments it may learn are bundled. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identifying whether payments that it processes are bundled or to seek to unbundle such payments"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An intermediary agent PSP is not responsible for determining whether the debtor agent PSP, or intermediary agent PSPs not immediately previous in the chain, have correctly applied the criteria set by the relevant competent authority for bundled payments (e.g. "many-to-many" scenarios), collection agent model payments, "goods or services" payments, or other payment types outside the scope of payment transparency regulation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have correctly applied the permissible criteria established by the relevant competent authority for bundled payments"</sub>

## 5. Creditor agent PSPs – beneficiary institutions/payee PSPs

- **descriptive / expectation**: A creditor agent PSP is responsible for identifying, verifying and conducting full CDD on its customer (the creditor), as well as related record keeping. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identifying, verifying, and conducting full CDD on its customer (the creditor), as well as related record keeping"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A creditor agent PSP is responsible for determining, based on the information available in the payment message, whether payments received by its customers represent potentially suspicious activity or violate applicable sanctions programmes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Determining, based on the information available in the payment message, if the payments received by its customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A creditor agent PSP is not responsible for conducting CDD on the debtor, the debtor PSP, or any intermediary PSPs, given that it is acting on behalf of the creditor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "given that the creditor agent PSP is acting on behalf of the creditor"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A creditor agent PSP is not responsible for determining whether the debtor agent PSP or intermediary agent PSPs in the chain have correctly applied the criteria established by the relevant competent authority for bundled payments, collection agent model payments, "goods or services" payments, or other payment types outside the scope of standard payment transparency regulation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for payments operating via a collection agent model, for payments considered as"</sub>

## Going forward

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group states that complying with AML/CTF laws and regulation is a commitment that must apply to any provider of payment services, and that because payments can now be originated by non-banks, the set of "gate-keepers" to the financial system has expanded. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Complying with AML/CTF laws and regulation is a commitment that must apply to any provider of payment services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group states that the debtor agent PSP originating the payment, whether bank or non-bank, is uniquely positioned to ensure the payment provides the maximum required level of transparency at its outset, so the principal responsibility for payment transparency compliance begins with the debtor agent PSP. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the principal responsibility for ensuring payment transparency compliance begins with the debtor agent PSP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group states that payment transparency challenges cannot be resolved solely by payment and compliance specialists within banks, but require agreement among all payments stakeholders, including competent authorities, PMIs within their countries and non-bank payment providers, on how the increase in payment actors and methods redistributes accountability for addressing financial crime risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require agreement among all payments stakeholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The revised Wolfsberg Payment Transparency Standards, supplemented with payment diagrams in Appendix A illustrating complexity and transparency challenges for PSPs, aim to build consensus in line with an effective financial crime compliance programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "aim to build this consensus in line with an effective financial crime compliance programme"</sub>

## APPENDIX A: Payment Flow Diagrams

- **descriptive / context**: The Group notes that payment innovation is fast-moving, with blurring lines between when a payment starts and ends and many actors involved; payment chains are often highly intermediated, with cross-border payments broken into a series of distinct domestic and cross-border transfers. The five diagrams (person-to-person, correspondent banking, payment processor, money remittance, and money remittance with cash pay-outs) are non-exhaustive illustrative examples. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payment chains are often highly intermediated, with cross-border payments broken down into a series of distinct domestic and cross-border transfers"</sub>

## Diagram 1 Person to person payment flow

- **descriptive / case example**: Diagram 1 shows a cross-border payment between two individuals using two PSPs with no intermediaries: the debtor agent PSP takes funds from the debtor's account and transfers them directly to the creditor agent PSP, which credits the creditor's account. The two PSPs either have a direct account relationship or are common members of a cross-border PMI. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will either have a direct account relationship with each other or be connected as common members of a cross-border PMI"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Group states that the Diagram 1 transaction poses few payment transparency challenges, provided full details are submitted by the debtor and passed from debtor agent PSP to creditor agent PSP. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The transaction poses few payment transparency challenges, provided full details are submitted by the debtor"</sub>

## Diagram 2 Correspondent banking

- **descriptive / case example**: Diagram 2 shows a furniture shop (debtor) in Country A making a single payment to its supplier (creditor) in Country B. Because the debtor agent and creditor agent do not share a direct relationship, the debtor agent PSP instructs its intermediary agent PSP in Country B to send the money to the creditor agent PSP, which receives the single payment with full debtor information. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As the Debtor Agent and Creditor Agent do not share a direct relationsip"</sub>

## Diagram 3 Payment Processor

- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 3 (payment processor, card payments for merchants), transaction data resides within the card scheme; the two bundled transfers do not contain card scheme data and the PSPs do not see the underlying customer details or transactions. Lower payment transparency may be permitted under local regulations because card transactions are viewed as transactions between a merchant and its end customers for goods or services; this is described as permissible under FATF 16. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The two bundled transfers do not contain card scheme data and the PSPs in this scenario"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 3, Card Issuers use their National Net Settlement Scheme to transfer a bundle of all their customers' transactions to the Card Scheme's bank account at IPSP (1), and are repaid by the Card Holder's bank account; the Card Scheme then transfers settlement of all funds from the Card Issuers from its account at IPSP (1) to the Payment Processor's disbursement bank, IPSP (2). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Card Issuers use their National Net Settlement Scheme to transfer in a bundle of all their customers’ transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 3, IPSP (2) disburses the required amounts to the individual merchant's bank account with the creditor agent PSP; transparency is limited in payment leg 3 because the merchants receiving payments, though not the debtors making them, are visible to the intermediary agent PSP. The diagram is a basic flow and does not represent the end-to-end card scheme payment cycle. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is limited transparency in payment leg 3 in so far as the merchants receiving the payments"</sub>

## Diagram 4 Money remittance

- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 4, individuals (debtors) in Country A enter payment instructions on their MSB account and fund each transaction by a single domestic bank transfer from their own bank (debtor agent PSP 1) to the MSB, which holds an account with intermediary agent PSP (2). For the debtor agent PSP and IPSP (2) the domestic payment may be subject to sanctions screening depending on local regulations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the domestic payment may be subject to sanctions screening, depending on local regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: In Diagram 4, the MSB holds the underlying details from its debtor customers and is aware it is facilitating a cross-border remittance, so it needs to conduct sanctions screening and adhere to applicable regulatory requirements for a cross-border payment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "thus, it needs to conduct sanctions screening and adhere to applicable regulatory requirements for a cross-border payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 4, IPSP (2) makes a bundled payment to IPSP (3), the MSB's local disbursement bank in Country B. The bundled payment may be (a) an aggregation of payments from multiple debtors to be disbursed to creditors, (b) treasury settlement by the MSB between the two countries, or (c) a combination of (a) and (b). Simultaneously the MSB sends IPSP (3) a payment data file with the creditors' names, bank and account numbers and payment details. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The bundled payment may be a) an aggregation of payments received from multiple debtors to be disbursed in Country B"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The Group identifies a payment transparency challenge in Diagram 4: IPSP (2) and IPSP (3) do not know whether the funds transferred and received are for aggregated payments, treasury settlement, or a combination of both. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IPSP (2) and (3) do not know if the funds being transferred and received by them respectively are for"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: In Diagram 4, irrespective of the purpose of the funds transfer, the MSB in Country A is the customer of IPSP (2), and the MSB's underlying debtors do not become customers of IPSP (2) for the purpose of applicable laws and regulations; the same applies to IPSP (3) and the MSB in Country B. There is no requirement or expectation on IPSP (2) and (3) to unbundle the payment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no requirement or expectation on IPSP (2) and (3) to unbundle the payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 4, IPSP (3) uses the payment data file from the MSB to make individual payments to creditor agent PSPs; creditor agent PSP (4) receives a single payment from IPSP (3) and credits the creditor's account. The creditor agent PSP may see the payment as domestic rather than cross-border unless the final payment message clearly indicates that the debtor agent and/or IPSP (2) original account location was in Country A not Country B. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the payment may be seen as domestic and not cross-border by creditor agent PSP (4)"</sub>

## Diagram 5 Money remittance with cash pay-outs

- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 5, debtors in Country A enter instructions on an MSB platform and fund each transaction by a single domestic bank transfer from their own bank to the MSB, which holds an account with IPSP 2. As in Diagram 4, the domestic payment may be subject to sanctions screening depending on local regulations, and the MSB, holding underlying details and aware it is a cross-border remittance, will need to conduct sanctions screening and adhere to applicable regulatory requirements for a cross-border payment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Similar to Diagram 4, for the debtor agent PSP and Intermediary Agent PSP (2), the domestic payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 5, the MSB aggregates payments with other funds going to Country B and instructs IPSP (2) to send a bundled payment to its local disbursement bank, IPSP (4); IPSP (2) sends it via its correspondent bank IPSP (3). The bundled payment sent by IPSP (2) may not be the same value as the one the MSB instructed, as IPSP (2) may set a separate value to suit its operational, treasury forecasting and/or foreign exchange needs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be a separate value determined by IPSP (2) to best suit its operational, treasury forecasting, and/or foreign exchange needs"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The Group states the same payment transparency challenges as Diagram 4 apply to Diagram 5, compounded by an additional intermediary agent (IPSP (3)); as in Diagram 4, there is no expectation for the IPSPs to unbundle the payment or regard the MSB's underlying debtors/customers as customers of IPSP (2) and (4). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "compounded by the presence of an additional intermediary agent (IPSP (3))"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Diagram 5, IPSP (3) makes a separate bundled payment to IPSP (4), and the MSB sends payment data to IPSP (4) to facilitate local disbursement. IPSP (4) reconciles the bundled payment to the MSB's data and credits creditors' accounts at creditor agent PSPs (5) and the MSB agent's bank, creditor agent PSP (6); the creditor can then collect cash from MSB agent PSP (7) using details received on their mobile phone. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IPSP (4) reconciles the bundled payment received to the data received from the MSB"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: In Diagram 5, because the customer relationship is held with the MSB and not with the MSB agent (acting as disbursement agent), the applicable laws and regulations and enforcement of sanctions compliance will apply to the MSB. Creditor agents PSP (5) and (6) may see the final payment as domestic rather than cross-border unless the payment message clearly indicates the original account location was in Country A. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As the customer relationship is held with the MSB and not with the MSB agent"</sub>

## Payment Transparency Challenges (and Limitations)

- **descriptive / context**: The Group states that highly intermediated payments involving different payment actor types, pursued for faster and cheaper payments, pose varied payment transparency challenges to PSPs because of limitations in the information each may receive, affecting how PSPs fulfil monitoring and sanctions compliance obligations in accordance with their role in the chain. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These payment flows can pose varied payment transparency challenges to PSPs not least because of limitations in the information"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Limited Payment Transparency: in diagrams 4 and 5 only the debtor agent PSP (here the MSB) has full visibility of the underlying debtors/creditors, their addresses and purpose of payment. For reasons such as bundling or limitations of the local payment scheme to carry all information, this information is not available to all downstream PSPs to perform (or know they need to perform) sanctions screening and effective transaction monitoring. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only the debtor agent PSP, in that case the MSB, will have the full visibility of the underlying debtors/creditors"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: While the MSB can monitor and file SARs/STRs in its own jurisdiction, the cross-border risks may not be shared with other parties in other jurisdictions in the payment chain, impacting their ability to take appropriate risk mitigation measures; competent authorities and law enforcement in intermediary and receiving jurisdictions may also be unaware of the cross-border nature of the transaction. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the cross-border risks may not be shared with the other parties in other jurisdictions involved in the payment chain"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Bundled Payments: bundled payments (which occur for reasons such as treasury settlement, foreign exchange conversion, and operational and cost efficiency) reduce payment transparency to downstream PSPs. Intermediary PSPs may not know they are processing a bundled payment, and not all PSPs who do know will, or will choose to, request and obtain the full underlying debtor/creditor information to monitor and sanction screen the transactions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Intermediary PSPs may not know that they are processing a bundled payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Ambiguity as to when a payment starts and ends: a single payment initiated by an individual in one jurisdiction may be bundled with other cross-border payments and sent via a series of domestic and correspondent-type transfers, typically for faster and cheaper settlement, by breaking cross-border payments into domestic payments and disbursements via domestic PMIs plus a separate bundled cross-border payment. It is therefore challenging for IPSPs to determine whether a payment's intent is domestic or cross-border, bundled or treasury (or both) without full visibility of underlying creditors/debtors. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is challenging for IPSPs to determine if the intent of the payment they are processing is domestic or cross-border"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Standards state that the limitation of IPSPs lacking visibility of whether a payment is domestic or cross-border, bundled or treasury should be reflected, and calibrated, into supervisory expectations and subsequent examinations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This limitation should be reflected, and calibrated, into supervisory expectations and subsequent examinations"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The Standards note that PSPs are not responsible for conducting CDD on debtors/creditors with whom they have no customer relationship; e.g. intermediary agent PSPs are not responsible for CDD on debtors/creditors, who are customers of the debtor agent PSP and creditor agent PSP respectively. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PSPs are not responsible for conducting CDD on debtors/creditors with whom they do not have a customer relationship"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Standards state that this distribution of accountability across all PSPs in the payment chain will need to be reflected, and calibrated, into supervisory expectations and subsequent examinations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This distribution of accountability across all PSPs in the payment chain will need to be reflected"</sub>

## 4. Intermediary agent PSPs

- **descriptive / expectation**: The Standards make an intermediary agent PSP responsible for keeping risk-based policies and procedures that decide when a payment is executed, rejected or suspended, consistent with the PSP's applicable AML/CTF and sanctions compliance obligations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Maintaining risk-based policies and procedures to determine when to execute, reject or suspend a payment"</sub>

## 5. Creditor agent PSPs – beneficiary institutions/payee PSPs

- **descriptive / expectation**: The Standards make a creditor agent PSP responsible for complying with its own local laws and regulations, including, where applicable, its obligations as a participant in the PMI it uses to settle the payment for its customer, the creditor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as a participant in the PMI through which the creditor agent PSP intends to facilitate settlement of the payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A creditor agent PSP is also responsible for maintaining risk-based policies and procedures on when to execute, reject or suspend a payment, in line with its AML/CTF and sanctions compliance obligations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Maintaining risk-based policies and procedures to determine when to execute, reject or suspend a payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A creditor agent PSP is responsible for telling its creditor how to use virtual reference number compound naming properly in settlement instructions given to counterparties, for example so that counterparties assigned virtual reference numbers do not displace the true account creditor, as can arise in merchant acquiring or collection-on-behalf-of scenarios. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those counterparties do not replace the true account creditor"</sub>

## Going forward

- **descriptive / context**: In December 2019 the Wolfsberg Group issued a Statement on Effectiveness setting out the Wolfsberg Factors, its view of the key elements of an effective AML/CTF programme: compliance with AML/CTF laws and regulations; highly useful information provided to relevant government agencies in defined priority areas; and a reasonable, risk-based set of controls to reduce the risk of an FI being used for illicit activity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In December 2019, the Group published a Statement on Effectiveness"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Standards say that giving government agencies highly useful information depends on payment messages carrying basic data on the debtor, the creditor and any intermediaries, and that required information must be properly structured and, where regulation demands, verified. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Required information must be structured appropriately and verified when required by regulation"</sub>
- **mandatory / context**: The Standards state that where existing regulation permits lower levels of transparency, the expectations competent authorities hold about spotting suspicious activity or enforcing sanctions must be adjusted accordingly. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "competent authority expectations on identifying suspicious activity or enforcing sanctions compliance must adjust"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: According to the Standards, intermediary agent PSPs, and to a degree creditor agent PSPs, frequently cannot satisfy monitoring and sanctions compliance expectations where lawful bundling, collection agency models or "goods or services" provisions allow payments to be started and settled with little underlying debtor or creditor information, since that information is absent from the payment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are often unable to meet monitoring and sanctions compliance expectations when legally permitted bundling"</sub>

## Diagram 2 Correspondent banking

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the Group's view the Diagram 2 correspondent banking flow raises few payment transparency challenges as long as the debtor supplies full details and these travel unchanged from the debtor agent PSP through the intermediary agent PSP to the creditor agent PSP. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "passed from the debtor agent PSP to the creditor agent PSP, via the intermediary agent PSP"</sub>

## Payment Transparency Challenges (and Limitations)

- **expected / expectation**: Even though bundling reduces transparency, and subject to applicable laws, PSPs should cooperate as fully as practicable with other PSPs in the payment process when asked for information about the parties involved. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PSPs should cooperate as fully as practicable with other PSPs in the payment process"</sub>
