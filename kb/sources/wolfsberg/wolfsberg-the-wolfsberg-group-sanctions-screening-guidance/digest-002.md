## 6.2 Internal Lists

- **descriptive / definition**: Internal lists, often called 'Private lists' or 'Grey lists', are lists of individuals and entities that may present a financial crime risk to the FI, identified through internal procedures or intelligence; they are generated and maintained within the FI's risk appetite and, ideally, applied in screening for a set time frame dependent on the risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Internal lists are often referred to as ‘Private lists’ or ‘Grey lists.’"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Regular reviews of internal list entries are helpful to ensure intelligence does not become stale or outdated. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regular reviews of entries are helpful to ensure intelligence does not become stale or outdated"</sub>

## 6.3 Identifying Information and Weak Aliases

- **descriptive / context**: Identifying information provided with list entries, such as date of birth, nationality (where legally permissible) and place of birth, assists in distinguishing a true match from a false positive; it does not need to be screened and is provided to assist with assessment of an alert. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This information does not need to be screened. It is provided to assist with the assessment of an alert."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Some authorities provide ancillary information including "weak aliases" or "low quality aliases", describing broad or generic names of sanctions targets that often add little value in confirming a match, and which may include nicknames and common acronyms. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "describes broad or generic names of sanctions targets that often will add little value in confirming a match"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: It is not expected, nor is it typically productive, to screen against weak aliases. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is not expected, nor is it typically productive, to screen against weak aliases."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Weak aliases can be identified by categories: short character length (shorter strings assumed less effective in screening than longer ones); presence of numbers (digits 0-9); common words generally constituting a nickname (e.g. Ahmed the Tall); references to geographic locations; and very common prefixes where the prefix was one of only two strings in the name (e.g. Mr. Smith). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Character length (shorter strings are assumed to be less effective in screening than longer strings)"</sub>

## 7 Historical Reviews (Lookbacks)

- **descriptive / context**: While a lookback is not exclusively a sanctions control, an FI may identify potential sanctions risk where a sanctions related data point was previously undetected by the screening system, for example due to a name variation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI may identify potential sanctions risk where a sanctions related data point may have been previously undetected"</sub>
- **recommended / expectation**: In such instances the FI should consider whether (i) changes to the sanctions screening system (e.g. configuration or lists) are warranted, and (ii) a historical review ("lookback") should be performed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FI should consider whether or not: (i) changes to the sanctions screening system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In considering a lookback to identify already processed transactions, an FI should give strong consideration as to whether such a review would be useful to the FI and/or public policy interests. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI should give strong consideration as to whether such a review would be useful"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Lookback considerations include: a clear understanding of the root cause; whether the matter is an isolated one-time event or likely to recur, and the consequences if repeated; whether the risk warrants mitigation and what steps (e.g. configuration changes, list content, non-screening controls); and whether there is a public policy or law enforcement interest in identifying and disclosing historical transactions/parties. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Whether the matter is an isolated, one-time event or is it likely to occur again"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further lookback considerations include mitigating factors for potential enforcement actions and regulatory disclosure, detecting possible conduct issues, and identifying customer behaviour or patterns that pose increased sanctions risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mitigating factors for potential enforcement actions and regulatory disclosure"</sub>

## 8. Conclusion

- **expected / recommendation**: While recognising the need to meet regulatory and legal obligations, the Wolfsberg Group believes FIs should seek to adopt a risk based approach to sanctions screening and consider all aspects of a comprehensive sanctions screening control framework. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should seek to adopt a risk based approach to sanctions screening"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FI's approach should recognise that while sanctions screening is a primary control it has limitations and should be deployed alongside a broader set of non-screening controls to be truly effective. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be deployed alongside a broader set of non-screening controls to be truly effective"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: It is important for FIs to document their systematic approach to screening by linking it directly to their risk appetite statements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important for FIs to document their systematic approach to screening by linking it directly"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The accuracy and completeness of the FI's own data is central to an effective and efficient sanctions screening process, and technology remains a key enabler in identifying financial crime risk through screening more efficiently and on a real-time basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The accuracy and completeness of the FI’s own data is central to an effective and efficient sanctions screening process"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Robust governance and oversight mechanisms must be put in place across the FIs to ensure transparency of risk decisions to key stakeholders and risk owners. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Robust governance and oversight mechanisms must be put in place across the FIs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FI should ensure that people involved in end-to-end risk event management are suitably trained and supervised, with appropriate levels of quality control and assurance in place to ensure compliance with requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "suitably trained, supervised and that the appropriate levels of quality control and assurance are in place"</sub>

## Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: Alert Spike is a substantial increase in the number of alerts generated, which could be caused by, for example, remediation exercises or changes or updates to policies, procedures or Watchlists. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Alert Spike is a substantial increase in the number of alerts generated"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Fuzzy Matching is a varied, algorithm based technique to match one name (a string of words) where the information being screened is not identical but its spelling, pattern or sound is a close match to the contents of a screening list. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fuzzy Matching is a varied and algorithm based technique to match one name"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Customer or Name Screening is the screening of the full legal name and any other name provided by the customer, such as known aliases, against applicable official sanctions lists. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Customer or Name Screening is the screening of full legal name and any other name provided by the customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Sectoral Sanctions Identifications aim to identify persons operating in sectors of the economy that may be subject to sectoral sanctions, and deals and transactions that are prohibited. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "aim to identify persons operating in sectors of the economy that may be subject to sectoral sanctions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Transaction Screening is the screening of a movement of value within the FI's records, including funds, goods or assets, between parties or accounts. To mitigate risk in trade finance transactions and international wire transfers, FIs conduct real-time screening of cross-border transactions against Sanctions Lists where any of the Sending, Originating, Receiving, Intermediary or Beneficiary Bank are in different countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs conduct real-time screening of cross-border transactions against Sanctions Lists"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: True Match is a screening result where the characters in the information being screened match the details of a designated entity on a list that is in scope for screening. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "True Match is a screening result, where the characters contained within the information being screened match"</sub>

## 6.1 Regulatory Sanctions Lists

- **expected / expectation**: Once new designations appear on regulatory lists, the guidance says the main aim of the list management function is to get those names into screening as fast and as accurately as possible. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the key priority for a list management function is to ensure the names are implemented into screening"</sub>

## 6.2 Internal Lists

- **recommended / recommendation**: Since the long-term effectiveness of internal lists often rests on the data quality of the entries added, an FI should consider setting minimum inclusion criteria for internal list entries (including minimum data attributes and quality) so that the lists work operationally, support alert investigation procedures and improve risk identification. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI should consider the minimum inclusion criteria for internal list entries to be operationally effective"</sub>

## 8. Conclusion

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg conclusion describes sanctions screening as a key control in preventing the financial crime risk to which FIs may otherwise be exposed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sanctions screening is a key control in the prevention of financial crime risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The conclusion states it is essential that sanctions screening is put in place and kept up as one part of a broader set of financial crime compliance controls, and that it sits within the FI's risk appetite. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is essential that it is implemented and maintained as part of a wider set of financial crime"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In the conclusion's list of framework aspects, an FI must maintain a strong financial crime compliance (FCC) programme with a defined strategy for sanctions screening, so as to reduce its exposure to sanctioned parties and countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must have a robust FCC programme with a clear strategy in respect of sanctions screening"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Management should be given robust management information that reports on the effectiveness, trends and performance of sanctions screening. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Robust management information should be made available to management to report effectiveness"</sub>

## Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: Four-Eye Review is the glossary term for a dual control arrangement in which an activity such as a decision or transaction needs sign-off from two people at minimum (a Maker and a Checker); it serves to improve transparency and the quality of reviews and later decisions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This dual control mechanism is used to increase transparency and ensure quality of reviews"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines Operational Risk as the potential for services supplied by an FI to be reduced, impaired or to fail, because of weaknesses in information systems or internal processes, mistakes by people, failures of management, or external disruptions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Operational Risk is the risk of potential reduction, deterioration or breakdown of services provided by an FI"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Sectoral Sanctions, per the glossary, refers to Ukraine- and Russia-related programmes that OFAC and the European Union launched in July 2014; they bar particular transaction types with targeted entities in the finance, energy and defence sectors and with entities that the targets own at 50% or more. OFAC calls them Sectoral Sanctions Identifications. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in July 2014, the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and the European Union introduced new"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Weak Aliases/Low Quality Aliases are broad or generic aliases, including nicknames and common acronyms, which risk producing many false hits in computer-based screening; the glossary says screening against them is not expected and is typically unproductive. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may generate a large volume of false hits when such names are run through a computer-based screening system"</sub>
