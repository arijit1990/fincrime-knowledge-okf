## The Wolfsberg Group - Guidance on the Risk-Based Approach

- **descriptive / context**: The application of a risk-based approach (RBA) is critical to the effective design and maintenance of an FI's financial crime risk management (FCRM) programme, covering its risk appetite, policies, measures and controls. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is critical to the effective design and maintenance of a financial institution"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Leadership, whether in the public or the private sector, should reinforce the need for an RBA through a strong "tone-from-the-top" that establishes a clear risk-based commitment to effective financial crime risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should reinforce this through a strong "tone-from-the-top" that establishes a clear risk-based commitment"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The Group recommends that FIs should demonstrate three key elements when designing and maintaining a risk-based FCRM programme: proportionality, prioritisation and effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Group recommends that FIs should demonstrate three key elements when designing and maintaining a risk-based FCRM programme"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The guidance elaborates on the three elements by detailing strategies and mechanisms FIs could adopt when implementing an RBA, and should be considered alongside other Group resources referred to where appropriate. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strategies and mechanisms that FIs could adopt when implementing an RBA"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Each FI is distinct, and a one-size-fits-all approach to financial crime risk management is not a risk-based approach. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a one-size-fits-all approach to financial crime risk management is not a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Open and constructive dialogue with the public sector, including law enforcement, can yield valuable insights on emerging threats and risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Open and constructive dialogue with the public sector, including law enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Establishing a financial crime risk appetite can determine the strategic and proportionate boundaries of an FI's FCRM programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Establishing a financial crime risk appetite can determine the strategic and proportionate boundaries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Set by senior management, risk appetite should define the level of financial crime risk an FI will accept to meet its objectives and the risks it will not tolerate, and considers the FI's values, regulatory obligations, business goals and risk management framework. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "risk appetite should define the level of financial crime risk an FI will accept to meet its objectives"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FIs must take financial inclusion expectations into account when defining their financial crime risk appetite, while ensuring not to compromise on robust financial crime risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs must also take financial inclusion expectations into account when defining their financial crime risk appetite"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The assessment of financial crime risk should also be a dynamic process embedded within business-as-usual (BAU) risk management activities, for example through regular oversight and reporting on management information and control effectiveness, providing insights for use on an ongoing basis to maintain a proportionate and relevant RBA. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also be a dynamic process embedded within business-as-usual (BAU) risk management activities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIs should avoid ineffective practices when designing and implementing an RBA. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should avoid ineffective practices when designing and implementing an RBA"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Risk assessments should be aligned and mutually reinforcing, as duplication, inconsistency or siloed approaches undermine the integrity of a FCRM programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk assessments should be aligned and mutually reinforcing, as duplication, inconsistency or siloed approaches undermine the integrity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Customer types (who the customer is): FIs should take steps to identify the characteristics of customer types that may potentially present higher inherent financial crime risk, for example non-resident customers, and companies with nominee shareholders or shares in bearer form. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should take steps to identify the characteristics of customer types that may potentially present higher inherent financial crime risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The entity's formation date should also be considered, as newly established entities may pose higher risk than those that are well-established. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "newly established entities may pose higher risk than those that are well-established"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Customer profile: longstanding relationships may provide a better view of customer behaviour and risk profile, as refreshed CDD, transaction history and regular contact can improve the FI's understanding of the customer's risk over time. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "longstanding relationships may provide a better view of customer behaviour and risk profile"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Customer risk insights: financial crime controls such as screening and monitoring for suspicious activity enable FIs to identify risks associated with customer attributes, behaviour and transactions, and customer risk insights should also be used to determine the higher-risk areas to prioritise and mitigate as part of an RBA. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Customer risk insights should also be used to determine the higher-risk areas to prioritise and mitigate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Effectiveness is an integral part of an RBA; an FI can demonstrate an effective and risk-based approach to identifying, assessing and managing its risks by adopting the elements the Group identified as key components of effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effectiveness is an integral part of an RBA and has been central to the Group"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Group's paper on Auditing for Effectiveness outlines key principles and measures internal audit should apply when measuring the effectiveness of a FCRM programme, including demonstrating appropriate governance, adherence to all relevant local laws and regulations, design and operating effectiveness of controls, and mechanisms allowing the sharing of highly useful information with relevant government agencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "outlines the key principles and measures that internal audit should apply when measuring the effectiveness"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIs should aim to ensure that governance remains effective, avoiding unnecessary complexity that can impede decision-making and divert resources from core risk management activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should, however, aim to ensure that governance remains effective"</sub>

## Risk-based supervision

- **descriptive / context**: FIs need flexibility to implement and evidence an RBA; a supervisory and examination regime across supervisors and internal/external auditors that supports an RBA, including moving away from lower-value measures and controls, is critical to success in the fight against financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs need flexibility to implement and evidence an RBA"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors and auditors should "move beyond a tick-box approach in monitoring the private sector's efforts" and not focus on the mere existence of information or setting unrealistic expectations (such as a "zero-failure" compliance environment or "no SAR left behind"). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "move beyond a tick-box approach in monitoring the private sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Unrealistic supervisory expectations can divert resources from effective risk management, increase costs and create unnecessary customer friction without improving (and often worsening) financial crime risk management outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "increase costs and create unnecessary customer friction without improving (and often worsening) financial crime risk management outcomes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors and internal/external auditors should prioritise assessing FCRM outcomes and the practical contribution made in the fight against financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should prioritise assessing FCRM outcomes and the practical contribution made in the fight against financial crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Ongoing public-private collaboration, consultation and regular auditor dialogue will be key to acceptance of an RBA that aligns to each FI's unique risks and business profile. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ongoing public-private collaboration, consultation and regular auditor dialogue will be key"</sub>

## Training and Culture

- **descriptive / context**: People are critical to maintaining an FI's systems and controls to prevent and detect financial crime, and applying a risk-based approach to the methods available for training gives each FI additional flexibility regarding the frequency, delivery mechanisms and focus of such training. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "gives each financial institution additional flexibility regarding the frequency, delivery mechanisms and focus of such training"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Staff in higher-risk roles should receive targeted, role-specific training and hone the skills required to identify, assess and manage financial crime risks effectively. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Staff in higher-risk roles should receive targeted, role-specific training"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Learning programmes should build capabilities in critical thinking and fostering curiosity so employees can apply appropriate judgement, spot risks and make informed decisions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Learning programmes should build out capabilities in critical thinking and fostering curiosity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Training should encourage staff confidence to speak up and escalate concerns, supporting a proactive risk-based culture. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Training should also encourage staff confidence to speak up and escalate concerns"</sub>

## Conclusion

- **descriptive / context**: By drawing on proportionality, prioritisation and effectiveness and using this guidance, FIs can demonstrate a credible approach to RBA design and implementation; this will only be possible with a supervisory and examination regime that enables, rather than hinders, an RBA, giving FIs flexibility to implement and evidence an approach tailored to their risk profile. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This will only be possible with a supervisory and examination regime that enables, rather than hinders, an RBA"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: When applied well, an RBA strengthens outcomes for both the private and public sectors; the Group will continue to collaborate with the FATF and across the industry to advance the shared priority of embedding a genuine RBA. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Group will continue to collaborate with the FATF, and more broadly across the industry"</sub>

## The Wolfsberg Group - Guidance on the Risk-Based Approach

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group first acknowledged the importance of a risk-based approach in 2006 with the original version of this paper, Guidance on an RBA for Managing Money Laundering Risks. Given the current standard-setting and regulatory reform agenda and its collaboration with the FATF on the RBA, it has defined the core elements of an RBA in its Statement on the RBA and updated this guidance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "first recognised the importance of an RBA in 2006, when it published the original version of this paper"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: In line with the FATF, the Group takes an RBA to mean that countries, competent authorities and FIs should be expected to identify, understand and assess the financial crime risks they face and to act proportionately, in a way that matches the level of identified risk and effectively mitigates it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be expected to identify, understand and assess the financial crime risks to which they are exposed"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An RBA supports effective financial crime risk management because it lets FIs concentrate on their highest-priority threats and stop, reduce or redesign activities that add little to the fight against financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enabling FIs to focus on responding to their highest priority threats while stopping, reducing and/or redesigning activities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Proportionality: an FI should build and maintain its FCRM programme to suit its business model, which is determined by its size, scale, footprint, customers and risk appetite (itself informed by the FI's risk assessment). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI should design and maintain a FCRM programme proportionate to its business model"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Because each FI is distinct, and a one-size-fits-all approach is not risk-based, each FI should have an FCRM programme scaled to the financial crime risk inherent in its business strategy and operating model; a variety of mechanisms can be used to identify, assess and manage those risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Each FI should have a FCRM programme that is proportionate to the level of financial crime risk inherent"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Where national risk assessments, priorities and strategies exist, they set out a country's financial crime risks, threats and vulnerabilities and where public authorities are focusing; FIs can draw on them to shape and prioritise the design of their risk-based FCRM programme while applying proportionality by concentrating on the elements relevant to their own business model. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs can leverage insights from such assessments to inform and prioritise risk-based FCRM programme design"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Group's work on Effectiveness through Collaboration holds that working and talking together produces much better results than isolated initiatives, helping FIs meet their obligations, helping national authorities fight financial crime more effectively, and lowering the risk that the RBA is applied divergently. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collaboration and dialogue will lead to far better outcomes than initiatives pursued in a silo"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Business-wide risk assessments draw on risk data grouped by customers, transactions, geographies, products and delivery channels to evaluate, both quantitatively and qualitatively, the overall risks an FI faces, and can feed an RBA. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use risk-focused data across categories such as customers, transactions, geographies, products and delivery channels"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Targeted risk assessments (for example by customer, industry, country and product) can also be used to segment inherent financial crime risk, commonly into low, medium and high ratings, and to steer risk-based measures such as the level of CDD, approval requirements, review frequency, governance and escalation protocols, and ongoing monitoring for suspicious activity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can also be used to segment inherent financial crime risk (commonly using ratings of low, medium and high)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Innovation and technology should be used to give scalable and effective risk management, quicker responses to business and regulatory demands, more proactivity and better operational efficiency. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Innovation and technology should also be leveraged to deliver scalable, effective risk management"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Prioritisation: an FI should direct its attention and resources to higher-risk customers and activities, which may also mean stopping, scaling back or redesigning existing measures judged redundant, duplicative or unproductive from a risk management standpoint. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI should prioritise attention, and the allocation of resources, to higher risk customers and activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The guidance warns that trying to focus on everything amounts to focusing on nothing; prioritisation lets FIs adapt to a changing risk landscape by steering attention and resources to higher-risk customers and activities and easing effort where risk is lower, based on understanding risk variables about who the customer is and what the customer does. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A focus on everything is a focus on nothing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Industry/business types: FIs should work out the characteristics of customers in potentially higher-risk industries or businesses, with examples given of cash-intensive retail and personal-service businesses, money service businesses and third-party payment processors. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should determine the characteristics of customers operating in potentially higher risk industries or businesses"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Publicly owned and traded customers (legal entity): companies, and their wholly owned subsidiaries, that are publicly owned or listed on a recognised exchange generally carry lower financial crime risk because they usually face multiple disclosure obligations under home-country rules, which may let FIs reduce the intensity and frequency of FCRM measures and controls for them. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "generally present reduced financial crime risk, as they are typically subject to multiple disclosure obligations"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Customers resident, incorporated or doing significant business in higher-risk countries: when prioritising measures and controls, FIs should consider credible assessments of weaknesses in a country's FCRM regime, for example FATF-listed jurisdictions, and whether the customer's exposure to those countries is a relevant risk factor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consider credible assessments of weaknesses in a country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: What the customer does: to understand customer behaviour, FIs should look at how the customer uses products and services, moves value and interacts with the FI over time. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should focus on how a customer uses products and services, moves value, and interacts with the FI over time"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Behavioural risk indicators depend on context and should be judged against the customer's stated financial needs, the purpose of the account, expected activity and known profile. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Behavioural risk indicators are context-dependent and should be assessed against the"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Customer profile: repeated or unusual changes to key customer profile information, especially without a clear business reason, may indicate risk, including evasive or obstructive conduct, unexplained delays in answering customer outreach, incomplete responses, or documentation that is inconsistent or contradictory. By contrast, longstanding relationships can give a better view of behaviour and risk through refreshed CDD, transaction history and regular contact. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "repeated and/or unusual amendments to key customer profile information, particularly where there is no clear business rationale"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Transactional behaviour: unusual activity may indicate risk, particularly where it does not fit the customer's stated account purpose, expected turnover, wealth, transaction size and volume, or business model. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unusual transactional behaviour, particularly where it does not align with the customer"</sub>
- **permissive / typology or red flag**: Consideration may also be given to activity through higher-risk corridors or jurisdictions that does not match the customer's known profile or the markets the customer is expected to transact with. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "activity involving higher-risk corridors or jurisdictions that do not align with the customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Product usage: unusual use of products, particularly without a clear business rationale, may indicate risk; this may include trying to misuse product features or access methods in ways that increase anonymity, add complexity or reduce end-to-end traceability. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "attempts to misuse product features or access methods in a manner that may increase anonymity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FIs must use sound judgement when assessing risk variables, including judging whether customers fit the organisation's risk appetite and adapting controls and measures to the risks of different customer profiles. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs must, however, apply sound judgement when assessing risk variables"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Effectiveness: an FI should concentrate on effective outcomes, in line with the Group's Demonstrating Effectiveness work, enabling a more responsive, forward-looking and dynamic approach to risk management instead of a one-size-fits-all, rules-based-only approach. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI should focus on effective outcomes, as aligned to the Group"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Group's Demonstrating Effectiveness paper builds on the Wolfsberg Factors with further advice on how an FI could assess risk in defined priority areas and show its FCRM programme is effective by focusing more on outcomes; its Auditing for Effectiveness paper sets out the principles and measures internal audit should apply when measuring effectiveness, including governance, adherence to local laws and regulations, design and operating effectiveness of controls, and mechanisms for sharing highly useful information with government agencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provides further guidance on how an FI could assess risk in defined priority areas"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An effective RBA should rest on traceable governance that makes decisions, processes and controls transparent so as to keep the trust of supervisors and auditors; FIs should aim to keep that governance effective and avoid needless complexity that slows decisions or draws resources from core risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An effective RBA should also be underpinned by traceable governance mechanisms"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors and internal or external auditors should set testing standards to fit each FI's business model and risk profile and avoid a one-size-fits-all, rules-based-only approach, so that FIs can show effectiveness through proportionate, prioritised RBA design and implementation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Testing standards should be tailored to each FI"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Training and culture: learning should keep up with technological change and innovation so that staff understand new tools and processes and can use them with confidence to improve financial crime risk management outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Learning should also keep pace with technological change and innovation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Leadership should set a strong tone-from-the-top, promoting the importance of financial crime risk management, leading by example and putting continuous learning first, so that decision-making is genuinely risk-based and FCRM programmes deliver effective outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by promoting the importance of financial crime risk management, leading by example and prioritising continuous learning"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In its Conclusion the guidance calls an RBA essential to an FI's FCRM programme: it keeps resources on the highest-priority risks and threats while stopping, reducing or redesigning activities that do not materially strengthen the fight against financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It ensures resources are focused on the highest-priority risks and threats"</sub>
