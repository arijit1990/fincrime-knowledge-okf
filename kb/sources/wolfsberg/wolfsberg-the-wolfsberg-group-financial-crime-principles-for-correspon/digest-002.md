## 7. Ongoing Review of Correspondent Banking Relationships

- **expected / expectation**: The ongoing review should include measuring the effectiveness of the Respondent's FCC programme on a risk-based approach and seek to determine whether the Respondent can demonstrate that its FCC programme is commensurate to the nature, scale, size and complexity of its business. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "commensurate to the nature, scale, size, and complexity of its business"</sub>

## 8. Integration with Financial Crime Compliance Programme

- **mandatory / requirement**: The Principles shall form an integral component of the institution's wider FCC programme, including controls against bribery and corruption, fraud and evasion of sanctions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These Principles shall form an integral component of the institution"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Group prepared the FAQs to give continuing guidance on Financial Crime controls for Correspondent Banking, based on its views on current best practices and, in some respects, on how it believes those practices should develop over time. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "based on the Group's views on current best practices and, in some respects, on how the Group believes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FAQ 1: Regulators and law enforcement continue to scrutinise due diligence and risk management in Correspondent Banking because of the inherent risks of processing transactions for other FIs and their customers, and documented cases in which Correspondent Banking accounts have been used to move illicit funds. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the documented cases in which Correspondent Banking accounts have been used to move illicit funds"</sub>

## Wolfsberg Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) on Correspondent Banking

- **descriptive / context**: Inter-relationships built over decades within Correspondent Banking networks form a mechanism of fundamental importance to the global economy, facilitating movement of money between persons and entities and between countries, and currency conversion. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a mechanism which is of fundamental importance to the global economy"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The efficiency of the correspondent banking mechanism may unintentionally facilitate those who seek to launder the proceeds of financial crime or to finance terrorism and other unlawful activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also unintentionally facilitate the activities of those who seek to launder the proceeds of financial crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Law enforcement and regulatory actions have resulted in significant financial penalties and highlighted the vulnerabilities FIs face when there are failures in the risk management framework, particularly in governance, customer due diligence, risk assessment and transaction monitoring. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement and regulatory actions have resulted in significant financial penalties"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Correspondent Bank accounts have been used to move the proceeds of illicit activity and to evade sanctions; this misuse highlights the need for proactive vigilance in maintaining an effective FCC programme for Correspondent Banking and will mean a continued heavy focus by regulators and law enforcement. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "highlights the need for proactive vigilance in maintaining an effective FCC programme for Correspondent Banking"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In dealing with Respondents, the Bank (the "Institution" in the FAQs) acts as its Respondent's agent or conduit, executing payments or other transactions for the Respondent's customers (individuals, legal entities or other FIs), and the beneficiaries may be customers of the Institution or of other FIs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is acting as its Respondent's agent or conduit, executing payments or other transactions for the Respondent's customers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 2: The Principles apply where a Respondent is a branch, affiliate or subsidiary of an existing Respondent customer; the risk factors applied to a Respondent need to be applied equally to their branches, affiliates and subsidiaries when they are direct customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The risk factors that are applied to a Respondent need to be applied equally to their branches, affiliates and subsidiaries"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: While it may be possible to be informed by the Head Office's FCC programme, branches, affiliates and subsidiaries may possess their own unique customer, product and geographic risks which must be considered. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those entities may possess their own unique customer, product and geographic risks which must be considered"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The net result is that the financial crime risk of a branch, affiliate or subsidiary may differ from the overall assessed risks of the parent. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the financial crime risk of a branch, affiliate or subsidiary may differ from the overall assessed risks of the parent"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Regulations in some jurisdictions set CDD standards and treat each distinct account holder as a customer; to the extent branches, affiliates and subsidiaries maintain their own accounts with the Institution, they may be subject to the regulatory requirements applicable to all account holders/customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "treat each distinct account holder as a customer for purposes of compliance with applicable requirements"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The level and scope of due diligence on such an affiliated customer shall be dependent upon the level of control exercised by the Institution's parent. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shall be dependent upon the level of control exercised by the Institution"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: An Institution's branches, subsidiaries and affiliates may engage in business with customer types posing varying levels of risk and may operate in a jurisdiction that is higher-risk for money laundering and corruption. Other risks to consider include additional controlling owners, differences in application of the Institution's FCC programme, and adverse information about the customer's FCC controls. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the presence of additional controlling owners, differences in application of the Institution"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Adverse information about an affiliated entity may not be limited to the public domain, as the Institution may hold internal information, such as results of internal audits or regulatory examinations, indicating potentially heightened risk associated with a branch, subsidiary or affiliate receiving Correspondent Banking services. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as the results of internal audits or regulatory examinations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The reasons given: although these institutions share the same currency they operate within different sovereign nations that may pose specific geographic risks, and notwithstanding the EU AML Directive(s), country-specific regulation and the strength of AML enforcement regimes may vary between EU member countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "country-specific regulation and the strength of AML enforcement regimes may vary between EU member countries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Institutions need to ensure they apply the Principles to Correspondent Banking relationships with other EU member institutions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutions need to ensure they apply the Principles to Correspondent Banking relationships with other EU member institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FAQ 5: It is not within the scope of the Group's work to advocate a general avoidance policy with respect to relationships with higher-risk Respondents. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not within the scope of the Group’s work to advocate a general avoidance policy"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Each Institution should assess their Respondents within the context of its own risk parameters and its ability to manage the risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Each Institution should assess their Respondents within the context of its own risk parameters"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Some relationships should be avoided, including relationships with shell banks; Institutions should also exercise care to ensure they do not knowingly deal with FIs which themselves deal with shell banks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutions should also exercise care to ensure that they do not knowingly deal with FIs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Relationships that should be avoided also include those with unlicensed and/or unregulated banks or NBFIs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Unlicensed and/or unregulated banks or NBFIs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Relationships that should be avoided also include any Respondent where the results of due diligence produce significant concerns that cannot be resolved. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any Respondent where the results from conducting due diligence produce significant concerns that cannot be resolved"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FAQ 6: The FATF recommendations define payable-through accounts as correspondent accounts that are used directly by third parties to transact business on their own behalf; the Institution allows its Respondent's accounts to be accessed directly by the Respondent's customers, e.g. with cheque writing privileges or the ability to give transaction instructions directly to the Institution. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "correspondent accounts that are used directly by third parties to transact business on their own behalf"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A payable-through arrangement differs from a traditional Correspondent Banking relationship, in which the Respondent executes transactions on behalf of its customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is different than a traditional Correspondent Banking relationship in which the Respondent is executing transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Payable-through arrangements pose greater risk to an Institution if it does not have access to information about the third parties accessing the account. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The arrangements pose greater risk to an Institution if it does not have access to information about the third parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FAQ 7: The Wolfsberg CBDDQ has been designed to provide a reasonable and enhanced view of a FI's FCC policies and practices, covering a Respondent's control environment so that Institutions gain a greater understanding of the cross-border and/or other higher-risk Correspondent Banking relationship they are entering into and/or continuing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designed to provide a reasonable and enhanced view of a FI"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Institutions should use the CBDDQ as part of their FCC programme's due diligence requirements for a particular Respondent. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutions should use the CBDDQ as part of their FCC programme"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 8: The Institution should set expectations of the Respondent, including partnership (clear and open communication related to FCC matters and provision of complete and accurate due diligence information). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clear and open communication related to FCC matters and the provision of complete and accurate due diligence information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Institution's expectations of the Respondent should include timely and fulsome responses to inquiries related to transactions in the account. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "timely and fulsome responses to inquiries related to transactions in the account"</sub>

## Footnotes

- **descriptive / definition**: Per the FATF Recommendations Glossary, a Shell bank is a bank with no physical presence in the country where it is incorporated and licensed and which is unaffiliated with a regulated financial group subject to effective consolidated supervision; physical presence means meaningful mind and management located within a country, and a local agent or low-level staff alone does not constitute physical presence. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a bank that has no physical presence in the country in which it is incorporated and licensed"</sub>

## 6. Monitoring and Reporting of Suspicious Activities

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under the Principles, an Institution's policies and procedures shall call for suitable monitoring of Respondent activity that draws on due diligence outputs such as the customer risk rating and other factors that matter for assessing transaction activity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shall require appropriate monitoring of the Respondent"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Due diligence information and transaction monitoring shall be linked on a continuous basis for as long as the Respondent relationship lasts, covering both the Respondent and any related suspect activity; this two-way link is commonly called the feedback loop. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shall be continuous throughout the life of the Respondent relationship"</sub>

## 7. Ongoing Review of Correspondent Banking Relationships

- **mandatory / requirement**: Institutions shall review each Respondent relationship on an ongoing basis in order to assess whether the relationship remains within the Institution's risk appetite. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutions shall review the relationship with the Respondent on an ongoing basis to assess whether"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When the risk-based review tests how effective the Respondent's FCC programme is, it should look at whether the Respondent can itself manage and detect financial crime risks in the transactions of its underlying customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The risk-based review to determine effectiveness of the Respondent"</sub>
- **permissive / data or feature**: Information that may be drawn on in the ongoing review includes: whether suspicious activity reports exist on the Respondent and its underlying customers and what they say; material adverse media; activity inconsistent with due diligence information or expected activity; material or unexplained shifts in transaction volume or value; failure to comply with payment transparency rules; sanctions metrics; and no response or an inadequate response to information requests. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "material/unexplained changes in volume and/or value of transactions, non-compliance with payment transparency rules"</sub>

## Wolfsberg Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) on Correspondent Banking

- **descriptive / limitation**: The FAQs state that the Institution generally has no direct relationship with the parties behind a transaction routed through it, so it cannot verify their identity or fully understand the specific transaction, especially for electronic wire transfers or cheque clearing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "typically has no direct relationship with the underlying parties to any transaction routed through it"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: FAQ 2 illustrates that a branch of a Respondent can carry its own risks: it might focus on a high-risk customer segment or higher-risk products, operate in a jurisdiction with higher corruption and money laundering risk, have attracted material negative media about its financial crime compliance, or work under an offshore banking license. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a particular branch may target a high-risk customer segment or offer higher-risk products"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FAQ 2 explains that local regulation and the degree of control and oversight exercised by the parent bank can shape how a subsidiary runs its FCC programme; for instance, a subsidiary that the Respondent parent does not wholly own may have other controlling owners and work to different FCC standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subsidiaries not wholly owned by the Respondent parent may have other controlling owners"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 3 answers yes to whether an Institution offering Correspondent Banking services should treat its own branches, subsidiaries and affiliates as distinct customers subject to the Principles, because the inherent risk of the relationship is the same when the service goes to its own group entities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The inherent risks of Correspondent Banking relationship do not differ when the service is offered to one"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An Institution that provides Correspondent Banking to its own branches, subsidiaries and affiliates should make sure its FCC programme can assess the risks of that customer using every relevant risk measure available inside the Institution, and that suitable transaction monitoring and suspicious activity reporting are in place. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should ensure that their FCC programme is designed to assess the risks of the customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 4 states that an EU-based Institution providing Euro clearing to other EU institutions should handle those customers as Foreign Correspondent Banking relationships. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Institution should treat these customers as Foreign Correspondent Banking relationships"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FAQ 5 says that once higher risks are identified, an Institution may decide, together with the Respondent, whether the risk can be brought to an acceptable level, which might involve restricting certain transactions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an Institution may decide, working with the Respondent, whether they can mitigate the risk to an acceptable level"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 5 lists, among relationships that should be avoided, those where the Respondent's FCC controls are judged inappropriate or insufficient and the Respondent fails to convince the Institution that the needed remedial action will be taken. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not satisfy the Institution that necessary remedial action will be undertaken"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FAQ 7 says Institutions remain responsible for designing their FCC programmes to meet regulatory requirements or expectations and internal risk management standards, and so decide exactly how the CBDDQ is used; detailed guidance on the Questionnaire is on the Wolfsberg website. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutions are responsible for ensuring their FCC programmes are designed to meet regulatory requirements/expectations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 8 says the Institution should expect the Respondent to understand the Institution's risk appetite and to comply with any restrictions that have been communicated. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and compliance with any restrictions communicated"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FAQ 8 says the Institution should keep assessing, on an ongoing basis and not only at periodic reviews, how far the Respondent meets the expectations set for it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Institution should assess the level of compliance with these expectations on an ongoing basis"</sub>
