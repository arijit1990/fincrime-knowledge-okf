## Introduction

- **expected / expectation**: Manual overrides and/or discretionary rating changes to FCCR outputs may be envisaged only in very limited circumstances; they should be strictly limited in number and subject to a stringent justification and governance process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these should be strictly limited in number and subject to a stringent justification and governance process"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Overriding FCCR ratings derived from credible sources can present significant risks (not limited to those listed). Interfering with the FCCR methodology and results puts the integrity of the methodology at risk, and recurring overrides are typically an indication that the methodology is not performing as intended, has limitations, or has a scope and purpose that are not well defined. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recurring overrides are typically an indication that, in some respect, the methodology is not performing as intended"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Excluding or ignoring the objective risk input criteria behind a methodology-produced rating, without comprehensive documentation of why, exposes the FI to regulatory, compliance, operational and reputational risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exposes the FI to regulatory, compliance, operational and reputational risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Example given: despite information on a country's well-known primary risk of drug trafficking or corruption and a methodology producing a 'high' rating, an FI approves a lower FCCR because the country's AML/CTF regulatory regime is considered acceptable. Customers from that country would then potentially not be subject to enhanced transaction monitoring or EDD, which could result in the FI failing to identify, and thereby possibly facilitating, the flow of illicit funds. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FI approves a lower FCCR due to the fact that the country’s AML/CTF regulatory regime is considered acceptable"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Failing to ensure uniform compliance with risk rating standards through complete internal reviews is a risk of overrides: reviewing and approving override requests in a vacuum and/or as ad-hoc requests could potentially result in inconsistent application of ratings across similarly situated and risk rated countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reviewing and approving override requests in a vacuum and/or as part of an ad-hoc request"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The more discretion an FI exercises in overriding, and the greater the number of employees/management permitted to exercise override authority, the greater the interference with and potential undermining of the methodology. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The more discretion an FI exercises in overriding and the greater the number of employees/management permitted to exercise override authority"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: It is imperative that override discretion is limited, properly controlled, and governed by the appropriate risk control or financial crime compliance function. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is imperative that discretion is limited, properly controlled, and governed by the appropriate risk control"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Whenever high-risk factors are present, the higher and more independent the override approval obtained for the decision, the better. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the higher and more independent the override approval obtained for the decision, the better"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Individual countries may need to check for specific local regulatory requirements to be considered in any FCCR methodology. If there are local regulatory requirements, they should be brought to the attention of the methodology owner for submission as a dispensation or a localisation process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these should be brought to the attention of the methodology owner for submission as a dispensation or a localisation process"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Any change request arising from local regulatory requirements should have a clear rationale (a copy of the local regulatory requirements) and appropriate financial crime compliance sponsorship, and all approvals should be documented as per the methodology governance process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any change request should have clear rationale (copy of the local regulatory requirements) and appropriate financial crime compliance sponsorship"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Overrides or discretionary risk rating changes should be very limited. To minimise the risk of undermining the methodology, supporting documentation should include, as appropriate, a detailed risk-based rationale for the override/change and details of how the residual risk of not complying with the ratings generated by the approved methodology will be mitigated. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supporting documentation should include, as appropriate, a detailed risk-based rationale for the override/change"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q13: The FCCR methodology should be owned centrally by a group-level unit independent from the business. Depending on the FI's organisational structure, an independent financial crime compliance, AML or other independent risk function would be an appropriate owner and approver, given that function's regulatory responsibility and the overarching governance and control principles underpinning it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FCCR methodology should be owned centrally by a group-level unit independent from the business"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Operational maintenance of the FCCR methodology may be delegated to another relevant unit outside the owning function (e.g. an intelligence or analytics unit), which nonetheless takes direction from, and/or reports into, the FCCR methodology owner. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "operational maintenance may be delegated to another relevant unit outside the function"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The unit that owns and/or maintains the FCCR methodology needs to be sufficiently resourced with appropriate regulatory and financial crime SMEs, who should have a profound understanding of the conceptual framework of, and methodologies associated with, country risk assessment, the data sources considered, the inter-linkages of country risk assessment with other internal AML principles and methodologies, and relevant information and industry trends. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "needs to be sufficiently resourced with appropriate regulatory and financial crime SMEs"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Analytical and technical modelling skills, including statistical expertise, serve as the basis for developing a robust FCCR methodology. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Analytical and technical modelling skills, including statistical expertise, serve as basis for the development of a robust methodology"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The unit owning the FCCR methodology should also have a good understanding of the geographic footprint of the FI's locations and customer base so that it can address any local regulatory requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "good understanding of the geographic footprint of the FI’s locations and customer base"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Q14: FCCR ratings have multiple users across an FI, including the Lines of Business, which should understand and evaluate the specific risks of doing business in, opening accounts for customers from, or facilitating transactions involving different countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There are multiple users of FCCR ratings across an FI, including the Lines of Business"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FCCR ratings feed, along with other factors, the FI's Customer Risk Assessment framework, which rates a customer so that appropriate levels of CDD are undertaken and their transactions or activities monitored accordingly. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCCR ratings will feed (along with other factors) the FI’s Customer Risk Assessment framework"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FCCR ratings should be considered when undertaking Enterprise Wide Risk Assessments (including the assessment of sanctions and bribery and corruption risks) and when setting thresholds and scenarios for transaction monitoring systems. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FCCR ratings should be considered when undertaking Enterprise Wide Risk Assessments"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FCCR ratings may be considered in other processes, e.g. business strategy decisions, operational risks processes, and third party and vendor management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be considered in other processes (e.g. business strategy decisions, operational risks processes, third party and vendor management, etc.)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Any delay beyond the set implementation date, or deviation from the FCCR ratings, should be agreed in line with the FI's financial crime compliance policy or procedures. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any delay beyond the date set or deviation from the FCCR ratings should be agreed in line with the FI’s"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Records should be maintained of the ratings, all changes, the rationale/evidence for any deviations, and the date they were enacted in each customer/transaction system. As part of the methodology implementation governance process, details of the systems into which the ratings are input locally should also be maintained. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Records of the ratings, all changes and the rationale/evidence for any deviations as well as the date they were enacted"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The records and system-details process is designed to ensure that FCCR ratings are implemented in relevant systems within the prescribed timeline and that an assurance process exists so that they are applied consistently across relevant systems, Lines of Business and countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "applied consistently across relevant systems, Lines of Business and countries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q15: In principle, CDD requirements applied to mitigate ML/TF risks should correlate with the level of customer risk determined by each FI's customer risk rating framework, of which country risk is one element. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CDD requirements that have to be applied to mitigate money laundering/terrorist financing (ML/TF) risks should correlate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: EDD is applied to mitigate risk associated with relationships with "higher-risk" customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "EDD is applied to mitigate risk associated with relationships with "higher-risk" customers"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: FIs may be required to implement local regulatory requirements for certain countries, e.g. countries subject to FATF's call for action (black list), countries subject to FATF's increased monitoring (grey list), or similar lists issued by competent authorities of jurisdictions posing heightened risk due to strategic deficiencies in their AML/CTF regimes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may be required to implement local regulatory requirements for certain countries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Country risk is one factor among many that an FI may consider when evaluating customer risk, and FIs should determine when exposure to a country should be a relevant factor in assessing ML/TF risk level. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should determine when exposure to a country should be relevant factor in the assessment of ML/TF risk level"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Relevant risk factors should be taken into account when determining the CDD requirements, both at on-boarding and at periodic review, which may in turn determine any appropriate EDD requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Relevant risk factors should be taken into account when determining the respective requirements for CDD, both at on-boarding"</sub>
- **recommended / expectation**: Q16: FIs should consider the level of granularity they require from the country risk assessment output, which will depend on how the methodology outputs are used in financial crime compliance processes and systems. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should consider the level of granularity they require from the country risk assessment output"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Output formats vary: some FIs use a country score for more precise risk differentiation (e.g. a numerical score in a customer risk assessment engine or transaction monitoring system); others use a country risk rating (e.g. low, medium, high, higher risk) that manual processes such as PEP due diligence can use more easily. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some FIs may use a country score as it gives them a more precise level of risk differentiation"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Others may prefer a traffic light symbol or colour (e.g. red/amber/green) as a more visual indication for individual users. The output can include other elements giving greater insight, e.g. a negative/positive trend direction or comments about disputed territories associated with the country. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "traffic light symbol or colour (e.g. red/amber/green)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FCCR ratings are usually considered in portfolio analysis (what percentage of an FI's customer base or customer assets derives from high risk countries), noting that the list of activities is not exhaustive. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Portfolio analysis: what percentage of an FI’s customer base, or overall customer assets, are derived from high risk countries?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FCCR ratings are usually considered in due diligence requirements, i.e. whether CDD or EDD should be applied, including the related approval process to accept certain levels of risk and the appropriate level of seniority (refer to Question 15). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Due diligence requirements: should CDD or EDD be applied?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FCCR ratings are usually considered in transaction monitoring: to what extent, at what frequency, at which thresholds and against which scenarios. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transaction monitoring: to what extent, at what frequency, at which thresholds, against which scenarios?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FCCR ratings are usually considered in risk appetite (how much risk an FI is prepared to accept) and in periodic or event-driven KYC review processes (how often reviews should be conducted, to what level and by whom). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Periodic or event-driven KYC review processes: how often should periodic reviews be conducted, to what level and by whom?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FCCR rating becomes a driver, amongst other risk factors, for a number of processes that are core elements of any financial crime control framework in a risk-based control environment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCCR rating becomes a driver, amongst other risk factors, for a number of processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FCCR rating methodologies only determine ratings that define the risk of a particular country. How an FI manages that risk through its controls, their effectiveness, and its iterative understanding of using country risk in the context of risk appetite and risk acceptance further determines considerations such as presence in, acceptance of customers from, or transactions to and from any given country. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FCCR rating methodologies will only determine ratings that define the risk of a particular country"</sub>

## Footnotes

- **permissive / context**: A footnote states that FIs may consider tax havens and offshore jurisdictions (Offshore Financial Centers or OFCs), such as the EU list of Non-Cooperative Jurisdictions for tax purposes, and refers to Question 8 for how FIs should determine countries and OFCs in scope. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may consider tax havens and offshore jurisdictions (so called “Offshore Financial Centers” or OFCs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: A footnote notes that national authorities publish many analyses, varying in prescriptiveness and impact, which taken as a whole can lead FIs to quite different conclusions; for Global FIs the challenge is managing the impact of different national risk assessment results, notably in home regulator markets, across their operations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs can potentially be led to reach quite different conclusions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote defines a third party in the context of methodology development as someone who may be indirectly involved but is not a principal party to development, i.e. has the competence to understand the content while maintaining sufficient independence to challenge relevant parts of the development process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "somebody who has the competence to understand the content, while maintaining sufficient independence to challenge relevant parts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote states that not every input to the methodology needs to be weighted equally. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Not every input needs to be weighted equally."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote states that ISO 3166 defines internationally recognised codes for countries and their subdivisions but does not define country names, which come from United Nations sources; country codes can be two-letter (alpha-2, recommended as the general-purpose code), three-letter (alpha-3, more closely related to the country name) or a three-digit numeric code. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a two-letter code (alpha-2) which is recommended as the general-purpose code"</sub>

## Introduction

- **expected / expectation**: Any approved exception to an FCCR rating should be given an expiry date and reviewed at least once a year, or sooner if significant changes in regulatory guidance or geographic developments call for it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Approved exceptions should be assigned an expiration date and be required to be reviewed at least annually"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Although an independent unit should own and maintain the FCCR methodology, the guidance recommends involving relevant subject matter experts and stakeholders (business lines, other risk functions, back office functions) when the methodology is developed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is recommended that relevant SMEs and stakeholders, such as business lines, other risk functions and relevant back office functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Q15 lists examples of where country exposure may matter to a customer's ML/TF risk: the customer's country of residence, incorporation or formation, and the country from which the customer's or beneficial owner's wealth originates. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The customer’s country of residence, incorporation, or formation and the country of origin of the customer’s"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Q15 further lists as relevant country exposures: the country where the customer's funds originate; the countries involved in a transaction, whether one-off or part of payments and services in a business relationship; specific industries, sectors and business activities that may be higher risk depending on the country of exposure; and the country of political exposure for a PEP. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The country of origin of the customer’s source of funds"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Wolfsberg states that, for customer risk rating and other control processes where applicable, FIs may take into account the country of the booking entity, including the FI's home jurisdiction, and what this means for the underlying customer exposure. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may consider the country of the booking entity (including the FI’s home jurisdiction)"</sub>

## Footnotes

- **descriptive / context**: A footnote records that the FATF's 2021 paper on Risk Based Supervision stressed that an approach tolerating no imperfections, especially in areas assessed as lower risk, works against an effective AML/CTF system and against risk-based supervision. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "zero-tolerance approach that does not tolerate imperfections, particularly in areas identified to pose lower risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote explains model sensitivity as an analysis of how far a country's final score shifts when the data fed into the model is varied across realistic and unrealistic changes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Model sensitivity analyses how much a country’s final score moves based on a range of changes"</sub>
