## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group issued a Country Risk FAQs document in 2018 on how FIs should capture country risk in their AML/CTF framework; it has updated the FAQs based on members' current best practices and to suggest how it believes those practices should develop over time, and the 2018 version has been retired. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The prior version of these FAQs, published in 2018, has now been retired."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state that country risk is one factor among many that an FI may consider in assessing a customer's risk; country ratings, with other factors, inform the customer risk assessment framework so appropriate CDD is undertaken and transactions or activities are monitored in that context, so country risk does not by itself determine the risk of a customer. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "country risk does not, by itself, determine the risk of a customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs note significant debate on whether financial crime country risk assessments should be considered a model, a methodology, a tool or an application (see Question 5), and use the term methodology for consistency unless explicitly referring to a model. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These FAQs use the term methodology for consistency"</sub>

## Q1. What is country risk in the context of financial crime compliance?

- **descriptive / definition**: The FAQs explain that FIs typically used 'country risk' to mean the additional risk of investing in, or lending cross border to, a foreign country in the context of credit facilities; with the risk-based approach, FIs identified country risk factors as relevant to assessing a customer's financial crime risk, which could include domicile, country of incorporation, centre of activity or other nexus such as country of tax residency. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Country-related factors could include a customer’s domicile, country of incorporation, centre of activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FAQs define Financial Crime Country Risk (FCCR) as country risk that seeks to assess the residual level of financial crime in a country after considering the inherent risk and the effectiveness of the country's AML/CTF framework. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which seeks to assess the residual level of financial crime in that country after considering the inherent risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state that an elevated FCCR should not necessarily preclude business being undertaken in a country, but FIs may need to consider whether their control environment is appropriately robust to manage that risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an elevated FCCR should not necessarily preclude business being undertaken in any particular country"</sub>
- **permissive / typology or red flag**: The FAQs state the elements underlying an FCCR methodology may cover the nature and extent of money laundering, sanctions, proliferation and terrorism financing, presence of known terrorist organisations, corruption, significant narcotics production and/or laundering of drug trafficking proceeds, tax evasion risks and human trafficking risks, amongst others. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The nature and extent of money laundering, sanctions, proliferation and terrorism financing"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The FAQs state FCCR elements may also cover factors influencing the effectiveness of a country's AML/CTF measures (including maturity and sophistication of the regulatory and supervisory regime), legal AML/CTF frameworks (e.g. as assessed by FATF or FSRBs in Mutual Evaluation Reports), structural elements (political stability, robust governance, high-level commitment to address AML/CTF, indicators of financial secrecy), and geographic nexus risks such as trading or cultural links, particularly for sanctions and terrorism financing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "what are commonly known as nexus risks, where geographic proximity may drive a higher risk rating"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FAQs state an FCCR rating seeks to determine the overall level of financial crime risk in a country by assessing the threats and mitigants relevant to FIs; it does not assess an FI's direct risk profile in that country, and how a customer or FI is vulnerable to that risk profile is measured using other risk rating methodologies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is not assessing an FI’s direct risk profile in that country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state an FI's country risk assessment should be iterative and evolve dynamically subject to external factors, requiring regular review to ensure appropriateness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be iterative and evolve dynamically subject to external factors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that once an FI has defined its FCCR methodology and validated it through normal governance, it should be used as widely and consistently as possible to inform decisions where country risk is a required factor; generally it is used across all business lines and activities, and any exceptions should only be granted under a robust governance process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should be used as widely and consistently as possible to inform decisions where country risk is a required factor"</sub>

## Q2. What data sources should be considered when developing a methodology to assess country risk?

- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state most FCCR assessments incorporate both subjective (analytical/qualitative) and objective (numeric/quantitative) data; data sets are usually purchased from external vendors or list/index providers and considered alongside the FI's experience of operating in countries and other relevant factors to determine the overall risk rating. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most assessments of FCCR incorporate both subjective (analytical/research based or qualitative) and objective"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The FAQs list Political Factors (political stability, regulatory effectiveness, levels of democratisation, rule of law, human rights, freedom of the press, civil liberties) and Regulatory Factors (expectations from home and host regulators on categorising countries, and how AML/CTF regimes are assessed by third parties such as FATF MERs) as risk dimensions for quantitative sources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "expectations from home and host regulators on how to categorise countries from a financial crime risk management perspective"</sub>
- **permissive / data or feature**: The FAQs state FIs may use other qualitative data such as national risk assessments and threat priorities, and note sanctions-related aspects are covered in Questions 3 and 4. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may use other qualitative data, such as national risk assessments"</sub>
- **permissive / data or feature**: The FAQs state that, depending on methodology, an FI may also wish to consider economic indicators (GDP, inflation and debt levels, ease of doing business, global competitiveness, unemployment), noting these may be covered by the FI's sovereign credit risk rating and so should be at the FI's discretion. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may also wish to consider economic indicators, such as gross domestic product"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state the most important criteria for choosing sources are reputability, credibility, relevance and quality of the sources and publishing institution, and sources with no editorial process, or with serious allegations against or challenges to their content (including completeness, data quality and number of countries covered), should be avoided. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sources with no editorial process or with serious allegations against, or challenges to, their content"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that, irrespective of methodology, the impact of changes in any data source on the country risk score/rating should be clearly identifiable and understandable for internal and external stakeholders. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the impact of changes in any data source to the country risk score/rating should be clearly identifiable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that an analysis of sources should be undertaken (including data quality checks that sources are reliable and data current), combined with the FI's own experiential judgment supported by a robust rationale, to yield a score. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an analysis of those sources should be undertaken (including data quality checks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state care should be taken to ensure that sources or qualitative considerations do not duplicate data inputs already used, so as not to skew the data through double-counting. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "so as not to skew the data through double-counting"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that the choice of sources, the balance between subjective and objective elements, the evaluation process and any decisions affecting financial crime compliance outcomes should be documented, and significant deviations from objective data sets should be clearly articulated and explained. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the balance between subjective and objective elements, the process for carrying out the evaluation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state the final determination of FCCR ratings and methodology should be approved by an authorised senior manager or appropriate governance forum with financial crime compliance knowledge and sufficient seniority to satisfy regulatory scrutiny, which should review the methodology, agree deviations and approve final ratings. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be approved by an authorised senior manager or appropriate governance forum"</sub>

## Q3. How often should data sources be refreshed and country risk ratings reviewed?

- **permissive / recommendation**: The FAQs state consideration may be given to revising ratings on an interim/ad-hoc basis when key trigger data with material impact on the rating are released. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consideration may be given to revising ratings on an interim/ad-hoc basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The FAQs give as examples of interim review triggers: FATF public statements showing material deficiencies in a country's AML/CTF framework (FIs could also periodically review FATF and FSRB MERs, which include technical compliance and effectiveness ratings), and countries becoming subject to sanctions or embargoes such as UN Security Council sanctions, OFAC Sanctions Programs, EU financial sanctions or national measures such as UK HMT. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries becoming subject to sanctions or embargoes"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The FAQs give as further examples of interim review triggers: regime change through a coup d'etat or significant political unrest relevant to the FI's operations; relevant changes to the FI's regulatory framework or legislation (such as authorities issuing lists of countries posing heightened risk due to strategic deficiencies); and publication of or amendment to a national risk assessment in a country where the FI has a significant presence. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regime change through a coup d’état or significant political unrest"</sub>

## Q4. How should sanctions be considered in country risk methodologies?

- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state that in most cases FIs default sanctioned regimes to the highest risk rating in their country risk methodology, and regulators generally expect relationships with customers associated with sanctioned countries, territories or regions to be subject to the highest levels of due diligence and approvals. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs will default sanctioned regimes to the highest risk rating in their country risk methodology"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state FIs should develop a methodology and decide how it will be used, such as in manual and automated financial crime prevention processes, and may want to consider the reasons why countries/territories are sanctioned, the risks present and how they link to financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should develop a methodology and decide how it will be used"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state the general practice is for both sanctions and money laundering to be included in an FCCR methodology, and FIs should be prepared to present separate or combined country risk assessments (e.g. money laundering, sanctions, bribery and corruption) depending on regulatory, operational and governance drivers, factoring in availability of data from trustworthy sources and timeliness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The general practice is for both sanctions and money laundering to be included in a FCCR methodology"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The FAQs state that if the FI decides not to use its FCCR methodology for sanctions compliance, it may decide to include fewer sanctions-related data points in the methodology. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may decide to include fewer sanctions-related data points in the methodology"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state an FI should determine whether comprehensive and/or sectoral sanctions may be integrated into the methodology's logic, and consider the effect of including different sanction types on downstream processes and systems. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FI should determine whether comprehensive and/or sectoral sanctions may be integrated"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state Specially Designated National (SDN) sanctions are not considered to have geographical relevance as they are specific to individual persons or entities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Specially Designated National (SDN) sanctions are not considered to have geographical relevance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state that where sanctions are specific to a market segment or activity (sectoral sanctions), a more appropriate methodology may exist; for example, if sanctions ban the supply of petroleum and related products from a country, the risk may be addressed more effectively by focusing on the FI's customers in specific industries rather than a blanket country approach. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "focussing on the FI’s customers in specific industries rather than a blanket approach"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The FAQs state an FI can pursue a broader approach by considering relevant economic ties between non-sanctioned and sanctioned countries, capturing the risk of an economic partner being exposed to a sanctioned country's risks; this element may be more subjective than data-driven because not all trading partners face the same risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "considering relevant economic ties between non-sanctioned and sanctioned countries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state care should be taken not to determine that all countries bordering sanctioned countries become sanctions-nexus countries by default. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Care should be taken not to determine that all the countries bordering on sanctioned countries"</sub>

## Q5. What methodologies are available to FIs to measure country risk?

- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state there is ongoing debate among financial crime SMEs and regulators on whether FCCR assessment should be considered a model, methodology, tool or application, and no industry standard on whether the different means of measuring financial crime risk (e.g. FCCR or customer risk assessment) are models, though some countries have issued their own guidance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no industry standard as to whether the different means of measuring financial crime risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that FCCR methodologies, while not subject to the same quantitatively focused independent model review as credit models, should be subjected to a robust governance process including a form of independent review and validation by competent third parties. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be subjected to a robust governance process, including a form of independent review and validation by competent third parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group's view is that whether an FI's FCCR rating mechanism is a model, a methodology or something else should be determined by the FI's governance protocols and modelling standards and any relevant regulatory requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be determined by the governance protocols and modelling standards of each individual FI"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FAQs state most internal FCCR indices and off-the-shelf vendor products include a composite index expressing FCCR as a number (e.g. ascending values from 0 to 10 for increasing risk) or category (e.g. red/amber/green; low, medium, high, higher risk). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "include a composite index to express FCCR as a number"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state most FIs do not use off-the-shelf FCCR products as their sole approach; vendor outputs may be used as data sources, as a basis of comparison for the FI's proprietary index, or as an input to a customised index. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most FIs do not use such products as their sole approach to measuring FCCR"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The FAQs state that regardless of approach, any methodology adopted by an FI must, at minimum, use publicly available data sources for its assessment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any methodology adopted by an FI must, at minimum, use publicly available data sources"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The FAQs state methodologies represent data-driven consistent application across the FI's footprint with minimal risk of interpretation issues, and FIs may elect to vary the application on an exceptional basis (e.g. driven by local knowledge or information, organisational and geographical footprint) subject to appropriate oversight and governance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may elect to vary the application of the methodology on an exceptional basis"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that, since there is no universally agreed approach to FCCR, many FIs and vendors integrate experiential judgment or SME expertise to sense check outputs, and governance committees should use institutional experience (in limited circumstances only, see Question 12) to ensure individual ratings are valid, plausible and capture the latest developments. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Governance committees within FIs should utilise their institutional experience"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FAQs state that, depending on the use of subjectivity in the rating process, the FCCR assessment may not be formally considered a 'model' in the same way as a more purely quantitative model. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCCR assessment may not be formally considered a “model”"</sub>

## Q6. What should FIs consider if they choose to purchase and use an off-the-shelf commercial product to determine their FCCR ratings?

- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that an FI considering using only a vendor product should first become familiar with the vendor's data sources, methodologies, modelling parameters and validation processes used to determine country risk ratings. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should first become familiar with the choice of data sources, methodologies, modelling parameters and validation processes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state FIs should, consistent with their risk appetite, be satisfied at a minimum that the vendor product has produced a documented set of FCCR ratings consistent with how the FI considers country risk, includes the risk parameters or dimensions covered in Question 2, and that the information used by the vendor is refreshed periodically. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The vendor’s product includes the risk parameters or dimensions covered in Question 2"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state FIs should ensure the vendor can produce documentation rationalising its choice of data sources, methodologies, modelling parameters and validation processes so the FI and any authorised third party understand how ratings were achieved, and the FI should confirm this documentation may be shared with supervisors, as needed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI should confirm that this documentation may be shared with supervisors, as needed."</sub>

## Q7. Is there a standard/conventional methodology to assess country risk?

- **descriptive / method**: The FAQs list approaches for assessing FCCR which may be combined or used independently: a statistically-based methodology using statistical tools to combine lists to a final score or classification; a weighted average score in which the FI's experts assign a weight to each input category or list; and a zero-risk approach which starts from zero risk and adds negative risk factors. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this methodology assumes a starting point of zero risk and adds negative risk factors to the score"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FAQs state the result of an FCCR assessment is usually expressed as a number (e.g. ascending values 0 to 10) or a category (e.g. red/amber/green; low, medium, high, higher risk). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Usually, the result of any assessment is expressed either as a number"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that common features across approaches are that an FI should identify the data inputs and output categories it wants to consider, and that a clearly articulated process should be implemented to 'normalise' different data lists into a numeric point scale, using mathematical algorithms (including different types of scaling) so long as they are coherent and robust with respect to the other rating parameters. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a clearly articulated process to “normalise” the different lists into a numeric point scale should be implemented"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state SMEs should review and approve the outputs of any methodology, and regardless of approach the means of conducting the assessment should be appropriately documented so the rationale can be clearly understood. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SMEs should review and approve the outputs of any methodology"</sub>

## Q8. How should FIs determine countries in scope for assessment?

- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state FIs can select the number of countries for assessment based on risk appetite, size, geographical presence and products and services (e.g. cross-border transactions), but given the global interdependent economy FIs should take into account the means by which they are connected across multiple jurisdictions and their global business networks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should take into account the means by which they are connected across multiple jurisdictions"</sub>
- **permissive / typology or red flag**: The FAQs state Free-Trade Zones (FTZs) or Offshore Financial Centers (OFCs) within the same jurisdiction may introduce elevated risks at a sub-national level, and an FI's assessment could aim to apply a targeted risk rating for FTZs/OFCs deemed equivalent or higher-risk than their parent countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Free-Trade Zones (FTZs) or Offshore Financial Centers (OFCs) within the same jurisdiction may introduce elevated risks"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The FAQs state FTZ/OFC risks could be mitigated by financial crime risk intelligence and controls (e.g. transaction monitoring or targeted data analytics), outcomes of technical competency and effectiveness assessments of the FTZ/OFC AML/CTF regimes, and risk assessments by local regulators and reports by the parent country's FIU, law enforcement or tax governance bodies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Outcomes from technical competency and effectiveness assessments performed on the AML/CTF regimes"</sub>

## Q9. How can FIs assess risk ratings for Overseas Countries & Territories and Dependencies (OCTs)?

- **permissive / method**: The FAQs state FIs may follow two approaches to risk rate OCTs: use the FCCR to derive a rating for the OCTs, or align the OCTs' ratings to their 'parent' country risk ratings. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may follow two distinct approaches to risk rate OCTs"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The FAQs state that if an OCT is subject to the parent's AML/CTF regime, FIs can assess the extent of the parent's control over the OCT (partial/full or direct/indirect) and the stability/maturity of the parent's regime; if the OCT has its own AML/CTF framework, FIs may follow their usual risk assessment approach; additional considerations include administration, location and distance from the parent, economic activities and population. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If an OCT has its own AML/CTF framework, FIs may follow their usual risk assessment approach"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that, as a general principle, FIs should implement a standalone risk rating for OCTs where the AML/CTF frameworks are considered different from their parent countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should implement a standalone risk rating for OCTs where the AML/CTF frameworks are considered different"</sub>

## Q10. How can FIs test and validate the effectiveness of their FCCR Models or Methodologies and how frequently should this be undertaken?

- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that if an FI has defined its FCCR rating process as a model, it should be subject to the normal cycles of model governance, including quantitative and qualitative validation, whose intent is to verify models perform as expected, in line with design objectives and business uses; validation may identify issues leading to model development or an approved change process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that model should be subject to the normal cycles of model governance, including quantitative and qualitative validation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state a model's effectiveness should be tracked regularly to check it is working appropriately and identify areas for improvement, which can be done through KPIs and periodic reviews; KPIs are usually defined through qualitative user feedback and quantitative analysis, with examples including model sensitivity, volatility and benchmarking against vendor tool ratings. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The effectiveness of any given model should be tracked regularly to check whether it is working appropriately"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that for FIs defining their FCCR rating process as a methodology, any review should occur in line with the minimum standard of review of the underlying data sources, i.e. at least annually; the review should address previously identified improvement areas and external factors, may include SME input, trend analysis and compliance with industry norms and regulatory expectations, and should re-validate coverage (jurisdictions, dependencies, territories, FTZs and OCTs). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any review should occur in line with the minimum standard of review of the underlying data sources, i.e. at least annually"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that, however an FI defines its FCCR rating process, there should be a sufficiently robust governance process around selection of data sources, development of scoring, SME input, change control and some form of independent review and validation so outcomes are justified, documented, stable and effective. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there should be a sufficiently robust governance process around the selection of data sources"</sub>

## Q11. How should an FI deal with missing data points?

- **permissive / method**: The FAQs state that, depending on whether the country list is universal (e.g. UN list of recognised jurisdictions) or non-universal, there may be a desire to substitute missing values with the highest risk level, the lowest risk level, or an average risk score or proxy value (a value identified for rationalised reasons as likely for the country with missing data). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The substitution may be with either the highest risk level, the lowest risk level, or an average risk score or proxy value"</sub>
- **mandatory / expectation**: The FAQs state care must be taken that substitution does not distort FCCR scores (e.g. making countries that should naturally be high risk appear lower risk); defaulting decisions should be documented clearly and should only occur after the methodology is created, unless a rationale is developed to leverage a similar datapoint for a comparable jurisdiction. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Care must be taken that this process does not distort the FCCR scores"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that, since unknown information is a priori a risk factor, there should be a data quality assessment when data is missing, and if there is a paucity of information for a country, a subjective override should be considered for that country. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there should be a data quality assessment done when data is missing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FAQs state that if a data source is limited in the number of countries it covers, appropriate consideration should be given to the impact on output, and if limitations are identified on a wholesale basis, consideration should be given to not using the source or, as a minimum, mitigating the impact on outputs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate consideration should be given to the impact of this on the output"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Country Risk FAQs point out that ignoring country risk could mean losing important context about a customer, while giving it too much weight could produce undesirable outcomes across the customer base. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Disregarding country risk could discount important context about a customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The FAQs state that each financial institution must find a balance in how it assesses country risk and in how far it takes that risk into account in its customer and other risk frameworks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Each FI must strike a balance in how it assesses country risk"</sub>

## Q1. What is country risk in the context of financial crime compliance?

- **expected / expectation**: According to Q1, when assessing a customer's risk profile FIs need to look not only at the financial crime risk tied to the customer profile but also at how effective the AML/CTF legal and regulatory frameworks are in the countries to which the customer has relevant exposure, and at the political and macro-economic environment in those countries. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs need to consider not only the financial crime risk related to the customer profile"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q1 says an FI's FCCR rating methodology should rest on the FI's initial understanding of a country's risks and context taken in the widest sense, together with the elements that feed into them. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be based on the FI’s initial understanding of the country’s risks and context in the widest sense"</sub>

## Q2. What data sources should be considered when developing a methodology to assess country risk?

- **expected / data or feature**: Q2 says the sources used for quantitative risk scoring should cover financial crime relevant risk dimensions, including Criminal Indicators, for which FIs may consider data sets such as corruption and terrorism indices, drug trafficking, countries regarded as tax havens, and countries susceptible to human smuggling, trafficking, modern slavery and environmental crime, among others. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The sources used for quantitative risk scoring should cover financial crime relevant risk parameters or dimensions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Q2 states that no set number of sources is prescribed for an FI's methodology, and that the underlying sources can be single data points or indices that combine several data points. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no prescribed number of sources an FI could or should use in its methodology."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q2 adds that the approach should differ depending on the methodology; for example, weight-based models should take into account the statistically significant contribution of each factor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The approach should also vary based on the methodology"</sub>

## Q3. How often should data sources be refreshed and country risk ratings reviewed?

- **expected / expectation**: Q3 says that, as a general rule, ratings should be updated at least once a year, after the underlying data sources are refreshed, since many of those sources are only updated annually. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is appropriate to update ratings no less frequently than annually"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q3 adds that, as far as possible, it is important to pick an update date when most of the data is up to date. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important to choose a date when the majority of the data is current"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q3 says FIs should work out in advance the specific scenarios in which a country's risk rating would have to be raised to the higher or highest rating, for example being placed on the FATF grey or black list. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should assess specific scenarios where a country’s risk rating would need to be elevated"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Q3 also says FIs should consider when a rating needs to be reassessed to reflect visible improvement in a country's risk framework, for example removal from the FATF grey or black list backed by evidence of long-term effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fis should consider where a risk rating needs to be reassessed to take account of visible improvements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q3 says the way the FCCR is updated should be clearly written into the FI's FCCR governance or methodology documentation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The process for updating the FCCR should be clearly set out"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q3 says FIs, using their internal governance and risk appetite, should decide whether a change to a country's rating triggers a retrospective review of existing exposure or only has a forward-looking effect. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should decide whether a change to a country rating leads to a retrospective review of exposure"</sub>

## Q4. How should sanctions be considered in country risk methodologies?

- **descriptive / context**: Q4 says an FI's presence, meaning where it is based, the markets it operates in and the markets in which its customers do business, may decide which economic and trade sanctions it should comply with, and that it may obtain a list of sanctioned regimes from the sanctioning bodies' websites or, aggregated, from third-party providers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may determine which economic and trade sanctions it should comply with"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Q4 observes that sanctions can change quickly as political, diplomatic or economic issues arise or are resolved, and that in general their effect is immediate. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In general, the effect of sanctions is immediate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q4 says it is important for FIs to keep up with changes to sanctions and respond to them accordingly. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important for FIs to keep abreast of any changes and respond accordingly"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q4 also says it is important to think about which processes and systems the FI uses to comply with sanctions, and what the sanctions mean for the FI's FCCR methodology. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is also important to consider what processes and systems the FI uses to comply with sanctions"</sub>

## Q8. How should FIs determine countries in scope for assessment?

- **descriptive / context**: Q8 states that there is no universally agreed approach setting how many countries an FI's country risk assessment should cover. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no universally agreed approach prescribing how many countries should be considered"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Q8 presents it as a best practice that FIs can risk assess the countries that have unique UN ISO 3166 codes (alpha-2 and alpha-3) and give each a risk rating as appropriate. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs can perform a risk assessment on the countries having unique UN ISO 3166"</sub>

## Q9. How can FIs assess risk ratings for Overseas Countries & Territories and Dependencies (OCTs)?

- **descriptive / context**: Q9 says most OCTs belong to Europe (for instance territories of Denmark, Finland, France, the Netherlands, Norway and the United Kingdom) or to the Americas (for instance American Samoa, Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The majority of these OCTs are part of Europe"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Q9 says that, whichever approach is chosen, an FI's assessment of OCT ratings can consider whether financial crime risk exposure is the same as or similar to that of the parent (mainland) country or different, with a particular check on whether the OCT has higher exposure. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The extent of financial crime risk exposure is the same or similar as the parent country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q9 says that, from a controls perspective, FIs should assess whether an OCT has to follow the parent country's AML/CTF regime or has a regime of its own. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should assess whether the OCT has to comply with the parent country’s AML/CTF regime or has its own regime"</sub>

## Q10. How can FIs test and validate the effectiveness of their FCCR Models or Methodologies and how frequently should this be undertaken?

- **permissive / context**: Q10 says that model validation may not always address whether the hierarchy of country risk ratings is appropriate, because that is the preserve of subject matter expertise or an expression of the FI's risk appetite, and validation may be confined to reviewing and challenging how the approved methodology was developed, documented and executed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "model validation may not involve determining the appropriateness of the hierarchy of country risk ratings in all cases"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Q10 says that where validation is limited in this way, senior financial crime compliance governance should challenge and approve the model's final outcome. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the final outcome of the model should be challenged and approved by senior financial crime compliance governance"</sub>

## Q11. How should an FI deal with missing data points?

- **permissive / recommendation**: Q11 says that, whatever FCCR methodology is used, FIs may deal with missing data in input lists or countries either by raising the country with missing data to a higher risk, or by leaving it out of the rating process and rating it subjectively. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may account for missing data in input lists and/or countries"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Q11 states that, following a risk-based approach together with professional judgment, FIs shall decide whether overrides are needed to offset missing inputs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs shall determine if overrides are required to mitigate missing inputs"</sub>
