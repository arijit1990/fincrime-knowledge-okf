## The Wolfsberg Group – Demonstrating Effectiveness

- **descriptive / context**: The paper builds on the Wolfsberg Group's prior work by suggesting ways in which an FI can assess risk in defined priority areas and demonstrate the effectiveness of its AML/CTF programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "suggesting ways in which a Financial Institution (FI) can assess risk in defined priority areas"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In December 2019 the Group published a Statement on Effectiveness outlining the key elements of an effective AML/CTF programme (the Wolfsberg Factors): complying with AML/CTF laws and regulations; providing highly useful information to relevant government agencies in defined priority areas; and establishing a reasonable and risk-based set of controls to mitigate the risks of an FI being used to facilitate illicit activity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In December 2019, the Group published a Statement on Effectiveness that outlined what it believes are the key elements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In August 2020 the Group published a follow-up Statement on Developing an Effective AML/CTF Programme suggesting steps for FIs to focus more on effective outcomes: assess risk in defined priority areas; implement/enhance controls; prioritise resources; engage with law enforcement; and demonstrate AML/CTF programme effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In August 2020, the Group published a follow-up Statement on Developing an Effective AML/CTF Programme"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Group believes designing, assessing, and measuring AML/CTF programmes based on performance against the defined outcomes would enable FIs to detect and deter criminal activity better and improve the effectiveness of the overall system to combat money laundering and terrorist financing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would not only enable FIs to detect and deter criminal activity better"</sub>

## Assessing Risk in Defined Priority Areas

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF requires countries to identify, assess and understand the ML/TF risks they are exposed to so they can implement measures that mitigate them; this risk-based approach is central to effective implementation of the FATF standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to identify, assess and understand the money laundering and terrorist financing risks they are exposed to"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Group states that most FIs today do not focus AML/CTF risk assessments on government priorities; largely in response to supervisory expectations they focus on technical compliance with requirements rather than the effectiveness of efforts to prevent and detect financial crime, culminating in an enterprise-wide risk assessment that tends to be very long, complex, and focused on data, documentation and process rather than outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most FIs do not focus their AML/CTF risk assessments on government priorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The more detailed the information governments share with FIs on these threats, publicly or privately, the better the FI will be able to evaluate, mitigate, and potentially provide useful information on these threats to law enforcement. The Group intends to discuss threat assessments in more detail in an upcoming paper. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the more detailed information that is shared, by governments with FIs, related to these threats"</sub>

## Complying with AML/CTF Laws and Regulations

- **mandatory / requirement**: FIs must comply with AML/CTF laws and regulations, which is the foundation for AML/CTF programmes; to do so FIs must understand the AML/CTF laws and regulations for each applicable jurisdiction or supra-national authority and implement reasonably designed processes to comply, as well as controls and reporting to demonstrate how they comply. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs must understand the AML/CTF laws and regulations for each applicable jurisdiction, or supra-national authority"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should clearly differentiate between legal or regulatory requirements that must be met and other areas where FIs are permitted to take a risk-based approach. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors, for their part, should clearly differentiate between legal or regulatory requirements that must be met"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Group states that FIs spend significant time and resources on activities considered "expectations" of an AML/CTF programme but not required by law or regulation; these are sometimes in non-binding guidance, sometimes unwritten, or can reflect the views of an individual examiner or auditor. Unless these activities lead to highly useful information for government authorities or help the FI materially prevent, detect, or deter actual crime, they are often counterproductive to an effective programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these “expectations” are often counterproductive to an effective AML/CTF programme"</sub>

## Providing Highly Useful Information to Government Authorities in Priority Areas

- **descriptive / context**: The extent to which governments define priorities and give feedback on reporting varies by jurisdiction, and risks related to published priority areas vary by FI by size, business model and geographic scope; demonstrating and assessing effectiveness in providing highly useful information therefore will necessarily vary by FI. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "demonstrating and assessing effectiveness in providing highly useful information to government authorities will necessarily vary by FI"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: For some FIs it may be helpful to track engagement with, and information provided to, government authorities; the metrics and programme attributes described could be used internally to measure effectiveness or externally to demonstrate to supervisors that the programme is reasonably designed to provide highly useful information. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may be helpful to track engagement with, and information provided to, government authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The metrics focus on the quality, not the quantity, of reporting; the best indicator of quality is direct feedback from government authorities and how that feedback influences enhancements to the FI's control framework. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The best indicator of quality is direct feedback from government authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The examples are neither an exhaustive list nor intended to imply a requirement to track these or any other particular type of information to demonstrate effectiveness; some may not apply to certain FIs based on size, business model, risk profile or available government feedback. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "neither an exhaustive list nor intended to imply that there be a requirement to track these"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: A smaller FI with a lower risk profile could have an effective AML/CFT programme even if it has not filed any STR/SAR, for as long as it has demonstrated an understanding of its financial crime risk and implemented a reasonably designed programme based on its risk profile. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could have an effective AML/CFT programme even if it has not filed any Suspicious Transaction Reports"</sub>
- **expected / limitation**: The metrics should not be used as a basis to compare one FI to another. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the metrics should not be used as a basis to compare one FI to another"</sub>

## Law Enforcement and Government Engagement

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Example metrics for law enforcement and government engagement are: use of intelligence from law enforcement and relevant government authorities including FIU communications; participation in public-private partnerships; results from PPP engagement (government action following tactical information sharing or strategic and policy work); engagement on priority areas including sharing information and typologies; and recognition/feedback from law enforcement and government authorities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Use of intelligence from law enforcement and relevant government authorities, including Financial Intelligence Unit communications"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Further example metrics are: high quality and timely responses to requests from law enforcement and government authorities; analysis of such requests; and the number of relationships/accounts maintained at the request of law enforcement ("Keep Open Requests"). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Number of relationships/accounts maintained at the request of law enforcement"</sub>

## Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs)/Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Example STR/SAR metrics are: STRs/SARs filed in priority areas aligned to nationally defined or published financial crime priorities, including those set by law enforcement; STRs/SARs identifying networks of interconnected suspicious activity typically involving several parties; and STRs/SARs filed following a complex investigation by the FI. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "STRs/SARs that identify networks of interconnected suspicious activity typically involving several parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Further example STR/SAR metrics are: percentage of STRs/SARs filed that result in follow-up enquiries from law enforcement or government authorities; percentage of STRs/SARs in priority areas with positive law enforcement or government feedback; and STRs/SARs where government action was later taken (e.g. the suspect was later charged or convicted, asset seizure). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Percentage of STRs/SARs filed that result in follow-up enquiries from law enforcement or government authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Additional example STR/SAR metrics are STRs/SARs filed following information sharing from the government, and STRs/SARs filed following information sharing from other FIs, where legally permissible. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "STRs/SARs filed following information sharing from other FIs, where legally permissible"</sub>

## Reasonable and Risk-Based Controls to Detect, Prevent or Deter Financial Crime

- **expected / expectation**: As financial crime risks vary by FI, so will the risk-based controls necessary to mitigate them. Risk-based controls should be reasonably designed both to prevent the FI from being used to facilitate illicit activity and to detect and report potential illicit activity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk-based controls should be reasonably designed to both prevent the FI from being used to facilitate illicit activity"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: An FI may also demonstrate effectiveness by applying lessons learned from government engagement or other priorities-focused work towards developing new or enhanced controls to detect, prevent or deter financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FI may also demonstrate effectiveness by applying lessons learned from government engagement"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Group states that auditors, consultants and supervisors today tend to focus their assessment of controls almost exclusively on technical implementation and execution; in a regime focused on effectiveness, supervisors and FIs would instead focus on whether controls make a material difference in helping mitigate risks and address defined AML/CTF priorities, as the FATF has emphasised that effectiveness should be judged on outcomes rather than process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "effectiveness should be judged on outcomes rather than process"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Risk-based means focusing controls on areas presenting higher risk to the FI. Quoting FATF, weaknesses in lower ML/TF risk areas may go undetected by supervisors under risk-based supervision; where an FI has reasonably focused on higher risk areas in line with its threat assessment, an undetected weakness in a lower risk area is not by default an indication of programme failure but a natural extension of a risk-based approach. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an undetected weakness in a lower risk area is not by default an indication of programme failure"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: It is entirely normal for an FI to decommission ineffective and inefficient controls, and the opportunity cost of failing to change or eliminate such a control should be part of an FI's overall assessment of its risk-based controls; the Group plans to address resource prioritisation in an upcoming paper. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The opportunity cost of failing to change or eliminate an ineffective or inefficient control should be a part of"</sub>

## Conclusion

- **expected / expectation**: Each FI should be able to demonstrate effectiveness by telling its unique story, based on its risks and corresponding AML/CTF programme; effectiveness for designated priorities or overall should be measured on its compliance with law and regulations, how it is designed to provide highly useful information in defined priority areas, and how it builds and maintains a reasonable and risk-based set of controls. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each FI should be able to demonstrate effectiveness by telling its unique story"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Group believes measuring AML/CTF programmes on performance against these outcomes will help achieve the FATF's goal of a more effective system to combat money laundering and terrorist financing while reducing friction on legitimate customers and supporting governments' financial inclusion objectives. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reducing friction on legitimate customers and supporting governments in achieving their financial inclusion objectives"</sub>

## Assessing Risk in Defined Priority Areas

- **expected / expectation**: The Wolfsberg Group takes the view that an effective AML/CTF programme should begin with understanding the priority risks that countries or supra-national bodies have identified in their assessments, and how far those risks apply to the FI. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the starting point for an effective AML/CTF programme should be an understanding of the priority risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The paper says FIs should concentrate on threats tied to national or supra-national priorities that are specifically defined, and that this assessment should be more agile and less prescriptive than the enterprise-wide risk assessment as presently carried out. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should focus on threats related to specifically defined national or supra-national priorities"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: If no specifically defined priorities exist, FIs can use the priorities set out in the jurisdiction's National Risk Assessment or an equivalent publication as their defined priority areas. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where specifically defined priorities do not exist, FIs can leverage priorities described in the"</sub>

## Evaluating and Demonstrating Effectiveness

- **expected / expectation**: If an FI can show, for a given threat or for the programme as a whole, that it complied with applicable AML/CTF laws and regulations, designed its programme to give highly useful information to government authorities, and put in place a reasonable, risk-based set of controls to prevent and detect financial crime, this should be treated as strong evidence of effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "implemented a reasonable and risk-based set of controls to prevent and detect financial crime"</sub>

## Providing Highly Useful Information to Government Authorities in Priority Areas

- **permissive / recommendation**: Where government feedback is scarce or absent, FIs could treat things such as how complex an investigation was, whether network activity was identified, or whether reporting falls in identified priority areas as quality indicators; the Group concedes these indicators are imperfect. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where feedback is limited or unavailable, FIs could consider factors like the complexity of the investigation"</sub>

## Reasonable and Risk-Based Controls to Detect, Prevent or Deter Financial Crime

- **expected / expectation**: Risk-based controls should cover at least (not exclusively) AML/CTF policies and procedures, risk assessments, customer due diligence (CDD) collection, enhanced due diligence (EDD) requirements where appropriate, business oversight, governance processes, transaction monitoring, economic sanctions screening, a way for employees to escalate possibly suspicious activity, and AML/CTF training. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AML/CTF policies and procedures, risk assessments, collection of customer due diligence (CDD), enhanced due diligence (EDD) requirements where appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: To show their controls are effective, FIs should be ready to tell supervisors how the controls in practice reduce risk and/or give highly useful information to government authorities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should be prepared to explain to their supervisors how their controls actually mitigate risk"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Where a control takes up substantial time and/or resources while achieving little risk mitigation, FIs should consider altering or removing it and moving the resources to controls with demonstrably better outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should consider changing or eliminating the control altogether and reallocating those resources"</sub>
