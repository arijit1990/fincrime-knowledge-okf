## The Wolfsberg Group - Principles for Auditing for Effectiveness

- **descriptive / context**: The Group believes Internal Audit (IA) can assist FIs in the fight against financial crime by measuring FCRM outcomes using the Wolfsberg Factors, and developed these Principles, as a joint exercise between member banks' second and third lines of defence, to give FIs a framework for such an assessment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as a joint exercise between member banks’ second and third lines of defence"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: IA, an independent function within an FI constituting the third line of defence, should conduct independent audits in an objective, thorough, and impartial manner in line with professional standards (e.g. Institute of Internal Auditors). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should conduct independent audits 4 in an objective, thorough, and impartial manner"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: IA should play an important role in confirming that an FI's FCRM programme is focused on risk-relevant activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confirming that an FI’s FCRM programme is focused on risk-relevant activities"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: IA is responsible for assessing the effectiveness of (key) controls over the FI's activities and entities and may focus on any aspect of their operations without any restriction. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may focus on any aspect of their operations without any restriction"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: IA should adopt an RBA that includes internal and external requirements (e.g. local regulations may have defined requirements). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should adopt an RBA that includes internal and external requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: To fulfil its mandate, IA should conduct a periodic risk assessment (a footnote states industry standard is at least annually, per standard 2010.A1 of the IIA International Standards) to determine audit priorities for annual and/or multi-year audit plans. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should conduct a periodic 6 risk assessment to determine audit priorities for annual and/or multi-year audit plans"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Following completion of audits, IA should issue conclusions in line with its methodology, which should include a standardised process to report, track, and escalate identified control deficiencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which should include a standardised process to report, track, and escalate identified control deficiencies"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: IA can leverage these Principles to complement its existing audit methodology while retaining its role as an independent review function; doing so will promote effective risk management, and adherence to the Principles will support the industry's effectiveness to detect, prevent and report financial crime. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA can leverage these Principles to complement their existing audit methodology"</sub>

## Factor 1: Complying with Financial Crime Laws and Regulations

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 1: FIs should establish and maintain a baseline for managing the legal requirements for FCRM, being the requirements that apply equally to all FIs engaged in similar activities or operating in the same jurisdiction(s). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should establish and maintain a baseline for managing the legal requirements for FCRM"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 1.1: the FI can evidence that local financial crime laws and regulations have been addressed in the FI's governance documents. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence that local financial crime laws and regulations have been addressed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On Measure 1.1: if an FI has decided to create a globally consistent policy imposing a higher standard than local law or regulation requires, the higher standard should be used unless there is a conflict with local law or regulatory expectation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the higher standard should be used unless there is a conflict with local law or regulatory expectation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On Measure 1.1: where local requirements deviate from global standards, a process to raise, oversee, and track those deviations should be implemented. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a process to raise, oversee, and track those deviations should be implemented"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 1.2: the FI can evidence that controls mapped to these elements of the governance documents are designed and operating effectively. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence that controls mapped to these elements of the governance documents are designed and operating effectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On Measure 1.2: the basic principle is that FI management is responsible for managing financial crime risks by designing and implementing appropriate and sustainable controls, which are then subject to assessment by IA. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FI management is responsible for managing financial crime risks through designing and implementing appropriate and sustainable controls"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 1.3: the FI can evidence a sufficiently governed process to assess the adequacy of the FCRM programme in addressing regulatory requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence a sufficiently governed process to assess the adequacy of the FCRM programme"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On Measure 1.3: the exercise noted in 1.1 needs to be refreshed periodically or on a trigger basis, for example to incorporate regulatory changes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "needs to be refreshed periodically or on a trigger basis, for example to incorporate regulatory changes"</sub>

## Principle 2

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 2: IA should evaluate whether the FI has a well-designed, reasonable and risk-based set of controls, and then assess the effectiveness of the controls. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should evaluate whether the FI has a well-designed, reasonable and risk-based set of controls"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Financial crime risk and risk appetite vary by FI, which affects the processes and controls implemented; FCRM is a dynamic area requiring continuous application of sound risk management practices, prioritisation of resources and adequate funding to manage risk in line with risk appetite. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FCRM is a dynamic area that requires the continuous application of sound risk management practices"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The FI must have sufficient subject matter experts to build and operate a proactive, effective, risk-based FCRM programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI must have sufficient subject matter experts to build and operate a proactive, effective, risk-based FCRM programme"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On Measure 2.1: judgment is required when determining how to apply control coverage reasonably and proportionately to the risk presented, and in adapting that coverage under appropriate governance in response to a changing threat landscape and evolving regulatory expectation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Judgment is required when determining how to apply control coverage in a manner that is reasonable and proportionate"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 2.2: the FI can evidence that the set of controls is effective. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence that the set of controls is effective."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On Measure 2.2: together with Measure 1.2 these measures reflect the standard IA assessment of defined controls; Measure 1.2 demonstrates technical compliance with the prescriptive elements of law and regulation, while Measure 2.2 demonstrates compliance with the principles-based elements. There is no requirement to map controls to each principle, but understanding the difference may help an FI identify opportunities to adjust its control strategy over time. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While there is no requirement to map controls to each principle"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On Measure 2.3: changes to an FI's controls are a necessity to keep pace with evolving business strategies, laws, regulations and threats, and enhancements may result from ongoing assessment of control vulnerabilities, including ineffective or redundant controls. Changes may include extending or starting new control activities (e.g. for a new product), or stopping, reducing or redesigning existing ones (e.g. redundant or duplicative). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Changes may include extending and starting new control activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On Measure 2.3: IA independently reviews and validates changes to ensure appropriate governance processes were followed and to confirm the FI retains a reasonable and risk-based set of controls, raising challenges as appropriate. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA independently reviews and validates changes to ensure appropriate governance processes were followed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As part of assessing the effectiveness of the set of controls, IA should take the opportunity to highlight where controls are not producing the intended risk management outcome or are simply no longer relevant. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should also take the opportunity to highlight where controls are not producing the intended risk management outcome"</sub>

## Factor 3: Providing Highly Useful Information

- **descriptive / context**: The third Wolfsberg Factor focuses on an FI's role in supporting law enforcement and relevant government agencies to keep communities safe from harm through the provision of relevant information. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supporting law enforcement and relevant government agencies to keep their communities safe from harm"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Factor 3 applies to information an FI provides through regulatory reporting mechanisms such as suspicious activity/transaction reporting, as well as collaborative mechanisms such as government-sponsored information sharing partnerships with industry (Public-Private Partnerships, PPPs). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "government-sponsored information sharing partnerships with industry"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The emphasis of Factor 3 is the quality and usefulness of the information shared rather than the existence of the information or purely technical compliance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is about the quality and usefulness of the information shared rather than the existence of the information"</sub>

## Principle 3

- **permissive / recommendation**: Principle 3: an FI may choose to establish quantitative and/or qualitative indicators relating to the sharing of highly useful information to relevant government agencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FI may choose to establish quantitative and/or qualitative indicators"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Such indicators are recognised as unlikely to provide an absolute measure of the usefulness of information provided to government agencies, but may be inferred to be indicative of the effectiveness of information sharing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such indicators are unlikely to provide an absolute measure of the usefulness of information provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Inherent challenges will remain in obtaining feedback from government agencies on reports filed; FI indicators, if established, can provide information that allows IA and government agencies to assess an institution's efforts to meet Principle 3. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Understandable inherent challenges will remain in relation to obtaining feedback from government agencies for reports filed"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 3.2: the FI can evidence that it is collecting the indicators it has set for itself. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence that it is collecting the indicators it has set for itself."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: On Measure 3.2: FIs may consider establishing internal standards or guidelines for the collection of this management information; if established, IA can use these to assess whether the FI is collecting the information in accordance with them, helping the FI discuss with regulators and law enforcement the effectiveness of this component of its FCRM programme and the balance of resources applied to each of the three principles. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may consider establishing internal standards or guidelines for the collection of this form of management information"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 3.3: the FI can evidence oversight through formal governance of its self-assessment on its provision of highly useful information to relevant government agencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence oversight through formal governance of its self-assessment"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: On Measure 3.3: an FI might include in its existing reporting to, and discussions with, the Board highlights of how it has provided useful information to law enforcement, with examples of how the information contributed to creating safer communities, which could help it understand its impact in this area. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI might include in its existing reporting to, and discussions with, the Board"</sub>

## Conclusion

- **descriptive / context**: The Group believes that, by measuring FCRM outcomes using the Wolfsberg Factors, IA can assist FIs in the fight against financial crime to best effect, promoting effective FCRM within FIs and supporting how supervisors may assess the effectiveness of their regulated entities and the industry as a whole. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA can assist their FIs in the fight against financial crime to their best effect"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In the Principles and Measures, the term "FI" refers to the first and second lines of defence as distinguished from the Internal Audit Function; first and second lines of defence are also responsible for assessing the effectiveness of controls. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is meant to refer to the first and second lines of defence as distinguished from the Internal Audit Function"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote notes that the order of Factors Two and Three is reversed compared with the 2019 Wolfsberg Group Statement on Effectiveness, to give a more coherent flow of the Factors as applied to an audit programme. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The order of Factors Two and Three has been reversed when compared to the 2019 Wolfsberg Group Statement on Effectiveness"</sub>

## The Wolfsberg Group - Principles for Auditing for Effectiveness

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group has urged FIs and regulators to concentrate on effective outcomes in Financial Crime Risk Management (FCRM) by applying the three Wolfsberg Factors: complying with financial crime laws and regulations; establishing a reasonable, risk-based set of controls to mitigate the risk of an FI being used to facilitate illicit activity; and providing highly useful information to relevant government agencies in defined priority areas. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has encouraged Financial Institutions (FIs 1 ) and regulators to focus on effective outcomes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Internal Audit should take a risk-based approach (RBA) that draws on applicable laws and regulations and on the risks identified in the FI's own risk assessment. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should adopt a risk-based approach (RBA) that is informed by applicable laws and regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Internal Audit has an important role in assessing how comprehensive and effective the FCRM programme is, confirming that it is dynamic and that it covers every regulatory requirement with thorough documentation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "validating that the programme is dynamic and covers all regulatory requirements in a thoroughly documented manner"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Internal Audit should assess how effective the FI's FCRM programme is, in particular making sure that policies and procedures reflect applicable rules, regulations, best practices and guidance so as to support effective operations, properly managed risk exposure, and a control framework that is relevant and sustainable. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "notably ensuring that policies and procedures take into account applicable rules, regulations, best practices, and guidance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Internal Audit should also evaluate how aware the FI is of risk and state its conclusions on compliance using its established audit methodology and professional practice expectations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide its conclusions on compliance leveraging its established audit methodology and expectations of professional practices"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Separately from issuing its conclusions, Internal Audit should confirm that remedial actions fix control deficiencies or mitigate the identified risk, including for issues raised by parties outside IA such as regulators or self-identified issues. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should validate remedial actions to address control deficiencies and/or mitigation of the identified risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Since complying with applicable laws and regulations is the foundation of an FCRM programme, sits with FI Management and is the first Wolfsberg Factor, Internal Audit activity should include a focus on the prescriptive requirements in law and regulation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consequently, IA activity should include a focus on the prescriptive requirements in law and regulation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As a baseline, Internal Audit should check whether the FI can show that its governance documents cover the requirements of all relevant local laws and regulations, and that it has an effective set of controls to ensure those requirements are met. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IA should assess whether the FI can demonstrate that its governance documents"</sub>

## Factor 1: Complying with Financial Crime Laws and Regulations

- **expected / expectation**: On Measure 1.1: some jurisdictions may set standards above or below group policy, so Internal Audit should be placed to assess each entity's controls against the standards that apply to its jurisdiction or function. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Certain jurisdictions may have higher or lower standards than group policy and IA should be positioned to assess each entity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On Measure 1.2: FIs should be able to show that their assessment programme links back to local legal and regulatory requirements and meets the baseline regulatory requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should be able to demonstrate that their assessment programme ties back to the requirements of local laws and regulations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On Measure 1.3: any regulatory gaps should be identified and should lead to remediation action plans, with remediation fully closing the gap, and an appropriate governance forum should oversee these processes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any regulatory gaps should be identified, require action plans to remediate"</sub>

## Factor 2: Establishing a Reasonable and Risk-Based Set of Controls

- **mandatory / requirement**: An FI must satisfy the risk management requirements in both the prescriptive elements of law and regulation (Principle 1) and the principles-based elements (Principle 2). It must also understand the financial crime risk inherent in its business strategy and operating model, its regulators' expectations, and its own risk appetite. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FI must meet the risk management requirements contained in the prescriptive elements of law and regulation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Only with this full understanding, and enough subject-matter expertise across the first, second and third lines of defence, can an FI design a reasonable set of risk-based controls that is proportionate to its risks and lets it stay within its risk appetite, reducing the chance its products and services are used for crime and protecting the integrity of the global financial system; this is the second Wolfsberg Factor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sufficient subject-matter expertise among all three lines of defence"</sub>

## Principle 2

- **descriptive / definition**: The audit principles use 'set of controls' broadly, to cover the FI's whole internal FCRM control framework however it is defined internally, including risk appetite, policy, procedures, processes and controls meant to mitigate financial crime risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "internal control framework, however defined internally, including items such as risk appetite, policy, procedures, processes, and controls"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Measure 2.3: the FI can demonstrate a sufficiently governed process for changing its set of controls, with that governance giving appropriate weight to financial crime risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FI can evidence a sufficiently governed process for changes to its set of controls"</sub>

## Principle 3

- **permissive / context**: FIs may choose their own definition of highly useful information and use their own judgment to build indicators that can be applied consistently across their information-sharing and reporting activities; these may be quantitative and qualitative, and although they are unlikely to give an absolute measure of usefulness, they may be taken as indicative of how effective information sharing is. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs may choose to define what they consider to be highly useful information"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Measure 3.1: the FI may consider developing a credible and reasonable set of indicators for assessing how well it provides highly useful information to relevant government agencies in defined priority areas. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may consider developing a credible and reasonable set of indicators upon which to assess its performance"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: On Measure 3.1: building and using such indicators will be new for many FIs, their IA teams and their regulators, so the first assessment may need to concentrate on the transformation effort itself; the industry has an opportunity to do more work to identify best practices and, where practical, promote consistency in the most relevant indicators. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "initial assessment may need to focus on the necessary transformation effort itself"</sub>

## Footnotes

- **descriptive / context**: A footnote notes that the effectiveness of an FI's support for national security and foreign policy objectives may be shown by the absence of reporting to relevant government agencies, for instance no downstream interdictions, subpoenas or asset blocking. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be evidenced through the absence of reporting to relevant government agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote attached to Measure 2.1 describes that measure as the counterpart to Measure 1.1, which dealt with the prescriptive requirements of law and regulation; it also brings in the FI's judgement-based requirements, such as interpreting what a reasonable set of controls is, defining a risk appetite, interpreting regulatory expectations and setting out what counts as suspicious activity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This measure is the counterpart to Measure 1.1 above, which focused on the prescriptive requirements of law and regulation"</sub>
