## Q1. What is Negative News?

- **descriptive / definition**: The Group recognises there is no universally agreed definition of Negative News; for this document it is broadly defined as information available in the public domain which FIs would consider relevant to the management of Financial Crime risk. It is also referred to as Adverse Media, Negative Media or Adverse News. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information available in the public domain 4 which FIs would consider relevant to the management of Financial Crime risk"</sub>

## Q2. Why might an FI conduct Negative News Screening in the context of fighting Financial Crime?

- **descriptive / context**: NNS can enhance an FI's awareness of potential Financial Crime Risk posed by existing and prospective customers and is a useful screening mechanism for understanding who it does business with and the risks to which it is exposed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It can be a useful screening mechanism which enables FIs to have a better understanding of who"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: NNS lets an FI leverage a range of information, data and analysis available in the public domain and can be a useful tool to supplement the CDD process and identify factors that may affect the customer's risk profile, helping the FI understand the Financial Crime and related Reputational risks of a business relationship so they can be managed appropriately. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can be a useful tool to supplement the Customer Due Diligence (CDD) process"</sub>

## Q3. How might NNS add value in fighting Financial Crime?

- **descriptive / context**: NNS can add value by informing the risk assessment and classification of the customer or business relationship and determining the extent of ongoing monitoring. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Informing the risk assessment and classification of the customer or business relationship"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: NNS can add value by forming a key component of a CDD trigger event strategy, resulting in more effective deployment of resources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Forming a key component of a CDD trigger event strategy resulting in the more effective deployment of resources"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: NNS can add value by providing additional context to support an investigation into potentially suspicious activity, which may itself be triggered initially by the identification of Negative News. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Providing additional context to support an investigation into potentially suspicious activity"</sub>

## Q4. How can an FI ensure NNS adds value without the need for excessive manual resources?

- **expected / expectation**: FIs should configure their approach to optimise effectiveness, considering the likelihood of identifying a risk-relevant productive alert/true match. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should configure their approach to optimise effectiveness"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: For high-profile customer types, broad based NNS (structured and unstructured sources) may be appropriate on a risk sensitive basis; for a typical retail or mass-market customer, with a lower public profile and much less media scrutiny, more targeted searches of structured news and media sources might be more appropriate to ensure effective use of resources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "more targeted searches of structured news and media sources might be more appropriate to ensure an effective use of resources"</sub>

## Q6. What information might be excluded from NNS?

  <sub>anchor: "civil proceedings may be excluded from NNS, e.g., speeding fines and public disorder offences"</sub>

## Q7. What is meant by the term Reputational Risk in the context of Financial Crime NNS?

- **descriptive / definition**: Reputational Risk in the context of Financial Crime NNS relates to the risk of negative public perception regarding the FI's association with a customer or business relationship, which may or may not be relevant from a Financial Crime perspective. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the risk of negative public perception with respect to the FI"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: By its nature all Financial Crime Negative News may also pose a Reputational Risk and should therefore also be considered through such a lens. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all Financial Crime Negative News may also pose a Reputational Risk and should therefore, also be considered"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: While the FI maintains a degree of control in managing its Financial Crime Risk, Reputational Risk is to some extent beyond its control as it varies significantly with what interests the public and according to culture and geography. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Reputational Risk, to some extent is beyond the control of the FI"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Example: an FI considering a new corporate customer may find Negative News alleging Money Laundering or Terrorist Financing by the UBOs; after EDD it may satisfy itself the allegations are false and the entity is owned and controlled by individuals of sound reputation, and can demonstrate the steps taken and basis for accepting the relationship, but would not necessarily be able to mitigate the Reputational Risk in the same manner. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FI may satisfy itself that the allegations are false"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Reputational Risk element should be assessed and considered in its own right and any decision taken accordingly by the relevant risk owner(s). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should therefore be assessed and considered in its own right and any decision taken accordingly by the relevant risk owner(s)"</sub>

## Q11. Screening in multi script languages can be challenging, what are the Key Terms used?

- **descriptive / definition**: Transcription is the process of transferring words from one language script to another in accordance with a certain conversion system; methods vary greatly, so one name can be transcribed into different variations or orderings (e.g., Piotr Czajkowski, Pyotr Tchaikovsky, Piotr Chaykovskiy). ## Q20. What are the limitations an FI should be aware of when using an Internet Search Engine for NNS?

- **descriptive / limitation**: Internet search engines, website owners or news purveyors have the right to purge, alter or archive content at any time, so screening results may differ with each search, even with the same phrase or key words, depending on timing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have the right to purge, alter or archive the content at any time"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FIs have no visibility nor oversight of Internet-based, publicly available content, so it cannot be easily determined whether results are the most accurate, relevant, appropriate or complete. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs have no visibility nor oversight of Internet-based, publicly available content"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Content search, selection, results distribution and ordering depend on numerous factors not visible to users; search engines are algorithm-driven and filter content to match individual browsing preferences, so results can vary greatly across users. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "filter out content to match individual browsing preferences hence results can vary greatly across different users"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: In many jurisdictions the names of individuals or organisations subject to official criminal prosecution are not publicly available unless there are exceptional circumstances, and content can be subject to local data protection regulation allowing removal of un-favourable information; in such scenarios an Internet search engine will be of limited value for screening. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the name of individuals or organisations subject to official criminal prosecution are not publicly available unless there are exceptional circumstances"</sub>

## Q21 What factors should be considered when determining an operating model for NNS alert investigations?

- **permissive / method**: An FI may choose a tier-based investigation approach, e.g., an initial operational level handling high volumes of alert investigations against an agreed set of matching/discounting rules and procedures, with subsequent levels used where alerts cannot be discounted or positive matches are identified and, given the subjective nature of NNS outputs, need specialist subject matter expertise. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI may choose to develop a tier-based investigation approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The alert investigation framework may differ between FIs, reflecting their size, scale, business activity and risk exposure. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reflecting their size, scale, business activity and risk exposure"</sub>

## Q22. What is meant by a Rules Based and Risk-Based approach to NNS Alert Management?

- **descriptive / definition**: A rules-based approach to NNS alert investigation is a more prescriptive set of steps required to determine an outcome, e.g., NNS alerts are closed when the date of birth in the FI's customer record differs from the one published in the media by +/- 1 year. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A rules-based approach is a more prescriptive set of steps required to be completed"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A risk-based approach enables use of additional parameters and judgment, i.e., while elements of data match between the FI's customer and an NNS alert, other factors can be considered to determine the outcome, such as the FI knowing that the subject of the Negative News is deceased while the alert relates to live customer transactions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A risk-based approach enables the use of additional parameters and judgment"</sub>
- **recommended / expectation**: Consideration should be given to the level of alert investigation needed and the knowledge and skills required by investigators. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consideration should be given to the level of alert investigation needed and the knowledge and skills required by investigators"</sub>

## Q23. Can Technology assist an FI in undertaking NNS?

- **permissive / context**: An FI can use technology to undertake NNS and manage alert volumes so that investigation resources are focused on alerts most likely to be productive, i.e. a 'positive' (or 'true') match/events for risk assessment, which may include auto-discounting rules embedded in the screening tool that automatically discount irrelevant alerts using pre-defined criteria. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "application of auto-discounting rules embedded within the screening tool"</sub>

## Introduction

- **expected / expectation**: The Wolfsberg Group states that Negative News Screening (NNS) should not be confused with Sanctions or Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) screening, which are traditionally list-based; the Group has published separate guidance papers on those two topics. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NNS should not be confused with other forms of searches such as Sanctions or Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) screening"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Wolfsberg Group acknowledges that NNS should not operate as a process with zero tolerance, and that an FI may decide NNS is unnecessary in some circumstances. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NNS should not be a zero-tolerance process and that FIs may conclude that NNS is not necessary in all circumstances"</sub>

## Q3. How might NNS add value in fighting Financial Crime?

- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group believes NNS adds value to financial crime risk management by uncovering a customer's involvement in criminal activity, which can indicate a need for additional due diligence and/or targeted reviews of past transaction activity, feeding the decision to onboard, keep or exit a relationship. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Revealing involvement in criminal activity which may determine the need for additional due diligence"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Wolfsberg Group believes NNS adds value by being an integral part of identifying and building up potential risks or controversies in the narrative about a customer's source of wealth and/or source of funds. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "potential risks or controversies with respect to a customer"</sub>

## Q4. How can an FI ensure NNS adds value without the need for excessive manual resources?

- **expected / expectation**: An FI should follow a risk-based approach to performing NNS in all cases; based on its assessment of risk and the value it will get from NNS, it should decide matters such as the extent, timing and configuration of the screening. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI should adhere to a risk-based approach with respect to the performance of NNS"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The benefit an FI obtains from NNS depends on how much information is available and on the credibility of public-domain media sources. High Net Worth individuals, PEPs and institutional customers (large corporates and FIs) are more likely to have a high public profile and so would be expected to attract more media scrutiny, both positive and negative. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The value an FI is able to extract from NNS is correlated to the availability of information"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Not conducting NNS at all may be appropriate in some cases. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It may also be appropriate not to conduct NNS"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: An FI would not be expected to spend resources on NNS where the added value is negligible or out of proportion to its Financial Crime Risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI would not be expected to devote resources to NNS where the added value is negligible or disproportionate"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Consideration should be given to whether alternative measures, such as anti-fraud databases, offer a more effective way of identifying risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consideration should be given to whether alternative measures such as anti-fraud databases provide more effective means of risk identification"</sub>

## Q5. What factors might an FI consider when establishing NNS?

- **expected / expectation**: An FI will need to decide at which stage of the offence (allegation, investigation, charge, conviction) it wants to be alerted to potential Negative News. Alert volumes are higher at the allegation stage; an alert at conviction, after the alleged crime has been investigated, may have less AML/CTF value. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FI will need to determine at which stage it wishes to be alerted to the potential Negative News"</sub>

## Q10. What Risk-Based decisions may an FI need to consider in the context of NNS?

- **expected / data or feature**: Scope of Screening is one risk-based decision: the FI's CDD/EDD process and technology should decide who is screened, for example customers, UBOs, related parties or non-customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should determine who should be screened (e.g., customers, UBOs, related parties or non-customers)"</sub>
- **expected / data or feature**: Frequency is one risk-based decision: an FI will need to decide how often, and at which stage of the customer life-cycle, screening takes place; examples given are daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annually, whenever CDD/EDD is performed (including onboarding), on a triggered event, periodically, or on an on-going basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI will need to determine at what frequency and stage of the customer life-cycle screening should take place"</sub>
- **permissive / data or feature**: Media Sources/Lists is one risk-based decision: an FI may choose particular media sources to be in scope of monitoring, for example by weighing the credibility of the source and how well it covers adverse information within a given geographical span. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an FI may establish specific media sources to be in-scope of monitoring"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Data Purge is one risk-based decision: taking reference data out of screening once it is no longer relevant to risk, or once a former customer relationship no longer needs to be screened. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "removal of reference data from screening once the data is no longer risk-relevant"</sub>

## Q12. Does an FI need to conduct NNS in multi script languages?

- **mandatory / requirement**: Where performing NNS is a regulatory requirement, consideration must be given to the available screening tools and their associated language capability. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where it is a regulatory requirement to perform NNS, consideration must be given to the screening tools available"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The consideration of screening tools and language capability should include whether both the input data and the media source data are in the same native script, or whether the input is in native script while the original source or watch-list is in Latin and the screening filter offers transliteration. For many markets the minimum standard is to screen customer names against media source names in Latin character sets. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This consideration should include the availability of both input and media source data being in the same native script"</sub>

## Q14. How might an FI assess the credibility of Negative News Media Sources?
