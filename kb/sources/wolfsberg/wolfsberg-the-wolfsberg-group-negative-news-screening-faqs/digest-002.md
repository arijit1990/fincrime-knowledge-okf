- **descriptive / data or feature**: Decision making (rules and risk-based guidance) should ensure alert adjudication follows a logical risk-based approach, with guidance on alert 'materiality': a false positive alert would be discounted, while a positive match could be considered 'material' where the information may indicate a heightened Financial Crime or Reputational Risk to the FI. - **descriptive / data or feature**: Discounting on the basis of a 'false positive' alert may include measures such as a significant difference between the customer's name and the name that is the subject of the news article, and differences in key secondary identifier information (e.g., date of birth, date of incorporation, gender). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Differences in key secondary identifier information (e.g., date of birth, date of incorporation, gender)"</sub>

## Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: Auto Discounting Rules are parameters applied to alerts to discount them without manual intervention; an example is auto discounting any alert where the customer's date of birth differs from the date of birth in the media source by 1 year or more. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "parameters which can be applied to alerts to discount them without the need for manual intervention"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Batch Screening is an automated screening process eliminating manual input of individual or entity names, screening at periodic intervals and provisioning alerts for review at a set time. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an automated screening process eliminating manual input of individual or entity names"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Keywords are the words of interest screened (e.g., 'money laundering' or 'bribe') to generate potential alerts and events of interest or for investigation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the words of interest that will be screened"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Manual Screening is the screening of names input on an individual basis rather than in bulk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the screening of names input on an individual basis rather than in bulk"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Media Sources are the sources of information used that contain the details of the risk events reported. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the sources of information being used that contain the details of the risk events reported"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Risk Category is the category of interest to which screening keywords can be linked, often the predicate offence, e.g., money laundering, human trafficking, terrorism. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the category of interest for which keywords used in screening can be linked"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Search Strings are often used in manual screening through internet search engines, where the subject of interest is searched alongside selected keywords, e.g., John Smith plus 'money laundering' or 'tax evasion'. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The subject of interest is searched alongside selected keywords"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Structured Data is data that is specific, stored in a predefined format and easily accessed and used by a person or computer programme, e.g., a database entry of a negative news event tagged with risk category and stage. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "data that is specific, stored in a predefined format and can be easily accessed and used"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Unstructured Data is unfiltered raw data that, when searched, will typically return broad results, e.g., sources from social media. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unfiltered raw data that when searched, will typically return broad results"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Transliteration is the process of transferring a word from the alphabet of one language to another to aid pronunciation, changing the letters of the original alphabet to similar sounding letters in a different alphabet. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transliteration changes the letters from the words original alphabet to similar sounding letters"</sub>

## Q24. What topics should be included in the procedures used by staff performing NNS?

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Under Q24, discounting an NNS alert as immaterial may rest on measures such as: the FI's customer is not the subject of the item (for example, the customer is a plaintiff); the source is judged not credible (for example blogs or tabloids); or the item is immaterial in nature, for instance not connected to legal or regulatory issues or any issue that could present a risk to the FI, or reported by a source whose information quality is questionable. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Source of NNS is deemed not to be credible (e.g., blogs, tabloids)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Q24, where an FI uses specific IT equipment, systems or tools for alert investigation, documentation and training should be in place so that investigators understand how and why those systems and tools should be used. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "documentation and training should be in place to ensure investigators understand how and why those systems"</sub>

## Q26. What considerations should be given to Quality Control and Quality Assurance in the NNS Process?

- **descriptive / context**: Quality Control (QC) and Quality Assurance (QA) activities are key to the effectiveness of the overall NNS framework and control environment and to mitigating Financial Crime Risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are key aspects in ensuring the effectiveness of the overall framework, control environment and mitigation of Financial Crime Risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In the NNS process QC operates as a preventative control, applied before the alert outcome is determined, whereas QA is regarded as a detective control, applied after the outcome; a strong control framework will include elements of both. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "QC is performed as a preventative control (pre-alert outcome determination)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An FI should make sure QC and QA processes are in place to establish whether alerts were evaluated appropriately and risk was managed in line with the FI's risk appetite. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FI should ensure QC and QA processes are established to determine if alerts have been evaluated appropriately"</sub>

## Q27. Are there specific elements of Quality Assurance (QA) an FI should undertake?

- **expected / expectation**: To keep NNS effective and efficient, FIs should evaluate and test the systems and processes used to assess quality, making sure they are fit for purpose; a comprehensive QA programme helps reveal vulnerabilities and process gaps that expose the FI to financial crime related risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should evaluate and test the systems and processes used to evaluate quality"</sub>

## Q28. What Key Management Information should be available for NNS?

- **expected / expectation**: Management should be given regular reports on application effectiveness and efficiency criteria that measure NNS performance, such as screening volume, volume of alerts generated, false positives and true matches, and quality assurance outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Screening volume Volume of alerts generated False positives and true matches Quality assurance outcomes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Management should also be given regular reports on the relationships the FI has reviewed and exited because of NNS. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The relationships reviewed and exited by the FI due to NNS"</sub>
