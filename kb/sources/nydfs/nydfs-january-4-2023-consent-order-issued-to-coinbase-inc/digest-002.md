## Introduction and Procedural History

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Department identified troubling examples of suspicious conduct at Coinbase that should have been identified, stopped, and in some cases reported but was not, at least initially, due to the TMS backlog, including possible money laundering, suspected CSAM-related activity and potential narcotics trafficking; the full extent of backlog activity has not been determined. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Department has identified troubling examples of suspicious conduct that should have been identified, stopped"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One primary reason for requiring a transaction monitoring system is to identify and prevent future suspicious transactions so bad actors cannot use a financial institution to facilitate illegal activity; because of the backlogs Coinbase's TMS failed to sufficiently accomplish that goal. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One of the primary reasons for requiring a TMS is so that a financial institution can identify and prevent future suspicious transactions"</sub>

## Suspicious Activity Reporting Deficiencies

- **mandatory / requirement**: Financial institutions have the obligation to investigate timely and report to the federal government any suspicious activity in the form of a SAR within 30 days of detection. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions have the obligation to timely investigate and report to the Federal government any suspicious activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase routinely failed to timely investigate and report suspicious activity as required by law, as uninvestigated TMS alerts languished for months in the backlog. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as uninvestigated TMS alerts languished for months in the backlog, Coinbase routinely failed to timely investigate"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Department's investigation found numerous SARs filed months, some more than six months, after the suspicious activity was first known to Coinbase. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Department’s investigation found numerous examples of SARs filed months, some more than six months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase's record keeping of suspicious activity investigations and reporting was insufficient; it could not meaningfully respond to the Department's request for 2018 and 2019 data because it did not adequately track or retain that information. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase was unable to meaningfully respond to the Department’s request for data related to suspicious activity"</sub>

## KYC and PEP Screening

- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF PEP list identifies individuals who are or have been entrusted with a prominent function; PEPs may present higher risk than other customers by having access to funds that may be proceeds of corruption or other illicit activity. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Politically Exposed Persons (“PEP”) list is a list of individuals who are or have been entrusted with a prominent function"</sub>
- **expected / context**: A PEP designation alone does not indicate illegal activity, but it should lead financial institutions, including Coinbase, to examine the PEP's transactions more closely, which may mean raising the customer's risk rating. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PEP designation is not itself an indicator of illegal activity, but should make financial institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Approximately 1,600 institutional customers were subject to sanctions and PEP screening at onboarding but were not subject to ongoing sanctions or PEP screening until December 2020. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While approximately 1,600 institutional customers were subject to sanctions and PEP screening at onboarding"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: According to Coinbase, and consistent with FinCEN regulations, PEP screening is conducted on a risk basis, covering customer relationships posing the greatest risk for potential illicit activity including all related parties of U.S. institutional clients. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PEP screening is conducted on a risk basis. Coinbase conducts PEP screening for its customer relationships"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Until PEP screening of its customer base was complete, Coinbase was insufficiently aware whether that base was at higher risk for corruption, bribery, money laundering and other illicit financial activity. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Until that screening was complete, Coinbase was insufficiently aware of whether members of that customer base"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: OFAC maintains geographical sanctions against broad sectors of the economies of nations such as Iran, Cuba, Syria, Russia and North Korea, requiring a company like Coinbase to understand where its users are physically located. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such prohibitions necessarily require a company like Coinbase to understand where its users are physically located"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Coinbase allows users to access its sites using VPNs or The Onion Router (TOR), which allow a user to appear to be located in a jurisdiction other than their actual physical location. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both methods allow a user to appear to be located in a jurisdiction other than that of the user’s actual, physical location."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase has never promulgated a risk-based policy, such as raising risk level when such tools are detected; instead it allows investigators to consider such activity as a factor in investigations. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase has never promulgated a risk-based policy"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase knows widely available technology exists to circumvent geographic restrictions and that some customers use it, and has not structured its compliance program to fully account for that use, even though it includes certain mitigating controls addressing VPNs. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has not structured its compliance program to fully account for the use of that technology"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: VPNs are described as a means of using a proxy web address as an interface between a user and a website. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VPNs are a means of using a proxy web address as an interface"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: TOR spreads web traffic across a distributed, anonymous network so that the network's exit nodes appear to be the user's web address. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TOR disseminates web traffic across a distributed and anonymous network"</sub>

## Cybersecurity Event Reporting Requirements

- **mandatory / requirement**: Coinbase was required by 23 NYCRR § 500.17 to report the cybersecurity event to the Department within 72 hours of its discovery. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase was required by 23 NYCRR § 500.17 to report this event to the Department within 72 hours"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase reported the event to the United States Secret Service on May 19, 2021 but did not report it to the Department until September 17, 2021, five months after the event. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "did not report this event to the Department until September 17, 2021, five months after the event occurred"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase has since updated its internal procedures to ensure timely notification of incidents to the Department. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase has since updated its internal procedures to ensure timely notification of incidents are made to the Department."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2021 about 6,000 Coinbase customers appear to have been victims of a phishing scam unrelated to Coinbase, which led to unauthorised access to their accounts; about $1.5 million was stolen from New York customers. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Approximately $1.5 million was stolen from Coinbase’s New York customers."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Coinbase reimbursed all customers who lost funds and worked closely with law enforcement to help hold the scam's organisers accountable. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase also reimbursed all customers who lost funds and worked closely with law enforcement"</sub>

## Coinbase’s Remediation Efforts

- **descriptive / context**: Coinbase has invested substantial time and resources in remediation, including a dynamic risk rating model for new accounts, a KYC Refresh of pre-existing customers, new periodic review procedures, an upgraded investigations portal, and new senior legal and financial crimes compliance hires; some systems have not been fully successful to date. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has implemented for all new accounts a dynamic risk rating model for both retail and institutional customers"</sub>

## Violations of Law and Regulations

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Department found Coinbase conducted business in an unsafe and unsound manner, in violation of New York Banking Law § 44. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase conducted business in an unsafe and unsound manner, in violation of"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase failed to maintain an effective and compliant BSA/AML program, in violation of 23 NYCRR § 200.15 and 3 NYCRR § 417.2. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase failed to maintain an effective and compliant BSA/AML program, in violation of 23 NYCRR § 200.15"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase failed to maintain an effective transaction monitoring program, in violation of 23 NYCRR § 504.3. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase failed to comply with its obligations to maintain an effective transaction monitoring program"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Coinbase failed to properly report a cybersecurity incident to the Department, in violation of 23 NYCRR § 500.17. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase failed to properly report a cybersecurity incident to the Department"</sub>

## Monetary Penalty

- **mandatory / requirement**: No later than ten days after the Effective Date, Coinbase shall pay a civil monetary penalty of fifty million U.S. dollars under Banking Law §§ 39 and 44 and Financial Services Law § 408, by wire transfer per Department instructions. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase shall pay a civil monetary penalty to the Department pursuant to Banking Law §§ 39 and 44"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Coinbase shall not claim or apply for any tax deduction or tax credit for any portion of the civil monetary penalty. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase shall not claim, assert, or apply for a tax deduction or tax credit"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Coinbase shall neither seek nor accept reimbursement or indemnification for the penalty, including through any insurance policy. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Company shall neither seek nor accept, directly or indirectly, reimbursement or indemnification"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In setting the penalty the Department considered the factors in New York Banking Law § 44(5); the egregiousness of the failures was an aggravating factor, while cooperation, willingness to enter an MOU, engagement with the Independent Consultant and Monitor, and remediation investment were mitigating. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Although the egregiousness of the compliance failures here are an aggravating factor"</sub>

## Continuation of the Independent Monitor

- **descriptive / context**: The Independent Monitor has been engaged since April 2022 under the MOU. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Independent Monitor selected by the Department has been engaged since April 2022"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The monitorship continues for a further twelve months from the Effective Date, extendable by the Department in its sole regulatory discretion, and Coinbase must cooperate fully with the Monitor. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The parties agree to extend the Independent Monitor’s work for a further twelve (12) months"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Independent Monitor shall issue a final report summarising remediation completed, evaluating the compliance program and recommending any further remediation necessary. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Independent Monitor shall issue a final report to the Department that will summarize the remediation efforts"</sub>

## Commitment to Invest in Compliance Improvements

- **mandatory / requirement**: Within 24 months of the Effective Date, Coinbase commits to spend no less than fifty million U.S. dollars on further compliance program improvements (the Compliance Investment), covering costs incurred after the Effective Date directly related to those improvements. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase commits to spend no less than fifty million U.S. dollars"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Within 60 days of the Effective Date, after consulting the Independent Monitor, Coinbase shall submit to the Department for approval an Investment Plan specifying the activities and engagements for the Compliance Investment funds and an expected timeline. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase shall submit to the Department for approval a plan identifying with specificity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: After the Department approves the Investment Plan, Coinbase shall provide a quarterly update on progress and expenditures under the plan. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase shall provide to the Department a quarterly update describing progress on the Investment Plan"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: If the Department finds disbursements were allocated to activities not on the approved plan or were otherwise inappropriate, the Department may deem those expenditures not deductible from the Compliance Investment amount. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Department may deem, in its sole regulatory discretion, that such expenditures will not be deducted"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Fees and costs paid to the Independent Monitor after the Effective Date may be counted as part of the Compliance Investment. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fees and costs paid to the Independent Monitor after the Effective Date of this Consent Order may be counted"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Material changes to the content or timing of the Investment Plan over the 24-month period should be submitted to the Department for approval. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any material changes to the content or timing of the Investment Plan over the"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Unless the Department has approved a different timeline, any part of the $50,000,000 Compliance Investment unspent after the 24-month period is forfeitable to the Department at its discretion. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any part of the $50,000,000 Compliance Investment that remains unspent after the 24-month period set forth above is forfeitable"</sub>

## Full and Complete Cooperation

- **mandatory / requirement**: Coinbase commits to fully cooperate with the Department regarding all terms of the Consent Order. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coinbase commits and agrees that it will fully cooperate with the Department regarding all terms"</sub>

## Further Action by the Department

- **descriptive / requirement**: The Department will take no further action against Coinbase or its successors for the conduct or remediation in the Consent Order, provided the Company fully complies with its terms. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No further action will be taken by the Department against the Company or its"</sub>

## Breach of Consent Order

- **descriptive / requirement**: Failure to make the required showing within the designated period is presumptive evidence of breach, and upon a finding of breach the Department has all remedies available under New York Banking and Financial Services Law. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "failure to make the required showing within the designated time period shall be presumptive evidence"</sub>

## Parties Bound by the Consent Order

- **descriptive / context**: The Consent Order binds the Department and Coinbase and their successors and assigns, but does not bind any federal or other state agency or law enforcement authority. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Consent Order does not bind any federal or other state agency or any law enforcement authority."</sub>

- **descriptive / context**: The parties state their understanding and agreement that no provision of the Consent Order is subject to review in any court, tribunal or agency outside the Department. (p. 26)[^original]

## Breach of Consent Order

- **mandatory / requirement**: If the Department gives notice of a suspected material breach, the Company must appear before the Department within ten days to show no material breach occurred, or that any breach is not material or has been cured; the Department may set a later date in its sole discretion.
