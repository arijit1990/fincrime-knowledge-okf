## I. Parties and Regulatory Framework

- **descriptive / context**: Paxos is a virtual currency company offering stablecoins, asset tokenization, settlement services and cryptocurrency brokerage, and operates in New York as a limited purpose trust company under a charter granted by DFS in 2015. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos operates as a limited purpose trust company in New York under a"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Companies conducting virtual currency business in New York must be authorized by DFS, either through the Limited Purpose Trust Charter or a BitLicense, and are subject to ongoing DFS supervision. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be authorized to do so by the Department"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: As a chartered limited purpose trust company, Paxos is obligated to abide by DFS laws and regulations, including establishing, implementing and maintaining an effective AML program plus transaction monitoring and filtering programs. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is obligated to abide by the Department’s laws and regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Superintendent has power to conduct investigations, bring enforcement proceedings, levy monetary penalties and order injunctive relief against violators of relevant laws and regulations. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "levy monetary penalties, and order injunctive relief against parties"</sub>

## B. Anti-Money Laundering Regulations

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under 3 NYCRR § 116.2, Paxos is required to establish and maintain an effective and compliant AML program including a robust customer due diligence program. At minimum it should provide: (1) internal controls, policies and procedures ensuring ongoing AML compliance; (2) independent testing by qualified internal personnel or a qualified external party; (3) designated qualified individual(s) coordinating and monitoring day-to-day compliance; (4) ongoing training for appropriate personnel. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos is required to establish and maintain an"</sub>

## C. Transaction Monitoring and Filtering Program Regulations

- **mandatory / requirement**: Part 504 of the Superintendent's Regulations sets minimum requirements for bank and non-bank institutions, including Paxos, on monitoring customer transactions and complying with OFAC screening requirements. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establishes certain minimum requirements governing financial institutions’ monitoring of customer transactions"</sub>

## II. Events at Issue

- **descriptive / case example**: In September 2018 Paxos partnered with Binance, described as the world's largest digital asset exchange, to list its PAX stablecoin; in 2019 it partnered again to market and distribute BUSD to expand market share and customer base. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In September 2018, Paxos partnered with Binance, the world’s largest digital asset exchange"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In July and August 2019 Paxos asked Binance for assurances on geofencing so U.S. customers could not access an unregulated platform. Binance's CCO said the policies were in effect and that US persons were completely restricted. Paxos accepted this without independent review or supporting documentation beyond its initial review for the PAX listing. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos accepted Binance at its word and did not undertake an independent review"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: When DFS asked about Binance's compliance program, Paxos drafted a letter for Binance to send to DFS in August 2019 describing IP-blocking of U.S. users and a manual KYC check for masked IPs or VPN use; Binance's CCO approved it and Paxos did not test or verify the claims. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos did not test or otherwise verify Binance’s claims."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: On July 24, 2020 Paxos signed the 2020 Letter Agreement with DFS. It obligated Paxos to review Binance's AML, sanctions, KYC and related policies, maintain, administer, monitor and revise effective controls against wrongful BUSD use, ensure Binance timely informed Paxos of material policy changes, and conduct periodic due diligence refreshes of Binance. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos was obligated to review Binance’s AML, sanctions, KYC"</sub>

## ii. Article About Binance’s Geofencing Failures

- **descriptive / context**: In October 2020 a press outlet reported that Binance accepted U.S. customers via VPNs to evade U.S. regulatory scrutiny and sought to undermine U.S. AML and sanctions enforcement. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Binance was accepting U.S. customers through the use of Virtual Private Networks"</sub>

## ii. Article About Binance’s Geofencing Failures and Paxos’s Response and Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: After the press report, Paxos asked Binance to support its assertion by providing its independent AML audit and a geofencing-specific audit. Binance provided its most recent KYC review report, and its CCO repeated that a U.S. person cannot even pass KYC. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Binance provided its most recent KYC review report to Paxos"</sub>

## ii. Article About Binance’s Geofencing Failures

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Binance's geofencing was deficient and circumventable by U.S. persons; since at least April 2019 Binance published a 'Beginner's Guide to VPNs' on Binance Academy saying VPNs can protect location privacy and unlock sites restricted in one's country. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Binance’s geofencing was deficient and circumventable by U.S. persons"</sub>

## ii. Article About Binance’s Geofencing Failures and Paxos’s Response and Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paxos's first monthly due diligence refresh identified 99 U.S.-based retail and institutional clients that transferred BUSD directly to Binance.com, including several large U.S. institutional trading firms and market makers. Two Paxos employees with legacy Binance.com accounts accessed the exchange and traded from New York IP addresses. The monthly refreshes routinely returned new and existing U.S. users on Binance.com; the number generally declined each month and never exceeded the initial 99, yet the geofencing restrictions were shown to be illusory. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos identified 99 U.S.-based Paxos retail and institutional clients"</sub>

## iii. Binance’s Exposure to Illicit Activity

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under the 2020 Letter Agreement Paxos had to conduct periodic risk assessments and due diligence refreshes of Binance and inform DFS immediately of any material increase in risk involving Binance. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inform the Department immediately if there was a material increase in any risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: DFS's 2022 Examination found Paxos failed to show appropriate controls to monitor significant illicit activity at or through Binance and failed to escalate red flags to senior management and the Board. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "failed to escalate red flags to Paxos’s senior management and its Board"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A blockchain analytics firm's EDD of Binance covering July 2017 to November 2022 on a select set of assets identified $1.6 billion in transactions with illicit actors (darknet and carding marketplaces, Ponzi schemes). Binance processed transactions with entities after OFAC sanctioned them, including Chatex, Hydra Market and Tornado Cash; over $32 million went to or from Chatex (Russian ransomware red flags), about $800,000 after its OFAC designation. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identified $1.6 billion in transactions flowing to or from the Binance platform"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In January 2023 Paxos's then CCO wrote that Binance appeared to have a reasonable compliance program but lacked an independent external audit report attesting to compliance, and QA reviews were incomplete with no remediation plans. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Binance does not have an independent external audit report of their AML/Sanctions program"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: On February 13, 2023 DFS ordered Paxos to cease minting Paxos-issued BUSD over unresolved issues in oversight of the Binance relationship; Paxos then notified customers it would end the BUSD relationship with Binance. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ordered Paxos to cease minting Paxos-issued BUSD"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: DFS's October 2022 closing letter found that, contrary to Paxos's position, Binance had been significantly exposed to potentially illicit high-risk activity since shortly after the 2020 Letter Agreement. Paxos's knowledge rested solely on review of Binance's policies and its external auditor's limited review of them, with no third-party assurance of Binance's actual compliance. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos did not have third-party assurances that attested to Binance’s degree of compliance"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2022 Examination required Paxos to report on Binance's exposure to sanctions, terrorist financing, darknet marketplaces and other illicit activity, which led Paxos to ask a third-party blockchain analytics firm to perform an EDD investigation of Binance. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which required Paxos to provide a report documenting Binance’s exposure to sanctions, terrorist financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paxos's failure to conduct proper due diligence on Binance's geofencing controls and deficient BSA/AML programs breached the 2020 Letter Agreement. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "constituted a breach of the 2020 Letter Agreement."</sub>

## B. Paxos’s Compliance Failures Unrelated to Binance

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Before 2023 Paxos's BSA/AML function was deficient: despite 2022 DFS guidance on strengthening KYC controls, it onboarded customers with limited insight into identity, business legitimacy or source of funds, letting customers with coordinated-activity characteristics open multiple accounts; it lacked standardized investigation guidelines; and its policies did not address certain trade-based money laundering risks nor was staff trained on them. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos onboarded customers with limited insight into their true identities"</sub>

## i. Paxos's Know-your-Customer Failures

- **descriptive / context**: DFS describes KYC as a core component of an effective BSA/AML program that enables establishing identity, assigning a customer risk rating and managing the risk; it is the first line of defense. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This first line of defense is critical to combating financial crime."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paxos's centralized compliance software lacked automated alerts for risky shared customer attributes and frontline search at onboarding, so customers sharing addresses, corporate documents, beneficial owners and behavioral characteristics could open multiple accounts undetected. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "did not include automated alerts to indicate potentially risky shared customer attributes"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Paxos onboarded 11 businesses in the same single-story South Florida strip mall without identifying shared attributes or generating an alert. Three were associated with an individual who transacted about $260 million over fourteen months and was listed as accountant for a company preparing books for at least four other customers. Staff acknowledged the system did not adequately handle linking of individuals and entities. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paxos onboarded 11 businesses located in the same single-story"</sub>

## B. Paxos’s Compliance Failures Unrelated to Binance

- **descriptive / context**: The BSA requires financial institutions to establish, implement and maintain an effective AML program, and New York law imposes the same requirements on regulated institutions such as Paxos. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "New York law imposes these same requirements on regulated financial institutions such as Paxos"</sub>

## i. Paxos's Know-your-Customer Failures

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Although Paxos's written policies required information on source of funds, occupation and account purpose, it failed to take reasonable steps to investigate suspicious account-opening or EDD materials, relied on customers' written responses without adequate supporting documentation, accepted unverified photographs and generalized answers, and onboarded customers with false or insufficient documents (e.g., invoices, bank statements) not matching stated business activity. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "failed to take reasonable steps to investigate account holders who submitted suspicious"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The order states that financial institutions can guard against money laundering by requiring verification of a prospective customer's source of funds. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions can guard against money laundering by requiring verification of a prospective customer’s source of funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In 2023 a third-party auditor covering July 2021 to June 2022 found deficiencies in customer due diligence at onboarding and after, warning of inaccurate identification and assessment of customer money laundering risk. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identified deficiencies in the company’s customer due diligence process at onboarding"</sub>

## ii. Paxos’s Transaction Monitoring Failures

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Money launderers often break large transactions into smaller ones to avoid alerts and use multiple real or fabricated identities on either side to anonymize; a compliant AML program will include control scenarios to detect structuring attempts. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "money launderers often break up large transactions into smaller transactions"</sub>

## i. Paxos's Know-your-Customer Failures

- **descriptive / case example**: In 2023 Paxos compliance employees commented on lax due diligence: one said every export or trading company on the platform seemed fake; another noted an unlicensed money services business was onboarded and that it was also an OTC desk with zero trades. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "I feel like every export or trading company we have on platform is fake"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A third compliance employee said that for a newer OTC desk nothing showed it should be conducting its volume of activity, only likely fake policies and org documents, and it was let go because it was an OTC desk; DFS called this illustrative of significant EDD failings. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we never received anything from them showing that they should be conducting this volume of activity"</sub>

## ii. Paxos’s Transaction Monitoring Failures

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paxos's 2019, 2020 and 2021 risk assessments rated its transaction monitoring 'fair' because systems were retrospective; its BSA Officer said in March 2020 that patterns or darknet-sourced Bitcoin would not be known until 2-4 weeks later. Manual, technologically limited real-time withdrawal monitoring missed easily detectable patterns, and QA found it failed to flag alerts as suspicious warranting investigation. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we would not know until 2-4 weeks later"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A trade-based money laundering (TBML) network used Paxos's exchange for about five years, with rapid fiat-to-crypto or crypto-to-fiat movement (U.S. dollar wire withdrawals of all funds to U.S. institutions), transactions minutes apart, many round-dollar deposits, rarely any balance, multiple accounts and small or zero end-of-day balances, likely layering. Customers stated an intent to buy and sell crypto but activity indicated otherwise, and went undetected. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is likely that the accounts were used to layer funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: By 2022 Paxos had improved its monitoring systems but still failed to tune them to AML risks by assessing the business rules and scenarios; that year DFS issued blockchain analytics guidance emphasizing tailored monitoring programs. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it was still failing to appropriately tune these systems to the relevant AML risks"</sub>

## iii. Paxos's Investigations Failures

- **mandatory / requirement**: DFS licensees must maintain protocols setting out how transaction monitoring alerts are investigated, how decisions on filing or other action are made, which operating areas and individuals decide, and how the investigation and decision process is documented. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Licensees must maintain protocols that set forth how alerts generated"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Before 2022 Paxos's investigations policy did not require investigation on a law enforcement request, leaving it to the investigator's discretion. A 2021 audit, a 2022 internal audit and a 2023 audit flagged missing minimum case investigation and due diligence requirements. An external consultant found Paxos failed to investigate 79 of 188 information requests in two years; the policy was updated in early 2023 to require investigation. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "failed to investigate 79 of 188 information requests it received"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Of the TBML network, 32 accounts had earlier come to Paxos's attention via law enforcement subpoenas or other financial institutions' requests, yet staff failed to identify the larger network. QA reported staff did not want or have time to investigate alerts and found a lack of accountability for poor quality work. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "32 accounts had previously been brought to Paxos’s attention"</sub>

## Cooperation

- **descriptive / context**: DFS concluded enforcement action including a monetary penalty is warranted. It gave substantial weight to Paxos's cooperation, including complying with the order to stop minting BUSD and cooperating in terminating the Binance relationship. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enforcement action is warranted, including a monetary penalty."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: DFS recognized Paxos's compliance improvements: growing its compliance team, expanding vendor support, dedicating non-compliance personnel to build compliance tooling, and engaging an outside consultant in January 2023 for a multi-year lookback and root cause analysis, shared with DFS, with remediation implemented in real time. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "growing its compliance team, expanding the scope of its vendor support and expertise"</sub>
