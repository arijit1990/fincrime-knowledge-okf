## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF report (July 2021) states that new technologies have the potential to make AML/CFT measures faster, cheaper and more effective, and can improve implementation of FATF Standards, ensure financial inclusion and avoid unintended consequences such as financial exclusion. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "New technologies have the potential to make anti-money laundering (AML) and counter terrorist financing measures (CFT) faster"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF is strongly committed to keeping abreast of innovative technologies and business models and ensuring global standards stay up-to-date to enable "smart" financial sector regulation that addresses risks and promotes responsible innovation. The project reviewed opportunities and challenges of new technologies, persisting obstacles to implementation and how to mitigate them, including regulatory technology (RegTech) and supervisory technology (SupTech). (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This project included the review and analysis of regulatory technology (RegTech) and supervisory technology (SupTech)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Innovative skills, methods and processes, and innovative uses of established technology-based processes, can help regulators, supervisors and regulated entities overcome many AML/CFT challenges. Technology can facilitate data collection, processing and analysis and help identify and manage ML/TF risks more effectively and closer to real time; faster payments, more accurate identification systems, monitoring, record keeping and information sharing also offer advantages. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "help actors identify and manage money laundering and terrorist financing (ML/TF) risks more effectively and closer to real time"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Digital solutions based on AI and its subsets (machine learning, natural language processing) can potentially help better identify risks and respond to, communicate and monitor suspicious activity. In the public sector, improved real-time monitoring and information exchange enable more informed oversight; in the private sector technology can improve risk assessments, onboarding, relationships with competent authorities, auditability, accountability and governance while saving cost. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At private sector level, technology can improve risk assessments, onboarding practices, relationships with competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The report identifies challenges to development, adoption and application of innovative solutions, many due to outstanding operational and regulatory constraints such as legacy AML/CFT compliance systems and traditional regulatory frameworks and oversight mechanisms. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legacy AML/CFT compliance systems and traditional regulatory frameworks and oversight mechanisms"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The complexity and cost of replacing or updating legacy systems make it challenging for industry and government to exploit innovative approaches. For industry, the cost-benefit analysis of adopting new technologies remains an obstacle, based in part on a real or perceived lack of regulatory incentives. Explainability and interpretability of digital solutions is another key challenge for industry and regulators, partly stemming from limited relevant expertise and lack of awareness of technologies' potential among AML/CFT professionals. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the cost-benefit analysis to adopt new technologies continues to be an obstacle to greater uptake"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Increased public-private communication and cooperation, informed by the report's analysis, together with an emphasis on responsible adoption and effectiveness (in particular regarding data protection regulations), will be key to overcoming these challenges and realising responsible innovation's promise. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Increased communication and cooperation between the public and private sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Combining the efficiency and accuracy of digital solutions with the knowledge and analytical skills of human experts produces more robust systems that can respond to AML/CFT requirements while being fully auditable and accountable. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Combining the efficiency and accuracy of digital solutions with the knowledge and analytical skills of human experts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Where innovative AML/CFT technologies are used responsibly and proportionally, they can help identify risks and direct compliance effort to existing and emerging challenges, yet manual review and human input stay very important. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "manual review and human input remains very important"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Even where the regulatory environment enables technology, human actors must be relied upon to identify and assess any residual risks that new technologies present and to put appropriate mitigation measures in place. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "human actors must be relied upon to identity and assess any residual risks presented by new technologies"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: That development, adoption and supervision must also ensure that innovative tools are used in a way that is compatible with international standards on data protection, privacy and cybersecurity. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "compatible with international standards of data protection, privacy, and cybersecurity"</sub>

## 1. Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: Thirty years after their initial adoption, CDD and related procedures have greatly increased the transparency of transactions and made it harder for criminals, terrorist financiers and proliferation financiers to misuse financial products. Customer identification/verification and monitoring is a key pillar of the AML/CFT framework but continues to present implementation and effectiveness challenges. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it continues to present challenges of implementation and effectiveness"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Non-risk targeted CDD efforts can be perceived as costly and inefficient, as they often do not translate into accurate risk assessment processes or smooth access to financial services. In light of accelerating innovation and digital transformation, the FATF launched an initiative to examine the potential of new technologies to mitigate ML/TF threats. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Non-risk targeted CDD efforts can be perceived to be costly and inefficient"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For the report, "new technologies for AML/CFT" refers to (a) innovative skills, methods and processes used to achieve goals relating to effective implementation of AML/CFT requirements, or (b) innovative ways to use established technology-based processes to comply with AML/CFT obligations. The terms digital solutions, digital tools, innovative solutions or systems are used interchangeably for them. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "innovative ways to use established technology-based processes to comply with AML/CFT obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: New technologies seek to improve speed, quality, efficiency and cost of some AML/CFT measures compared with traditional methods. The most relevant technologies are cross-cutting, enabling new digital ways to collect, process, analyse and communicate data; many of their capabilities and implications are still largely unknown, but it is essential to understand their current capabilities and potential impact. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Many of these new technologies’ capabilities and implications are still largely unknown."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Digital identity solutions can enable non-face-to-face customer identification/verification and updating of information, improve authentication for more secure account access, and strengthen identification and authentication for in-person onboarding and transactions, promoting financial inclusion and combating money laundering, fraud, terrorist financing and other illicit financing. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "digital identity solutions can enable non-face-to-face customer identification/verification and updating of information"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Natural language processing can support more accurate, flexible and timely analysis of customer information, reduce inaccurate or false information and enable more efficient matching and search for additional data. Better, more up-to-date customer profiles mean more accurate risk assessments, better decisions and fewer instances of unintended financial exclusion. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "natural language processing can support more accurate, flexible and timely analysis of customer information"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: AI and machine learning solutions applied to big data can strengthen ongoing monitoring and reporting of suspicious transactions by automatically monitoring, processing and analysing suspicious transactions and other illicit activity, distinguishing it from normal activity in real time while reducing the need for initial front-line human review. They can also generate more accurate and complete ongoing CDD and customer risk assessments, updated in real time for new and emerging threats. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "distinguishing it from normal activity in real time, whilst reducing the need for initial, front-line human review"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: AI/ML solutions vary greatly in technology and use and may present significant risks, which the report discusses later. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI/ML solutions vary greatly in both technology and use and may present significant risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Innovative solutions such as APIs, Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT), data standardisation and machine readable regulations can help regulated entities (financial institutions, VASPs and DNFBPs) report more efficiently to supervisors and competent authorities, and enable alerts, report follow-ups and other communications between authorities, regulated entities and their customers. More advanced analytics by regulators can strengthen examination and supervision, potentially giving more accurate and immediate feedback. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can help regulated entities2 report more efficiently to supervisors and other competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Embrace of new technologies for AML/CFT compliance and supervision has in some instances been impeded by concerns about whether and how innovative technologies may be used under the FATF Recommendations and under countries' AML/CFT regulatory frameworks. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has been impeded in some instances by concerns as to whether and how innovative technologies may be used"</sub>

## 1.1. FATF Commitment to Responsible Innovation and Digital Transformation

- **descriptive / context**: As a global standard setting body, the FATF is committed to keeping abreast of innovative technologies and business models and ensuring global AML/CFT standards remain relevant and effective amid accelerating digital transformation, so its requirements can enable "smart" regulation that drives responsible innovation furthering both AML/CFT and financial inclusion objectives. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "committed to keeping abreast of innovative technologies and business models in the financial sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF formally endorsed responsible innovation for AML/CFT in a public statement issued in Buenos Aires on 3 November 2017, which said it strongly supports responsible financial innovation in line with the AML/CFT requirements in the FATF Standards and will continue to explore the opportunities new financial and regulatory technologies may present for improving effective implementation of AML/CFT measures. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF strongly supports responsible financial innovation that is in line with the AML/CFT requirements found in the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The 2017 statement built on earlier FATF efforts to support responsible innovation while addressing illicit finance risks and AML/CFT regulatory and supervisory challenges of emerging technologies, including numerous guidance and best practices papers, updating the Recommendations to address virtual assets (FATF, 2019), and extensive private sector engagement through public-private workshops and the FATF Private Sector Consultative Forums (PSCF). (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "updating the Recommendations to address virtual assets (FATF, 2019[1])"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: UN Security Council Resolution 2462(2019) called upon all States to enhance traceability and transparency of financial transactions, including by fully exploiting new and emerging financial and regulatory technologies to bolster financial inclusion and contribute to effective implementation of AML/CFT measures. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enhance the traceability and transparency of financial transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Despite acknowledged benefits, effective use of innovative technologies for AML/CFT has been limited by a variety of factors, affecting different regulated entities and supervisors to different degrees. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the effective use of innovative technologies for AML/CFT has been limited by a variety of factors"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF German Presidency launched a digital transformation initiative with three projects: (1) the study underlying this report, on opportunities and challenges of new technology for private sector and supervisors' AML/CFT implementation; (2) a study of opportunities and challenges for operational agencies, aimed at making systems to detect and investigate ML/TF and understand risks more efficient; and (3) a stocktake on data pooling, collaborative analytics and data protection, aimed at helping the private sector improve use of AI and big data analytics while ensuring a high level of data protection. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A stocktake on data pooling, collaborative analytics and data protection"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report aims to: increase awareness of and identify opportunities to leverage new technologies; identify conditions, policies and practices supporting further adoption, illustrated by case studies; examine regulatory obstacles or other factors impeding adoption and where relevant propose additional FATF projects on policy responses; and provide a common set of definitions, conceptual framework and suggested actions for government authorities and private sector stakeholders. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Examine regulatory obstacles or other factors impeding the successful adoption of new technologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report focuses on how new technologies may help jurisdictions and regulated entities implement AML/CFT standards more effectively, in particular digital solutions that enable better understanding, assessment and mitigation of risks, customer due diligence and monitoring, and communication with supervisors. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "digital solutions which enable a better understanding, assessment and mitigation of risks"</sub>

## 1.2. Scope and Methodology

- **descriptive / definition**: The report addresses implementation of new technologies known as RegTech, such as AI, machine learning, big data and advanced cognitive analytics/algorithms targeting customer identification and verification requirements and broader AML/CFT compliance obligations. RegTech is defined in a footnote as a sub-set of FinTech focused on technologies that may deliver regulatory requirements more efficiently and effectively than existing capabilities. The project also considers SupTech, the use of innovative technology by supervisory agencies to support supervision, for example risk assessment tools and data visualisation tools. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The project also considers SupTech5 or technologies used by supervisory agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The research considers, where technologies have been deployed successfully, the preconditions that enabled effective use, the benefits achieved, and any new requirements resulting from successful use of innovative solutions. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "what were the preconditions which enabled their effective use, what were the benefits achieved"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report also considers cases where promising technologies were not successfully deployed, identifies obstacles, and explores whether coordinated global action is needed to enable greater use of innovative technology-based solutions, including structural challenges such as data quality, changing legacy systems, cost constraints and lack of regulatory incentives. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issues of data quality, changing legacy systems, cost constraints and the lack of regulatory incentives"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Where technologies offer real benefit and respond effectively to threats, the FATF analyses use-cases from early adopters so other regulated entities and authorities can implement them most effectively. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF analyses use-cases from early-adopters of new technologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Technologies relevant to better implementation of FATF Standards but not proposed for analysis in this report include data management and sharing tools, and analytic tools including the use of machine learning and big data analytics by FIUs. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Analytic tools including the use of machine learning and big data analytics by FIUs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report relies on desk-based research and responses to an online digital transformation Questionnaire disseminated by the FATF Secretariat to government authorities and public and private sector experts, plus consultation with stakeholders including a virtual High-Level Roundtable on 10 March 2021. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "responses to an online digital transformation Questionnaire6 which the FATF Secretariat disseminated"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FATF Digital Transformation questionnaire sought views on main adopters of new technologies, purposes and added value of technology-based solutions under AML/CFT and other regulatory frameworks, their impact on users' relationships with supervisors, obstacles to implementation, and the relationship of new technologies to the FATF Standards; it encouraged case studies. It collected 188 responses. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It collected 188 responses, including case-studies and examples of digital solutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: 54% of questionnaire respondents identified as private sector representatives, mainly large banks and technology developers; at public sector level, the majority of responses were submitted by supervisors. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "54% of respondents identified as private sector representatives, mainly large banks and technology developers"</sub>
