## Annex C: Case Studies - Brazil

- **descriptive / case example**: Brazil: the Central Bank of Brazil's Integrated System for Supervision Support (SisCom, APS-Siscom from 2018) is a 2014 web-based system with a strong methodology that lets the BCB interact with supervised entities (SEs) securely and supports supervisory work. It provides a secure way to require and receive from SEs policies, manuals, managerial and audit reports, KYC files on specific clients and transactions, and SEs' written replies. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is a 2014 web-based system, supported by a strong methodology"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: APS-Siscom features: interaction during inspections to clarify issues and request complementary information; standardised inspection procedures allowing several inspections simultaneously; inspection templates (tailored requisition forms for a group of SEs, a sector or a single SE) stored in a portfolio, with a query showing how many SEs received a requisition; and automatic supervisory reports that can be assembled as an audit dossier. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "BCB supervisors can create tailored requisition forms for a group of SEs, SEs sector or a SE in particular"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: At the end of an inspection, deficiencies and breaches are communicated through the system and SEs must present, also through APS-SisCom, a correction plan subject to supervisor approval; the system controls and signals due dates and gives an up-to-date tally of deficiencies and breaches by completion status in a Business Intelligence report, and query features let supervisors track every inspection of a specific SE. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SEs are required to present, also through APS-SisCom, a correction plan subject to supervisor approval"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2018 Siscom was incorporated into the new BCB supervision platform SisAPS, which integrates several systems and databases and serves inspectors, supervisors and managers with a record panel of inspection work plus managerial information and monitoring reports. APS-SisCom brought an enormous productivity gain for BCB supervisory teams and avoids time-consuming visits to SEs. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Siscom was incorporated in the new BCB supervision platform SisAPS, which integrates several systems and databases"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Data collected by APS-Siscom feeds a methodology that lets the BCB segment and supervise banks and non-banking financial institutions (NBFI) by risk category; supervisors analyse quantitative and qualitative data to view level of compliance with specific regulatory requirements and a risk assessment using a rating categorisation. The BCB says this enables effective AML/FT supervision of hundreds of medium and small SEs across Brazil. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enabling effective AML/FT supervision of hundreds of medium and small SEs"</sub>

## Annex C: Case Studies - Hong Kong

- **descriptive / case example**: Hong Kong (HKMA): working with banks, the HKMA has encouraged exploration and responsible adoption of AML/CFT Regtech through its Fintech Supervisory Sandbox and Chatroom and an AML/CFT Regtech Forum in November 2019. It identified network analytics applications as a supervisory priority, supporting banks' contribution to outcomes of Hong Kong's public-private partnership, the Fraud and Money Laundering Intelligence Taskforce. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whichsupport banks to add greater value to outcomes being achieved"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Throughout 2020 the HKMA engaged banks on factors and dependencies affecting network analytics applications, to prepare responses for banks asking how to start. It shared a case study (HKMA, 2021) of a bank studying network analytics for several years, tracking adoption since 2013 and how it enhanced the bank's ability to identify a network of high ML/TF risk, including how challenges were overcome and some results. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The bank’s adoption of analytics is tracked since 2013"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The HKMA communicated Regtech as a key focus in its 2021 AML/CFT supervisory programme and detailed how it will use practices from its recent publication to build industry acceptance of key technologies and create conditions for all banks to explore and use Regtech, including network analytics. (p. 66)[^original]

## Annex C: Case Studies - Monetary Authority of Singapore

- **descriptive / case example**: Singapore (MAS), problem statement: MAS supervises FIs' ML/TF risk management and runs risk surveillance to detect systemic risks and target higher-risk areas and FIs. FIs' STRs provide useful information; complex typologies often involve multiple accounts at multiple FIs and manifest as multiple STRs over time, so MAS developed an STR network analytics tool to join the dots across FIs and across time. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Complex typologies often involve multiple accounts at multiple FIs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: MAS reports its STR network analytics tool helped identify concerning clusters of individuals/entities with suspicious behaviours and the FIs involved, sharpening prioritisation and targeting of risks in AML supervision. Insights and emerging risks are shared via ACIP, industry workshops, advisory notes and supervisory guidance, raising industry risk awareness and prompting FIs to expedite adoption of innovative data analytics. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "helped MAS identify concerning clusters of individuals/entities that exhibited suspicious behaviours"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Several concerning networks detected via the tool were escalated to Singapore's interagency committee of law enforcement and supervisory agencies, which investigates and develops risk mitigation plans for priority ML/TF cases, for deliberation and coordinated action. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Several concerning networks detected through our STR network analytics have been escalated"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Data inputs for the MAS network analysis initially comprised mainly structured data fields in STRs. MAS is enhancing the dataset by developing NLP models to extract information from unstructured STR text (e.g. narratives on unusual transactions and relationships between counterparties) and by ingesting more transaction data and company profile information, to strengthen identification of hidden connections and prioritisation of systemic risk concerns. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "developing natural language processing (NLP) models to extract information from the unstructured, textual data within STRs"</sub>

## Annex C: Case Studies - Malaysia

- **descriptive / case example**: Malaysia: the Financial Technology Regulatory Sandbox established by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) since 2016 lets BNM monitor the potential impact of innovation before setting formal regulatory requirements. Before 2017, Malaysian money service business (MSB) players could not transact without face-to-face contact with new customers unless a relationship and CDD had first been established; through the Sandbox two digital MSB players tested non face-to-face onboarding via e-KYC with risks adequately mitigated. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prior to 2017, Malaysian MSB players were not permitted to undertake any transaction without face-to-face contact"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Drawing on Sandbox lessons, BNM introduced a regulatory requirement on non face-to-face onboarding verification for the MSB sector at end-2017, with safeguards such as independent contact with the customer and transaction limits; seven remittance companies have been approved for e-KYC onboarding. The roll-out was gradual: e-KYC began in the remittance segment and was extended to money-changing in 2019. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To date, seven remittance companies have been approved to conduct e-KYC for on-boarding of new customers."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2020 BNM issued a revised AML/CFT policy document and an e-KYC policy document applicable to all financial institutions, setting out regulatory expectations on adopting e-KYC technology. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "BNM issued a revised AML/CFT policy document and e-KYC policy document applicable to all financial institutions in 2020"</sub>

## Annex D: Additional RegTech case studies

- **descriptive / case example**: Case study (smart alert management): a financial institution worked with a Singapore-based RegTech company on a machine learning solution for transaction monitoring and name screening, creating workflows that prioritise alerts by risk level so the compliance team focuses on the alerts that matter most. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "effectively creating workflows for prioritising alerts based on their risk levels"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The solution combines supervised and unsupervised machine learning to detect suspicious activity and high-risk clients, triaging transaction monitoring and name screening alerts into three risk buckets, L1, L2 and L3, with L3 the highest risk. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "segregating them into three risk buckets – L1, L2 and L3"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The tool has a self-learning mechanism for continuous learning and a patent-pending explainable AI framework that explains the rationale behind each alert prediction in a manner comprehensible to business users. On spotting a suspicious pattern it creates a smart rule added to the AML typology library so models detect similar patterns later, which over time is said to filter false positives and free staff for in-depth investigations. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "explains the rationale behind each alert prediction by the machine learning model"</sub>

## Case Study: Risk Management Solution

- **descriptive / case example**: Case study (risk management): a multinational financial institution uses big data and automated Contextual Monitoring to detect and disrupt financial crime in international trade. Contextual Monitoring joins data from different systems and sources to create context and identify significant connections, using advanced algorithms for more sophisticated scoring and analysis; customer activity can be continuously assessed and scored for risk. (p. 69)[^original]

## Annex D: Additional RegTech case studies

- **descriptive / method**: In this case study's machine-learning AML solution, the transaction monitoring module can rank known alerts by risk score and can also detect new, previously unknown suspicious patterns. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The transaction monitoring module is able to prioritise known alerts based on their risk scores"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The name screening module of the same solution has three core components: better name matching that covers a wider range of complex name permutations; fewer undetermined hits thanks to inference features; and accurate alert detection using primary and secondary information. Together these help distinguish false hits from true hits. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enhanced name matching through a wider range of complex name permutations"</sub>

## Case Study: Robotic Process Automation solutions

- **descriptive / case example**: Case study (RPA): a financial institution is developing Robotic Process Automation initiatives to improve efficiency of suspicious transaction investigations, PEP name screening, and KYC onboarding and recertification, with some natural language (translation) solutions. Its current machine learning AML detection solutions are rule-based models combined with data analytics, rule-based models with alert scoring, and rule-based models enriched with external data such as company registry data (not RPA-related). (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Current Machine learning solutions in place specifically on AML detection field include rule based models"</sub>

## Case Study: Digital ID solution

- **descriptive / case example**: Case study (digital ID): a membership body is developing a scheme for a single Digital ID meeting relevant regulatory requirements (KYC and AML), positioned to consumers as the prime means of securely identifying themselves to UK financial services. It works with Government on a National Trust Framework so the identity can be used across sectors through interoperable standards, and will depend on increased use of biometrics/video KYC, machine learning, NLP and blockchain/DLT; consumers could re-use verified identity and KYC attributes to open and access online financial services. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "develop a National Trust Framework so the scheme will allow the consumer to use their Digital Identity across multiple sectors"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: For proper risk scoring of transactional data, identifying all parties and geographies mentioned is key, but this can be challenging given varied transaction formats, human error and/or attempts by bad actors to obfuscate their identity; the RegTech team uses various techniques to extract and normalise data. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "attempts by bad actors to obfuscate their identity"</sub>

## Case Study: Risk and compliance firm addresses issues of data quality and consistency

- **descriptive / method**: Case study (data quality): a risk and compliance firm offering technology-based data handling for AML/CFT compliance first holds conversations with stakeholders, SMEs and technical teams, before data acquisition, to identify key data elements (KDEs). Once data is received, the team makes a copy of the original (Golden Source) to preserve integrity and auditability, then runs high-level analytics to understand data integrity and identify gaps. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the necessary technical teams, to identify key data elements (KDEs)."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: String normalisation removes special characters, extra white space and common corporate terms (LLC, OOO, Limited) to allow better grouping, classification and identification. Entity extraction is essential to any risk model and complicated by dirty or incomplete data; relying solely on identified KDEs can miss hidden entities. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Removal of special characters, extra white space, and common corporate terms (LLC, OOO, Limited)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: NLP is commonly used to identify parts of speech and scan the whole dataset for proper nouns that could indicate an individual or company; results still need additional analysis and cleansing because transactional data rarely follows typical grammar, so scans are supplemented by internal intelligence on the tokenised string. The team then builds a unique list of normalised entities while maintaining lineage to the original source. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transactional data rarely follow typical grammar"</sub>
