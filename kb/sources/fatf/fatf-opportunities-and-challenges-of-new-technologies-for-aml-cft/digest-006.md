## Annex A: Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: Advanced analytics is defined as autonomous or semi-autonomous examination of data or content using sophisticated techniques and digital tools, typically beyond traditional business intelligence, to discover deeper insights, make predictions or generate recommendations. Techniques include data/text mining, machine learning, pattern matching, forecasting, visualisation, semantic and sentiment analysis, network and cluster analysis, multivariate statistics, graph analysis, simulation, complex event processing and neural networks, and typically rely on big data. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Advanced analytics refers to the autonomous or semiautonomous examination of data or content"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: an application is computer software designed to help a user perform specific tasks; an algorithm is a set of step-by-step instructions to perform a specific task. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An application is computer software designed to help a user perform specific tasks."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: an Application Programming Interface (API) is a set of definitions and protocols for building and integrating application software, letting digital products or services readily communicate with other products and services. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An API is a set of definitions and protocols for building and integrating application software."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: an AI system is a machine-based system that can, for a set of human-defined objectives, make predictions, recommendations or decisions influencing real or virtual environments, operating with varying levels of autonomy (OECD, 2020). AI aims to automate some aspects of analysis, potentially saving human labour for subtler tasks. There is no consensus on what constitutes "thinking", "intelligence" or "fully autonomous"; AI systems generally combine intentionality, intelligence and adaptability, and machine learning is currently the most familiar and developed form of AI. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no consensus as to what constitutes “thinking” and “intelligence” or what is “fully autonomous,”"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: black box refers to AI/machine learning and other technologies that are opaque, non-intuitive and do not provide adequate information on their decision-making and predictions/results; i.e. black box technology lacks explainability. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Black box refers to AI/machine learning and other technologies that are opaque, non-intuitive"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: benchmarking determines actual and relative capabilities of a technology-based process, product or service and identifies performance gaps by testing against the best performance achieved (within the entity, industry-wide or in another industry) using hard performance data and specified criteria. It may compare new technology against legacy systems, or one new technology against alternatives. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Benchmarking is an approach to determining the actual and relative capabilities of a technology-based process"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: in collaborative analytics, data is not moved to a central location to be analysed with other data assets; instead analytical tools come to the data, making it easier to keep data secure and control who accesses what data for what purposes. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the analytical tools come to the data, not the other way around"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: cybersecurity, a broader term than data security, is the comprehensive process of protecting data and the systems for moving, storing and authenticating that data. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cybersecurity, a broader term than data security"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: the Financial Stability Board (FSB, 2017) defines big data as the enormous volume of data produced by the growing use of digital tools and information systems, with examples including financial transaction data, social media data and machine data such as Internet of Things, computer and mobile phone data. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Big data: The Financial Stability Board defines big data as"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: data pooling is a process where digital data from different sources are combined into a fuller, more useful data set for analysis (including by multiple parties), with pools organised in a centralised manner. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data pooling refers to a process where digital data from different sources are combined"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: data security is the process of protecting data from unauthorised access and corruption throughout its lifecycle, including encryption, hashing, tokenisation and key management across applications and platforms; it is narrower than cybersecurity. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data security refers to the process of protecting data from unauthorised access and data corruption"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: data standardisation converts data to a uniform format so users can process and analyse it; it is essential to big data processing, advanced analytics and other innovative digital tools, for example converting financial data that varies within and across entities into a common form. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data standardisation is the process of converting data to a uniform format"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: digital ID systems/solutions carry out identifying/verifying a natural or legal person's identity, bind the proofed identity to a digital credential, and use the credential(s) and potentially other authentication factors to confirm that a person claiming the identity is the proofed person. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "binding the proofed identity to a digital credential"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT, a.k.a. blockchain) is a technology protocol enabling simultaneous access, validation and updating of an immutable ledger distributed across multiple computers (typically multiple entities or locations), creating a distributed digital database. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "simultaneous access, validation, and updating of an immutable ledger (digital record)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: Deep Learning (DL) is an advanced type of machine learning where artificial neural networks with numerous (deep) layers learn from large amounts of data in highly autonomous ways, repeatedly tweaking a task to improve outcomes without human intervention. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DL is an advanced type of machine learning in which artificial neural networks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: digitalisation is the use of digital technologies and digitised data to change a business model, how work gets done and how customers and companies interact, and to provide new revenue and value-producing opportunities; digitisation is the conversion of analogue data, text, pictures, sound or other representations into digital (binary) form that computers can process. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Digitisation is the conversion of data, information, text, pictures, sound or other representations"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: dynamic data is a continuous real-time stream of data points in constant flux so the data set constantly changes over time, as distinct from static or persistent data mostly unaffected by time. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Dynamic data refers to a continuous real-time digital stream of data points"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: explainability means technology-based processes, solutions or systems can be explained, understood and accounted for; it gives adequate understanding of how solutions work and produce results, is a basic condition for trust and responsible use, and explainable AI provides transparency into the data, variables and decision points used to reach a result. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Explainability is a basic condition for trust and responsible use."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: FinTech refers broadly to the use of new and emerging digital technologies in the financial sector. Initially it meant technology-based innovations for new customer-facing products and services (e.g. mobile payments, online marketplace lending, algorithmic savings and investment tools, virtual currency payments, crowd funding, mobile banking); it now also covers automated mid- and back-office functions using algorithms, big data, AI, machine learning and link analytics, including regulatory compliance (RegTech). (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FinTech now also encompasses the use of new and emerging technologies to provide automated mid- and back-office enterprise functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: fuzzy logic is a subset of AI that processes an open, imprecise spectrum of input to produce output with a range of intermediate possibilities between YES and NO (e.g. certainly yes, possibly yes, cannot say, possibly no, certainly no). It produces definite output from incomplete, ambiguous, distorted or inaccurate input, simulating human decision making more closely than yes/no logic, and can be implemented in hardware, software or both. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fuzzy logic is a subset of AI that takes an open, imprecise spectrum of data"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: the Internet of Things (IoT) is the global network of Internet-enabled devices and machines that can collect, send, share and act on data using embedded sensors, processors and communication hardware without human interaction, generating enormous amounts of real-time data. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The global network of all Internet-enabled devices and machines"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: interoperability is the ability of different IT systems and software applications to communicate, exchange data and use the information seamlessly in real time, enabling all participants to operate across all systems. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to the ability of different information technology systems and software applications to communicate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: machine learning is a subset of AI that trains computer systems to learn from data, identify patterns and make decisions with minimal human intervention; it is a method of data analysis that automates analytical model building through experience and evolving pattern recognition algorithms. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is a method of data analysis that automates analytical model building"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: machine readable regulation replaces rules written in natural legal language with computer code to enable use of artificial intelligence for regulatory reporting purposes; natural language processing (NLP) is a branch of AI enabling computers to understand, interpret and manipulate human language, allowing humans to talk to machines. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Machine readable regulation replaces rules written in natural legal language with computer code"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: Privacy Enhancing Technologies are described (Maxwell, 2020) as specialist cryptographic capabilities allowing computations on underlying data without the data owner necessarily divulging it, and keeping search queries and results encrypted or undisclosed so only the requester sees them. The term encompasses encryption-based technologies useful primarily for protecting privacy as data is used. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Specialist cryptographical capabilities, which allow computations to take place on underlying data"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: real-time analytics is a machine learning process in which a system processes and analyses instantaneously loaded data and almost immediately generates meaningful output; real-time data (RTD) is information delivered immediately after collection, enabling real-time analytics, and can be dynamic or static. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Real-time analytics is a machine learning process in which a system processes and analyses data"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: Regulatory Technology (RegTech) is a sub-set of FinTech that uses new technologies to comply with regulatory requirements more efficiently and effectively than existing capabilities; responsible innovation is innovation that is fit for purpose and complies with applicable regulatory requirements, including AML/CFT, consumer protection, cybersecurity and privacy protections. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "RegTech is a sub-set of FinTech that uses new technologies to comply with regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: responsible innovation is defined as innovation that is fit for purpose and complies with applicable regulatory requirements, including AML/CFT, consumer protection, cybersecurity and privacy protections. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Innovation is responsible when it is fit for purpose and complies with applicable regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: smart machines are computer hardware and software systems using AI algorithms to make decisions, often with real-time data; unlike passive machines with predetermined responses, they use sensors, digital data and remote inputs, combine and analyse sources instantly and act on the insights. Static data is a fixed data set that remains the same after collection. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Smart machines are designed to make decisions, often using realtime data."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: supervised learning is a machine learning process teaching algorithms predictive models by feeding input data with known outcomes (labelled data), using the training set to adjust the model to minimise error. It uses a validation data set to measure learning progress and a test data set to evaluate performance on never-before-seen data, to determine whether the model learned effectively and can generalise. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervised learning uses a validation data set to measure the algorithm’s progress in learning the model"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: Supervisory technology (SupTech) is the use of innovative technology by supervisory authorities to support supervision and examination. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SupTech is the use of innovative technology by supervisory authorities to support supervision and examination."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: unsupervised learning is a machine learning process that lets algorithms analyse and cluster unlabelled datasets to discover hidden patterns, groupings or anomalies without human intervention, determining correlations without an answer key; its modelling becomes more accurate and refined as data exposure grows. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "analyse and cluster _unlabelled_ datasets to discover hidden patterns"</sub>

## Annex B: Suggested Actions to Support the Use of Technology in AML/CFT

- **descriptive / context**: Annex B states that responsible use of new technologies, including digital identity and cutting-edge transaction monitoring and analysis (including collaborative analytics), can assist effective, risk-based implementation of the FATF Standards by public and private sectors and promote financial inclusion. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can assist effective, risk-based implementation of the FATF Standards by the public and private sectors"</sub>
- **mandatory / expectation**: Annex B states that new technologies for AML/CFT must be developed and implemented in a way which reflects threats as well as opportunities, ensuring their use is compatible with international standards of data protection and privacy, and cybersecurity; the principles advance the 2017 San Jose Principle to pursue positive and responsible innovation. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "New technologies for AML/CFT must be developed and implemented in a way which reflects threats as well as opportunities"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 1 is to create an enabling environment, by both government and the private sector, for responsible innovation that enhances AML/CFT effectiveness, covering (i) innovative solutions that facilitate implementation of AML/CFT measures (including risk assessments and CDD) and strengthen their supervision and examination; (ii) good practices for updating internal legacy systems or replacing them with new technologies; and (iii) appropriate safeguards and features for new AML/CFT solutions, including explainability and transparency of processes and outcomes, human oversight, respect for privacy and data protection, strong cybersecurity, and alignment with global, national and technical standards and best practices. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Create an enabling environment by both government and the private sector for responsible innovation"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 2 (privacy and data protection) is to ensure there is a valid legal basis for processing personal data when deploying new technologies; protect personal information in line with national and international legal frameworks; process data for explicit, specified and legitimate purposes consistent with national and international rules; and support responsible development and adoption of innovative privacy-preserving technologies to enable robust AML/CFT information sharing and analysis while preserving privacy. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ensure there is a valid legal basis for the processing of personal data when deploying new technologies."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 3 is to promote AML/CFT innovation that supports financial inclusion by design: mitigate obstacles to financial inclusion through developing and implementing innovative solutions, and ensure responsible innovation consistent with the FATF objective of promoting financial inclusion. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Promote AML/CFT innovation which supports financial inclusion by design"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 4 is to develop and communicate innovation policies and regulatory approaches that are flexible, technology-neutral, outcomes-based and in line with the risk-based approach, including (i) considering the impact of new technologies holistically, with accompanying structural and organisational changes, possible unintended consequences, and overall impact on AML/CFT effectiveness and financial inclusion; (ii) issuing and/or updating clear policy statements, guidance, use cases, best practices or regulations as necessary to inform and encourage responsible use of new technologies for AML/CFT; and (iii) consulting counterparts and regulated entities to inform policy and decision-making. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "flexible, technology-neutral, outcomes-based and in line with the risk-based approach"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 5 (informed oversight) starts with building expertise in new technologies to enable informed regulation and supervision of their use, including for specific AML/CFT compliance purposes. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Build expertise in new technologies, to enable informed regulation and supervision of their use"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 5 (informed oversight) also covers identifying explicit, well-defined uses of new technologies for AML/CFT supervision and examination; understanding the risks and benefits of new technologies and appropriate risk-mitigation measures that preserve their benefits; and using technology to enhance AML/CFT supervision. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identify explicit, well-defined uses of new technologies for AML/CFT supervision and examination"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Annex B suggested action 6 (promote and facilitate cooperation) covers co-operating and co-ordinating with all relevant authorities, including data protection and privacy authorities, for a comprehensive approach to the risks and benefits of new technologies for AML/CFT; considering developing collaborative environments for cross-government and/or public-private research and development of new technologies and innovative solutions; and participating in international efforts to develop global principles for use of new technologies for AML/CFT, aligned with human rights, improved global AML/CFT implementation, cybersecurity, data privacy and protection, and relevant technical standards and trust frameworks. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consider developing collaborative environments to facilitate cross-government and/or public private research and development"</sub>
