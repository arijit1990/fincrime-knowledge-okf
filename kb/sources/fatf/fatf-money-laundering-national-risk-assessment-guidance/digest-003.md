  <sub>anchor: "INR.1 clarifies that countries should take steps to identify and assess their ML risks"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "a country should design a process considering its capacity, risk and context"</sub>

## Considering the Country Context

- **descriptive / method**: It is useful to begin by considering on a relative basis whether the country is generally a lower or higher ML risk environment. (p. 32)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "a brief scan of the data that has been collected, context on the crime environment in the country"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "The purpose in undertaking such an initial exercise is to later test the assumptions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The country's ML risk level should be reflected in the risk ratings given to threats and vulnerabilities, and aggregate ratings of a sector's constituents should be coherent with the sector's vulnerability rating. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ML risk level of the country should be reflected in the risk ratings attributed to the threats and vulnerabilities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: If internal inconsistencies arise across the risk assessment process, countries should re-examine their process and conclusions to identify and resolve them. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should re-examine their process and conclusions to identify and resolve the inconsistencies"</sub>

## Identifying Threats and Vulnerabilities

- **expected / method**: To complete the environmental scan, the country should develop an initial list of the threats and vulnerabilities it faces. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the country should develop an initial list of threats and vulnerabilities the country faces"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Good practice (i): regular discussions among stakeholders, for example designated conferences and workshops, standing working groups or regular intra- and inter-agency meetings on threat topics, to share knowledge on developments in the ML and TF landscape. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these discussions offer a platform for sharing knowledge, expertise, and perspectives on the latest developments"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Good practice (ii): engaging academia and industry experts, whose research, opinions and studies can deepen understanding of the factors behind the evolution of ML and of innovative approaches to counter it. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Academic research, expert opinions, and studies can provide a deeper understanding of the underlying factors"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Good practice (iii): referring to LEA and FIU observations, whose operational experience and investigations show emerging crime trends, typologies and modus operandi; regional reports such as those of Europol give a broader regional view. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "operational experiences and investigations can offer real-world perspectives on emerging crime trends, typologies, and modus operandi"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Good practice (iv): leveraging private sector input from FIs, DNFBPs, VASPs, industry associations and other relevant organisations on emerging risks and vulnerabilities from a business perspective. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this includes FIs, DNFBPs, VASPs, industry associations, and other relevant organisations"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Good practice (v): monitoring international developments, trends and typologies, including work of the FATF and Global Network and reliable information on conflicts, macro-economic trends and geopolitical dynamics, to frame internally derived information, particularly on predicate threats and threat actors. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Reliable sources of information on ongoing conflicts, macro-economic trends, geopolitical dynamics"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Those identifying threats and vulnerabilities must keep an open mind so that all relevant factors are identified and key issues contributing to the country's ML risk are not inadvertently overlooked. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Those involved in identifying threats and vulnerabilities must keep an open mind"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: There should be no off-limit topics in the NRA brainstorm. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There should be no off-limit topics in the NRA brainstorm."</sub>
- **expected / method**: In the environmental scan, the country's initial list of threats and vulnerabilities should attempt to be comprehensive, and the identification process should also be dynamic, so that new or previously undetected risks can be considered at any stage of the NRA analytical process. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should also be dynamic in that new or previously undetected risks identified may also be considered"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Countries may use more formal techniques such as surveys and quasi-statistical analysis of past events, or a brainstorming exercise among experts producing a list or tree diagram of related events or circumstances. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "more formal techniques such as surveys and quasi-statistical analysis of past events or circumstances"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An item on the initial list is not automatically classified as higher or lower risk; it has simply been identified as relevant enough to be analysed. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "something identified on the list at this stage is not automatically classified as having higher (or lower) risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The environmental scan should result in a listing of threats and vulnerabilities the country faces, after which the analytical process can proceed. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be a listing of threats and vulnerabilities that the country faces"</sub>

## Analytical Process

- **descriptive / definition**: The analytical process is the critical part of the NRA, moving from describing ML/TF risks to a complete understanding of the nature, extent and possible impact of the identified threats and vulnerabilities. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The analytical process is the critical part of the NRA."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The goal is to analyse the identified threats and vulnerabilities separately to understand their nature, sources, likelihood and consequences, then assign each a relative value or importance, for example a 1-10 scale based on criticality. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "analyse the identified threats and vulnerabilities separately in order to understand their nature, sources, likelihood and consequences"</sub>

## Consequence, Likelihood and Extent

- **descriptive / limitation**: A country that does not consider the likelihood, extent and consequences of the activity will have a diminished ability to reach conclusions about the relative importance, severity and urgency of each factor; these are key assessment factors. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not consider the likelihood and extent of the activity and the consequences it will have diminished ability"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: There is no one-size-fits-all method for assessing the likelihood, extent and consequence of ML. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no one-size-fits-all method for assessing the likelihood, extent and consequence of ML."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Analytical techniques can combine the nature and extent of each threat's or vulnerability's consequences with the likelihood that the risk materialises to determine a level of risk, often presented in a matrix. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "combining those results to determine a level of risk, which is often presented through the use of a matrix"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Countries may use formal techniques such as expert surveys or statistical analysis of the frequency of past ML-related activity, or may rely on conclusions of a group discussion or workshop, to develop consequence and likelihood information. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "surveys of experts or statistical analysis of the frequency of past ML risk related activity"</sub>

## Analytical Process

- **expected / method**: In the NRA analytical process, the analysis of threats and of vulnerabilities should give regard to the likelihood, extent and consequence of the money laundering that those threats and vulnerabilities would produce. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should give regard to the likelihood, extent and consequence of money laundering"</sub>

## Consequence, Likelihood and Extent

- **recommended / recommendation**: When assessing and assigning ratings for both threats and vulnerabilities, countries should consider analysing the likelihood and extent to which ML will occur and the consequence it will produce. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider analysing the likelihood and extent to which ML will occur, and the consequence"</sub>
