- **descriptive / context**: Increasing the granularity and detail of risk assessments can improve risk understanding and support financial inclusion. ## Box 26. Challenges and Solutions in Identifying Lower Risk Areas

- **descriptive / case example**: New Zealand has faced challenges in defining and managing lower risk areas, particularly where ML activity is infrequent; lack of evidence does not necessarily equate to low risk. It emphasises robust risk assessment methodologies and clear guidance that differentiate between "real," "possible," and "theoretical" risks. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the lack of evidence does not necessarily equate to low risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: New Zealand recognises the risk of confirmation bias and advocates a broader view in the identification phase, drawing on sources such as case data, intercept transcripts and interviews with criminals, and on intelligence from diverse sources including overseas, to understand unknown threats. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "New Zealand recognises the risk of confirmation bias and advocates for a broader view in the identification phase"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: New Zealand cites adherence to best practices such as ISO 31000 as strengthening its risk assessment methodologies, and integrates domestic and international insights to manage ML risks even in areas considered low risk due to limited domestic data. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Adherence to best practices, like ISO 31000, strengthens New Zealand's risk assessment methodologies."</sub>

## Box 27. Financial Inclusion

- **descriptive / case example**: India's Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) allows simplified CDD in opening "small accounts" as defined in its rules, and other accounts for low-risk client categories. The rules do not permit simplified measures where there is suspicion of ML/TF, where specific higher-risk scenarios apply, or where the risk identified is not consistent with the national risk assessment. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The rules do not permit simplified measures where there is a suspicion of ML/TF"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Because significant transactions in India's informal economy happen in cash, and based on its assessment of cash risks, India introduced policies known as Jan Dhan, Aadhaar and Mobile to encourage use of the formal financial system, expanding affordable access to bank accounts and financial services using a biometric identification system and supporting a digital mobile payment system. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "India introduced policies (known as Jan Dhan, Aadhaar and Mobile) to encourage use of the formal financial system"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: India's prioritisation of digital payment infrastructure led to digital transaction volumes rising from 20.7 billion transactions in 2017-18 to 134.6 billion in 2022-23, and access to financial services rose from 35% of the total population in 2011 to 80% in 2017. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from 20.7 billion transactions in 2017-18 to 134.6 billion in 2022-23"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the Philippines' second NRA, financial inclusion products were rated lower risk due to small amounts of funds and low threshold amounts, limited product functionalities and a small target market (the underserved local population). The analysis mainly used data from Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas-supervised financial institutions, collected systematically on an ongoing basis, and informs the third NRA in process. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial inclusion products were rated as lower risk, due to the small amounts of funds involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The second NRA's results supported the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' financial inclusion strategy, and the level of formalisation of the Philippine economy has improved from medium-low to medium since the second NRA, owing to government financial inclusion initiatives targeting the unbanked and underserved. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The level of formalisation of the economy has improved to medium from medium-low since the second NRA"</sub>

## Outreach and Stakeholder Engagement

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should proactively communicate the findings of the NRA, including any updates, during the interim periods between formal publications of the NRA. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should proactively communicate the findings of the NRA, including any updates thereof"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Continuous principle: countries should adopt a continuous approach to the communication plan and outreach of NRA findings so that target audiences' needs and expectations are considered throughout. Continuous communication enhances stakeholders' risk awareness, develops accountability for implementing risk-mitigating measures, and helps governments obtain up-to-date information on emerging risks. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should adopt a continuous approach to the communication plan and outreach of the NRA findings to stakeholders"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Transparent principle: countries can share the NRA publicly to support decision making, public participation, stakeholder engagement and private sector/civil society feedback. Sharing findings alone is not sufficient as it lacks context, so sharing some insights from the methodology helps supervisors and the private sector understand risks and conduct their own risk assessments. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sharing the findings of the NRA alone is not sufficient as it lacks enough context to be useful to stakeholders"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Some aspects of the NRA (for example confidential intelligence sources, national security concerns and the potential economic impact of risks materialising) may be withheld from public release. Countries are recommended to disseminate restricted information through secure platforms such as GoAML or via supervisors to their respective sectors. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries are recommended to disseminate restricted information through secure platforms such as GoAML"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Under the continuous approach to NRA communication, countries can use communication of the NRA findings to promote information sharing among stakeholders, for instance through workshops and forums for sharing best practices and challenges. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries can also use the communication of the NRA findings to promote information sharing among stakeholders"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Typologies and red-flag indicators, as other publications, should also be used to communicate risk updates and emerging or new risks more agilely. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Other publications, such as typologies and red-flag indicators should also be used for more agile communication of risk updates"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Under the differentiated approach, a distinction may be made between public and nonpublic communication, possibly using different media platforms for different stakeholders. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A distinction may be made between public and nonpublic communication."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some countries issue several NRA versions (a restricted one for government agencies, a public one, and sector-specific ones); the private sector has described this as good practice for understanding the findings while protecting sensitive information. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some countries publish different versions of NRAs, such as a restricted version for government"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Outreach intended to ensure understanding of risk should be similarly differentiated, concentrating on the details relevant to each stakeholder or sector. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Outreach to ensure understanding of risk should be similarly differentiated to focus on the details relevant to each stakeholder/sector."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The British Virgin Islands produced a sanitised public version of its NRA with sensitive law enforcement information removed, while a full version is made available to relevant competent authorities. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the British Virgin Islands produced a sanitised version of its NRA with sensitive law enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Inclusion in process: it is vital to include stakeholders, including the private sector, in the early discussions, planning and drafting phases of the NRA and to ensure planned outreach corresponds to their needs. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is vital to include stakeholders, including the private sector, in the early discussions, planning and drafting phases"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Publication of the NRA: countries frequently produced a public version and an adapted version for reporting entities targeted to their needs. Publicising methods cited include social media, information videos, websites of competent authorities and mailing lists; countries could consider translating the NRA where there is more than one official language and to make it accessible to other countries. Updates and refinements in red-flag indicators based on the NRA can be very useful for reporting entities. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries could consider translating the NRA to enhance understanding in countries with more than one official language"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Linking the NRA to supervisory activity: some countries suggested incorporating the NRA into on-site inspections to encourage up-to-date risk understanding among reporting entities and to ensure NRA findings are incorporated into industry-level risk assessments; the NRA lead agency can hold regular meetings with reporting entities to raise awareness and receive feedback. It is important to emphasise the practical implications of the NRA findings for the RBA that supervisors and the private sector must apply. (p. 61)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "some countries introduced further initiatives to support risk understanding among the private sector"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Countries may consider a phased approach to outreach, targeting the higher-risk stakeholders first"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Communication and outreach to higher-risk sectors may be more detailed and bespoke and held in person, with more discussion, consultation and follow-up, to secure comprehensive risk understanding. (p. 61)[^original]

## Annex A. Types of Risk Assessments that Complement the NRA

- **descriptive / context**: Sectoral and thematic risk assessments allow countries to focus on emerging sectors or areas of increasing risk without waiting for or carrying out an NRA update where impractical or inefficient, and to develop more detailed understanding of threats, vulnerabilities and existing mitigating measures for a specific area. Their results complement the NRA, can coordinate authorities' actions and inform the private sector, and may be considered in subsequent NRA updates. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sectoral and thematic risk assessments allow countries to focus on emerging sectors or areas of increasing risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Advantage of sectoral/thematic assessments - deep dive: narrowing scope to a specific sector allows development of typologies on how the sector may be abused for ML and by whom, and deeper understanding of industry-specific vulnerabilities and mitigating measures; it can be effective for new/emerging sectors or sectors where risks have changed due to emerging threats or legislative changes. The NRA methodology may be adapted to the sectoral/thematic assessment. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Narrowing the scope of a risk assessment to a specificsector allows for the development of typologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Australia developed a risk assessment on remittance corridors between Australia and the Pacific Islands, to provide more information on ML/TF risks of remittances sent through remittance providers from Australia to Pacific Island countries and to help remittance providers and banks identify lower-risk transaction types and scenarios (AUSTRAC 2017). (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Australia developed a risk assessment on remittance corridorsbetween Australia and the Pacific Islands"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Advantage - agility: thematic and sectoral risk assessments can be more flexible and adaptable than broad national-level assessments, allowing quicker responses to emerging threats, regulatory changes or shifts in industry practices. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Thematic and sectoral risk assessments can be more flexibleand adaptable than broad national-level assessments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Advantage - targeted risk mitigation measures: sectoral risk assessments facilitate development of targeted risk mitigation measures and regulatory interventions tailored to the specific needs and characteristics of the industry. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sectoral risk assessments facilitatethe development of targeted risk mitigation measures and regulatoryinterventions"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Shortcoming - limited scope: isolated sectoral risk assessments may have a narrow focus and ignore cross-cutting issues affecting multiple sectors (e.g., use of cash); a thematic assessment, though broader, may lack analysis of broader systemic issues and interconnections between typologies and sectors, which may lead to gaps in risk identification and mitigation. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Isolated Sectoral risk assessments may havea narrow focus and ignore cross-cutting issues"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Shortcoming - data challenges: countries may find it difficult to assess certain sectors or themes (e.g., emerging sectors, unsupervised sectors or those recently brought under regulation); they may rely on academic reports and open-source intelligence, which may limit the accuracy of findings and conclusions. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may rely on academic reports and open-sourceintelligence to develop the risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Shortcoming - fragmented approach: addressing the risks of only one sector or theme may lead a country to address its risks in isolation without considering broader consequences across the AML system. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Addressing the risks of only onesector or theme may lead to a country attempting to addressitsrisksinisolation"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Shortcoming - lacks holistic view: these approaches may not consider factors such as macroeconomic trends, geopolitical events or legislative or regulatory changes that may affect multiple sectors or themes, and without input from other stakeholders such as LEAs and prosecution, supervisors cannot have an accurate picture of the national crime context and environment. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These approaches may not be able toconsider factors such as macroeconomic trends, geopoliticalevents"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Listed as an advantage of sectoral and thematic risk assessments: industry collaboration, since the approach encourages collaboration and information sharing among governmental authorities, industry participants, supervisory authorities, specialised experts and other stakeholders. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "encourages collaboration andinformation sharing practices among governmental authorities,industry participants,"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Denmark has developed several thematic risk assessments, for example on trade-based ML (because of high risks and a lack of understanding of the associated ML risks) and on use of cash in the criminal economy (because new legislation was introduced to combat it and the associated vulnerabilities had changed). (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Denmark has developed several thematic risk assessments on areas such as trade-based ML"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The listed potential shortcomings of sectoral and thematic risk assessments are relevant particularly when these assessments are done in isolation. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These potential shortcomings are relevant particularly when sectoral or thematic risk assessments are done in isolation."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Sectoral risk assessments may inform the NRA, but NRA findings may also reveal a lack of understanding in certain sectors that the country could then prioritise for a sectoral risk assessment after adopting the NRA. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sectoral risk assessments may inform the NRA, but the findings of the NRA may also show a lack of understanding"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Thematic risk assessments focus on specific themes or cross-cutting issues that create ML risks. Countries may choose an established risk (e.g., use of cash or digital payments affecting many sectors) or an emerging threat (e.g., cyber enabled fraud) not yet explored in detail but with potential to become significant. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Thematic risk assessments focus on specific themes or cross-cutting issues that create ML risks."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Governments should prioritise themes to explore based on the threat they pose to the country. A thematic risk assessment should aim to understand the risks of the chosen area by analysing vulnerabilities, modus operandi, typologies and potential likelihood and consequences of abuse for ML, including exploitable gaps; thematic assessments generally have a broader reach than sectoral ones because themes can affect multiple sectors or industries. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Governments should prioritise themes to explore based on the threat they pose to the country."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In the industry collaboration advantage entry, countries should aim to foster a culture of continuous information sharing and cooperation between stakeholders. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "other stakeholders. Countries should aim to foster a culture of"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Listed as an advantage of sectoral and thematic risk assessments: reviewing existing measures, as both approaches let a country explore the impact of existing legislation, regulations, supervisory activity and other risk mitigation measures on the sector or theme. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Reviewing existing measures – Both approaches allow country to explore the impact of existing legislation"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A sectoral risk assessment aims to give a targeted analysis of ML risks in a particular sector, sub-sector or industry, covering its distinctive characteristics, vulnerabilities and typologies, including all its activities, services and products, customer profiles, business models, how financial transactions take place, and established and emerging usage trends. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "targeted analysis of money laundering risks within a specific sector, sub-sector, or industry"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A sectoral risk assessment should also examine the sector's exposure to cross-border threats, which can differ with the size of the sector, the location of the country and whether clients are citizens of the country. (p. 67)[^original]
