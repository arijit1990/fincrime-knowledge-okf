- **descriptive / context**: The FATF NRA guidance says understanding and anticipating new and evolving financial-crime risks can be challenging and calls for ongoing monitoring, collaboration and diverse intelligence sources. ## Box 17. Horizon Scanning

- **descriptive / case example**: Australia uses strategic intelligence methodologies and techniques employed by its law enforcement and intelligence agencies to assess the trajectory of predicate crimes and sectors/channels. The near-term (three years) trajectory of each predicate crime and sector/channel was assessed as increasing, emerging, stable, or decreasing. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The near term (three years) trajectory of each predicate crime and sector/channel was assessed"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Australia's 2024 ML NRA analysed scams, noting that criminals leverage the growing number of customers interacting through digital channels to reach more victims more efficiently, for example by impersonating phone numbers, email addresses or websites of legitimate organisations, or creating fake adverts, social media profiles and reviews. Australia concluded this threat is likely to increase over the next three years and will become increasingly challenging to disrupt given criminal use of technology and artificial intelligence. ## Section 3: Post-NRA Actions

- **expected / requirement**: FATF INR.1 states that countries should take appropriate steps to identify and assess ML/TF risks for the country on an ongoing basis in order to: (i) inform potential changes to the AML/CFT regime, including laws, regulations and other measures; (ii) assist in the allocation and prioritisation of AML/CFT resources by competent authorities proportionate to ML/TF risks; and (iii) make information available for AML/CFT risk assessments conducted by financial institutions and DNFBPs. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should take appropriate steps to identify and assess the money laundering and terrorist financing risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The purpose of developing a granular understanding of national ML risks is to tailor national AML strategies to that risk. Tailoring means areas of higher risk should be subject to enhanced mitigation measures, but this alone is an incomplete implementation of the risk-based approach; lower risk areas should also be subjected to simplified or lesser measures. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Lower risk areas should also be subjected to simplified or lesser measures."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An important goal of the NRA is that all relevant stakeholders fully understand the country's ML risks and take effective mitigation measures. Dissemination and communication of NRA results is an important milestone, but the NRA process should be seen as continuous and the NRA document as a snapshot of current ML risks based on an appropriate method of evaluation for the country's context. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is essential that the NRA process is seen as continuous"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The risk environment faced by a country is dynamic, and mitigation measures can displace ML to other parts of an economy or drive threats to act in different ways. It is important that countries feed back on what went well in the NRA process and take steps to improve future iterations. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mitigation measures can displace ML to other parts of an economy or drive threats to act in different ways."</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: The guidance identifies three parallel activities countries should undertake after finalising their ML NRA: (i) align AML strategies and policies with risk; (ii) outreach and stakeholder engagement; and (iii) feedback and improving the NRA process. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "three parallel activities that countries should undertake after the finalisation of their ML NRA"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.1 further says countries should keep their risk assessments current and should have mechanisms for giving suitable information on the results to all relevant competent authorities and SRBs, financial institutions and DNFBPs. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should keep the assessments up-to-date and should have mechanisms to provide appropriate information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: R.2 says countries should identify, assess and understand their own ML and TF risks, and should act on them. Such action includes designating an authority or mechanism to coordinate risk assessment and applying resources so that the risks are effectively mitigated. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should identify, assess, and understand the money laundering and terrorist financing risks for the country"</sub>

## Align AML Strategies and Policies with Risk

- **expected / expectation**: The NRA process and its outcomes should be useful in informing the country's AML regime, and an NRA should not be a bureaucratic exercise without outcomes. It should serve as a foundation for decision makers to align national strategies and policies with the risk environment, including (per INR.1) informing changes to the AML/CFT regime and prioritising AML/CFT resources, and (as required in R.2) national AML/CFT/CPF policies informed by the identified risks. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The conduct of an NRA should not be a bureaucratic process without outcomes."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should not see the NRA as a tick-box exercise. It should be considered a tool to develop understanding of ML risks and have a positive impact on the AML system at a strategic and operational level; case studies show a well-positioned and effectively utilised NRA can bring significant benefits. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should not see the NRA as a tick-box exercise"</sub>

## Box 18. Integration of NRA Findings into AML Strategy

- **descriptive / case example**: Luxembourg's National AML/CFT Strategy is based on the NRA results and its periodic updates. Its definition and implementation are ensured by the Interministerial Steering Committee and the Committee for the Prevention of ML/FT (Prevention Committee), both supported by a permanent Executive Secretariat. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The National AML/CFT Strategy is based on the results of the NRA and its periodic updates."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Luxembourg's Steering Committee comprises the Ministry of Justice, the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of Interior Security and the Ministry of Foreign and European Affairs. It draws up the multiyear National AML/CFT Strategy for adoption by the Government Council, which sets main priorities and high-level strategic objectives refined by operational objectives, and it reports to the Government on implementation progress. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Steering Committee draws up the multiyear National AML/CFT Strategy, presented to the Government Council for adoption."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Luxembourg's Prevention Committee coordinates implementation of the strategy through operational members (Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Finance, supervisory authorities and SRBs, the FIU, prosecution and investigation authorities, professional associations) and has five working groups: Risk and statistics (responsible for conducting the NRA process), Supervision, Preventive measures, Transparency of legal persons and legal arrangements, and Detection, investigation and prosecution. (p. 49)[^original]

## Development of an Action Plan

## Amending or Developing Legislation and Regulation

- **descriptive / context**: The NRA will often show gaps or weaknesses in the AML framework that criminals could exploit, and a common outcome is changes to the legislative or regulatory framework. For example, the scope of DNFBPs can be expanded, as necessary, based on the risk assessment results. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the scope of the DNFBPs can be expanded, as necessary, based on the results of the risk assessment"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Law-making in response to the NRA takes time and involves multiple stakeholders (policy makers, legal experts, government agencies) so that laws are comprehensive, effective and aligned with international standards and good practices. Early and ongoing consultation with non-governmental stakeholders such as the private sector and civil society is recommended to address concerns early and avoid unintended consequences. ## Box 20. Risk Mitigating Measures in Response to NRA Findings

- **descriptive / case example**: As a result of its NRA, France introduced measures to limit anonymity in sensitive sectors such as funds transfers and money exchange, and limited the use of cash and of transactions carried out using pre-paid cards to mitigate some of the most important typologies identified. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "France introduced several measures to limit anonymity in sensitive sectors such as funds transfers and money exchange."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: France extended AML/CFT obligations to certain financial and non-financial sectors beyond the scope of the FATF requirements to mitigate major risks, including supervision of the handling of funds channelled through self-regulated professions: judicial trustees and notaries operate in conjunction with the Deposit and Consignment Office (CDC) and lawyers with the management fund for lawyers' fees (CARPA) when handling funds. Including CARPAs in the AML/CFT regime in 2020 strengthened measures to identify and mitigate the most important risks for the profession. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "France also extended AML/CFT obligations to certain financial and non-financial sectors beyond the scope of the FATF requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Korea's 2018 NRA identified abuse of virtual assets (VA) as a high-risk vulnerability due to the anonymity permitted in transactions; in response Korea brought VASPs within the scope of the AML/CFT framework and imposed enhanced consumer protections and limitations on use of VA. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it brought VASPs within the scope of the AML/CFT framework"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Korea's NRA also noted abuse of cash as a high-risk vulnerability due to anonymity and a review of ML case studies showing cash is the most common ML instrument. To address this, Korea lowered the threshold for cash transaction reports and aimed to improve CTR reporting. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Korea lowered the threshold for cash transaction reports."</sub>

## Developing and/or Improving National and International Cooperation Mechanisms

- **descriptive / context**: The NRA process brings together national authorities across operational, policy and supervisory functions, often involves international cooperation with neighbouring countries, international organisations and IFIs, and involves non-governmental stakeholders. Maintaining these relationships builds trust, promotes a more holistic approach to risk, and allows more efficient follow-up and monitoring of risk mitigation measures and their efficacy. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some countries develop Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs) that continue after the NRA"</sub>

- **expected / recommendation**: Domestically, the NRA can offer platforms and fora for exchanging ideas and information. Such exchange should be encouraged continuously, because it removes silos between agencies, builds a shared understanding of ML risk and shows the NRA to be a continuous process. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which should be encouraged on an ongoing basis as it breaks down silos between different agencies"</sub>

## Expending Resources, Budget and Conducting Capacity Building

- **permissive / context**: The NRA could lead to the hiring of specialists, for example financial investigators, intelligence analysts and legal experts, to strengthen the country's capacity to detect, investigate and prosecute financial crime. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as financial investigators, intelligence analysts, and legal experts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Budget and resources may be reallocated to higher risk areas, for example more funding for certain LEAs and better investigations of the highest risk predicate offences, or for supervisors to enhance on-site inspection capacity in particular sectors. A risk-based approach involves directing resources away from areas assessed as lower risk towards higher-risk areas, and can help countries with less mature NRA processes manage resources more effectively. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A riskbased approach involves directing resources away from areas assessed in the NRA as lower risk"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Increasing staff numbers in high-risk areas can be a good first step, but countries should ensure all staff operating in the AML system have the capacity and knowledge to do their jobs effectively. The NRA can identify where training is lacking or outdated and inform tailored training programmes covering topics such as risk assessment methodologies, financial investigation techniques, regulatory compliance requirements and emerging trends in financial crime.   <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that all staff operating in the AML system have the capacity and knowledge"</sub>

## Risk-based Supervisory Actions

- **mandatory / requirement**: The FATF Standards require supervision to be proportionate to the risks arising from different FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs. NRA findings play a fundamental role in shaping prioritised supervisory actions, such as the allocation of resources to supervise sectors on a risk basis. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Standards require supervision to be proportionate to the risks arising from different FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF issued guidance on risk-based supervision in 2021 and provided a chapter on the role of supervisors in its 2025 Guidance on Anti-Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing Measures and Financial Inclusion (section 3.2.2), outlining how supervisors can prioritise activities in line with risk. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF issued guidance on risk-based supervision in 2021"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Risk understanding is a two-way process: the NRA relies on supervisors' understanding of sectoral risks, and supervisors update their understanding of threats to their sector from predicate crimes, typologies and ML/TF threats through involvement in the NRA process. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk understanding is a two-way process"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should consider whether the NRA's sector-level conclusions are coherent with their understanding of the aggregate entity-level risk ratings they have developed. This analysis should influence supervisory actions such as the type and intensity of licensing controls, the frequency and intensity of on-site and off-site supervision, guidance to covered sectors and other risk-mitigation strategies. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should consider whether the conclusions of the NRA on a sector-basis are coherent"</sub>

## Box 24. Risk-based Supervision

Sectors considered high risk (banks/credit union, securities sector, private trustees and corporate service providers) are prioritised, and all supervised entities are subject to onsite examinations and offsite monitoring. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sectors considered high risk (banks/credit union, securities sector, private trustees and corporate service providers) are prioritised"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Onsite Examinations are decided upon at the beginning of the year"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: The NRA offers good opportunities for coordinated regulatory response, particularly for high-risk sectors; for example, where real estate poses higher ML risk, supervisors of real estate agents, lawyers, financial institutions providing housing loans and mortgages, and TCSPs can coordinate their response. (p. 55)[^original]

## Investigations, Prosecutions and Asset Recovery

- **expected / expectation**: The NRA should shape the risk-based approach to investigation and prosecution of ML and associated asset recovery. LEAs should actively contribute to the NRA by providing data and qualitative insights and take ownership of its findings. Measures introduced should let LEAs focus investigations, prosecutions and asset recovery in line with identified risks; focusing on the highest risk predicate offences or types of ML can lead to more impactful ML cases. (p. 55)[^original]
