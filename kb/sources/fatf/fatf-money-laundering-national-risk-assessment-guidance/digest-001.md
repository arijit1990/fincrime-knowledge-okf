## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF guidance (updated August 2025) describes understanding of money laundering risk as a foundational element on which a national AML/CFT system is built. Recommendation 1 requires countries to identify, assess and understand their ML risks and to take proportionate action so that these risks are mitigated effectively. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money laundering risk understanding is a foundational element on which a national AML/CFT system is built."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Developing an accurate, up-to-date risk understanding is an ongoing, dynamic process that requires responding to changing environmental factors, assessing new information and scanning the horizon for emerging risks. A national risk assessment (NRA) is one common structured approach for a country to take. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The development of an accurate and up-to-date risk understanding is an ongoing, dynamic process for a country."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The guidance defines an NRA as a comprehensive process used by countries to identify, assess and understand the ML and/or TF risks they face, involving evaluation of threats and vulnerabilities, determination of the level of risk, and development of strategies aligned to ML risks, including enhanced measures where risks are higher and simplified or lesser measures where risks are lower. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An NRA is a comprehensive process used by countries to identify, assess, and understand the ML and/or TF risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The end result of the NRA process may or may not be a written product; the understandings reached and the follow-on actions taken are of paramount importance. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While the end result of the process may or may not be a written product"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The NRA provides an evidence-based foundation for informed policymaking, resource allocation proportionate to the risks, and effective risk-based AML/CFT measures, and helps align national strategies with the country's risk landscape, addressing domestic and international threats to the extent connected to the country. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The NRA provides an evidence-based foundation for informed policymaking, resource allocation proportionate to the risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The guidance assists countries in conducting an NRA focused on ML risks. It draws on the experience of countries across the FATF Global Network over the last decade, with more than 90 contributing, and is meant to demonstrate good practices that countries can tailor to their own national context. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "more than 90 contributed to this guidance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Section 1 of the guidance (NRA Preparation and Set-up) identifies prerequisites to a successful NRA: political commitment, an inclusive NRA mechanism, objective setting, and acquisition of information and data. These steps are not necessarily linear, but all should be considered and in place before the analytical work begins. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These steps are not necessarily linear steps, but all should be considered and in place before the analytical work"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Section 2 provides a linear, structured approach to analysing threats, vulnerabilities and risks. There is no set methodology that countries must follow and the process should have regard to the country's capacity, risk and context, but the series of steps is identified as a good practice. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no set methodology that countries must follow"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Section 3 (Post-NRA Actions) covers simultaneous practices a country should undertake after the analytical process, including measures to ensure ML mitigation is commensurate with identified risks, communication of NRA findings, and refining of the NRA process. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section identifies simultaneous practices that a country should undertake after the conclusion of the analytical process."</sub>

## Purpose, Scope and Objectives

- **descriptive / context**: Identifying, assessing and understanding ML, TF and PF risks is an essential part of implementing a national AML/CFT regime. NRA results should provide useful information to financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs to support their own risk assessments and mitigation measures. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The results of a national risk assessment (NRA) should provide useful information to financial institutions (FIs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Properly understanding ML/TF risks informs and supports application of proportionate AML/CFT measures (the risk-based approach central to the FATF Standards) and should assist countries in prioritising and efficiently allocating resources in proportion to the risks. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should assist countries in prioritisation and efficient allocation of resources proportionate to the risks"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF introduced Recommendation 1 in 2012, emphasising the need to identify, assess, understand and mitigate ML/TF risks as a fundamental basis for effective implementation of the FATF's 40 Recommendations. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF has underlined this importance by introducing Recommendation 1 in 2012"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF produced national ML and TF risk assessment guidance in 2013, updated its TF NRA guidance in 2019, and introduced PF NRA guidance in 2021. The present guidance updates the 2013 guidance but addresses only ML NRAs. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This present guidance document updates the 2013 guidance, but addresses only ML NRAs."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: An ML NRA is a comprehensive evaluation by a country to identify and assess its ML risks, analysing ML threats and vulnerabilities across factors such as sectors, activities, geographic locations, client types, products and channels, and developing evidence-based risk mitigation measures. It promotes a holistic approach and aims to build a baseline risk understanding among all stakeholders. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An ML NRA is a comprehensive evaluation conducted by a country to identify and assess its ML risks."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An NRA should encompass cross-border risks, and its impact could extend beyond the country's borders, supporting understanding and reduction of ML in both national and international contexts. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An NRA should encompass cross-border risks, and its impact could extend beyond the"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FATF's Report on the State of Effectiveness and Compliance with the FATF Standards (April 2022) showed that compliance with R.1 increased by 24% through the 4th round follow-up process, but improvements are still needed particularly in data collection, stakeholder training and awareness, and effective communication of NRA findings. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "although compliance with R.1 increased by 24% through the 4th round follow-up process"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The guidance draws on inputs from 90 countries across the FATF Global Network in two questionnaires, Mutual Evaluation Reports (MERs), published NRAs, studies from international bodies such as the World Bank Group and IMF, and a public consultation. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inputs received from 90 countries across the FATF Global Network in two questionnaires"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The guidance is not a FATF Standard and is not intended to designate specific actions necessary to meet obligations under Recommendation 1, INR.1 or other FATF Standards. Technical compliance and assessment criteria are in the FATF assessment methodology, and the practices described are examples that may facilitate implementation compatibly with the Standards. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This guidance document is not a FATF Standard"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The guidance is designed to be flexible for countries at different maturity levels, whether preparing a first NRA or operating an established mechanism, recognising that the level of detail and complexity will vary based on a country's risk and context. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recognising that the level of detail and complexity will vary based on a country"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Countries with less mature NRA processes often face obstacles such as limited resources, insufficient data and lack of experienced personnel, and are often at an early stage of establishing a culture of ongoing risk assessment; the guidance outlines strategies tailored to these challenges. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may encounter obstacles such as limited resources, insufficient data and lack of experienced personnel"</sub>

## Core FATF Obligations Related to ML NRAs

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 1 calls on countries to identify, assess and understand the ML risks they face, including designating an authority or mechanism to coordinate actions to assess risk, and to apply a risk-based approach so that measures to prevent and mitigate ML are proportionate to the risks identified. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "R.1 lays out several basic principles regarding ML risk."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.1 clarifies that countries should identify and assess their ML risks on an ongoing basis to (1) inform potential changes to the AML regime, including laws, regulations and other measures, and (2) assist competent authorities in allocating and prioritising AML resources in proportion to ML risks. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR.1 clarifies that countries should take steps to identify and assess their ML risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should have a mechanism to provide information on the results of risk assessments to all relevant competent authorities and self-regulatory bodies (SRBs), FIs and DNFBPs. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have a mechanism to provide information on the results of the risk assessment(s)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In the context of encouraging simplified measures, INR.1 requires countries to identify area(s) of lower risk, for example through national or subnational risk assessments, to support FIs and DNFBPs in applying measures proportionate to those risks. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to support FIs and DNFBPs to apply measures proportionate to those risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: A footnote clarifies that countries need not designate certain areas as lower risk in every assessment, but could highlight lower-risk areas where available so that regulated entities can consider simplified measures. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries need not designate certain areas as lower risk in every assessment"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 2 provides that countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies informed by the risks identified and regularly reviewed, as well as interagency frameworks for coordinating and exchanging information on the development and implementation of those policies. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies which are informed by the risks identified"</sub>

## Methodology

- **descriptive / context**: The guidance update was co-led by Mexico and Hong Kong, China, with input from a project team of 33 FATF and FATF-Style Regional Body (FSRB) members and observer organisations. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "co-led by Mexico and Hong Kong, China, with input from a project team of 33 FATF and FATF-Style Regional Bodies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: 59 delegations answered the first questionnaire and 70 delegations the second follow-up questionnaire, and the FATF Secretariat reviewed NRAs from 69 FATF and FSRB members and MERs from across the Global Network. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "59 delegations provided responses to the first questionnaire and 70 delegations to the second follow-up questionnaire"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Private sector input was sought through the FATF's Private Sector Consultative Forum in April 2024 and a public consultation in July 2024, which received 501 responses from financial institutions, DNFBPs, academics, NPOs and consultants. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The project team received 501 responses to the public consultation"</sub>

## Key Concepts Relevant to Assessing and Understanding ML Risks

- **descriptive / definition**: Risk occurs when a threat successfully takes advantage of a vulnerability to produce a consequence. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk occurs when a threat successfully takes advantage of a vulnerability to produce a consequence."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A threat is, in general, a person, group or activity with the potential to cause harm to the state, society or the economy. In the ML context it refers to criminal individuals, groups or entities and their facilitators seeking to conceal the illicit origins of funds through past, present and future ML activities, not the predicate offences themselves. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is a person, group or activity with the potential to cause harm to the state, society or the economy."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The threat assessment typically serves as an essential starting point in understanding ML risk, so understanding the environment in which predicate offences are committed and proceeds generated, and their nature, size and volume, is important. In some instances certain threat assessments may serve as a precursor for an ML risk assessment. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understanding the environment in which predicate offences are committed and proceeds of crime are generated"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A vulnerability can be exploited by the threat or may support or facilitate its activities. In the ML context, looking at vulnerabilities distinct from threats means focusing on features such as the inherent characteristics of a sector, financial product or service that make them attractive and feasible for ML, and on country characteristics such as a large financial, trade or company formation sector. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A vulnerability can be exploited by the threat or may support or facilitate its activities."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Vulnerabilities may also relate to weaknesses in law, regulation, supervision or enforcement; deficiencies in AML controls and non-compliance or partial compliance of national legislation with the FATF Recommendations affect a country's vulnerabilities. Countries may include control adequacy in the vulnerability assessment or focus on inherent vulnerabilities and assess controls as a standalone component of ML risk. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may include the adequacy or quality of AML controls in vulnerability assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A consequence is the impact or harm that ML may cause, including effects of the underlying criminal activity on financial systems and institutions, the economy and society. Consequences can be domestic and international, short or long term, and can harm populations, communities, the business environment and national or international interests, and can undermine the reputation and attractiveness of a country's financial sector. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A consequence refers to the impact or harm that ML may cause"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A ML risk assessment should cover threats (including predicate offences and characteristics of those committing predicate and ML offences) and vulnerabilities (including sectors, channels, geographic location, client type, products and services, and weaknesses of laws, regulations, supervision and enforcement) to reach conclusions about national risk. It should be a starting point to inform national strategies and policies, an action plan, and prioritisation of mitigating measures and resources. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a ML risk assessment should cover threats (including predicate offences"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A risk-based approach (RBA) means that countries, competent authorities, financial institutions and DNFBPs identify, assess and understand the ML risk they are exposed to and take appropriate and proportionate mitigation measures, with enhanced measures where risks are higher and simplified or lesser measures where risks are lower. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A risk-based approach (RBA) means that countries, competent authorities, financial institutions and DNFBPs identify, assess and understand the ML risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Risk mitigating measures are actions, controls or strategies implemented to manage identified ML risks, and can include legislative, regulatory, supervisory, law enforcement or other administrative actions within the national AML framework. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk mitigating measures are actions, controls or strategies implemented to manage the identified ML risks."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The guidance describes a national ML risk assessment as a process built on a methodology that all involved parties have agreed. It seeks to identify, analyse and understand ML risks and is the first step in addressing them. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A national ML risk assessment is a process based on a methodology, agreed by all parties involved"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The risk assessment should be a starting point for informing national strategies and policies, developing an action plan, and prioritising risk-mitigating measures and resource allocation to strengthen the AML system in line with the risks identified. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The risk assessment should be a starting point to inform the national strategies and policies"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: On consequences, the guidance says countries might focus on gaining a comprehensive understanding of their threats and vulnerabilities while considering potential consequences as part of the overall risk assessment. It says this approach allows effective prioritisation of mitigation efforts and helps strengthen the resilience of financial and economic systems against ML impacts. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries might focus on gaining a comprehensive understanding of their threats and vulnerabilities while considering the potential consequences"</sub>

## Section 1: NRA Preparation and Set-up

- **descriptive / context**: The Section 1 steps are not explicitly covered by the FATF Standards but are necessary to perform what the Standards require. They are not linear and should all ideally be in place before the NRA begins. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The steps covered in this section are not explicitly covered by the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The critical prerequisites are political commitment, an inclusive national mechanism to assess risk, appropriate scoping and objective setting, and collection of information and data. These remain prerequisites regardless of the country's capacity, risk and context, though countries are encouraged to calibrate the NRA to those factors. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries are encouraged to take an approach that is appropriate to their capacity, risk and context"</sub>

## Political Commitment to Conduct the NRA

- **descriptive / context**: Political commitment is crucial for a successful risk assessment. It shows the NRA is a national priority, for example through public endorsement by senior officials, and strong commitment is indicated when the NRA coordinator and/or working group are empowered and all experts are given appropriate autonomy and protection. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An indicator of strong political commitment can be seen when the NRA coordinator and/or working group are empowered"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Political support should help bring together subject matter experts from policy, law enforcement, the FIU, supervisory, regulatory, sanctions and other competent authorities, and encourage coordination, information sharing and collaboration among agencies. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such support should help bring the right subject matter experts across policy, law enforcement, Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: High-level political commitment ensures resources and budget are dedicated across all involved agencies, that NRA timelines are respected and that excessive staff turnover is avoided. It can include investment in technological solutions, such as data collection tools that improve the accuracy and reliability of NRA findings and reduce reliance on external sources, and can aid international cooperation on ML risk assessments. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "High-level political commitment ensures that resources and budget are dedicated to the process across all involved agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In Box 1, project questionnaire respondents linked NRA coordination challenges to lack of political commitment at the highest levels: lack of national coordination (35%), limitations in budget, resources or technical solutions (21%) and lack of commitment from those involved (9%). (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lack of national coordination (35%), limitations in budget, resources or technical solutions (21%) and lack of commitment"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A World Bank study found that for 6% of the countries analysed, lack of political commitment significantly impeded the quality of their NRA. It also found that 50% of countries' most significant challenges were tied to lack of political commitment, for example difficulties obtaining resources, coordination problems and the NRA working group lacking empowerment. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for 6% of the countries analysed, a lack of political commitment was a significant impediment to the quality"</sub>
