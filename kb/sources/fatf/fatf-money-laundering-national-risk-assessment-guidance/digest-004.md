## Predicate Crimes

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should first identify the most prevalent predicate crimes driving ML in their contexts, to understand threat severity and prioritise efforts. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should first identify the most prevalent predicate crimes driving ML activities within their contexts."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should evaluate the scale and impact of identified predicate offences, analysing prevalence using domestic criminal, intelligence and FIU data such as the number of predicate offences committed. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "domestic criminal, intelligence and FIU data such as: overall number of the predicate offences committed"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Prevalence figures (offence numbers, share of ML investigations, size of proceeds, STRs, prosecutions, convictions, seizures and confiscations) need cautious interpretation, since detected cases are only a portion of actual crime. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "detected cases represents only a portion of the actual volume of crimes"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Detected crime numbers may be skewed by reporting behaviour: crimes with direct victims (like robbery) are usually reported more, while others (like human trafficking) are under-reported. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Crimes with direct victims (like robbery) are usually reported more, while some other crimes are under-reported (like human trafficking)"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Alongside crime numbers it is important to consider the total and average proceeds of crime for each crime category. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important to consider the total and average proceeds of crimes for each crime category"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Theft cases will be far more numerous than other crimes in most countries, but this does not necessarily mean property theft is the most important ML threat. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this does not necessarily mean that property theft is the most important ML threat in the country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Beyond domestic criminal data, countries should rely on other sources of information and mechanisms to build a more holistic overview, subject to their context and profile. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should further rely on other sources of information and mechanisms to develop a more holistic overview"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Countries can establish a mechanism to identify prevalent predicate offences using quantitative data complemented by in-depth discussions or scoping exercises with LEAs, FIUs and sector supervisors and by consulting other stakeholders to validate and refine the understanding. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using quantitative data complemented by in-depth discussions or scoping exercises with LEAs, FIUs, sector supervisors"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: It is important to understand how domestically generated criminal proceeds are laundered in the country or abroad, and how illicit foreign proceeds are laundered in the country. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "how criminal proceeds being generated domestically can be laundered in the country or outside of it"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Failing to account for cross-border flows results in a substantially incomplete understanding of actual risks, as criminals exploit gaps in AML systems across countries. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failing to account for these crossborder flows results in a substantially incomplete understanding of the actual risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Examining cross-border flows further minimises the impact of unknown unknowns; for example, many informal requests from a foreign country about a specific predicate could reveal domestic predicate threats that would otherwise go undetected. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "high numbers of informal requests for information from a foreign country relating to a specific predicate"</sub>

- **descriptive / method**: Under Economic Importance-Based Weighting, the NRA working group assigns weights to each FI and DNFBP category from 1 (Very Low) to 10 (Very High), reflecting its significance in the country's economy. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assign weights to each FI and DNFBP category on a scale from 1 (Very Low) to 10 (Very High)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Because no single indicator suits all sectors (total asset size suits banking but is less pertinent to money transfer, insurance, real estate or lawyers), the World Bank advises using several indicators together to set relative weights. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including asset size, GDP contribution, annual turnover, employee count, and client base"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In the economic-importance approach the country's overall sectoral vulnerability is calculated as a weighted average of sector-specific vulnerability ratings. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "overall sectoral vulnerability is then calculated using a weighted average of sector-specific vulnerability ratings"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Under Vulnerability-Based Weighting, more vulnerable sectors carry more weight in the overall rating. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sectors with greater vulnerabilities carry more weight in the overall vulnerability rating"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Vulnerability-based weighting takes the top 20 percentile of sectors by vulnerability rating and uses their average to compute overall sectoral vulnerability, giving more conservative results. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prioritises the top 20 percentile of sectors based on their vulnerability ratings and uses their average"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The vulnerability-based method is also known as the open door approach, a reference to the risk to a house with all but one entryway secured, highlighting risk through the least protected area. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with a reference to the risk to a house with all but one entryway secured"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The first (economic importance) method is the tool's default and is widely used by countries. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is the tool's default and is widely utilised by countries"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Users can select either approach to represent overall vulnerability or analyse it as a band between the two levels. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Users can select either approach to represent overall vulnerability or analyse it as a band between the two levels."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The weighting can be applied to sectoral risks rather than vulnerabilities, which is preferred in more recent applications of the NRA Tool. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This weighting can be done for sectoral risks (rather than vulnerabilities), which is preferred more in recent applications"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: While a structured, data-driven approach is a useful starting point, the vulnerability assessment should ultimately involve a more comprehensive, contextual analysis of the jurisdiction's circumstances and interdependencies. (p. 43)[^original]

## Risk Assessment

- **descriptive / definition**: Inherent risk is the extent of risk present without considering any risk mitigation measures; residual risk takes into account the impact of a country's mitigation measures. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inherent risk is the extent of risk present without the consideration of any risk mitigation measures"</sub>

## Inherent Risk

- **permissive / method**: Countries can use different approaches to rate final risk, such as a risk matrix, heat maps, or a mathematical or statistical formula. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as a risk matrix, heat maps, or a mathematical or statistical formula"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: There is no single, definitive model; those involved in risk analysis might collectively rank or categorise each identified risk by degree and relative importance. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While there is no single, definitive model, those involved in the risk analysis might collectively rank"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Inherent risk is defined as the level of risk that exists before any mitigating measures are introduced. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inherent risk is defined as the level of risk that exists before introducing any mitigating measures."</sub>
- **expected / method**: Inherent risk should be the starting point for countries when they conduct a risk assessment. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This should be the starting point for countries when conducting a risk assessment."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The goal of an overall risk assessment is to determine how threats may interact with vulnerabilities in order to reach the final risk understanding. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The goal of an overall risk assessment is to determine how threats may interact with vulnerabilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Exploring how threats and vulnerabilities interact often does not materially change their relative importance (for example a vulnerability rated 7/10 generally stays so). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exploring the intersection of how threats and vulnerabilities interact will not lead to any (material) change"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Analysing the interaction is nonetheless critical for risk understanding because it gives a more granular picture of the risk environment. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "But this analysis of the interaction is critical for risk understanding"</sub>

## Residual Risk

- **descriptive / definition**: Residual risk is the level of risk that remains after risk mitigation measures have been introduced. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Residual risk is defined as the level of risk that remains after risk mitigation measures have been introduced."</sub>
- **permissive / method**: The NRA could assess existing mitigating measures, including policy and operational measures, the degree of compliance by FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs, the efficacy of suspicious activity reporting and the impact of supervisory actions. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The NRA could include the assessment of existing risk mitigating measures."</sub>
- **permissive / method**: The maturity and embeddedness of mitigating measures and known effectiveness challenges could be factored in; for example a new beneficial ownership registry or cash reporting requirement may not have its anticipated impact for several years. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a new beneficial ownership registry or new cash reporting requirement may not yet have anticipated impact for several years"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "residual risk is lower than or at most equal to the inherent risk depending on the quality of controls"</sub>
