## A. WHAT IS THE RBA?

- **expected / definition**: A risk-based approach to AML/CFT means countries, competent authorities and financial institutions are expected to identify, assess and understand their ML/TF risks and take AML/CFT measures commensurate with those risks to mitigate them effectively. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are expected to identify, assess and understand the ML/TF risks to which they are exposed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When assessing ML/TF risk, countries, authorities and FIs should analyse how identified risks affect them; the risk assessment is the basis for risk-sensitive application of AML/CFT measures. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the risk assessment therefore provides the basis for the risk-sensitive application of AML/CFT measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The RBA is not a 'zero failure' approach: an institution may take all reasonable measures and still be used for ML or TF. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The RBA is not a “zero failure” approach"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: An RBA does not exempt countries, authorities and FIs from mitigating ML/TF risks assessed as low. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A RBA does not exempt countries, competent authorities and financial institutions from mitigating ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Where ML/TF risks are assessed as low, INR 1 allows countries not to apply some FATF Recommendations and INR 10 allows Simplified Due Diligence (INR 1 para 6, 11 and 12; INR 10 para 16 and 21). (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR 10 allows the application of Simplified Due Diligence measures"</sub>

## B. THE RATIONALE FOR A NEW APPROACH

- **descriptive / context**: The 2012 FATF Recommendations made the RBA an 'essential foundation' of a country's AML/CFT framework and an over-arching requirement for all relevant Recommendations, whereas the 2003 Recommendations applied it only in some areas. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the 2012 Recommendations consider the RBA to be an ‘essential foundation’"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Per the Introduction to the 40 Recommendations, the RBA lets countries adopt more flexible measures to target resources effectively and apply preventive measures commensurate with the nature of risks. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adopt a more flexible set of measures in order to target their resources more effectively"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Applying an RBA is not optional but a prerequisite for effective implementation of the FATF Standards. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The application of a RBA is therefore not optional"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The effectiveness of risk-based measures is assessed in mutual evaluations, which measure achievement of defined outcomes; assessors must account for risks and RBA flexibility when judging deficiencies. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will be assessed as part of the mutual evaluation of the national AML/CFT regime"</sub>

## C. APPLICATION OF THE RISK-BASED APPROACH

- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 1, countries should extend their AML/CFT regime to additional institutions, sectors or activities posing higher ML/TF risk, and could consider exempting some from certain obligations where conditions are met, such as an assessment of low risk. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should extend their regime to additional institutions, sectors or activities if they pose a higher risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: AML/CFT supervisors should consider a bank's own risk assessment and mitigation and the discretion allowed under the national RBA, and INR 26 requires supervisors themselves to adopt an RBA to supervision. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR 26 further requires supervisors to themselves adopt a RBA to AML/CFT supervision"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where ML/TF risk is higher, competent authorities and banks must take enhanced measures, meaning stronger range, degree, frequency or intensity of controls. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the range, degree, frequency or intensity of controls conducted will be stronger"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Where ML/TF risk is lower, standard measures may be reduced: each required measure must still be applied, but with lighter degree, frequency or intensity. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each of the required measures has to be applied, but the degree, frequency or the intensity"</sub>

## D. CHALLENGES

- **expected / expectation**: Banks' identification and assessment of their own ML/TF risk should consider national risk assessments under Recommendation 1 and the national legal and regulatory framework, including prescribed significant risk areas and mitigation measures. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Banks’ identification and assessment of their own ML/TF risk should consider national risk assessments"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Where ML/TF risks are higher, banks should always apply enhanced due diligence, even if national law does not prescribe exactly how (e.g., varying the degree of enhanced ongoing monitoring). (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where ML/TF risks are higher, banks should always apply enhanced due diligence"</sub>

## ALLOCATING RESPONSIBILITY UNDER A RBA

- **permissive / requirement**: Banks may be given flexibility in deciding how to address other risks, but their mitigation strategy must reflect national legal, regulatory and supervisory frameworks. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Banks may be granted flexibility in deciding on the most effective way to address other risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In deciding how much discretion banks have, countries should consider the banking sector's ability to identify and manage ML/TF risks and supervisors' expertise and resources, and may consider evidence on sector compliance levels. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their banking sector’s ability to effectively identify and manage ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries with emerging financial sectors or developing frameworks may limit RBA flexibility and adopt a more prescriptive implementation until sector understanding and experience improve, possibly based on mutual evaluation reports, follow-up reports or FSAP. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a more prescriptive implementation of the AML/CFT requirements may be appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Institutions should not be exempted from AML/CFT supervision even where capacity and compliance are good, though the RBA lets authorities focus more resources on higher risk institutions. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutions should not be exempted from AML/CFT supervision even where their capacity and compliance is good"</sub>

## IDENTIFYING ML/TF RISK

- **mandatory / requirement**: Access to accurate, timely and objective ML/TF risk information is a prerequisite for an effective RBA; INR 1.3 requires countries to have mechanisms to share risk assessment results with competent authorities, FIs and other interested parties. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR 1.3 requires countries to have mechanisms to provide appropriate information on the results"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Where information is unavailable (inadequate data, inability to share sensitive information, or restrictions such as censorship or data protection), banks will find it hard to identify ML/TF risk and may fail to assess and mitigate it. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "restricted by, for example, censorship or data protection provisions"</sub>

## ASSESSING ML/TF RISK

- **expected / method**: Assessing ML/TF risk means determining how identified threats will affect the entity by analysing the likelihood of risks occurring and their impact on individual banks, the sector and, for large systemic institutions, the national economy. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understand the likelihood of these risks occurring, and the impact that these would have"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: ML/TF risks are often classified as low, medium and high, with combinations such as medium-high or low-medium, to aid understanding and prioritisation. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ML/TF risks are often classified as low, medium and high"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: ML/TF risk assessment goes beyond collecting quantitative and qualitative information; it is the basis for mitigation and should be kept up-to-date. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be kept up-to-date to remain relevant"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Banks are not necessarily required to perform probability calculations, which may not be meaningful given unknown volumes of illicit transactions. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Banks are not necessarily required to perform probability calculations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities and banks should have skilled and trusted personnel, recruited through fit and proper tests where appropriate, and be technically equipped commensurate with the complexity of the bank's operations. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have skilled and trusted personnel, recruited through fit and proper tests"</sub>

## MITIGATING ML/TF RISK

- **expected / requirement**: Under the FATF Recommendations, banks, countries and authorities applying an RBA should take enhanced measures where risk is higher and may apply exemptions or simplified measures in low risk situations, subject to the national framework providing for Simplified Due Diligence. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in low risk situations, exemptions or simplified measures may be applied"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries seeking to exempt institutions, sectors or activities from some AML/CFT obligations should assess the associated risk and be able to demonstrate it is low and that the conditions of an INR 1.6 exemption are met. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "be able to demonstrate that the risk is low, and that the specific conditions required"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries and banks applying simplified measures should assess risks of the targeted customer or product category, establish the lower risk level, and define the extent and intensity of required AML/CFT measures. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establish the lower level of the risks involved"</sub>

## DEVELOPING A COMMON UNDERSTANDING OF THE RBA

- **expected / expectation**: RBA effectiveness depends on a common understanding between authorities and banks; supervisors in particular should issue guidance on how they expect banks to meet AML/CFT obligations in a risk-sensitive way, and ongoing communication is an essential prerequisite. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issue guidance to banks on how they expect them to meet their legal and regulatory AML/CFT obligations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should accept that not all banks will adopt identical controls and that a single isolated incident of insignificant, crystallised risk may not invalidate a bank's AML/CFT controls; banks must understand flexibility does not exempt them from effective controls. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a single isolated incident of insignificant, crystallised risk may not necessarily invalidate"</sub>

## FINANCIAL INCLUSION

- **expected / requirement**: Financial exclusion does not automatically mean low ML/TF risk; institutions should not apply simplified due diligence or exemptions solely because a customer is financially excluded, which may stem from e.g. poor credit rating or criminal background. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Being financially excluded does not automatically equate to low or lower ML/TF risk"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: An RBA may foster financial inclusion for low-income individuals; countries may set exemptions based on proven low risks or allow FIs more flexible CDD in lower risk cases, and inclusion increases transparency and traceability of financial flows. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial inclusion will contribute to greater transparency and traceability of financial flows"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: As a general rule CDD measures, including the prohibition on anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names, apply in all cases. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the prohibition for financial institutions to keep anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Paragraphs 2 and 6 of INR 1 let countries, in strictly limited and justified circumstances with proven low ML/TF risk, not apply certain Recommendations to a particular type of FI, activity or DNFBP; some countries use this for financial inclusion. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in strictly limited circumstances and where there is a proven low risk of ML/TF"</sub>

## DEVELOPING A COMMON UNDERSTANDING OF THE RBA

- **expected / expectation**: Countries and competent authorities should take account of the need for effective supervision of all entities covered by AML/CFT requirements; this supports a level playing field between all banking service providers and avoids higher risk activities shifting to institutions with insufficient or inadequate supervision. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "avoid that higher risk activities shift to institutions with insufficient or inadequate supervision"</sub>
