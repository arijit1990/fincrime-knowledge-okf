## ITALY

- **descriptive / method**: The Bank of Italy conducts systematic, periodic off-site analysis of bank-reported data (annual AML compliance function report, control body reports on irregularities, post-inspection follow-up reports) and holds AML meetings with board members or compliance officers when needed. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "annual report of AML compliance function; reports by control bodies on specific irregularities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Bank of Italy inspections, planned from off-site results, may be full scope, targeted (business areas, specific risks, operational profiles, corrective action follow-up) or thematic; after the 2008 Italian AML Law AML controls run within general or dedicated thematic inspections. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inspections may be: full scope, targeted"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In Italy, the FIU checks, both off-site and on-site, whether financial institutions comply with suspicious transaction reporting duties and whether STRs were omitted; it does so mainly on a risk-based approach. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cases of omitted reporting of STRs mainly on a RBA basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In Italy, the FIU recognises the areas of risk for its STR compliance checks from information transmitted by law enforcement, financial sector supervisory authorities, professional associations or other FIUs. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The areas of risk are recognised by information transmitted by law enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Where there are infringements or major organisational disorder at a financial institution, close coordination between the Italian FIU and the Bank of Italy and other supervisory authorities is ensured through MoUs. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In case of infringements or major organisational disorder at the financial institution"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Since 2008 the Bank of Italy has run yearly targeted AML inspections of bank branches in high risk areas (organised crime, tax evasion, tobacco smuggling, usury), with short 3/5 day visits using a questionnaire on CDD, registration, reporting and training plus sample testing of customer positions; major deficiencies trigger corrective action requests. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "short on-site visits (3/5 days) in a number of pre-selected branches"</sub>

## FRANCE

- **descriptive / method**: The French ACPR uses an annual AML-CFT questionnaire systematically studied off-site, with priority levels and corrective action timelines depending on deficiency seriousness; the questionnaire reflects revised FATF Recommendations, thematic inspection results (e.g., wealth management) and has group and sector-specific questions. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the answers to the questionnaire are systematically studied by off-site control services"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: ACPR off-site tools include internal audit reports, on-site inspection reports, meeting information, and annual internal control reports covering business, risks, internal control changes, governance and ML/TF risks. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Annual internal control reports, with an overview of business conducted and risks incurred"</sub>

## THE NETHERLANDS

- **descriptive / method**: DNB thematic reviews are in-depth reviews of a specific risk area across selected institutions, enabling benchmarking and identification of outliers and best practices; themes come from risk analyses, prior reviews or known incidents. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Thematic reviews allow DNB to benchmark practices, identify outliers and best practices"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: DNB selects entities by size, risk profile and past compliance weaknesses, requests information beforehand (policies, transactions, STRs, customer information), and on-site holds management discussions, samples files and examines risk assessment procedures. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "previous experience of compliance weaknesses etc"</sub>

## UNITED KINGDOM

- **descriptive / method**: The FCA allocates specialist resource by firm ML risk and ad hoc; off-site reviews analyse regulatory returns with financial crime questions, policies, audit reports, minutes, prior visit reports and external intelligence; on-site visits include staff interviews, control testing and file reviews. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regulatory returns (which include specific financial crime questions)"</sub>

## AUSTRALIA

- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC supervision frequency correlates with an RE Group's compliance risk; a key performance indicator is assessing each high-risk RE Group within a three year period, alongside population-wide data-mining to spot outlier entities. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each high-risk RE Group within a three year period"</sub>

## GERMANY

- **descriptive / method**: In Germany, credit institutions with balance sheets below a specific threshold need only biennial assessment unless risk factors indicate higher risk; 'big players' get more frequent special inspections, and high-risk classification can trigger regular special inspections regardless of size. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only need to be assessed on a biennial basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: German special inspections may also follow serious deficiencies in prior reports or ad hoc ML/TF 'bad news' from law enforcement, newspapers, whistleblowers or internet research. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "revealed through investigations of law enforcement agencies, newspapers, whistleblowers, internet research"</sub>

## MEXICO

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Mexico's supervision frequency factors include risk ratings, unusual increases in number and threshold of transactions, increases in STRs or CTRs, business line profile changes, new products or lines of business, compliance level, and follow-up of corrective measures. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Unusual increases in the number and threshold of transactions performed by financial entities"</sub>

## THE NETHERLANDS

- **descriptive / method**: DNB assigns more ongoing supervision resources to higher risk profile institutions based on size, activities and compliance history, and uses thematic reviews for specific high risk areas. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DNB assigns more resources to institutions which have a higher risk profile"</sub>

## UNITED KINGDOM

- **descriptive / method**: FCA ML risk bands: highest band firms are covered by the Systematic AML Programme on a four-year rolling cycle lasting several months; second band firms get two or three day on-site visits every two years; lower risk firms are visited on events-driven basis or in thematic samples. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The programme operates on a four-year, rolling cycle"</sub>

## UNITED STATES

- **mandatory / requirement**: US Federal Banking Agencies are required by law to conduct BSA examinations of insured depository institutions during regular examinations on a 12-18 month statutory cycle (12 U.S.C. §§ 1820(d) and 1784). (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "on a 12-18 month cycle, which is required by statute"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: US Federal Banking Agencies (FBAs) are required to conduct the core examination procedures for assessing compliance with the BSA/AML compliance program, regulatory requirements and related topics. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FBAs are required to conduct required core examination procedures for assessing compliance with the BSA/AML compliance program"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For US FBAs, expanded examination procedures for products, services, persons and entities are required depending on risk. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Expanded procedures for products, services, persons and entities are required depending on risk"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For US FBAs, transaction testing is required during each BSA examination regardless of the level and nature of risk present in the institution. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transaction testing is required during each examination regardless of the level and nature of risk present in the institution"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: For larger, more complex institutions, some US FBAs maintain resident on-site examiners who provide continuous supervision and obtain at least quarterly updates on the institution's condition and risk assessment. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For larger, more complex institutions, some FBAs maintain resident on-site examiners who provide continuous supervision"</sub>

## AUSTRALIA

- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC prioritises entities with high inherent ML/TF risk and inadequate controls for remediation and/or enforcement; remediation uses a compliance assessment report requiring specific actions within timeframes, monitored by AUSTRAC. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these entities are prioritised for remediation and/or enforcement action"</sub>

## GERMANY

- **descriptive / method**: BaFin's supervision intensity follows its risk matrix; on-site inspections always include sample testing, and many deficiencies in a field can trigger a 'full-size check', e.g. checking identification of all new customers from the past 6 months. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the identification process of all new customers acquired in the past 6 months is checked"</sub>

## MEXICO

- **descriptive / method**: CNBV follows its Institutional Manual of Supervision and may extend reviews beyond the planned programme where weaknesses appear, e.g. verifying a bank's automated system if a transaction report reveals a deficiency. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a deficiency is detected in the automated system of the bank"</sub>

## THE NETHERLANDS

- **descriptive / method**: DNB assigns each institution to a supervision regime such as low, neutral, high or urgent, based on the chance identified risks harm supervisory objectives, ranging from no substantial intervention to immediate intervention. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "low, neutral, high and urgent"</sub>

## UNITED KINGDOM

- **descriptive / method**: The FCA's Systematic AML Programme (SAMLP) involves extensive interviews with senior management, compliance and front office in the UK and elsewhere and detailed control testing, lasting several months and repeated every four years. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A typical SAMLP lasts several months and is repeated every four years"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Lower band FCA firms are supervised mainly via thematic reviews (off-site policy assessment plus a two to three day on-site visit) and event-driven supervision whose intensity depends on the suspected breach. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The intensity of event-driven supervision depends on the nature of the suspected breach"</sub>

## AUSTRALIA

- **descriptive / context**: AUSTRAC publishes guidance notes and AML/CTF Guides, including for pubs and clubs with electronic gaming machines, independent remittance dealers, bookmakers, and the business profile form, plus guidelines under the Financial Transaction Reports Act for cash dealers. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AML/CTF compliance guide for independent remittance dealers"</sub>

## Compliance Guide

- **descriptive / context**: The AUSTRAC Compliance Guide consolidates guidance, explains AML/CTF Act obligations with examples and helps entities design systems and controls. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The _AUSTRAC Compliance Guide_ consolidates a range of AUSTRAC guidance material"</sub>

## CANADA

- **descriptive / context**: Canada's FINTRAC publishes guidelines, including Guideline 4 on implementing a compliance regime including the RBA; OSFI issues guidance for Federally-Regulated Financial Institutions. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FINTRAC has recently issued new guidance (Guideline 4) on how to implement a compliance regime"</sub>

## ITALY

- **descriptive / context**: The Bank of Italy gives supervised entities general feedback on controls and guidance on AML/CFT risks and internationally requested measures (e.g., FATF black list, UN sanctions). (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF black list, UN sanctions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The Italian FIU issues guidance on anomalous conduct patterns linked to ML/TF, e.g. possible loan sharking (2011), ML risk of factoring (2012), gambling and betting (2013), and alert notes such as on pre-paid cards (2012). (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "alert note on pre-paid cards in 2012"</sub>

## THE NETHERLANDS

- **descriptive / method**: DNB benchmarks off-site and on-site results to find outliers and good practices, gives institutions individual feedback with possible enforcement, provides industry-wide feedback and publishes thematic themes annually. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the results are benchmarked to determine outliers and good practices"</sub>

## Regulatory guidance

- **descriptive / context**: The FCA's financial crime guidance sets expectations for systems and controls, includes self-test questions and examples of good and poor practice from on-site visits. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lists examples of good and poor practice observed during on-site visits"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FCA financial crime guidance is not binding and departure is not presumed a breach, but firms are expected to use it proportionately and risk-sensitively; changes are publicly consulted. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCA will not presume that a firm’s departure from this guidance constitutes a breach of its rules"</sub>

## Industry guidance

- **descriptive / context**: The FCA has regard to Joint Money Laundering Steering Group (JMLSG) guidance, written by UK trade associations, formally approved by the UK Government and referenced in the FCA rulebook. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is formally approved by the UK Government and referred to in the FCA’s rulebook and guidance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: US FBAs issue guidance alone and jointly with FinCEN; the FFIEC BSA/AML Exam Manual prescribes required examiner procedures for consistent, transparent exams and is updated regularly for emerging ML/TF risks. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jointly with the FIU (FinCEN)"</sub>
