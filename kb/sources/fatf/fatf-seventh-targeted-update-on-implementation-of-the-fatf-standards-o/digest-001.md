## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Since the 2025 Targeted Update, jurisdictions have continued to progress on R.15 (risk assessments, regulatory approaches, licensing or registering VASPs, Travel Rule implementation, supervisory and enforcement action). Significant gaps remain in translating risk assessments into effective mitigation, operationalising licensing or registration in practice, identifying persons or entities conducting VASP activities, and ensuring effective risk-based supervision and enforcement. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "significant gaps remain in translating risk assessments into effective mitigation measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report highlights emerging and increasing VA risks: growing industrialisation of VA-enabled fraud by organised crime groups, misuse of stablecoins, P2P transactions through unhosted wallets, offshore VASPs operating outside effective oversight, and continuing challenges with DeFi arrangements. It sets out recommendations for the public and private sectors on R.15 implementation, risk mitigation and domestic, international and public-private co-operation. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the growing industrialisation of VA-enabled fraud by organised crime groups, the misuse of stablecoins"</sub>

## Key Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: There has been some improvement in R.15 implementation since 2025. More jurisdictions reported conducting VA/VASP risk assessments, but effectively using them to inform risk-based preventive, mitigation, supervisory and enforcement measures remains a challenge for many. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "More jurisdictions reported having conducted VA/VASP risk assessments"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: More jurisdictions have identified their regulatory approach, including permitting VA/VASP activity or adopting full or partial prohibitions, but further analysis is needed to understand how far these frameworks have been effectively operationalised. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "further analysis is needed to understand the extent to which these frameworks have been effectively operationalised"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Jurisdictions taking a prohibition approach have not progressed in supervisory or enforcement actions to sanction VASPs operating illegally. Prohibition frameworks, while allowed by the FATF Standards, can represent significant risks to the VASP and global financial system if not enforced effectively. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The use of prohibition frameworks, while allowed by the FATF Standards, can represent significant risks"</sub>

## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / context**: This targeted update reviews how far Recommendation 15 (R.15) has been implemented across the FATF Global Network, and what gaps remain, following the extension of the AML/CFT Standards to virtual assets (VAs) and virtual asset service providers (VASPs). Following the FATF Virtual Assets Contact Group (VACG) Roadmap to strengthen R.15 implementation, it updates the overview of measures FATF and FSRB jurisdictions, including those with materially important VASP activity, have taken to regulate and supervise the VA/VASP sector. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assesses progress and remaining gaps in the implementation of Recommendation 15 (R.15) across the FATF Global Network"</sub>

## Key Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the 2026 survey, 83% of respondents said they had passed legislation implementing the Travel Rule. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For the 2026 survey, 83% of respondents reported having passed legislation implementing the Travel Rule."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Travel Rule enforcement experience remains limited: almost half of the jurisdictions that have introduced Travel Rule legislation have not yet taken any Travel Rule-related supervisory or enforcement action. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enforcement experience remains limited, with almost half of jurisdictions that have introduced Travel Rule legislation"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Since 2025, VA-enabled illicit activity has grown more complex and convergent, including scam centre operations linked to organised crime groups, "pigbutchering" scams, DPRK-related cyber theft, TF/PF, sanctions evasion and cross-border money laundering. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VA-enabled illicit activity has become more complex and convergent, including through Organised Crime Groups-linked scam centre operations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Stablecoins, P2P transactions through unhosted wallets, offshore VASPs, OTC brokers, cross-chain tools and DeFi-related activity continue to pose significant risks, which the update says points to the need for stronger public-private cooperation, enhanced monitoring and concrete risk mitigation by both jurisdictions and the private sector. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Stablecoins, P2P transactions through unhosted wallets, offshore VASPs, OTC brokers, cross-chain tools and DeFi-related activity continue to present significant risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Progress continued in licensing and registering VASPs, including inspections and enforcement, but further progress is needed in practice and jurisdictions still struggle to identify natural or legal persons conducting VASP activities. Offshore VASPs remain a significant challenge, with more than a third of jurisdictions with licensing or registration frameworks applying a broader approach requiring certain offshore VASPs to be licensed or registered. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions continue to face difficulties in identifying natural or legal persons that conduct VASP activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Identifying individuals or entities exercising control or sufficient influence over DeFi arrangements remains challenging. While 31% (44 of 142) of jurisdictions reported risk mitigation measures apply to DeFi arrangements, only four have imposed licensing or registration requirements and just two have licensed or registered such arrangements in practice. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only four jurisdictions have imposed licensing or registration requirements, and just two have actually licensed or registered"</sub>

## Recommendations for the public and private sectors

- **expected / recommendation**: The priority R.15 recommendations state that jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity should expedite effective implementation of R.15. Those that have not yet implemented it should start by prioritising risk assessments and setting up a legal framework, and those that have taken steps should prioritise practical supervisory activity. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity should expedite the effective implementation of R.15."</sub>

## Recommendations for the Public Sector - Understand and comprehensively assess the ML/TF/PF risks posed by VAs and VASPs

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 1 (public sector): jurisdictions should develop a clear and thorough understanding of ML/TF/PF risks posed by VAs and VASPs, even where choosing to prohibit VASPs, in order to allocate supervisory and enforcement resources proportionate to national risk and context. The assessment should consider the jurisdiction's overall exposure (materiality) and the specific risks of VASPs established in the jurisdiction, VASPs operating or offering services in it (oVASPs), stablecoin issuers and DeFi arrangements with controllers based or operating there, peer-to-peer transactions and unhosted wallets, and emerging technologies, business models, trends and methods. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Develop a clear and thorough understanding of the ML/TF/PF risks posed by VAs and VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 2 (public sector): jurisdictions should rely on a broad range of credible resources for the VA/VASP risk assessment. The FATF does not prescribe a specific methodology, but jurisdictions should be able to demonstrate that they have identified and understood the ML/TF/PF risks posed by VAs and VASPs and implemented appropriate and effective mitigating measures. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While the FATF does not prescribe a specific methodology for assessing the VA/VASP sector"</sub>

## Recommendations for the Public Sector - Adopt a robust supervisory framework for VAs and VASPs

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 3 (public sector): jurisdictions should prioritise efforts and allocate supervisory resources to identify and assess ML/TF/PF risks associated with VAs and VASPs and implement an appropriate supervisory framework in line with Recommendation 15, including licensing or registration, supervisory inspections and guidance, and enforcement against unlicensed activity. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prioritise efforts and allocate supervisory resources to identify and assess the ML/TF/PF risks associated with VAs and VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Under Recommendation 3(i), jurisdictions should impose appropriate licensing or registration requirements on VASPs incorporated or established in the jurisdiction, including stablecoin issuers and qualifying DeFi arrangements within the scope of the FATF Standards, and, where relevant, require VASPs operating cross-border (oVASPs) to be licensed or registered. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Imposing appropriate licensing or registration requirements on VASPs incorporated or established in the jurisdiction"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Under Recommendation 3(ii)-(iii), jurisdictions should conduct both off-site and on-site inspections of VASPs and provide feedback and guidance on the effectiveness of their AML/CFT/CPF compliance frameworks including Travel Rule implementation, and adopt effective measures to identify and sanction natural or legal persons that in practice conduct VASP activities without the required licence or registration. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Conducting both off-site and on-site inspections of VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 1 states that the FATF distinguishes three broad categories of DeFi arrangements: (i) those with identifiable controllers ('centralised'); (ii) those centralised in practice but whose controllers cannot be readily identified; and (iii) truly decentralised ones. The FATF Standards apply to the first two categories; truly decentralised arrangements fall outside the Standards because they cannot readily be applied, but still present significant risks requiring alternative, risk-based mitigation measures. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Standards apply to the first two categories."</sub>

## Recommendations for the Public Sector - Enhance international and public-private cooperation to investigate and seize illicit assets

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 4 (public sector): jurisdictions should substantially strengthen collaboration with foreign counterparts and relevant private-sector stakeholders so that criminal activities linked to ML/TF/PF risks can be disrupted in practice, whether involving VASPs, peer-to-peer transactions or unhosted wallets. This may be achieved by creating and using formal and informal international and domestic cooperation channels for timely information sharing, and by developing operational infrastructure, legal frameworks and protocols with public and private partners for rapid tracing, freezing and seizure of illicit virtual assets. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Substantially strengthen collaboration with foreign counterparts and relevant private-sector stakeholders"</sub>

## Recommendations for the Private Sector

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 5 (private sector): VASPs, including stablecoin issuers and qualifying DeFi arrangements, should establish robust AML/CFT/CPF compliance frameworks and implement the Travel Rule in line with R.15. They should thoroughly understand their institutional risk and assess emerging risks from stablecoins, P2P transactions, unhosted wallets, oVASPs and DeFi-related activities. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should establish robust AML/CFT/CPF compliance frameworks and implement the Travel Rule in line with the requirements of R.15"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Under Recommendation 5, VASPs should ensure their AML/CFT controls, including KYC, wallet screening, blacklisting and, where appropriate, whitelisting mechanisms, plus freezing and blocking capabilities, adequately mitigate identified risks and can be swiftly updated for emerging ML/TF/PF trends and typologies. They should also proactively collaborate with competent authorities and private sectors to identify, investigate and prosecute illicit activity by sharing typologies, red flags and good practices, and ensure they can provide tactical and operational assistance in law enforcement investigations. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "wallet screening, blacklisting, and, where appropriate, whitelisting mechanisms"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 6 (private sector), based on the FATF Targeted Reports, states that VASPs should strengthen monitoring of transactions involving unhosted wallets by applying enhanced due diligence to higher-risk wallet activity, and identify suspicious patterns and rapid transfer of VAs using transaction monitoring and blockchain analytics tools. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strengthen the monitoring of transactions involving unhosted wallets by applying enhanced due diligence measures for higher-risk wallet activity"</sub>

## Next steps

- **descriptive / context**: In February 2023 the FATF adopted a Roadmap to strengthen implementation of R.15. In line with it, the FATF updated the table (the MIVA table) setting out R.15 implementation status for all FATF members and other jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity, and published Targeted Reports on Travel Rule implementation, offshore VASP risks, ML/TF/PF risks of stablecoins and unhosted wallets via P2P transactions, and DeFi arrangements. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In February 2023, the FATF adopted a Roadmap to strengthen implementation of R.15."</sub>

## Recommendations for the Private Sector

- **expected / recommendation**: In paragraph 6(iv) of the recommendations, VASPs should assess risks arising from DeFi activities, including exposure to protocols, bridges, mixers and cross-chain tools, and apply appropriate AML/CFT/CPF measures to mitigate them. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assess risks arising from DeFi activities, including exposure to protocols, bridges, mixers, cross-chain tools"</sub>

## Next steps

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Targeted Update Reports and updated MIVA table consistently show modest year-on-year improvements in R.15 implementation across the Global Network, with persistent challenges in risk assessments, the Travel Rule, regulation/supervision and enforcement actions. The FATF will continue to monitor R.15 implementation and emerging VA risks, with particular attention to jurisdictions with significant VASP sectors, using assessment, risk analysis and capacity building. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consistently show modest year-on-year improvements in the implementation of Recommendation 15"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: Following the October 2018 update to R.15, which brought VAs and VASPs within the FATF's AML/CFT requirements, and the June 2019 adoption of the Interpretive Note to R.15, the FATF has undertaken substantial work on implementation gaps, guidance and evolving risks. Table 1.1 lists this work: R.15 amended in 2018; in 2019 the Interpretive Note, creation of the VACG and initial risk-based approach guidance (updated in 2021); in 2020 the first 12-month review, a G20 report on so-called stablecoins and a list of red flag indicators of ML/TF through VAs; and targeted updates in 2022, 2023, 2025 and 2026 alongside 2026 targeted reports on stablecoins and unhosted wallets, offshore VASPs and DeFi. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Following the October 2018 update to Recommendation 15 (R.15), which brought virtual assets (VAs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 2 states that the 2021 Guidance updates focused on six areas: clarifying definitions of virtual assets and VASPs; applying the Standards to so-called stablecoins; risks and tools for peer-to-peer transactions; licensing and registration of VASPs; additional guidance for the public and private sectors on Travel Rule implementation; and principles of information-sharing and cooperation amongst VASP supervisors. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The 2021 Guidance includes updates focusing on the following six key areas"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: This is the FATF's seventh update on global implementation of its VA/VASP Standards. It covers R.15 implementation including the Travel Rule, focusing on technical requirements (legal and regulatory frameworks, enforceable measures, and the existence, powers and procedures of competent authorities), key challenges, progress, emerging good practices, and emerging VA risks and responses. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This report is the FATF’s seventh update on the global implementation of its Standards for VAs and VASPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The analysis is based on a 2026 survey of jurisdictions' R.15 implementation (147 jurisdictions responded: 38 FATF members and 109 FSRB members), VACG meetings (September 2025; May 2026) and the VACG symposium (December 2025), and results from 149 published mutual evaluation reports (MERs) and follow-up reports (FURs) assessing R.15 as of April 2026. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The survey collected responses from 147 jurisdictions (38 FATF members and 109 FSRB members)."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Survey responses were self-reported and not independently verified. The survey used skip logic, so the number of respondents varied by question group (145 on risk assessment and policy approach; 130 on licensing/registering and supervising VASPs; 129 on Travel Rule; 142 on DeFi; 138 on NFTs, unhosted wallets and P2P). The report infers that the 58 of 205 jurisdictions that did not respond have not made progress on R.15, including the Travel Rule. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Responses were self-reported and have not been independently verified."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report has three main sections plus an annex: Section 1 on R.15 and Travel Rule implementation and challenges in risk assessment, licensing/registration and offshore VASPs; Section 2 on market developments and emerging risks, including increasing use of stablecoins by criminals and VAs used to launder predicate offence proceeds (particularly fraud and scams), fund terrorism and finance proliferation; Section 3 on next steps; and Annex A with the updated table of jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in particular the increasing use of stablecoins by criminals across all crime types"</sub>

## 2.2. Overall Status of R.15 Implementation in Mutual Evaluation and Follow-up Reports

- **descriptive / finding or result**: As of April 2026, 149 jurisdictions had been assessed for technical compliance with R.15 and INR.15. Largely compliant (LC) ratings rose from 29% (40 of 138) in 2025 to 34% (51 of 149) in 2026, and partially compliant (PC) ratings fell from 50% (68 of 138) to 43% (64 of 149). (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "increased from 29% (40 of 138 jurisdictions) in 2025 to 34% (51 of 149 jurisdictions) in 2026"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The proportion of jurisdictions not compliant (NC) with R.15 stayed similar, from 21% (29 of 138) in 2025 to 22% (33 of 149) in 2026, and as in 2025 only one jurisdiction is fully compliant (C). Implementation varies by region, with lower capacity jurisdictions struggling to implement R.15. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only one jurisdiction continues to be fully compliant (C) with R.15"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Compared to 2025, jurisdictions made positive progress in 2026 on most requirements overall, but many still face challenges in conducting risk assessments and implementing the Travel Rule. The assessment methodology criteria are R15.3 (risk assessment and risk-based approach), R15.4 (licensing/registration), R15.5 (identifying persons conducting VASP activities), R15.6 (supervision/regulation), R15.7 (guidelines), R15.8 (sanctions compliance), R15.9 (preventive measures including the Travel Rule), R15.10 (targeted financial sanctions) and R15.11 (international cooperation). (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many jurisdictions still face challenges in conducting risk assessments and implementing the Travel Rule"</sub>

## 2.3. Challenges assessing ML/TF/PF risks of VAs and VASPs

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the 2026 survey, 86% (124 of 145) of jurisdictions reported having conducted an ML/TF/PF risk assessment for VA/VASP risks, up from 76% in 2025 (124 of 163). (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the vast majority of respondents (86%; 124 of 145 jurisdictions) reported having conducted an ML/TF/PF risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Despite many risk assessments, challenges remain in implementing preventive and mitigation measures in line with identified risks or using results for risk-based supervision. Mutual evaluation findings show only 48 of 149 assessed jurisdictions met or mostly met sub-criterion 15.3 on assessing risk and taking a risk-based approach. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only 48 out of 149 assessed jurisdictions met or mostly met sub-criterion 15.3"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Jurisdictions are encouraged to refer to the FATF's 2021 Guidance (factors for VA risk assessment and risk-based approach), may consult the FATF Community Workspace on Virtual Assets (examples of VA risk assessments and December 2025 VACG symposium materials), and are encouraged to consult the FATF ML NRA Guidance, whose updates are expected to include a VA/VASP annex later in 2026. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions are encouraged to refer to the FATF’s 2021 Guidance"</sub>
