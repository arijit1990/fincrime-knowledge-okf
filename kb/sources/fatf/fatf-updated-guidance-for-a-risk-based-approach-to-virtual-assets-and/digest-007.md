## Considerations for licensing or registering VASPs

- **expected / expectation**: VASPs that are licensed or registered should be required to meet appropriate licensing and registration criteria set by relevant authorities. The criteria should give national supervisors confidence that the VASPs will be able to comply with their AML/CTF obligations. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs that are licensed or registered should be required to meet appropriate licensing and registration criteria"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The licensing criteria should include an obligation to demonstrate that, prior to launch, the VASP's AML/CFT programs (policies, procedures and organization, taking into account the characteristics of its activity such as types of VAs and transactions, targeted customers and distribution channels) are implemented or able to be implemented once launched. This could include judgement as to the competence and trustworthiness of compliance staff. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are implemented or able to be implemented once launched"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Assessment of these criteria is all the more efficient when performed during the licensing or registration process, when there is time to ensure risk controls are in place prior to launch. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The assessment of these criteria is all the more efficient when it is performed in the course of the licensing or the registration process"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: When a licensing or registration scheme is established, many VASPs may apply at once. Relevant authorities may consider how to ensure adequate resources and enough flexibility to prioritise incoming requests. Countries may consider a range of factors to prioritize applications but should rank them based on the supervisor's judgement and capacity. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a significant number of VASPs may seek licencing or registration at the same time"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such measures against criminal ownership or control of VASPs should include a requirement that VASPs obtain the authorities' prior approval before making substantive changes to their shareholders, business operations and structures. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such measures should include requiring VASPs to seek authorities’ prior approval for substantive changes in shareholders"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: On the basis of risk, authorities may impose conditions on VASPs seeking a license or registration to enable effective supervision. Depending on VASP size and nature, these could include a resident executive director, substantive management presence, specific financial requirements, disclosure of held registrations/licenses in marketing materials, websites and mobile apps, and requirements to ensure authorities can access data related to outsourced functions and the premises of any subcontractors. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring a resident executive director, substantive management presence, specific financial requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Like other entities subject to the FATF Standards, VASPs should put AML/CFT compliance in place prior to launch when designing or building a new product or service, as it is much more difficult to do so later. Careful risk assessment and thorough evaluation of mitigation measures at the licensing and registration stage is therefore especially important. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should put in place AML/CFT compliance prior to launch when designing or building a new product or service"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Once licensing and registration has taken place, AML/CFT mitigations built into products and services should be maintained and be the subject of supervision. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AML/CFT mitigations which are built into products and services should be maintained and be the subject of supervision"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Jurisdictions may consider emphasizing AML/CFT requirements to VASPs through public communication and events (education campaigns, forums or 'office hours' with the VA ecosystem). Providing certainty about the legal framework through advisories or guidance supports a culture of compliance, and countries may consider the incentive effect of publicising enforcement actions against unregistered or unlicensed VASPs. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions may consider emphasizing AML/CFT requirements to VASPs through public communication and events"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Subject to their own discretion, countries may consider designating all VASPs from countries that do not effectively implement licensing or registration requirements as higher risk, so that dealing with a counterpart in a country without an effective licensing regime is designated high risk activity by the supervisor and may incur additional reporting requirements (see also EDD under Recommendation 10 and counterparty VASP due diligence under Recommendation 16). (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may also consider designating all VASPs from countries which do not effectively implement licensing or registration requirements as higher risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: All jurisdictions should encourage a culture of compliance with all applicable legal and regulatory requirements, which may address policy objectives such as investor and consumer protection, market integrity, prudential requirements and national and economic interests, in addition to AML/CFT. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All jurisdictions should encourage a culture of compliance with all of a jurisdictions’ applicable legal and regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Some jurisdictions may decide to underscore the culture of compliance by not permitting VASPs to obtain a license from prudential or other authorities that is separate from AML/CFT-related authorization. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not permitting VASPs to obtain a license from prudential or other authorities which is separate from AML/CFT-related authorization"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Jurisdictions should ensure that VASPs and authorities devote sufficient resources to their AML/CFT compliance functions to cope with expected user and transaction volume. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should also ensure that VASPs and authorities devote sufficient resources to their AML/CFT compliance functions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: ML/TF risks of stablecoins, particularly those with potential for mass-adoption that can be used for P2P transactions, should be analysed in an ongoing and forward-looking manner and mitigated before such arrangements are launched. Where a VASP or other obliged entity proposes to create or use a stablecoin, assessment and mitigation of the ML/TF risks should form part of the licensing or registration process as appropriate. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an assessment of the ML/TF risks and mitigation of the risks should form part of the licensing or registration process as appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should be especially cautious of claims that stablecoins involve no entity that qualifies as a VASP or other obliged entity, especially in the pre-launch phase, as the process of creating and developing an asset for launch is unlikely to be able to be automated. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should be especially cautious of claims that stablecoins involve no entity that qualifies as a VASP or other obliged entity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 139 of the FATF guidance says that the potential for mass adoption of a stablecoin should be included as an important factor to consider in the licensing or registration procedure and in the risk assessment for all VASPs. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The potential for mass-adoption should be included as an important factor meriting consideration in the licensing or"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Co-ordination between national authorities involved in regulating and licensing/registering VASPs is important (as described for Recommendation 2), since various authorities may hold information on unauthorised providers or activities. This is particularly important where a country has multiple different licensing or registration schemes for VASPs rather than one central regime. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "since various authorities may hold information relating to unauthorised providers or activities"</sub>

## Co-operation with domestic and international partners

- **descriptive / expectation**: International co-operation in the registration and licensing process is important. Authorities may inform counterparties that VASPs they previously registered or licensed are operating in the counterparties' jurisdictions. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities may also inform their counterparties that VASPs, which they have previously registered or licensed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should be supervised or monitored by a competent authority, not a self-regulatory body (SRB), which should conduct risk-based supervision or monitoring. SRBs should not be supervisors, though SRBs and industry groups may assist supervisors in facilitating contact, information-sharing and outreach to the VASP sector. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should be supervised or monitored by a competent authority, not a self-regulatory body (SRB)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should make clear who their VASP supervisors are to their foreign counterparts for the widest range of international co-operation. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should make clear who their VASP supervisors are to their foreign counterparts"</sub>

## Supervision or Monitoring

- **descriptive / expectation**: Given the cross-border nature of VASPs' activities and the challenges in associating a VASP with a single jurisdiction, international co-operation between supervisors is of specific importance, as underlined in paragraph 8 of INR. 15 (see also paragraphs 257-260). Jurisdictions could refer to work of other international standard-setting bodies such as the International Organization of Securities Commissions and the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "international co-operation between relevant supervisors is also of specific importance, as underlined in paragraph 8 of INR. 15"</sub>

## Considerations for licensing or registering VASPs

- **expected / expectation**: As a general matter, the licensing or registration procedure for VASPs and obliged entities that launch or are involved in stablecoins should resemble the procedure that applies to VAs generally. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As a general matter, the licensing or registration procedure for VASPs and obliged entities launching, or involved in, stablecoins"</sub>

## Co-operation with domestic and international partners

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 141 of the FATF guidance says countries should have suitable channels for sharing information, as appropriate, so that unlicensed or unregistered VASPs can be identified and sanctioned. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have in place relevant channels for sharing information as appropriate to support the identification and sanctioning"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Authorities should also consider the Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation amongst VASP Supervisors for further guidance on co-operating with counterparts during licensing or registration (see Section VI). (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should also consider the Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation amongst VASP Supervisors for further guidance"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Recommendation 15, countries are required to place VASPs under effective systems for AML/CFT supervision or monitoring. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 15 requires countries to subject VASPs to effective systems for AML/CFT supervision or monitoring"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Consistent with Recommendations 26 and 27, paragraph 5 of INR. 15 requires countries to ensure that VASPs are also subject to adequate AML/CFT regulation and supervision or monitoring and that they effectively implement the FATF Recommendations in line with their ML/TF risks. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "paragraph 5 of INR. 15 similarly requires countries to ensure that VASPs are also subject to adequate regulation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors of VASPs, and of other obliged entities engaged in VA activities, should have adequate powers to supervise or monitor them and enforce compliance with ML/TF requirements, including powers to inspect, access books and records, compel production of information, and impose a range of disciplinary and financial sanctions, including withdrawing, restricting or suspending a licence or registration where applicable. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should have adequate powers to supervise or monitor and ensure compliance by VASPs"</sub>

## Supervision or Monitoring

- **expected / expectation**: When a DNFBP engages in VASP activity (see paragraphs 227-228), countries should subject the entity to all relevant measures for VASPs set forth in the FATF Recommendations, including supervision or monitoring. Footnote 36 adds that regardless of whether jurisdictions call VASPs FIs or DNFBPs, VASPs remain subject to the same level of regulation and supervision as FIs, in line with their financial activities and services. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when a DNFBP engages in VASP activity, countries should subject the entity to all of the relevant measures for VASPs"</sub>

## Preventive Measures

- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 7 of INR. 15 makes clear that all of the preventive measures in Recommendations 10 through 21 apply to both countries and obliged entities in the context of VAs and VA financial activities. Recommendations 9, 22 and 23 also have indirect applicability. Section IV gives guidance specific to VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities on implementing preventive measures. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all of the preventive measures contained in Recommendations 10 through 21 apply to both countries and obliged entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: In general, preventive measures in Recommendations 10 to 21 apply to VASPs in the same manner as FIs, with two qualifications: the occasional transaction designated threshold above which VASPs must conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000 (rather than USD/EUR 15 000), and the Recommendation 16 wire transfer rules apply to VASPs and VA transfers in a modified form (the 'travel rule'). (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the occasional transaction designated threshold above which VASPs are required to conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000"</sub>

## Financial institution secrecy laws

- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 9 is intended to ensure that FI secrecy laws do not inhibit implementation of the FATF Recommendations. Countries should similarly ensure that secrecy laws do not inhibit implementation of the FATF Recommendations to VASPs, although Recommendation 9 does not explicitly mention VASPs. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should similarly ensure that secrecy laws do not inhibit the implementation of the FATF Recommendations to VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 10, countries and obliged entities should design CDD processes to meet the FATF Standards and national legal requirements, helping VASPs (and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities) assess ML/TF risks associated with covered VA activities, business relationships or occasional transactions above the threshold. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries and obliged entities should design CDD processes to meet the FATF Standards and national legal requirements."</sub>

## Customer due diligence

- **descriptive / requirement**: Initial CDD comprises identifying the customer and, where applicable, the beneficial owner, and verifying identity on a risk basis using reliable and independent information, data or documentation, to at least the extent required by the applicable legal or regulatory framework. It also includes understanding the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship, where relevant, and obtaining further information in higher risk situations. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "verifying the customer’s identity on a risk basis and on the basis of reliable and independent information, data, or documentation"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: VASPs can open and maintain accounts (establish a customer relationship) and should collect relevant CDD information when they provide services to, or engage in covered VA activities for or on behalf of, customers. For an occasional transaction, the threshold above which VASPs must conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000, per INR. 15, paragraph 7(a). (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the designated threshold above which VASPs are required to conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000, in accordance with INR. 15, paragraph 7(a)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 37: the FATF agreed to lower the threshold amount for VA-related transactions to USD/EUR 1 000, given the ML/TF risks of, and cross-border nature of, VA activities. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF agreed to lower the threshold amount for VA-related transactions to USD/EUR 1 000"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Regardless of the nature of the relationship or transaction, countries should ensure VASPs have effective procedures to identify and verify, on a risk basis, a customer's identity: when establishing business relations; where the VASP may have ML/TF suspicions, regardless of any threshold exemption; and where it doubts the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained identification data. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where VASPs may have suspicions of ML/TF, regardless of any exemption of thresholds"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Some jurisdictions may treat use of VA kiosks (sometimes called VA 'ATMs') as an occasional transaction, with the provider or operator and the customer transacting one-off. Other jurisdictions may not consider such transactions occasional, with resulting consequences for CDD obligations. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some jurisdictions may consider the use of VA kiosks"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Given VA risks (global reach, rapid settlement, P2P capability, potential for increased anonymity and obfuscation of flows and counterparties), countries may go further than Recommendation 10 requires by requiring CDD for VA transfers or transactions by VASPs (and other obliged entities such as banks engaged in VA activities), including occasional transactions, at a threshold below USD/EUR 1 000, in line with national legal frameworks. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may therefore go further than what Recommendation 10 requires by requiring CDD for VA transfers or transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A lower CDD threshold is consistent with the RBA in Recommendation 1 provided it is justified on the basis of the country's risk assessment (for example through identification of higher risks). (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided that it is justified on the basis of the country’s assessment of risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In establishing regulatory and supervisory regimes, jurisdictions should consider how a VASP can determine and ensure that transactions are in fact conducted only on a one-off or occasional basis rather than a more consistent, non-occasional basis. In choosing an approach to occasional transactions, countries should take into account the products and services VASPs provide in their jurisdiction. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider how the VASP can determine and ensure that the transactions are in fact only conducted on a one-off or occasional basis"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may ask VASPs to identify low-risk, one-off VA transfers where the VASPs can accept the residual risk, as an input into how the country approaches occasional transactions in the VA space. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may request VASPs to identify low risk, one-off VA transfers"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: INR. 10 describes circumstances of higher ML/TF risk in which enhanced CDD measures must be taken. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there are circumstances where the ML/TF risk is higher and where enhanced CDD measures must be taken"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: For VA-related activities and VASPs, countries should consider country- or geographic-specific risk factors. VASPs located in, or VA transfers from or associated with, particular countries present potentially higher ML/TF risk (INR. 10, paragraph 15(b)). (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider country- or geographic-specific risk factors"</sub>

## Enhanced due diligence and simplified CDD

- **descriptive / context**: There is no universally agreed definition or methodology for determining whether a jurisdiction in which a VASP operates, or from which VA transactions emanate, is higher ML/TF risk. Country-specific risks considered with other risk factors provide useful information for determining potential ML/TF risks. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no universally agreed upon definition or methodology for determining whether a jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators of higher country risk include countries or geographic areas identified by credible sources as providing funding or support for terrorist activities or having designated terrorist organisations operating within them. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as providing funding or support for terrorist activities or that have designated terrorist organisations operating within them"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators of higher country risk include countries subject to sanctions, embargoes or similar measures issued by international organisations such as the United Nations. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries that are subject to sanctions, embargoes, or similar measures issued by international organisations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators of higher country risk include countries identified by credible sources as having weak governance, law enforcement and regulatory regimes, including countries identified by FATF statements as having weak AML/CFT regimes, especially for VASPs, for which VASPs and other obliged entities should give special attention to business relationships and transactions. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "having weak governance, law enforcement, and regulatory regimes"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 38: 'credible sources' means information produced by reputable and universally recognised international organisations and other bodies that make it publicly and widely available; besides the FATF and FATF-style regional bodies, examples may include the International Monetary Fund, the World Bank and the Egmont Group of Financial Intelligence Units. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information that is produced by reputable and universally recognised international organisations and other bodies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Because nearly all VAs include one or more of these features, countries may determine that activities in this space are inherently higher risk, based on the very nature of VA products, services, transactions or delivery mechanisms. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may result in countries determining that activities in this space are inherently higher risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: EDD measures that may mitigate the higher risks include corroborating customer-provided identity information, such as a national identity number, with third-party databases or other reliable sources, and potentially tracing the customer's IP address. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "corroborating the identity information received from the customer, such as a national identity number"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: One indicator of higher country risk, listed in paragraph 154(b), is a country that credible sources identify as having significant organised crime, corruption or other criminal activity, including source or transit countries for illegal drugs, human trafficking, smuggling and illegal gambling. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries identified by credible sources as having significant levels of organized crime, corruption, or other criminal activity"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries also should consider risk factors tied to the VA product, service, transaction or delivery channel, including whether it involves pseudonymous or anonymous transactions, non-face-to-face business relationships or transactions, and/or payments received from unknown or unassociated third parties (INR. 10 15(c); see paragraph 31 of the Guidance). (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries also should consider the risk factors associated with the VA product, service, transaction, or delivery channel"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further EDD measures that may mitigate higher risks include the use of analysis products such as blockchain analytics, and searching the Internet for corroborating activity information consistent with the customer's transaction profile, provided data collection is in line with national privacy legislation. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "searching the Internet for corroborating activity information consistent with the customer’s transaction profile"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may encourage VASPs to collect additional information on high-risk customers and transactions to identify and avoid prohibited activities and enable RBA follow-up. Such information may include the purpose of the transaction or payment; the nature, end use or end user of the item; proof of funds ownership; parties to the transaction and their relationship; sources of wealth and/or funds; identity and beneficial ownership of the counterparty; and export control information such as export-control licenses and end-user certification. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may also encourage their VASPs to collect additional information on highrisk customers and transactions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should inform VASPs of prohibited activities through sharing typologies and, where it would support investigation or disruption and would not undermine an ongoing investigation, on a case-by-case basis. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should inform VASPs of prohibited activities through sharing of typologies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: If a VASP suspects a customer is engaged in prohibited activity, perhaps unwittingly, it should undertake further CDD measures that do not tip off the customer, seek to mitigate risk, take appropriate action including rejecting or blocking the transaction when identified as prohibited activity, and file an STR where required. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should undertake further CDD measures that do not tipoff the customer, seek to mitigate risk"</sub>

## Ensuring CDD information is up-to-date

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should require VASPs and other obliged entities providing VA products and services to keep CDD documents, data and information up-to-date and relevant by reviewing existing records, particularly for higher-risk customers or categories of VA products or services, and conducting ongoing due diligence. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should require VASPs and other obliged entities that engage in or provide VA products and services to keep documents"</sub>

## Enhanced due diligence and simplified CDD

- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries also should consider the EDD measures set out in INR. 10, paragraph 20: obtaining additional information on the customer and the intended nature of the business relationship, information on the customer's source of funds, information on the reasons for intended or performed transactions, and enhanced monitoring of the relationship and transactions. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries also should consider the EDD measures detailed in INR. 10, paragraph 20"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider the measures required of FIs that carry out non-face-to-face fiat-denominated activity (such as mobile services), or activity comparable to VA transactions, when assessing their risks and developing mitigating controls. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider the measures required for FIs that engage in fiat-denominated activity that is non-face-to-face"</sub>

## Ensuring CDD information is up-to-date

- **descriptive / context**: Transactional and record reviews are vital for effective supervision and are an important data source for the transfer of required customer information for compliance with the 'travel rule' (Recommendation 16). (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such transactional and record reviews are vital for effective supervision"</sub>

## Record-keeping

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 11 requires countries to ensure VASPs maintain all records of transactions and CDD measures for at least five years, so that individual transactions can be reconstructed and relevant elements provided swiftly to competent authorities. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain all records of transactions and CDD measures for at least five years"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should require VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities to keep transaction records and CDD information, including identification of relevant parties, public keys (or equivalent identifiers), addresses or accounts involved (or equivalent identifiers), the nature and date of the transaction, and the amount transferred, for example. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the public keys (or equivalent identifiers), addresses or accounts involved (or equivalent identifiers)"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Public blockchain or distributed ledger information may provide a beginning foundation for recordkeeping, provided institutions can adequately identify their customers, but relying solely on the ledger for recordkeeping is not sufficient for compliance with Recommendation 11. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reliance solely on the blockchain or other type of distributed ledger underlying the VA for recordkeeping is not sufficient"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Ledger information may enable authorities to trace transactions back to a wallet address but may not readily link the address to an individual's name. The wallet address contains a user code serving as a digital signature (a public key), a unique string of numbers and letters, and additional information is necessary to associate the address with a real or natural person. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "additional information will be necessary to associate the address to a real or natural person"</sub>

## Politically exposed persons

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 12 requires countries to implement measures requiring obliged entities such as VASPs to have appropriate risk management systems to determine whether customers or beneficial owners are foreign PEPs or related or connected to one, and if so to take additional measures beyond normal CDD (Recommendation 10) to determine if and when they are doing business with them, including identifying the source of funds when relevant. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to have appropriate risk management systems in place to determine whether customers or beneficial owners are foreign politically exposed persons"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 41 (FATF Glossary): foreign PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country, for example Heads of State or government, senior politicians, senior government, judicial or military officials, senior executives of state owned corporations, and important political party officials. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country"</sub>

## Correspondent banking and other similar relationships

- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 13 stipulates that countries should require FIs engaged in cross-border correspondent relationships to apply certain measures in addition to normal CDD. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 13 stipulates that countries should require FIs to apply certain measures in addition to performing normal CDD measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: INR. 13 stipulates that, for correspondent banking and other similar cross-border relationships, FIs should apply criteria (a) to (e) of Recommendation 13 in addition to normal CDD. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR. 13 stipulates that for correspondent banking and other similar cross-border relationships, FIs should"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In this context, 'other similar relationships' covers MVTS where one MVTS provider acts as an intermediary for other MVTS providers, or where an MVTS provider obtains banking or similar services through the account of another MVTS customer of the bank (see the 2016 FATF Guidance on Correspondent Banking Relationships). (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "includes MVTS when MVTS providers act as intermediaries for other MVTS providers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 13 applies to VASPs. A correspondent relationship can involve a wide range of services that do not all carry the same level of ML/TF risk. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A correspondent relationship may involve a wide range of services which do not all carry the same level"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where VASP-sector relationships currently have, or may later have, characteristics similar to cross-border correspondent banking, especially a nested relationship in which a VASP holds an account at another VASP, countries should apply the Recommendation 13 preventive measures to the VASPs (and other obliged entities in the VA space) that develop such relationships. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should implement the preventive measures in Recommendation 13 to VASPs (and other obliged entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In the Guidance, a 'correspondent relationship' is the provision of VASP services by one VASP to another VASP or FI. Like its banking equivalent, it is characterised by its on-going, repetitive nature. It could include one VASP white-labelling its platform functionality to another VASP and providing nested services (accounts for smaller VASPs for access to liquidity and trading pairs). (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a ‘correspondent relationship’ is the provision of VASP services by one VASP to another VASP or FI"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should require a VASP providing services to another VASP or FI in a cross-border correspondent relationship to gather sufficient information about the other VASP or FI to understand fully the nature of its business and its AML/CFT risk control framework, including what types of customers it intends to serve through the relationship. (p. 55)[^original]

- **expected / requirement**: Countries should require a VASP in such a relationship to gather sufficient information and determine from publicly available sources the other VASP's or FI's reputation, the quality of supervision it is subject to, and whether it has been subject to an ML/TF investigation or regulatory action. (p. 55)[^original]
