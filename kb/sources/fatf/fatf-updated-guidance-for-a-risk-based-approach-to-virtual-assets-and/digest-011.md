## PART FOUR: APPLICATION OF FATF STANDARDS TO VASPs AND OTHER OBLIGED ENTITIES

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Recommendations apply both to countries and to VASPs and other obliged entities (including banks, securities broker-dealers and other FIs) that provide covered VA-related services, financial activities or operations. Section IV gives additional guidance specific to VASPs and other obliged entities that may engage in covered VA activities. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Recommendations apply both to countries as well as to VASPs and other obliged entities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: In addition to identifying, assessing and taking effective action to mitigate their ML/TF risks under Recommendation 1, VASPs and other obliged entities should apply all the preventive measures in Recommendations 9 through 21, including CDD, when engaging in any covered VA activities. DNFBPs should be aware of their AML/CFT obligations when engaging in covered VA activities per INR. 15 and paragraphs 227-228. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should apply all of the preventive measures in Recommendations 9 through 21 as set forth above in Section III"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Section IV paragraphs on individual preventive measures give additional specific guidance on certain issues. The lack of a dedicated paragraph for each Recommendation does not mean the respective Recommendations or preventive measures do not apply to VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not mean that the respective Recommendations or preventive measures contained therein do not also apply"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: In general, Recommendations 9 to 21 apply to VASPs in the same manner as FIs, with two qualifications: the occasional transaction designated threshold above which VASPs must conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000 (rather than USD/EUR 15 000), and the Recommendation 16 wire transfer rules apply in a modified form (the 'travel rule'). (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In general, the preventive measures set out in Recommendation 9 to 21 apply to VASPs in the same manner as FIs"</sub>

## Customer due diligence

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 10 also sets out when FIs must carry out CDD: on establishing business relations; for occasional transactions above the designated threshold (USD/EUR 1 000 for VA transactions); for occasional transactions that are wire transfers under Recommendation 16 and its Interpretive Note (also USD/EUR 1 000 for VA transfers); where ML/TF is suspected; and where the FI doubts the veracity or adequacy of customer identification data obtained earlier. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 10 also describes the scenarios under which FIs must undertake CDD measures"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: When setting operating procedures and processes for accepting customers and facilitating transactions, VASPs should consider how they can determine and ensure that transactions are really conducted only on a one-off or occasional basis, rather than a more consistent (non-occasional) one. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider how they can determine and ensure that transactions are in fact only conducted on a one-off or occasional"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: While countries may adopt a de minimis threshold of USD/EUR 1 000 for VA transactions they deem occasional or for VA transfers (all treated as cross-border qualifying wire transfers for Recommendation 16), banks, broker-dealers and other FIs must still adhere to their respective CDD thresholds when engaging in covered VA activities. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks, broker-dealers, and other FIs must still adhere to their respective CDD thresholds when engaging in covered VA activities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: DNFBPs such as casinos that engage in covered VA activity should apply the de minimis threshold of USD/EUR 1 000 for occasional transactions and for occasional transactions that are wire transfers. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should apply the _de minimis_ threshold of USD/EUR 1 000 for occasional transactions"</sub>

## When to conduct CDD

- **expected / requirement**: Regardless of the nature of the relationship or VA transaction, VASPs and other obliged entities should have CDD procedures that they effectively implement to identify and verify on a risk basis a customer's identity: when establishing business relations; where they have suspicions of ML/TF, regardless of any threshold exemption; and where they doubt the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained identification data. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have in place CDD procedures that they effectively implement and use to identify and verify on a risk basis the identity of a customer"</sub>

## How to conduct CDD

- **expected / requirement**: In conducting CDD under Recommendation 10, VASPs should obtain and verify the customer identification/verification information required under national law, typically the customer's name and further identifiers such as physical address, date of birth and a unique national identifier number (for example national identity number or passport number). (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should obtain and verify the customer identification/verification information required under national law"</sub>

## When to conduct CDD

- **descriptive / context**: The designated CDD thresholds for occasional transactions and for occasional wire transfers are USD/EUR 3 000 for casinos and USD/EUR 15 000 for dealers in precious metals and stones respectively. Even so, when DNFBPs engage in any covered VA or VASP activities they are subject to the CDD standards of INR. 15, i.e. a de minimis threshold of USD/EUR 1 000 for occasional transactions and for occasional transactions that are wire transfers. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when DNFBPs engage in any covered VA or VASP activities, they are subject to the CDD standards"</sub>

## How to conduct CDD

- **descriptive / method**: A customer risk profile can be set at the level of the individual customer (for example the nature and volume of trading activity or the origin of virtual funds deposited) or at cluster level, where a group of customers shares homogenous characteristics (for example clients conducting similar types of VA transactions or involving the same VA). (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where a cluster of customers displays homogenous characteristics"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should periodically update the customer risk profiles of their business relationships so that the appropriate level of CDD is applied. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should periodically update customer risk profiles of business relationships in order to apply the appropriate level of CDD"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Depending on national legal frameworks, VASPs are encouraged to collect additional information to help verify identity at onboarding, authenticate customers for account access, determine the customer's business and risk profile and conduct ongoing due diligence, and mitigate ML/TF risks of the customer and its activities. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs are also encouraged to collect additional information to assist them in verifying the customer’s identity"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Additional, non-core identity information some VASPs currently collect could include an IP address with associated time stamp, geo-location data, device identifiers, VA wallet addresses and transaction hashes. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an IP address with an associated time stamp; geo-location data; device identifiers; VA wallet addresses; and transaction hashes"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For covered VA activities, VASPs should complete verification of customer and beneficial ownership information before or during the course of establishing the relationship (see also 2015 VC Guidance, paragraph 45). (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the verification of customer and beneficial ownership information by VASPs should be completed before or during the course of establishing the relationship"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where a VASP cannot apply the appropriate level of CDD, Recommendation 10 requires it to not enter into a business relationship or carry out an occasional transaction, or to terminate an existing business relationship, and to consider making an STR in relation to the customer. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 10 requires the VASP to not enter into a business relationship or carry out an occasional transaction"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Based on a holistic view of CDD information (traditional and non-traditional), VASPs and other obliged entities should be able to prepare a customer risk profile in appropriate cases. The profile determines the level and type of ongoing monitoring potentially necessary and supports the decision to enter into, continue or terminate the relationship. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs and other obliged entities should be able to prepare a customer risk profile in appropriate cases"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: If a VASP uncovers VA addresses it has decided not to establish or continue business with, or transact with, due to ML/TF suspicions, it should consider making available its list of 'blacklisted wallet addresses', subject to the laws of its jurisdiction. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the VASP should consider making available its list of “blacklisted wallet addresses,”"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A VASP should screen its customer's and counterparty's wallet addresses against available blacklisted wallet addresses as part of ongoing monitoring, and should make its own risk-based assessment of whether additional mitigating or preventive actions are warranted on a positive hit. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A VASP should screen its customer’s and counterparty’s wallet addresses against such available blacklisted wallet addresses"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: VASPs and other obliged entities must increase the amount or type of information obtained, or the extent of verification, where risks associated with the relationship or VA activities are higher (as described in Section III). They may simplify the extent of CDD where risk is lower. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must therefore increase the amount or type of information obtained or the extent to which they verify such information"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in covered VA activities may vary the extent of CDD measures, to the extent allowed or required by their national regulatory requirements, in line with the ML/TF risks of the individual business relationships, products or services, and VA activities. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may adjust the extent of CDD measures, to the extent permitted or required by their national regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: VASPs and other obliged entities may not apply simplified CDD, or an exemption from other preventive measures, simply because natural or legal persons carry out VA activities or services on an occasional or very limited basis (INR. 1.6(b)). Simplified CDD is also not acceptable whenever there is a suspicion of ML/TF or where specific higher-risk scenarios apply. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may not apply simplified CDD or an exemption from the other preventive measures simply on the basis that natural or legal persons carry out the VA activities"</sub>

## Ongoing CDD and monitoring

- **descriptive / definition**: Ongoing monitoring on a risk basis means scrutinizing transactions to determine whether they are consistent with the VASP's information about the customer and the nature and purpose of the relationship, wherever appropriate. It also involves identifying and keeping up-to-date changes to the customer profile (for example behaviour, use of products, amounts involved), which may require enhanced CDD. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "scrutinizing transactions to determine whether those transactions are consistent with the VASP’s"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs and other obliged entities should adjust the extent and depth of monitoring in line with their institutional risk assessment and individual customer risk profiles, including the type of transactions they allow (for example transactions to/from unhosted wallets). (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should adjust the extent and depth of their monitoring in line with their institutional risk assessment"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: If VASPs assess the risks of transfers to/from unhosted wallets as unacceptably high, they may consider subjecting such wallets to enhanced monitoring or limiting or not accepting transactions with them. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the VASPs may consider choosing to subject such wallets to enhanced monitoring or to limit or not accept transactions with such wallets"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Enhanced monitoring should be required for higher-risk situations and extend beyond the immediate transaction between the VASP and its customer or counterparty. The adequacy of monitoring systems and factors leading to adjusted monitoring levels should be reviewed regularly for continued relevance to the AML/CFT risk programme. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Enhanced monitoring should be required for higherrisk situations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs and other obliged entities should document and state clearly the criteria and parameters used for customer segmentation and risk-level allocation for each cluster of customers, where applicable, and the criteria deciding frequency and intensity of monitoring of customer (or VA product) segments should be transparent. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should document and state clearly the criteria and parameters used for customer segmentation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs and other obliged entities should properly document, retain and communicate to relevant personnel and national competent authorities the results of their monitoring as well as any queries raised and resolved. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "properly document, retain, and communicate to the relevant personnel and national competent authorities the results of their monitoring"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Monitoring transactions is an essential component in identifying potentially suspicious transactions, including VA transactions. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Monitoring transactions is an essential component in identifying transactions that are potentially suspicious"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Transactions that are inconsistent with the behaviour expected from a customer's profile, or that depart from the usual pattern of transactions, may be potentially suspicious. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transactions that do not fit the behaviour expected from a customer profile, or that deviate from the usual pattern"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Monitoring should be carried out continuously and may also be triggered by specific transactions. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Monitoring should be carried out on a continuous basis and may also be triggered by specific transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Where large volumes of transactions occur regularly, automated systems may be the only realistic way of monitoring transactions. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "automated systems may be the only realistic method of monitoring transactions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Transactions flagged by monitoring should go through human or expert analysis to determine whether they are suspicious. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "flagged transactions should go through human/expert analysis to determine if such transactions are suspicious"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs and other obliged entities should understand the rules their monitoring operates by, check their integrity regularly, and check that they reflect the identified ML/TF risks associated with VAs, products or services, or VA financial activities. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should understand their operating rules, verify their integrity on a regular basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Under a risk-based approach to monitoring, VASPs or other obliged entities can set monetary or other thresholds that determine which activities will be reviewed. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "create monetary or other thresholds to determine which activities will be reviewed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The defined situations or thresholds used for this purpose should be reviewed regularly to determine whether they remain adequate for the risk levels established. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Defined situations or thresholds used for this purpose should be reviewed on a regular basis"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Recommendation 12, for domestic PEPs and international organisation PEPs, obliged entities such as VASPs must take reasonable measures to determine whether a customer or beneficial owner is one and then assess the risk of the business relationship. For higher-risk relationships with such PEPs, they should take additional measures consistent with those for foreign PEPs, including identifying source of wealth and source of funds when relevant. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must take reasonable measures to determine whether a customer or beneficial owner is a domestic or international organisation PEP"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 54 and 55 (FATF Glossary): domestic PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted domestically with prominent public functions (for example Heads of State or government, senior politicians, senior government, judicial or military officials, senior executives of state owned corporations, important political party officials); persons entrusted with a prominent function by an international organisation means members of senior management, i.e. directors, deputy directors and board members or equivalent. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "individuals who are or have been entrusted domestically with prominent public functions"</sub>

## Correspondent banking and other similar relationships

- **descriptive / definition**: Under Recommendation 13, 'correspondent banking' does not include one-off transactions but is characterised by its on-going, repetitive nature. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not include one-off transactions (see Recommendation 13 in Section III), but rather is characterised by its on-going, repetitive nature"</sub>

## Data submission technology

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF takes a technology-neutral approach and does not prescribe a particular technology or software for Recommendation 16 compliance. Any solution is acceptable so long as it enables the ordering and beneficiary institution (where present) to comply with its AML/CFT obligations. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF takes a technology-neutral approach and does not prescribe a particular technology or software approach"</sub>

## Correspondent banking and other similar relationships

- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should set up their control framework by defining and assessing the characteristics of their counterparty VASP relationships, including whether they are carrying out activities similar to correspondent banking. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should establish their control framework, by defining and assessing the characteristics of their counterparty VASP relationships"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: This assessment should include considering the views of their competent authorities on any counterparty VASP relationships identified as high risk. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This should include considering their competent authorities’ views on any identified high risk counterparty VASP relationships"</sub>

## Wire transfers and the ‘travel rule’

- **mandatory / requirement**: Providers of VA services must comply with Recommendation 16 (the travel rule). This includes obtaining, holding and submitting the required originator and beneficiary information for VA transfers so as to identify and report suspicious transactions, take freezing actions and prohibit transactions with designated persons and entities. It applies to VASPs and to other obliged entities such as FIs when they send or receive VA transfers for a customer. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "providers in this space must comply with the requirements of Recommendation 16"</sub>

## Data submission technology

- **expected / expectation**: Technological solutions for travel rule compliance should enable VASPs to comply effectively and efficiently, including by locating counterparty VASPs for VA transfers, submitting required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information immediately when a VA transfer is conducted on a DLT platform, and submitting a reasonably large volume of transactions to multiple destinations in an effectively stable manner. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These technological solutions should enable VASPs to comply with the travel rule in an effective and efficient manner"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such solutions should also enable a VASP to transmit data securely, protecting the integrity and availability of the required information to facilitate record-keeping, and protect its use by receiving VASPs or other obliged entities and its disclosure to unauthorised parties, in line with national privacy and data protection laws. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable a VASP to securely transmit data, i.e. protect the integrity and availability of the required information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such solutions should also provide a VASP with a communication channel for follow-up with a counterparty VASP, both for due diligence on that counterparty and for requesting information on a transaction to determine whether it involves high-risk or prohibited activities. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide a VASP with a communication channel to support further follow-up with a counterparty VASP"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Example solutions for obtaining, holding and transmitting required information could be code built into the VA transfer's underlying DLT transaction protocol or running on top of the DLT platform (for example a smart contract, multiple-signature or other technology); an independent (non-DLT) messaging platform or application program interface (API); or any other effective means. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an independent ( _i.e_ ., non-DLT) messaging platform or application program interface (API)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs or other obliged entities should implement mechanisms to ensure effective scrutiny of transactions to identify STRs, taking account of information obtained through the communication infrastructure, potentially combining other customer information, transaction history and additional transaction data obtained from the customer by it or its counterparty VASP. VASPs should also screen transactions to meet sanctions obligations. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should implement mechanisms to ensure effective scrutiny of transactions to identify STRs"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: VASPs and other obliged entities in VA transfers, as ordering or beneficiary institution, should consider how they might leverage existing commercially available technology to comply with Recommendation 16 and INR. 15 paragraph 7(b), as a foundation for identifying beneficiaries and transmitting required originator and beneficiary information in near real-time before a VA transfer is conducted on a DLT platform. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider how they might leverage existing commercially available technology to comply with the requirements of Recommendation 16"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Existing technologies listed as possible foundations include public and private keys (created in pairs, used to encrypt and decrypt so that only sender and recipient can read the information; the public key is available to everyone and the private key known only to its creator) and TLS/SSL connections, which use public and private keys to secure almost all Internet transmissions and keep data between web server and browser private and secure. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transport Layer Security/Secure Sockets Layer (TLS/SSL) connections_"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Further technologies listed include X.509 certificates (digital certificates administered by certificate authorities using the X.509 PKI standard to verify a public key belongs to the identity in the certificate); X.509 attribute certificates (encoding attributes such as name, date of birth, address and unique identifier number, cryptographically attached to the X.509 certificate and administered by attribute certificate authorities); API technology; and other commercially available technology or software or data sharing solutions. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "digital certificates administered by certificate authorities that use the X.509 PKI standard"</sub>

## Counterparty VASP identification and due diligence

- **descriptive / context**: VASPs vary in size from small independent businesses to large multinationals, and no country's AML/CFT regime for VASPs is exactly the same, with measures introduced at different paces. Entities pose higher or lower risk depending on products, services, customers, geography, the AML/CFT regime in the VASP's jurisdiction and the strength of the compliance program. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Not all VASPs are the same. They vary in size from small independent businesses to large multinational corporations."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should analyse and seek to understand how the ML/TF risks they identify affect them and take appropriate measures to mitigate and manage them. The risk assessment provides the basis for risk-based application of AML/CFT measures. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should analyse and seek to understand how the ML/TF risks they identify affect them"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As long as global implementation of the FATF Standards on VASPs remains lacking, managing counterparty relationships will pose a continuing challenge. VASPs will have to consider additional control measures for countries with weak implementation, such as intensive monitoring of transactions with VASPs based there, amount restrictions on transactions, or intensive and frequent due diligence. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs will have to consider additional control measures for countries with weak implementation"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Examples include restricting VA transfers to within the VASP's customer base (internal transfers within the same VASP), only allowing confirmed first-party transfers outside the customer base (originator and beneficiary confirmed to be the same person) and enhanced monitoring of transactions. Otherwise the VASP may face a tough decision on whether to deal with VASPs based in a country with weak or non-existent implementation. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may face a tough decision in whether to deal with VASPs based in a country with weak or non-existent implementation"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: VASPs and FIs should consider this Guidance in conjunction with the FATF Guidance on Correspondent Banking Services. A counterparty VASP relationship may not be a correspondent banking relationship, but there are similarities in the approach to counterparty due diligence, so the Recommendation 13 process is referenced. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs and FIs should consider this Guidance in conjunction with the FATF Guidance on Correspondent Banking Services"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: In practice, assessing a counterparty VASP's AML/CFT controls could involve reviewing the counterparty's AML/CFT systems and controls framework. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such an assessment could involve reviewing the counterparty’s AML/CFT systems and controls framework"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The assessment should include confirming that the counterparty's AML/CFT controls are subject to independent audit, which could be external or internal. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The assessment should include confirming that the counterparty’s AML/CFT controls are subject to independent audit"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 58 notes that the Wolfsberg questionnaire, one of the tools used in the banking sector to meet correspondent banking obligations, provides a starting point for a potential framework for VASP counterparty due diligence. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This provides a starting-point for a potential framework in the VASP counterparty due diligence context"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should be able to fall back on altered procedures, including the possibility of not sending user information, when they reasonably believe a counterparty VASP will not handle it securely, while still carrying out the transfer if they consider the AML/CFT risks acceptable. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should have recourse to altered procedures, including the possibility of not sending user information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In those circumstances VASPs should identify an alternative procedure whose control design their supervisors can review when requested. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should identify an alternative procedure, whose control design could be duly reviewed by their supervisors when requested"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: When establishing a new counterparty VASP relationship, a VASP may obtain information required by Recommendations 10 and 13 directly from the counterparty VASP; under those Recommendations this information should be verified. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a VASP may obtain information set out by Recommendations 10 and 13 directly from the counterparty VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Examples of reliable, independent sources for verifying identity and beneficial ownership of legal persons and arrangements include corporate registries, registries maintained by competent authorities on creation or regulated institutions lists (for example VASP lists maintained by jurisdictions where available), registries of beneficial ownership and other examples in the BCBS General Guide on Account Opening. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "corporate registries, registries maintained by competent authorities on the creation or regulated institutions list"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Potential sources of information on risk level include AML/CFT laws and regulations of the home or host country and how they apply; public databases of legal decisions and regulatory or enforcement actions; annual reports filed with a stock exchange; country assessment reports or other publications by the FATF, FSRBs, BCBS, IMF and World Bank; FATF International Cooperation Review Group lists; reputable newspapers and open source media; third party databases; national or supranational risk assessments; information from the respondent's management and compliance officer(s); and public regulator and supervisor information. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lists issued by the FATF in the context of its International Cooperation Review Group process"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The VASP would need to assess the counterparty VASP's AML/CFT controls to avoid submitting customer information to illicit actors or sanctioned entities, and should consider whether there is a reasonable basis to believe the counterparty can adequately protect sensitive information. This is similar to Recommendation 13 sub-paragraph (b) but in a more risk-based manner. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also consider whether there is a reasonable basis to believe the VASP can adequately protect sensitive information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A VASP needs to undertake counterparty VASP due diligence before transmitting the required information under paragraph 7(b) of INR.15 to its counterparty. It does not need to do so for every VA transfer, but should refresh the counterparty due diligence information periodically or when risk emerges, to determine whether it remains comfortable submitting information in line with its RBA controls. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a VASP needs to undertake counterparty VASP due diligence before they transmit the required information"</sub>

## Submission of required information

- **expected / requirement**: As set forth in INR. 15 paragraph 7(b), it is vital that VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA transfers submit required information securely, to protect user information against unauthorized disclosure and enable receiving entities to comply with their own AML/CFT obligations (identifying suspicious VA transfers, freezing, and prohibiting transactions with designated persons and entities). (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is vital that VASPs and other obliged entities that engage in VA transfers submit the required information in a secure manner"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: VASPs must transmit relevant originator and beneficiary information as set out in INR. 16. Countries may adopt a de minimis threshold for VA transfers below which verification of customer and beneficiary information is not required unless there is ML/TF suspicion. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs must transmit relevant originator and beneficiary information as set out in the INR. 16."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: For occasional VA transfers below USD/EUR 1 000 (or the local currency equivalent or as defined in local regulations), INR.16 requirements apply: the name of the originator and beneficiary and a wallet address for each or a unique transaction reference number are requested. This information does not have to be verified unless there are suspicious circumstances related to ML/TF, in which case the customer information should be verified. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the name of the originator and of the beneficiary will be requested, as well as a wallet address for each or a unique transaction reference number"</sub>

## VA transfers to/from unhosted wallet

- **expected / expectation**: When VASPs and obliged entities transfer to non-obliged entities (unhosted wallets), the VASP should obtain the required originator and beneficiary information from its customer, as it cannot obtain it from another VASP. Such transfers fall within the VASP's broader AML/CFT obligations, such as transaction monitoring and targeted financial sanctions compliance. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a VASP should obtain the required originator and beneficiary information from their customer, because they cannot obtain the relevant information from another VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: VA transfers to/from unhosted wallets and related P2P transactions may be attractive to illicit actors due to anonymity and the lack of limits on portability, mobility, transaction speed and usability. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such transactions may be attractive to illicit actors due to anonymity, the lack of limits on portability, mobility, transaction speed, and usability."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should be aware of the risk of unhosted wallet and P2P transactions and should collect data on their unhosted wallet transfers, and monitor and assess it as necessary to determine to what extent a transaction is within their risk appetite, the appropriate risk-based controls to apply to the transaction/individual customer, and to meet STR obligations (see Recommendation 20). Similar logic applies to risks posed by VASPs not yet licensed/registered and supervised for AML/CFT because they are based in a jurisdiction that has not implemented the FATF Standards for VAs/VASPs. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should collect data on their unhosted wallet transfers, and monitor and assess that information as necessary"</sub>

## Submission of required information

- **expected / expectation**: The guidance says it is essential that providers submit the required information immediately, meaning simultaneously or concurrently with the transfer itself, given the cross-border nature, global reach and transaction speed of VA activities. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is essential that providers submit the required information immediately—that is, simultaneously or concurrent with the transfer itself"</sub>

## VA transfers to/from unhosted wallet

- **permissive / recommendation**: A VASP may choose to impose extra limits, controls or prohibitions on transactions with unhosted wallets in line with its risk analysis. One potential measure is to study the feasibility of accepting transactions only from or to VASPs and other obliged entities, and/or unhosted wallets that the VASP has assessed as reliable. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "studying the feasibility of accepting transactions only from/to VASPs and other obliged entities, and/or unhosted wallets"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: A VASP may choose to impose additional limitations, controls or prohibitions on transactions with unhosted wallets in line with its risk analysis. Potential measures include enhancing its existing risk-based control framework to account for specific risks of unhosted wallet transactions (for example specific users, patterns of observed conduct, local and regional risks, and information from regulators and law enforcement). (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A VASP may choose to impose additional limitations, controls, or prohibitions on transactions with unhosted wallets"</sub>
