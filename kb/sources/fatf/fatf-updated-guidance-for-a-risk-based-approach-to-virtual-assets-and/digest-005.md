## Scope of the definitions

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 80: the FATF envisions very few VA arrangements without VASPs involved at some stage if countries apply the definition correctly, despite frequently changing marketing terms and innovative business models. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF envisions very few VA arrangements without VASPs involved at some stage"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 81: the FATF Standards are technology neutral; the FATF does not seek to regulate the technology underlying VAs or VASP activities, but the natural or legal persons behind such technologies that conduct VASP activities as a business on behalf of another natural or legal person. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF Standards are technology neutral"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 82: a person that creates or sells a software application or VA platform (a software developer) may not constitute a VASP when solely creating or selling it. Using the application or platform to engage in VASP functions as a business on behalf of others would change this determination. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may therefore not constitute a VASP, when solely creating or selling the application or platform"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 82: a party directing the creation and development of software or a platform so that it can provide VASP services as a business for or on behalf of another person likely also qualifies as a VASP, in particular if it retains control or sufficient influence over the assets, software, protocol, platform or any ongoing business relationship with users, even if exercised through a smart contract. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "likely also qualifies as a VASP, in particular if they retain control or sufficient influence over the assets"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 82: such a VASP is responsible for complying with the relevant AML/CFT obligations, and should undertake ML/TF risk assessments prior to launch or use of the software or platform and take appropriate measures to mitigate the risks in an ongoing and forward-looking manner. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should undertake ML/TF risk assessments prior to the launch or use of the software or platform"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 83: the FATF does not seek to regulate as VASPs persons providing ancillary services or products to a VA network, such as hardware wallet manufacturers or providers of unhosted wallets, to the extent they do not also engage in or actively facilitate as a business any covered VA activities for or on behalf of another person. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF also does not seek to regulate as VASPs natural or legal persons that provide ancillary services or products to a VA network"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 83: persons that solely operate a VA network and do not engage in or facilitate VASP activities on behalf of customers (for example offering internet network services and infrastructure, cloud computing resources, and creating, validating and broadcasting blocks of transactions) are not VASPs under the FATF Standards, even if they do so as a business. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not VASPs under the FATF Standards, even if they conduct those activities as a business"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 80: when businesses claim their model is decentralised or distributed, countries should take particular care in assessing those claims and should form their own view of the business model, judged against its risk and against the country's capacity to mitigate that risk. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should take particular care to assess any claims that businesses may make as to models of decentralization"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 84: the FATF does not seek to regulate individual users (not acting as a business) of VAs as VASPs, though such users may still be subject to compliance obligations under a jurisdiction's sanctions or enforcement framework. It also does not seek to capture closed-loop items that are non-transferable, non-exchangeable and cannot be used for payment or investment, such as airline miles, credit card awards or loyalty points that cannot be sold onward in a secondary market outside the closed-loop system. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the types of closed-loop items that are non-transferable, non-exchangeable, and cannot be used for payment or investment purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 84: the VA and VASP definitions are intended to capture specific financial activities and operations (transfer, exchange, safekeeping and administration, and financial services associated with issuance) and assets convertible or interchangeable at a market rate, whether virtual-to-virtual, virtual-to-fiat or fiat-to-virtual. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assets that are convertible or interchangeable at a market rate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 84: a merchant's acceptance of VAs as payment for goods and services does not constitute a VASP activity, but a service that facilitates companies accepting VA as payment would be a VASP. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A service that facilitates companies accepting VA as payment would, however, be a VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 85: AML/CFT regulations apply to covered VA activities and VASPs regardless of the type of VA involved (for example a VASP using or offering AECs), the underlying technology (for example mainnet or embedded layering or other scaling solutions), or additional services the platform incorporates (such as a mixer or tumbler or other obfuscation features). (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AML/CFT regulations will apply to covered VA activities and VASPs, regardless of the type of VA involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 86: a stablecoin arrangement involves a range of entities and may have a central developer or governance body. A governance body consists of one or more natural or legal persons who establish or participate in establishing the rules governing the arrangement (for example the stablecoin's functions, who can access it, and whether/how AML/CFT preventive measures are built in). It may also carry out basic functions such as managing the stabilization function (or delegate them), manage integration of the stablecoin into telecommunications platforms, or promote adherence to common rules. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A governance body consists of one or more natural or legal persons who establish or participate in the establishment of the rules"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 87: where a central body exists in a stablecoin arrangement, it will in general be covered by the FATF Standards either as a FI or a VASP, particularly if it carries out other functions in the arrangement. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they will, in general, be covered by the FATF Standards either as a FI or a VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 88: not all stablecoins may have a readily identified central body that is a VASP or FI, but a party may be more likely to need to exist to drive development and launch before release. If that entity was a business carrying out VASP functions, this would create scope for regulatory or supervisory action in the pre-launch phase. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If this entity was a business and carried out VASP functions, this would create scope for regulatory or supervisory action in the pre-launch phase"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 89: other entities in a stablecoin arrangement, such as exchanges or custodial wallet services, may also have AML/CFT obligations, and the exact details of any arrangement must receive independent scrutiny to make these determinations (a hypothetical case is in Box 3). (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the exact details of any arrangement must receive independent scrutiny to make these determinations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 87: where a stablecoin arrangement has a central body, it will generally fall under the FATF Standards as a financial institution or a VASP, especially if the governance body performs other functions in the arrangement. Such a body should carry out ML/TF risk assessments before the stablecoin is launched or used, and take appropriate measures before launch to manage and mitigate risks across the arrangement. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such a body should therefore undertake ML/TF risk assessments prior to the launch or use of the stablecoin"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 88: where no VASP or financial institution can be clearly identified in a stablecoin arrangement, a country should carefully consider the risks the stablecoin poses and whether any mitigation measures are needed; the guidance points, as an example, to the P2P mitigation measures in paragraphs 105-106. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a country should carefully consider the risks that a given stablecoin poses and the need for mitigation measures, if any"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 88: the approach is not intended to implicate people who only develop software code. It is aimed at persons in stablecoin arrangements who conduct or provide financial services covered by the limbs of the VASP definition. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this is not meant to implicate those only developing software code"</sub>

## Box 3. Hypothetical case study of a stablecoin arrangement and the application of the FATF Standards

- **descriptive / case example**: Box 3 scenario: a Company designs a DLT-based platform to issue a stablecoin (Coin) backed by assets in accounts at global FIs (the Reserve Fund) managed by the Company. Market value is maintained via an Authorised Participant mechanism, and only Authorised Participants can purchase or redeem Coins from the Reserve Fund through the Company. The Company and third parties (Validators) operate a permissioned blockchain on third-party cloud infrastructure, and the Company raises funds through an ICO. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only Authorised Participants will be able to purchase or redeem Coins from the Reserve Fund through the Company"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 3 scenario: the Company, third parties and individual users can access, use and transact with the Coin; third parties such as trading platforms and custodial wallet providers need Company approval to connect to the network. Wallets let users send, receive and store the Coin and developers/third parties can offer customized wallets; transfers follow rules defined by the Company and assessed by regulators before operation; merchants can accept the Coin as payment. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any third parties, such as trading platforms and custodial wallet providers, will need to obtain approval from the Company"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 3 analysis: the Company is a VASP or FI under the FATF Standards as its functions include exchanging the Coin with Authorised Participants, including through its ICO fundraising. It has AML/CFT obligations in addition to those of other third parties in the ecosystem. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Company in this Scenario is a VASP or FI under the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **expected / case example**: Box 3 analysis: under the FATF Standards the Company should take appropriate measures to mitigate ML/TF risks across the ecosystem (for example in the design of the Coin and the criteria and process for approving Authorised Participants). (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Company should take appropriate measures to mitigate the ML/TF risks across the ecosystem"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 3 analysis: Authorised Participants are VASPs because their function includes facilitating issuance, distribution and trading of VAs. Trading platforms are VASPs (exchanging Coins with fiat and other VAs, transferring Coins, safekeeping/administration). Custodial wallet providers are VASPs (transferring Coins and safekeeping/administration). (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorised Participants are also VASPs as their function includes facilitating the issuance, distribution, and trading of VAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Box 3 caveats: the exact details of any arrangement must receive prior adequate and independent scrutiny, and exact application of AML/CFT measures depends on each country. It is illustrative only and not all stablecoins are organized this way. Depending on the country, laws on securities, commodities and derivatives may also be implicated, and countries can adopt other measures if they consider ML/TF risks unacceptably high, such as for potential P2P transactions (paragraphs 105-106). (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is an illustrative example only and not all stablecoins are organized in this way"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 90: for platforms offering VA transfers directly between individual users, whether parties are VASPs is decided on a functional basis under the broad reading of the definitions, not on self-description or technology. Only entities providing very limited functionality short of exchange, transfer, safekeeping, administration, control, and financial services associated with issuance will generally not be a VASP, for example websites offering only a forum for buyers and sellers to identify and communicate. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not on the basis of self-description or technology employed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 91: for self-described P2P platforms, jurisdictions should focus on the underlying activity, not the label or business model. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions should focus on the underlying activity, not the label or business model"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 91: some "matching" or "finding" services may qualify as VASPs even if not interposed in the transaction. The FATF takes an expansive view of the VA and VASP definitions and considers most arrangements currently operating, even if self-categorized as P2P, may have at least some party at some stage of development and launch that is a VASP. Automating a process designed to provide covered services for a business does not relieve the controlling party of obligations. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Automating a process that has been designed to provide covered services for a business does not relieve the controlling party of obligations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 92: the determination of whether a provider meets the VASP definition should take into account the lifecycle of products and services. Launching a service that will provide VASP services imposes VASP obligations even if functions will proceed automatically later, especially if the provider keeps some control or sufficient influence, for example by setting parameters, holding an administrative key, retaining platform access or collecting fees or realizing profits. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The determination of whether a service provider meets the definition of a VASP should take into account the lifecycle of products and services"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 3 analysis: the following ecosystem participants do not fall under the VASP definition. Global FIs whose only function is managing the Reserve Fund (still covered as FIs); Validators other than the Company whose only function is validating transactions; cloud providers whose only function is operating infrastructure; hardware wallet makers whose only functions are manufacturing and selling the devices; unhosted wallet software providers whose only functions are developing and/or selling software or hardware; merchants only providing goods and services in exchange for Coins; software developers who undertake no VASP functions; and individual users. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Participants in the ecosystem who do not fall under the definition of VASPs in the FATF Standards include"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 92: controlling parties that qualify as VASPs should carry out ML/TF risk assessments before the platform is launched or used and should take appropriate risk mitigation measures. Paragraph 92 adds that one option is to effectively hand implementation of the AML/CFT obligations to another person, natural or legal, who is involved in the platform. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "controlling parties qualifying as VASPs should undertake ML/TF risk assessments prior to the launch or use of the platform"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Paragraph 93: the FATF recognises an expansive approach can bring practical challenges to competent authorities in identifying which entities are VASPs and defining the regulatory perimeter. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an expansive approach can bring practical challenges to competent authorities in identifying which entities are VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 93: where VASP status is unclear, general guiding questions include who profits from use of the service or asset, who established and can change the rules, who can make decisions affecting operations, who generated and drove creation and launch of the product or service, who maintains an ongoing business relationship with a contracting party or another person who possesses and controls the data on operations, and who could shut down the product or service. The list is not definitive and offers only some examples. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who profits from the use of the service or asset, who established and can change the rules"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 94: the FATF Standards, including the VA and VASP definitions, are a minimum standard for countries to introduce. In line with the RBA, countries may choose to extend their AML/CFT regimes to other digital assets and entities beyond the FATF definitions; such a determination should be made on the basis of an ML/TF risk assessment, with consideration of the challenges of regulatory divergence. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may choose to extend their AML/CFT regimes to include other digital assets and entities"</sub>
