## Correspondent banking and other similar relationships

- **expected / requirement**: For accounts or custodial wallets that customers of the other VASP or FI can use directly to transact on the customer's own behalf, a VASP in a cross-border correspondent relationship should be satisfied that the other VASP or FI has conducted CDD on those customers and can provide relevant CDD information on request, to the extent permitted by privacy and data protection regulations in both jurisdictions (item e of the list of correspondent measures). (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "be satisfied that the other VASP or FI has conducted CDD on such customers and is able to provide relevant CDD information on request"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 42 clarifies that one-off transactions, and a mere messaging relationship in the context of non-customer relationships, do not amount to correspondent relationships (paragraph 13 of the FATF Guidance on Correspondent Banking). (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One-off transactions or the mere messaging relationship in the context of non-customer relationships"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Cross-border correspondent relationships with VASPs located in jurisdictions with weak or non-existent AML/CFT regulation or supervision of VASPs will likely carry higher ML/TF risk. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cross-border correspondent relationships with VASPs in jurisdictions that have weak or non-existent AML/CFT regulation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: For domestic counterpart VASPs, the main difference from the cross-border case is that a VASP will likely be able to assume that other regulated VASPs in the same jurisdiction are subject to the same level of regulation and supervision. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs will likely be entitled to assume that other regulated VASPs in the same jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Correspondent relationships that include arrangements for services obscuring the existence of underlying customers and the nature of their transactions, or that involve a significant flow of transactions and execution of third-party payments, will also likely carry higher ML/TF risks. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "arrangements for providing services which obscure the existence of underlying customers and the nature of their transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In such higher-risk circumstances, performing focused transaction monitoring, with a clear understanding of the underlying customer attributes provided by the respondent VASP, would be an example of appropriate risk mitigation. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "performing focused transaction monitoring with the clear understanding of those underlying customer attributes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 13 does not apply to the domestic equivalent of correspondent relationships, but a VASP should undertake risk-based CDD under Recommendation 10 on a domestic counterpart VASP when conducting VA activities for or on its behalf. Such due diligence could, on a risk basis, include consideration of the existence and quality of the counterpart's AML/CTF controls and the services provided to underlying customers. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a VASP should undertake risk-based CDD under Recommendation 10 on a domestic counterpart VASP"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Considering the nature of VAs and VASPs, countries could recommend that VASPs document how they identify their respondent VASP as domestic or cross-border, since this is not as easy as it is for banks. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries could recommend that VASPs document how they identify their respondent VASP as domestic or cross-border"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Counterparty due diligence for complying with Recommendation 16 is distinct from obligations applicable to cross-border correspondent relationships. Unlike banking, VA transfers for or on behalf of another person can occur between VASPs even without a correspondent or any other relationship. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "counterparty due diligence for the purpose of complying with Recommendation 16 is distinct from the obligations applicable to cross-border correspondent relationships"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Where VASPs transfer VA without a correspondent relationship, the VASPs involved may undertake counterparty due diligence to ensure they can comply with the travel rule and apply measures to mitigate ML/TF risk. A correspondent relationship between two VASPs in a VA transfer may partly or wholly fulfil the counterparty due diligence requirements. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may, however, partly or wholly fulfil the requirements for counterparty due diligence"</sub>

## MVTS

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 14 directs countries to register or license natural or legal persons providing MVTS in the country and ensure their compliance with relevant AML/CFT measures, including subjecting MVTS to monitoring for compliance with registration or licensing and other AML/CFT measures (2015 VC Guidance). (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 14 directs countries to register or license natural or legal persons that provide MVTS in the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The registration and licensing requirements of Recommendation 15 apply to all VASPs, even those engaging in MVTS activities (for example domestic entities providing convertible VA exchange services between virtual and fiat currencies as a business). (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The registration and licensing requirements of Recommendation 15, however, apply to all VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A VASP that uses agents should be required to bring them within its AML/CFT programme and to monitor them for compliance with that programme. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a VASP that use agents should be required to include them in their AML/CFT programme"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Agents of a VASP should also be licensed or registered by a competent authority. Alternatively, the principal VASP should be required to keep a current list of its agents that is accessible to competent authorities in the countries where the principal VASP and its agents operate. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Agents should also be licensed or registered by a competent authority, or the principal VASP should be required"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Recommendation 14 also applies to agents of MVTS providers: any natural or legal person conducting MVTS activities on behalf of a VASP, by contract with or under the direction of the entity. The agent provides services to third parties on behalf of the principal, not VASP services to the principal. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any natural or legal person conducting MVTS activities on behalf of a VASP"</sub>

## New technologies

- **descriptive / context**: In October 2018 the FATF adopted updates to Recommendation 15 reinforcing the RBA and related obligations for countries and obliged entities regarding new technologies, clarifying application to VAs, covered VA financial activities and VASPs. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In October 2018, the FATF adopted updates to Recommendation 15"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 15 requires countries to identify and assess ML/TF risks relating to new products and business practices (including new delivery mechanisms) and the use of new or developing technologies for new and pre-existing products. It also requires countries to ensure FIs licensed by or operating in their jurisdiction take appropriate measures to manage and mitigate those risks before launching new products or business practices or using new or developing technologies (see Annex A). (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "take appropriate measures to manage and mitigate the associated ML/TF risks before launching new products or business practices"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The October 2018 update clarifies that countries should manage and mitigate risks emerging from VAs and ensure VASPs are regulated for AML/CFT, licensed or registered, and subject to effective systems for monitoring and ensuring compliance with relevant FATF measures. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should manage and mitigate the risks emerging from VAs and ensure that VASPs are regulated for AML/CFT purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INR. 15, adopted by the FATF in June 2019, defines more specifically how FATF requirements apply to VAs, covered VA activities and VASPs, including assessing ML/TF risks; licensing or registration; supervision or monitoring; preventive measures such as CDD, recordkeeping and suspicious transaction reporting; sanctions and other enforcement measures; and international co-operation (see Annex A). (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR. 15, which the FATF adopted in June 2019, further clarifies Recommendation 15"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure VASPs licensed by or operating in their jurisdiction can manage and mitigate risks of activities involving anonymity-enhancing technologies or mechanisms, including but not limited to AECs, mixers, tumblers, privacy wallets and other technologies obfuscating the identity of the sender, recipient, holder or beneficial owner of a VA. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including but not limited to AECs, mixers, tumblers, privacy wallets and other technologies that obfuscate the identity"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: If a VASP cannot manage and mitigate the risks posed by engaging in activities using anonymity-enhancing technologies, the VASP should not be permitted to engage in such activities. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "then the VASP should not be permitted to engage in such activities"</sub>

## Wire transfers and the 'travel rule'

- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 16 and INR. 16 were developed to prevent terrorists and other criminals from having unfettered access to electronically-facilitated funds transfers for moving funds and to detect such misuse. In line with the functional approach of the FATF Recommendations, the Recommendation 16 requirements apply to all providers of such services, including VASPs providing services, such as VA transfers, functionally analogous to wire transfers. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the requirements relating to wire transfers and related messages under Recommendation 16 apply to all providers of such services"</sub>

## Overview of R.16 and its application to VAs and VASPs

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 16 applies to both domestic and cross-border wire transfers. Countries should ensure FIs include required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information on wire transfers and related messages, monitor wire transfers to detect those lacking required originator and/or beneficiary information, and screen transactions to comply with relevant UNSCR resolutions (Recommendations 6 and 7). (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that FIs include required and accurate originator information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: As set out in INR. 15, countries should apply Recommendation 16 to VA transfers and VASPs, regardless of whether the value of the traditional wire transfer or VA transfer is denominated in fiat currency or a VA. Given the unique properties of VAs, Recommendation 16 applies in an amended way to VAs per paragraph 7(b) of INR. 15; this application is called the travel rule. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 16 applies in an amended way to VAs as set out in paragraph 7(b) of INR.15"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Recommendation 16 requirements apply to VASPs whenever their transactions, in fiat or VA, involve (a) a traditional wire transfer, (b) a VA transfer between a VASP and another obliged entity (for example two VASPs, or a VASP and a bank or other FI), or (c) a VA transfer between a VASP and a non-obliged entity (an unhosted wallet). The full requirements apply to (a) and (b) but not (c); for transfers involving unhosted wallets the requirements apply in a specific way. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The full requirements of Recommendation 16 apply to (a) and (b) but not (c), as set out below."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should treat all VA transfers as cross-border wire transfers, in accordance with INR. 1, rather than domestic wire transfers, based on the cross-border nature of VA activities and VASP operations. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should treat all VA transfers as cross-border wire transfers, in accordance with INR. 1"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Transaction fees relating to a VA transfer are not within scope of the travel rule, so VASPs do not need to identify the recipient of the transaction fee, who is not the originator or recipient of the VA transfer itself. Footnote 43 defines transaction fees as amounts of VA that may be collected by the miner who includes the transaction in a block (gas or block rewards by other names), also outside scope. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transaction fees43 relating to a VA transfer are not within scope of the travel rule."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Where technical reasons require a VASP to send a greater amount of VA than the amount to be transferred, with the difference automatically refunded to the ordering VASP, the travel rule does not apply to the recipient VASP in respect of the refund, as the refund forms part of the transfer by the ordering VASP. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with the difference automatically refunded to the ordering VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Recommendation 16 treats a wire transfer as any transaction made for an originator through a FI by electronic means so that funds become available to a beneficiary person at a beneficiary FI, whether or not the originator and beneficiary are the same person. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any transaction carried out on behalf of an originator through a FI by electronic means"</sub>

## Requirements to obtain, hold and submit required and accurate originator and required beneficiary information

- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure ordering institutions (VASP or other obliged entity such as a FI) in a VA transfer obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information, and submit it to beneficiary institutions, if any. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "involved in a VA transfer, _obtain_ and _hold_ required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure beneficiary institutions obtain and hold required (but not necessarily accurate) originator information and required and accurate beneficiary information, as set forth in INR. 16. Footnote 44: data accuracy is not required for the beneficiary VASP receiving originator information from an ordering VASP, which may assume the data has been verified by the ordering VASP. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtain and hold required (but not necessarily accurate"</sub>

- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 7(b) wording on Recommendation 16: countries should ensure that originating VASPs obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information on virtual asset transfers. 'Accurate' is used to describe information that has been verified for accuracy. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should ensure that originating VASPs obtain and hold required and accurate originator information"</sub>

## Interpretive Note to Recommendation 16

- **expected / expectation**: As reproduced from INR. 16 paragraph 6, information that accompanies all qualifying wire transfers should always contain: the originator's name; the originator's account number where an account is used to process the transaction; the originator's address, or national identity number, or customer identification number, or date and place of birth; the beneficiary's name; and the beneficiary's account number where an account is used to process the transaction. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information accompanying all qualifying wire transfers should always contain"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The ordering institution must obtain and hold, as required information: the originator's name (the sending person's accurate, i.e. verified, full name); the originator's account number where used to process the transaction (in the VA context, potentially the wallet address); and the originator's physical address, or national identity number, or customer identification number (not a transaction number) uniquely identifying the originator to the ordering institution, or date and place of birth. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "originator’s name ( _i.e._ , the sending person’s accurate (i.e. verified) full name)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For transmitting the originator's geographical address, the address must be one verified for accuracy by the originator VASP as part of its KYC process (see paragraphs 148-160 on CDD). (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the address which has been verified for accuracy by the originator VASP as part of its KYC process"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The ordering institution must also obtain and hold the beneficiary's name (the person identified by the originator as receiver of the VA transfer) and the beneficiary account number where used to process the transaction (potentially the wallet address). The beneficiary name is not required to be verified for accuracy by the ordering institution but should be reviewed for STR monitoring and sanction screening. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is not required to be verified by the ordering institution for accuracy, but should be reviewed for the purpose of STR monitoring and sanction screening"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The beneficiary institution must obtain from the originator institution and hold the originator's name, account number where used (potentially the wallet address), and physical address or national identity number or customer identification number (not a transaction number) or date and place of birth. It does not need to verify the originator's name for accuracy but should review it for STR monitoring and sanction screening. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The beneficiary institution does not need to be verify the originator’s name for accuracy"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The beneficiary institution must verify the beneficiary's name for accuracy if its customer's name has not previously been verified, so it can confirm that the beneficiary name and account number obtained from the ordering institution match its verified customer data. It must also obtain and hold the beneficiary's account number where used (potentially the wallet address). (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The beneficiary institution must verify the beneficiary’s name for accuracy, if the name of their customer has not previously verified."</sub>

(p. 61)[^original]

- **descriptive / context**: Any technology or software solution is acceptable provided it enables ordering and beneficiary institutions to comply with Recommendation 16 and does not impede their ability to comply with other AML/CFT obligations under the FATF Recommendations. (p. 62)[^original]

## Sanctions screening for VA transfers

- **expected / requirement**: Countries should require both ordering and beneficiary institutions to take freezing actions and prohibit transactions with designated persons and entities, that is, to screen and hold required VA transfer information in order to comply with targeted financial sanctions obligations. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require both ordering and beneficiary institutions to take freezing actions and prohibit transactions with designated persons"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The ordering and beneficiary institutions should have screened their customer's name for targeted financial sanctions compliance at onboarding (and upon name changes), and must then screen the names of the other party (originator or beneficiary) when they conduct the VA transfer (see Table 1). (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They must then screen the names of the other party (the originator or the beneficiary) when they conduct the VA transfer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should require VASPs or other obliged entities to implement an effective control framework enabling compliance with targeted financial sanction obligations, taking into account the nature of VA transfers. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require VASPs or other obliged entities to implement an effective control framework"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Examples of controls a VASP or other obliged entity could implement include putting a wallet on hold until screening is completed and confirmed that no concern is raised, and arranging to receive a VA transfer with a provider's wallet linked to a customer's wallet and moving the VA to the customer's wallet only after screening has confirmed no concern. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "putting a wallet on hold until screening is completed and confirmed that no concern is raised"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because the information identifying the originator and beneficiary can be held separately from the VA transfer system (for example, the blockchain), a VA transfer can be completed even if that information is missing or the transfer has not been screened for suspicious and prohibited transactions. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the VA transfer can be completed even with such information missing or without screening the transfer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Given this, VASPs or other obliged entities should screen the required VA transfer information separately from the direct settlement of the transfer. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs or other obliged entities should screen required VA transfer information separately to such direct settlement"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: VASPs may need to consider mitigation measures that fit their business process and the technical nature of VAs. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs may need to consider mitigation measures that fit their business process and the technical nature of VAs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should be aware that VA transfers differ from traditional fiat wire transfers and should require VASPs and other obliged entities to document their remediation control action to facilitate effective supervision. Countries could potentially ask obliged entities to document this control in their AML/CFT risk assessment. (p. 64)[^original]
