## PART SIX: PRINCIPLES OF INFORMATION-SHARING AND CO-OPERATION AMONGST VASP SUPERVISORS

- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 states that countries should rapidly, constructively and effectively provide the widest possible range of international co-operation on money laundering, predicate offences and terrorist financing relating to VAs, on the basis set out in Recommendations 37 to 40. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should rapidly, constructively, and effectively provide the widest possible range of international co-operation"</sub>

## Objectives

- **mandatory / requirement**: Each country must designate at least one competent authority as its supervisor of VASPs for AML/CFT purposes. The FATF Standards do not specify who that authority should be, other than that it cannot be a self-regulatory body (SRB). (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Each country must designate at least one competent authority as their supervisor of VASPs for AML/CFT purposes."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Countries have designated a range of authorities as VASP supervisors, including financial services supervisors, central banks, securities regulators, tax authorities, FIUs and specialist VASP supervisors. Some countries have a single supervisor and others multiple. Some treat VASPs as a clearly identifiable specific category of business, while others treat them as a sub-set of existing categories (for example money service businesses). (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries have designated a range of different authorities as VASP supervisors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FATF Standards make clear that supervisors should exchange information promptly and constructively with foreign counterparts. Given the pseudonymous, fast-paced, cross-border nature of VAs, international co-operation between VASP supervisors is all the more critical, so FATF developed Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation between VASP Supervisors to facilitate co-operation and exchange of relevant information. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Given the pseudonymous, fast-paced, cross-border nature of VAs, international co-operation is all the more critical"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One aim of the Principles is to outline possible triggers for proactive information sharing or requests, for example a cybersecurity incident with potential AML/CFT impact on other jurisdictions, or a foreign-based VASP potentially conducting unregulated VASP activity in a jurisdiction. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "outline possible triggers for proactive information sharing/requests"</sub>

## PART SIX: PRINCIPLES OF INFORMATION-SHARING AND CO-OPERATION AMONGST VASP SUPERVISORS

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 352 of the FATF guidance says that supervisors of VASPs should exchange information with their foreign counterparts promptly and constructively, whatever the supervisors' nature or status and whatever the differences in the naming or status of VASPs. It adds that Section III covers how Recommendations 37 to 40 apply to VAs. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors of VASPs should exchange information promptly and constructively with their foreign counterparts"</sub>

## Objectives

- **descriptive / context**: One intended purpose of the Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation between VASP Supervisors is to give authorities a shared understanding of the kinds of supervisory information and other background knowledge that are useful to share with each other, and of when to share it. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide a common understanding of the type of supervisory information and other background knowledge"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One aim of the Principles is to set out possible methods of sharing and types of supervisory assistance or feedback that could be adopted in certain circumstances, in line with confidentiality provisions. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "set out possible methods of sharing and types of supervisory assistance/feedback that could be adopted"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One aim of the Principles is to set out possible roles and expectations where multiple AML/CFT supervisors are working together on a specific case or issue. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "set out possible roles and expectations where multiple AML/CFT supervisors are working together"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One aim of the Principles is to suggest possible guidelines for jurisdictions dealing with VASPs in jurisdictions that lack regulatory frameworks, or seeking to facilitate supervisory co-operation for multijurisdictional VASPs. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "suggest possible guidelines for jurisdictions, when dealing with issues with VASPs in jurisdictions that do not have regulatory frameworks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One aim of the Principles is to set out best practice on the types of information countries should maintain on licensed or registered VASPs, as part of their directories or websites. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "set out best practice in relation to the types of information countries should maintain on licensed/registered VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The Principles are non-binding guidance for supervisors. Supervisors are not obliged to adopt them, nor to share information or render co-operation unless it is consistent with their domestic frameworks (which could condition co-operation on legal arrangements such as Memorandums of Understanding) and does not contradict obligations arising from R. 37-40. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These Principles are non-binding on supervisors. They are intended as guidance for supervisors."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Principles apply to all countries, whether they permit or prohibit VASPs. Countries that prohibit VASPs must have a legal basis permitting their relevant competent authorities to exchange information on issues related to virtual assets and VASPs; that authority may not be a supervisor and could be, for example, a law enforcement agency. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries that prohibit VASPs must have a legal basis for permitting their relevant competent authorities to exchange information"</sub>

## Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation

- **expected / expectation**: International co-operation between supervisors should be encouraged and based on mutual trust. Information-sharing arrangements must recognize and allow room for case-by-case solutions to specific problems. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International co-operation between Supervisors should be encouraged and based upon a foundation of mutual trust."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 2: supervisors should have a clear mechanism by which to receive inquiries relating to VASPs, for example a specific email address for VASP-related inquiries. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should have a clear mechanism by which to receive inquiries relating to VASPs."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 3: to facilitate timely co-operation, supervisors should ensure that information on licensed or registered VASPs under their purview is readily accessible by foreign authorities, for example through public registers of obliged entities or a licensed/registered entities database that can be queried through secure information exchange. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on licensed or registered VASPs under their purview is readily accessible by foreign authorities"</sub>

## Information exchange

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 4: supervisors should exchange relevant information on VASPs with foreign Supervisors regardless of their status, and to this end should have an adequate legal basis for providing co-operation on money laundering, associated predicate offences and the financing of terrorism. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should exchange relevant information on VASPs with foreign Supervisors, regardless of their status."</sub>

## Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation

- **mandatory / requirement**: Principle 1 states that countries must clearly identify their Supervisor(s) of VASPs for AML/CFT purposes. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries must clearly identify their Supervisor(s) of VASPs for AML/CFT purposes."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Principle 1, where a country has several Supervisors, it should clearly identify the scope of each Supervisor's regulatory remit. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where a country has multiple Supervisors, the country should clearly identify the scope"</sub>

## Information exchange

- **permissive / recommendation**: Principle 5 observes that, given the cross-border nature of VASPs, developing supervisory colleges for larger multilateral VASPs could help improve overall AML/CFT supervision of those entities. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the development of supervisory colleges for larger multilateral VASPs could serve to enhance overall AML/CFT supervision"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Under Principle 6, the types of information exchanged between supervisors depend on factors such as the trigger for the exchange, the statutory and/or blockchain data held by the Supervisor providing assistance, and countries' domestic legal frameworks. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The types of information exchanged between supervisors would depend on a range of factors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 6 says that, where available and legally permitted, supervisors should provide relevant information such as a VASP's regulatory status, details of its shareholders and directors, transaction-related data and user information, which could have come from supervisory activities, statutory returns, and blockchain surveillance and analytical tools. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where available and legally permitted, supervisors should provide where relevant, information such as a VASP’s regulatory status"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 7 says a Supervisor requesting information should tell the Supervisor handling the request why it is asking and, as far as possible, what the information will be used for, and should give enough information for the receiving Supervisor to respond lawfully. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A Supervisor requesting information should disclose, to the Supervisor that will process the request, the reason for the request"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Principle 10 states that information received, processed, held or disseminated by requesting Supervisors must be securely protected, and exchanged and used only in accordance with agreed procedures, policies and applicable laws and regulations. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information received, processed, held or disseminated by requesting Supervisors must be securely protected, exchanged and used only"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Principle 5: supervisory information could be exchanged by several methods. Most typically it is exchanged bilaterally on request from one Supervisor to another. Where VASPs are multilateral in nature, supervisors may share information multilaterally with all other regulators of the VASP. Less sensitive supervisory information could be shared as necessary at supervisory colleges organized by lead supervisors of multilateral VASPs. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most typically, information could be exchanged bilaterally, upon request from one Supervisor to another."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Under Principle 6, supervisors should consider exercising their supervisory powers to obtain further information from the VASP, where necessary. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should also consider exercising its supervisory powers to obtain further information from the VASP, where necessary."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 8: supervisors should acknowledge receipt of requests, respond to requests for information, and provide interim partial or negative responses in a timely manner. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should acknowledge receipt of requests, respond to requests for information, and provide interim partial or negative responses"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 9: supervisors should not prohibit or place unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions on exchanging information or providing assistance. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should not prohibit or place unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions on exchanging information or providing assistance."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Principle 9, supervisors should not refuse a request for assistance on the ground that laws require FIs, DNFBPs or VASPs to maintain secrecy or confidentiality, except where the information sought is held under circumstances where legal privilege or legal professional secrecy applies. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "except where the relevant information that is sought is held under circumstances where legal privilege or legal professional secrecy applies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Principle 9, supervisors should not refuse a request for assistance on the ground that there is an inquiry, investigation or proceeding underway in the country receiving the request, unless the assistance would impede that inquiry, investigation or proceeding. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is an inquiry, investigation or proceeding underway in the country receiving the request, unless the assistance would impede"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Principle 9, supervisors should not refuse a request for assistance on the ground that the nature or status of the requesting counterpart authority differs from that of its foreign Supervisor. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nature or status of the requesting counterpart authority is different to its foreign Supervisor"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 11: exchanged information should be used only for the purpose for which it was sought or provided. Any dissemination to other authorities or third parties, or any use for administrative, investigative, prosecutorial or judicial purposes beyond those originally approved, should be subject to prior authorization by the requested Supervisor. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Exchanged information should be used only for the purpose for which the information was sought or provided."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 11 says that, at a minimum, the requesting financial supervisor should promptly tell the requested Supervisor about any legal obligation it has to disclose or report the information to a third party. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the requesting financial supervisor should promptly inform the requested Supervisor of its legal obligation to disclose or report"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 12 says supervisors should raise material issues and concerns with other Supervisors proactively, and should respond in a timely and satisfactory manner when others raise such issues and concerns with them. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should be proactive in raising material issues and concerns with other Supervisors"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Principle 13: supervisors should consider proactively sharing or requesting information as necessary to carry out their supervisory functions. Possible triggers include a cybersecurity incident at a local VASP with potential AML/CFT impact on other jurisdictions. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should consider proactively sharing information or requesting information as necessary to carry out their supervisory functions."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Further possible triggers under Principle 13 are a foreign-based VASP potentially conducting unregulated VASP activity in a jurisdiction, and a local VASP strongly suspected of facilitating illicit ML/TF activity that has substantial operations based in foreign jurisdictions. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a local VASP is strongly suspected to be facilitating illicit ML/TF activity, and has substantial operations based in foreign jurisdictions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 14: upon request and whenever possible, supervisors should give foreign counterparts feedback on the use of the information provided and on the outcome of the analysis conducted based on it. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Upon request and whenever possible, Supervisors should provide feedback to their foreign counterparts on the use of the information provided"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 15: supervisors should communicate emerging issues and developments of a material and potentially adverse nature, including supervisory actions, to other relevant Supervisors of the VASP in a timely manner. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should communicate emerging issues and developments of a material and potentially adverse nature"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 16: supervisors should share with other relevant Supervisors of the VASP information affecting the regulated entity for which they have responsibility, including supervisory actions, except in unusual circumstances when supervisory considerations dictate otherwise. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "except in unusual circumstances when supervisory considerations dictate otherwise"</sub>

## Co-operation

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 19: supervisors should be able to conduct queries on behalf of foreign Supervisors and exchange with them all information that they would be able to obtain if such queries were carried out domestically. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should be able to conduct queries on behalf of foreign Supervisors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Principle 17, a primary Supervisor could be identified where a VASP has a significant proportion of its business operations in one jurisdiction, and supervisors should work together to identify a primary Supervisor who could be the focal point for co-ordinating information sharing and co-operation. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should work together to identify a primary Supervisor who could act as a focal point"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Identifying a primary Supervisor is not mandatory and does not relieve other Supervisors of their responsibility to supervise the VASP in their own jurisdictions. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such identification is not mandatory and does not absolve other Supervisors of the responsibility to supervise the VASP"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 18 says supervisors should use the most efficient means of co-operating. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should use the most efficient means to co-operate."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where bilateral or multilateral agreements or arrangements such as a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) are needed, they should be negotiated and signed in a timely way with the widest possible range of foreign Supervisors, in the context of international co-operation against money laundering, associated predicate offences and terrorist financing. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these should be negotiated and signed in a timely way with the widest possible range of foreign Supervisors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Principle 20: when requesting co-operation, supervisors should make best efforts to provide complete, factual and, as appropriate, legal information including a description of the case, and should indicate any need for urgency to enable timely and efficient execution. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should make their best efforts to provide complete, factual and, as appropriate, legal information"</sub>

## Annex A. Recommendation 15 and its Interpretive Note and FATF Definitions

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 15 states that countries and financial institutions should identify and assess ML/TF risks arising from (a) development of new products and new business practices, including new delivery mechanisms, and (b) use of new or developing technologies for new and pre-existing products. For financial institutions, the risk assessment should take place prior to launch of the new products, practices or technologies, and they should take appropriate measures to manage and mitigate those risks. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such a risk assessment should take place prior to the launch of the new products"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 15, to manage and mitigate risks emerging from virtual assets, countries should ensure that VASPs are regulated for AML/CFT purposes, licensed or registered, and subject to effective systems for monitoring and ensuring compliance with the relevant FATF Recommendation measures. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that virtual asset service providers are regulated for AML/CFT purposes, and licensed or registered"</sub>

## Interpretative Note to Recommendation 15

- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 1: for applying the FATF Recommendations, countries should consider virtual assets as "property," "proceeds," "funds," "funds or other assets," or other "corresponding value", and should apply the relevant FATF measures to virtual assets and VASPs. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider virtual assets as “property,” “proceeds,” “funds,” “funds or other assets,”"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 2: in accordance with Recommendation 1, countries should identify, assess and understand the ML/TF risks from virtual asset activities and VASP operations, and apply a risk-based approach so that preventive or mitigating measures are commensurate with the risks identified. Countries should require VASPs to identify, assess and take effective action to mitigate their ML/TF risks. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require VASPs to identify, assess, and take effective action to mitigate their money laundering and terrorist financing risks."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 3: VASPs should be required to be licensed or registered. At a minimum, they should be licensed or registered in the jurisdiction(s) where they are created; where the VASP is a natural person, in the jurisdiction where its place of business is located. Footnote 1 notes that references to creating a legal person include incorporation of companies or any other mechanism used. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At a minimum, VASPs should be required to be licensed or registered in the jurisdiction(s) where they are created."</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 3: jurisdictions may also require VASPs that offer products or services to customers in, or conduct operations from, their jurisdiction to be licensed or registered there. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions may also require VASPs that offer products and/or services to customers in, or conduct operations from, their jurisdiction"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 4: a country need not impose a separate licensing or registration system on natural or legal persons already licensed or registered as financial institutions in that country, which under that license are permitted to perform VASP activities and are already subject to the full range of applicable obligations under the FATF Recommendations. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A country need not impose a separate licensing or registration system with respect to natural or legal persons"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The same INR. 15 paragraph says countries should act to identify natural or legal persons that carry out VASP activities without the requisite licence or registration, and apply appropriate sanctions. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should take action to identify natural or legal persons that carry out VASP activities without the requisite license"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 5: countries should ensure that VASPs are subject to adequate regulation and supervision or monitoring for AML/CFT, and effectively implement the relevant FATF Recommendations. VASPs should be supervised or monitored by a competent authority (not a SRB), which should conduct risk-based supervision or monitoring. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should be supervised or monitored by a competent authority (not a SRB), which should conduct risk-based supervision or monitoring."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 5: supervisors should have adequate powers to supervise or monitor and ensure VASP compliance, including authority to conduct inspections, compel production of information and impose sanctions, including a range of disciplinary and financial sanctions and the power to withdraw, restrict or suspend the VASP's license or registration, where applicable. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the authority to conduct inspections, compel the production of information, and impose sanctions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 6: countries should ensure a range of effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, whether criminal, civil or administrative, is available for VASPs that fail to comply with AML/CFT requirements, in line with Recommendation 35. Sanctions should apply not only to VASPs but also to their directors and senior management. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sanctions should be applicable not only to VASPs, but also to their directors and senior management."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 7: the preventive measures in Recommendations 10 to 21 apply to VASPs, subject to qualifications set out in the following paragraphs. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the requirements set out in Recommendations 10 to 21 apply to VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 8(a), on R.10: the occasional transactions designated threshold above which VASPs are required to conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The occasional transactions designated threshold above which VASPs are required to conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 9(b), on R.16: countries should ensure originating VASPs obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information on virtual asset transfers, submit it to the beneficiary VASP or financial institution (if any) immediately and securely, and make it available on request to appropriate authorities. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "originating VASPs obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 9(b): the other R.16 requirements (including monitoring availability of information, and taking freezing action and prohibiting transactions with designated persons and entities) apply on the same basis as in R.16. The same obligations apply to financial institutions when sending or receiving virtual asset transfers on behalf of a customer. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The same obligations apply to financial institutions when sending or receiving virtual asset transfers on behalf of a customer."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 3 to INR. 15 states that required information can be submitted either directly or indirectly, and need not be attached directly to virtual asset transfers. Footnote 2 states that the beneficiary information is as defined in INR. 16 paragraph 6, or the equivalent information in a virtual asset context. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The information can be submitted either directly or indirectly."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR. 15 paragraph 10: countries should rapidly, constructively and effectively provide the widest possible range of international co-operation on ML, predicate offences and TF relating to virtual assets, on the basis of Recommendations 37 to 40; VASP supervisors should exchange information promptly and constructively with foreign counterparts regardless of the supervisors' nature or status and differences in the nomenclature or status of VASPs. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors of VASPs should exchange information promptly and constructively with their foreign counterparts, regardless of the supervisors’ nature or status"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF definition describes a virtual asset as a digital representation of value that can be traded or transferred digitally and used for payment or investment purposes. It excludes digital representations of fiat currencies, securities and other financial assets already covered elsewhere in the FATF Recommendations. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A virtual asset is a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded, or transferred"</sub>
