## Sanctions screening for VA transfers

- **descriptive / context**: A VA transfer can be directly settled through distributed consensus on the blockchain between wallet addresses alone, without an intermediary. For a VASP to transmit required information to another VASP, however, it must identify its counterparty VASP. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A VA transfer can be directly settled, i.e. through distributed consensus on the blockchain between wallet addresses alone"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A VASP would also need to conduct due diligence on its counterparty VASP before transmitting the required information, to avoid dealing with illicit or sanctioned actors unknowingly. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conduct due diligence on their counterparty VASP before they transmit the required information to avoid dealing with illicit actors or sanctioned actors unknowingly"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs do not need to undertake counterparty VASP due diligence for every individual VA transfer with VASPs for which they have previously conducted it, unless there is a suspicious transaction history or other information (such as adverse media or published information about regulatory or criminal penalties) indicating they should. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs do not need to undertake the counterparty VASP due diligence process for every individual VA transfer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should expect a VASP to refresh its counterparty due diligence information periodically or when risk emerges from the relationship, in line with its defined RBA control structure, and should expect their obliged VASPs to implement this control mechanism. VASPs should use the process to determine whether a counterpart can reasonably be expected to protect the confidentiality of information shared with it. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should expect a VASP to refresh their counterparty due diligence information periodically or when risk emerges from the relationship"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Counterparty due diligence has broadly three phases, which are not prescriptive actions but guidance on how it could be undertaken. Phase 1: determine whether the VA transfer is with a counterparty VASP, as opposed to an unhosted wallet. This determination arises from the technology underpinning VAs rather than being purely an AML/CFT requirement. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These are not intended as prescriptive actions that VASPs must take, but guidance on how counterparty due diligence could be undertaken"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Phase 2: identify the counterparty VASP, as after Phase 1 a VASP only knows the 'name' of the counterparty. A VASP may identify a counterparty VASP itself using a reliable database in line with any country guidelines on when to rely on such data. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A VASP may identify a counterparty VASP themselves using a reliable database"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 196 of the FATF guidance says FATF expects countries to implement paragraph 7(b) of INR. 15, bearing in mind the unique nature of VA transfers and the future control framework in the private sector for travel rule solutions (see Recommendation 16 in Section IV). (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF expects countries to implement paragraph 7(b) of INR. 15, taking into account the unique nature of VA transfers"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: In describing Phase 1 of counterparty due diligence, the guidance states that FATF knows of no technically proven means of exhaustively, precisely and accurately identifying, in all circumstances and from the VA address alone, the VASP that manages a beneficiary wallet. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF is not aware of any technically proven means of identifying the VASP that manages the beneficiary wallet"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: A country does not need to impose a separate licensing or registration system for VASPs on natural or legal persons that are already licensed or registered as FIs in that country. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A country need not impose a separate licensing or registration system for VASPs with respect to natural or legal persons"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries with such frameworks may tell their private sector that these FIs might not appear on designated VASP lists, or might not be under the same regulator's supervision, so as to avoid unnecessary de-risking. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries that have such frameworks may clarify to their private sector that such FIs might not be on"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: To clarify the scope of the Guidance, competent authorities should require VASPs to implement Phase 3 preventive measures to assess the counterparty VASP where VASPs first have a business relationship, and then review the due diligence results periodically. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "competent authorities should require VASPs to implement preventive measures in ‘Phase 3’ to assess the counterparty VASP"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should maintain reliable, independent sources of information for Phase 2 to help VASPs identify the counterparty VASP. This could include regulated institutions lists such as VASP lists where available, registries of beneficial ownership where available, and other examples in the BCBS Guideline. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also maintain reliable, independent sources of information for ‘Phase 2’"</sub>

Given increased digitalized processes, countries should be encouraged to use a machine-readable format. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should be encouraged to use a format that is machine-readable"</sub>

## Figure 1. Overview of generalised counterparty VASP due diligence process

- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 49 quotes BCBS (2014, rev. 2020) Annex 2 paragraph 21: banks should also consider gathering information from public sources, such as the respondent bank's supervisory authority website (for cross-checking identification data with licensing information or AML/CFT administrative sanctions) and public registries.   <sub>anchor: "Banks should also consider gathering information from public sources."</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Originating entities may wish to require travel rule compliance from their beneficiaries, by contract"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Subject to their own discretion and ML/TF risk assessment, jurisdictions may also consider treating all VASPs from countries that do not effectively implement licensing or registration requirements as high-risk. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions may also consider designating all VASPs from countries which do not effectively implement licensing or registration requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should clarify that VASPs should make a risk-based decision on whom to transact with, acknowledging that risk mitigating measures taken by each VASP may vary. Business decisions are generally made by each VASP based on its AML/CFT risk analysis and other compliance issues, including data storage and security and the profitability of the relationship. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should also clarify that VASPs should make a risk-based decision on whom to transact with"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Countries implement AML/CFT frameworks for VASPs at different paces, so some jurisdictions will require VASPs to comply with the travel rule before others (the 'sunrise issue'). This is a challenge for VASPs in deciding how to deal with VASPs in jurisdictions where the travel rule is not yet in force. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some jurisdictions will require their VASPs to comply with the travel rule prior to other jurisdictions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Regardless of lack of regulation in the beneficiary jurisdiction, originating entities can require travel rule compliance from beneficiaries by contract or business practice. The level of compliance a VASP implements with INR. 15 paragraph 7(b) should form part of risk-based business decisions, and VASPs and FIs should take into account the ML/TF risk of each customer/counterparty VASP and any risk mitigation measures it has implemented. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs and FIs should take into account the level of ML/TF risk of each individual customer/counterparty VASP"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Given the sunrise issue, countries should adopt a RBA in assessing VASP business models and should consider the full context of travel rule compliance, including whether the VASP has taken sufficient risk mitigation measures to manage the attendant ML/TF risks. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should adopt a RBA in the assessment of the business models presented by VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Examples of robust VASP control measures regardless of national regulation include restricting VA transfers to within the VASP's customer base (internal transfers within the same VASP), only allowing confirmed first-party transfers outside the customer base (originator and beneficiary confirmed to be the same person), and enhanced monitoring of transactions. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only allowing confirmed first-party transfers outside of their customer base"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: While introducing relevant regulations is important in itself, the absence of relevant regulations in one country does not necessarily preclude the effectiveness of measures a VASP introduces on its own. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the absence of relevant regulations in one country does not necessarily preclude the effectiveness of measures introduced by a VASP on its own"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FATF expects countries to put paragraph 7(b) of INR. 15 into effect as soon as possible. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF expects countries to implement paragraph 7(b) of INR.15 as soon as possible."</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Countries may wish to take a staged approach to enforcement of travel rule requirements"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "should continue to ensure that VASPs have alternative measures in place to suitably mitigate the ML/TF risks"</sub>

## VA transfers to/from 'intermediary VASPs'

- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure that intermediary institutions (VASPs or other obliged entities) facilitating VA transfers as an intermediate element in a chain also comply with Recommendation 16 as set forth in INR. 15, including treatment of all VA transfers as cross-border qualifying transfers. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should ensure that such intermediary institutions (whether a VASP or other obliged entity) also comply with the requirements of Recommendation 16"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: As a traditional intermediary FI must ensure required originator and beneficiary information accompanying a cross-border wire transfer is retained with it, an intermediary VASP or comparable intermediary institution must ensure the required information is transmitted along the chain of VA transfers, maintain necessary records and make the information available to appropriate authorities upon request. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "so too must an intermediary VASP or other comparable intermediary institution that facilitates VA transfers ensure that the required information is transmitted"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Intermediary institutions in VA transfers also have general obligations to identify suspicious transactions, take freezing actions and prohibit transactions with designated persons and entities, like ordering and beneficiary VASPs. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have general obligations to identify suspicious transactions, take freezing actions, and prohibit transactions with designated persons and entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 50: when a VASP, FI or other intermediary obliged entity facilitates a VA transfer as an intermediate element in a chain of VA transfers, and the activity or business has been classified as a VASP in the Guidance, it is classified as an 'intermediary VASP'. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "then they would be classified as an “intermediary VASP”"</sub>

## VA transfers to/from unhosted wallets

- **expected / requirement**: Unlike traditional fiat wire transfers, not every VA transfer involves two obliged entities. Where only one obliged entity is on either end (for example an ordering VASP sends VAs to an individual VA user's unhosted wallet), countries should still ensure that obliged entity adheres to Recommendation 16 with respect to its customer (the originator or beneficiary, as the case may be). (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should still ensure that the obliged entity adheres to the requirements of Recommendation 16 with respect to their customer"</sub>

## VA transfers to/from ‘intermediary VASPs’

- **expected / requirement**: Where technical limitations prevent the required originator or beneficiary information from staying with a required data submission, the receiving intermediary VASP should keep a record, for at least five years, of all the information it received from the ordering VASP or another intermediary VASP. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a record should be kept, for at least five years, by the receiving intermediary VASP"</sub>

## VA transfers to/from unhosted wallets

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF does not expect VASPs and FIs that originate a VA transfer to submit the required information to individuals who are not obliged entities. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF does not expect that VASPs and FIs, when originating a VA transfer, to submit the required information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs that send or receive a VA transfer to or from an entity that is not a VASP or other obliged entity (for example, from an individual VA user to an unhosted wallet) should obtain the required originator and beneficiary information from their customer. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should obtain the required originator and beneficiary information from their customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should require VASPs or other obliged entities to implement mechanisms ensuring effective scrutiny of transfers with unhosted wallets, in particular to meet STR and sanctions implementation obligations (see Recommendation 20), and may choose to impose additional limitations or controls on such transfers. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require their VASPs or other obliged entities to implement mechanisms to ensure effective scrutiny of such transfers"</sub>

## Reliance on third parties

- **permissive / requirement**: Recommendation 17 allows countries to permit obliged entities to rely on third parties to introduce business and/or perform part of the CDD process, including identification and verification of customers' identities. The third party must be a regulated entity supervised and monitored for AML/CFT by competent authorities, with measures for CDD and record-keeping compliance. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 17 allows countries to permit obliged entities to rely on third parties to introduce business"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Reliance on a third party will not relieve the obliged entity of its obligations or responsibility in the event of a breach. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reliance on a third party will not relieve the obliged entity of its obligations or responsibility in the event of a breach"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries may permit VASPs to act as third parties in accordance with their status under Recommendation 15. Besides checking the third party's regulated status, obliged entities should select on a risk basis, considering risks the third party may pose, the nature of its business, its customer groups or target markets and its business partners, where relevant. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obliged entities should conduct their selection on a risk basis"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where a VASP relies on another VASP for business introduction or CDD, the VASP-to-VASP reliance, particularly for VA transfers, should occur in a manner consistent and compliant with Recommendation 16. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should occur in a manner consistent and compliant with the requirements of Recommendation 16"</sub>

## Internal controls and foreign branches and subsidiaries

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 18 requires countries to require obliged entities such as VASPs to have internal controls to establish the effectiveness of AML/CFT policies and processes and quality of risk management across operations, departments, branches and subsidiaries, domestically and where relevant abroad. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 18 requires countries to require obliged entities, such as VASPs, to have internal controls in place"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Internal controls should include appropriate governance arrangements with AML/CFT responsibility clearly allocated and a compliance officer appointed at management level; controls to monitor staff integrity under applicable local legislation; ongoing staff training; and an (external or internal) independent audit function to test the system. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a compliance officer is appointed at management level"</sub>

## Higher risk countries

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 20 requires all FIs that suspect or have reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are proceeds of crime or related to TF to report their suspicions promptly to the relevant FIU. Countries should accordingly ensure VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in covered VA activities file STRs (see Section IV). (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that VASPs as well as any other obliged entities that engage in covered VA activities file STRs"</sub>

## STRs and tipping-off

- **expected / requirement**: Consistent with paragraph 7 of INR. 15, countries should require VASPs to comply with all relevant requirements of Recommendation 16 in the countries in which they operate (see Section IV). (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries also should require VASPs to comply with all of the relevant requirements of"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should also apply the tipping-off and confidentiality measures of Recommendation 21 to VASPs, as set out in paragraph 7 of INR. 15. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also apply such measures to VASPs, as set forth in paragraph 7 of INR. 15"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 21, VASPs and, where applicable, their directors, officers and employees should be shielded by law from criminal and civil liability for breaching any restriction on disclosing information, and prohibited by law from disclosing (tipping-off) STRs. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be protected by law from criminal and civil liability for breach of any restriction on disclosure of information"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In some jurisdictions with comprehensive AML/CFT obligations for VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities, STRs referencing VAs have proven invaluable in furthering law enforcement investigations and improving the FIU's ability to understand and analyse providers and activities in the VA ecosystem. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "STRs that reference VAs have proven invaluable in furthering law enforcement investigative efforts"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Footnote 51: STRs filed by both depository institutions and VASPs (specifically exchangers) enabled U.S. law enforcement to act in 2017 against BTC-e, an Internet-based money transmitter that exchanged fiat and VAs and facilitated transactions involving ransomware, computer hacking, identity theft, tax fraud, public corruption and drug trafficking, by helping identify VA wallet addresses used by BTC-e and detect illicit streams moving through the exchange. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enabled U.S. law enforcement to take action in 2017 against BTC-e"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Although VASPs are not required to submit verified required information on the beneficiary, a VASP may suspect the accuracy of data it processes from discrepancies it notes. These could be identified with the support of blockchain analytic tools, information from its counterparty VASP or external authorities, or its transaction history and records. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there could be the situation where a VASP has suspicion on the accuracy of data it processes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Discrepancies due to inaccurate or incomplete information from a customer (for originator VASPs) or from originator VASPs (for beneficiary VASPs) should be evaluated together with transactions requested by or related to the same customer to understand whether suspicions arise. Such recognition could be highly valuable information for FIUs, LEAs and investigators, so countries should require VASPs to implement mechanisms for effective scrutiny of suspicious transactions and to meet sanctions implementation requirements. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this should be evaluated together with the transactions requested or related to the same customer in order to understand if suspicions arise"</sub>

## Transparency and Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons and Arrangements

As Recommendations 24 and 25 note that countries should prevent misuse of legal persons and arrangements for ML/TF, countries should take measures to prevent misuse of VASPs and consider measures to facilitate access to beneficial ownership and control information by VASPs undertaking Recommendations 10 and 22. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should therefore take measures to prevent the misuse of VASPs and consider measures to facilitate access to beneficial ownership"</sub>

(p. 70)[^original]

## International Co-operation

- **descriptive / expectation**: Given the cross-border and mobile nature of VA activities, international co-operation and implementation of Recommendations 36 through 40 (particularly 37 through 40) is critical. Effective implementation also limits providers in one jurisdiction having an unfair competitive advantage over providers in more regulated jurisdictions, and limits jurisdiction shopping or hopping or regulatory arbitrage. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "limit jurisdiction shopping or hopping or regulatory arbitrage"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 8 of INR. 15 underscores applying Recommendations 37 through 40 to mitigate VA risks. Countries should have tools to co-operate and provide mutual legal assistance (Recommendation 37); help identify, freeze, seize and confiscate proceeds and instrumentalities of crime that may take the form of VAs (Recommendation 38); and provide effective extradition assistance in VA-related crimes (Recommendation 39), among other international capabilities. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide effective extradition assistance in the context of VA-related crimes or illicit actors who engage in illicit activities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As with other Recommendations containing funds- or value-based terms, countries should apply the confiscation and provisional measures for property laundered from, proceeds from, or instrumentalities used or intended for use in ML, predicate offences or TF, or property of corresponding value, in the context of VAs. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should apply the confiscation and provisional measures relating to"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 8 of INR. 15 specifically requests that supervisors of VASPs exchange information promptly and constructively with foreign counterparts, regardless of the supervisors' nature or status or differences in the nomenclature or status of VASPs. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors of VASPs exchange information promptly and constructively with their foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: International co-operation is also relevant for VASPs that register or license in one jurisdiction but provide products or services 'offshore' to customers in other jurisdictions. It is important that FIUs cooperate and exchange relevant STR information with counterparts in a timely manner, especially for cross-border VA activities or VASP operations. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important that FIUs cooperate and exchange relevant information on relevant STRs with their counterparts in a timely manner"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Sufficient oversight of VASPs operating in a jurisdiction enables better investigatory assistance and international co-operation. At present, lack of regulation and investigation capacity in many countries may obstruct meaningful international co-operation, and many countries lack legal frameworks to criminalize certain VA-related ML/TF activities, which could limit mutual legal assistance where dual criminality is required. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the lack of regulation and investigation capacity in many countries may present obstacles"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Authorities should also consider the Principles of Information-Sharing and Cooperation amongst VASP Supervisors for guidance on how supervisors can co-operate with counterparts (see Section VI). (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should also consider the Principles of Information-Sharing and Cooperation amongst VASP Supervisors"</sub>

## DNFBPs that Engage in or Provide Covered VA Activities

- **expected / requirement**: When a DNFBP engages in VASP activity (for example a casino offering VA-based gaming), countries should subject the entity to all measures for VASPs set forth in the FATF Recommendations. Recommendations 22 and 23 set out CDD, recordkeeping and other requirements for casinos, real estate agents, dealers in precious metals and stones, lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants, and trust and company service providers in specified situations. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should subject the entity to all of the measures for VASPs set forth in the FATF Recommendations"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 28 requires countries and competent authorities to subject DNFBPs to regulatory and supervisory measures as set out in the FATF Recommendations. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 28 requires countries and competent authorities to subject DNFBPs to regulatory and supervisory measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The guidance states, referring back to its earlier position, that countries should supervise and regulate VASPs, including DNFBPs engaged in VASP activities, at a level on par with FIs rather than at DNFBP level. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subject VASPs, including DNFBPs that engage in VASP activities, to a level of supervision and regulation on par"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where a DNFBP (for example a casino) engages in covered VASP activities, countries should subject it to a higher level of supervision (for example 'DNFBP plus' supervision), consistent with the higher level applied to all VASPs and equivalent to that for FIs under Recommendations 26 and 27. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should subject the DNFBP to a higher level of supervision"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 22 notes that Recommendations 10, 11, 12, 15 and 17 apply to DNFBPs. For DNFBPs engaged in VASP activities, countries should refer to those Recommendations and others relevant to VASPs, and apply appropriate CDD, recordkeeping and other measures. Countries should also ensure such DNFBPs are registered/licensed for those VASP activities (see paragraphs 123-139). (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should also ensure that DNFBPs engaging in VASP activities are registered/licensed"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In such instances the entity is in essence a VASP engaging in specified financial activities and not a DNFBP, regardless of what a country may call it. This approach helps ensure a level regulatory playing field across the VASP sector globally. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the entity is, in essence, a VASP engaging in specified financial activities and not a DNFBP"</sub>

## Risk-Based Approach to Supervision or Monitoring of VASPs

- **descriptive / context**: The RBA aims to identify, understand and assess ML/TF risks and develop prevention or mitigation measures commensurate with the risks identified. In supervision, the RBA applies to how supervisory authorities allocate resources and to supervisors discharging their functions in a way conducive to VASPs' application of the RBA. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the case of supervision, the RBA applies to the way in which supervisory authorities allocate their resources."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In March 2021 the FATF released Guidance for supervisors on the risk-based approach to AML/CFT supervision, with guidance for supervisors broadly and additional guidance and practical advice for VASP supervisors specifically; it should be read in conjunction with this Guidance. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In March 2021, the FATF released _Guidance for supervisors on the risk-based approach to AML/CFT supervision_"</sub>
