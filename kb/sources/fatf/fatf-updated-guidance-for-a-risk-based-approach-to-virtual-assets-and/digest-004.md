## What is a virtual asset?

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 53: digital assets that are unique rather than interchangeable and used in practice as collectibles rather than payment or investment instruments can be called non-fungible tokens (NFTs) or crypto-collectibles. Depending on their characteristics, such assets are generally not considered VAs under the FATF definition. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are generally not considered to be VAs under the FATF definition"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: It is important to consider the nature of the NFT and its function in practice, not the terminology or marketing terms used, because the FATF Standards may cover them regardless of terminology. Some NFTs that do not appear on their face to be VAs may fall under the VA definition if used for payment or investment purposes in practice. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important to consider the nature of the NFT and its function in practice"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Other NFTs are digital representations of other financial assets already covered by the FATF Standards; they are excluded from the VA definition but covered as that type of financial asset. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Other NFTs are digital representations of other financial assets already covered by the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 54: the FATF reaffirms its G20 report statements that a stablecoin is covered by the Standards as either a VA or a financial asset (for example a security), according to the same criteria used for any other digital asset, depending on its exact nature and the country's regulatory regime. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a stablecoin is covered by the Standards as either a VA or a financial asset"</sub>

## What is a VASP?

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 55: the FATF Glossary defines a VASP as any natural or legal person not covered elsewhere under the Recommendations who as a business conducts, for or on behalf of another natural or legal person, one or more of: (i) exchange between VAs and fiat currencies; (ii) exchange between one or more forms of VAs; (iii) transfer of VAs; (iv) safekeeping and/or administration of VAs or instruments enabling control over VAs; (v) participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer and/or sale of a VA. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer’s offer and/or sale"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 56: like the VA definition, the VASP definition should be read broadly. Countries should take a functional approach and apply the underlying concepts to determine whether an entity is undertaking VASP functions, and should not apply the definition based on the nomenclature the entity adopts for itself or the technology it employs. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should not apply their definition based on the nomenclature or terminology which the entity adopts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 57: the VASP definition applies only to entities 'not covered elsewhere under the Recommendations', so it excludes FIs or DNFBPs covered elsewhere. Jurisdictions have to apply the most appropriate definition based on the conceptual foundations of each. The primary difference between VASPs and traditional FIs for this Guidance is the application of Recommendations 10 and 16, so jurisdictions may wish to apply the definition giving more thorough regulatory and supervisory coverage. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "so jurisdictions may wish to apply the definition that provides more thorough regulatory and supervisory coverage"</sub>

## What is a virtual asset?

- **recommended / recommendation**: Because the VA space is evolving rapidly and the functional approach matters particularly for NFTs and similar digital assets, the FATF says countries should consider applying the FATF Standards to NFTs case by case. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should therefore consider the application of the FATF Standards to NFTs on a case-by-case basis"</sub>

## What is a VASP?

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 56 of the FATF guidance explains that AML/CFT obligations arise from the financial services an entity actually provides, whatever its operating model, technology tools or ledger design. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The obligations in the FATF Standards stem from the underlying financial services offered"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 56 adds that the examples using general terms for common business models are only illustrations; the VASP definition is applied by assessing whether the entity provides a qualifying service, and not by reference to those labels. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these should not obscure the fact that the definition is meant to be applied based on an assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote says the differences between VASPs and FIs relate to CDD (Recommendation 10), a lower CDD occasional transaction threshold, and wire transfer rules (Recommendation 16) which apply in an amended way to VA transfers (the travel rule). (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to lower the CDD occasional transaction threshold, and wire transfer rules (Recommendation 16)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 58: the first use of 'person' in the VASP definition refers to the person conducting the activity or operation in limbs (i)-(v), which can be a legal person such as a company or a natural (individual) person. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The person can be either a legal person, such as a company, or a natural (individual) person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 59: 'conducts' includes provision and/or active facilitation of a service, meaning active involvement in activities under limbs (i)-(v). It is meant to exclude ancillary participants that do not provide or actively facilitate covered activities, such as entities providing Internet or cloud services. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is meant to exclude ancillary participants that do not provide or actively facilitate any of these covered activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 60: 'as a business' is meant to exclude those who carry out a function very infrequently for non-commercial reasons. To satisfy it, the entity must carry out the function for or on behalf of another natural or legal person rather than itself, for commercial reasons, and on at least a sufficiently regular basis rather than infrequently. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is meant to exclude those who may carry out a function on a very infrequent basis for non-commercial reasons"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 61: 'for or on behalf of another natural or legal person' includes carrying out the function in the course of providing a covered service to another person (a 'user' or 'customer'). An internal transfer of VAs by a single legal person within itself (for example within company units) would not qualify as a VASP unless done for or on behalf of another person in the context of providing VASP services. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an internal transfer of VAs by a single legal person within that legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 62 states that a person who satisfies the preceding elements (person, conducts, as a business, for or on behalf of another) is a VASP once it carries out at least one of the five categories of activity: virtual/fiat exchange, virtual/virtual exchange, transfer, safekeeping and/or administration, and participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer and/or sale. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A person who meets these requirements will then be a VASP if it carries out one or more"</sub>

## Exchange and transfer

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 63: limb (i) covers any service in which VAs can be exchanged for fiat currency or vice versa, and limb (ii) covers services where one kind of VA can be used as a means of exchange or payment for another VA. The offerer or provider acting as a business is a VASP. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "if parties can use one kind of VA as means of exchange or form of payment for another VA"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Limbs (i) and (ii) apply regardless of the service provider's role vis-a-vis users as a principal, a central counterparty for clearing or settling, an executing facility or another intermediary. A VASP does not have to provide every element of the exchange or transfer to qualify, so long as it conducts the exchange activity as a business on behalf of another natural or legal person. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A VASP does not have to provide every element of the exchange or transfer in order to qualify as a VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 64: limb (iii) covers any service allowing users to transfer ownership or control of a VA to another user, or to transfer VAs between addresses or accounts held by the same user. The Standards define it as conducting a transaction on behalf of another person that moves a VA from one VA address or account to another. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any service allowing users to transfer ownership, or control of a VA to another user"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: To illustrate limb (iii), consider the nature of the VA after a purported transfer: if a new party has custody or ownership of the VA, can pass control to others, or can benefit from its use, a transfer has likely occurred. This control need not be unilateral, and multi-signature processes are not inherently exempt where a VASP acts as a business on behalf of another person. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If a new party has custody or ownership of the VA, has the ability to pass control of the VA to others"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote defines a multi-signature process as one in which a person needs several digital signatures (and therefore several private keys) to perform a transaction from a wallet. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a person needs several digital signatures (and therefore several private keys) to perform a transaction from a wallet"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 65: limb (iii) is conceptually similar to what Recommendation 14 on money and value transfer services (MVTS) covers for financial assets. A covered example is actively facilitating or allowing users to send VAs to others, as in a personal remittance payment, payment for non-financial goods or services, or payment of wages; a provider offering such a service will likely be a VASP. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The limb is conceptually similar to what Recommendation 14 on money and value transfer services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote refers to Recommendation 14 for MVTS obligations in the VASP context, including that MVTS providers include agents in their AML/CFT programmes and monitor them for compliance. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requirements that MVTS providers include agents in their AML/CFT programmes and monitor them for compliance"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 66: exchange or transfer services may occur through decentralized exchanges or platforms. A 'decentralized or distributed application (DApp)' is a software program operating on a blockchain or similar technology, and such applications often still have a central party with some involvement or control, such as creating and launching a VA, developing DApp functions and interfaces, holding an administrative key, or collecting fees. DApps can facilitate or conduct VA exchange or transfer, and where they offer VASP-type financial services the term 'decentralised finance' (DeFi) is commonly used. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "often still have a central party with some measure of involvement or control"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Under paragraph 64, a person keeping unilateral control of their own assets at all times may indicate that the provider is not offering a qualifying service under limb (iii), although limb (iii) can still apply where the provider actively facilitates the transfer. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "if a person maintains unilateral control of their assets at all times"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 64 states that limb (iii) also reaches transfers among users of the same VASP, even when the VASP records them in an internal off-chain ledger while the VA stays in the same on-chain omnibus wallet or account. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This also includes transfers between and among users of the same VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 67: a DeFi application (the software program) is not a VASP under the FATF standards, as the Standards do not apply to underlying software or technology. However, creators, owners and operators or others who maintain control or sufficient influence in DeFi arrangements, even if they seem decentralized, may fall under the VASP definition where they provide or actively facilitate VASP services, even if other parties play a role or parts are automated. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A DeFi application (i.e. the software program) is not a VASP under the FATF standards"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Owners/operators of a DeFi arrangement can often be distinguished by their relationship to the activities: control or sufficient influence over assets or aspects of the service's protocol, and an ongoing business relationship with users, even if exercised through a smart contract or voting protocols. Countries may wish to consider other factors, such as whether any party profits from the service or can set or change parameters. These are illustrative, not the only characteristics, and additional VASPs may interact with a DeFi arrangement depending on its operation. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether any party profits from the service or has the ability to set or change parameters"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 68: countries will need to evaluate the facts and circumstances of each situation to determine whether an identifiable person(s) is providing a covered service. Marketing terms, self-identification as DeFi, or the specific technology are not determinative, and jurisdictions should apply the VASP definition without respect to self-description, since DeFi arrangements commonly call themselves decentralized when they include a person with control or sufficient influence. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Marketing terms or self-identification as a DeFi is not determinative"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should apply the Standards' principles interpreting the definitions broadly but with regard for the practical intent of the functional approach, guided by the principle that the FATF intends to cover natural or legal persons who conduct the covered financial services as a business. If they meet the VASP definition, owners/operators should undertake ML/TF risk assessments prior to launch or use of the software or platform and take appropriate measures to manage and mitigate risks in an ongoing and forward-looking manner. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "owners/operators should undertake ML/TF risk assessments prior to the launch or use of the software or platform"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 68 states that where a person can buy governance tokens of a VASP, the VASP should retain the responsibility for meeting AML/CFT obligations. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the VASP should retain the responsibility for satisfying AML/CFT obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 70: other common VA services may also constitute exchange or transfer under items (i)-(iii), and the persons behind them would be VASPs if they provide the activity as a business on behalf of another person. They include VA escrow services (including with smart contract technology) used by buyers to send, receive or transfer fiat currency in exchange for VAs where the entity has custody over the funds, and brokerage services that facilitate issuance and trading of VAs on behalf of users. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VA escrow services, including services involving smart contract technology"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Also covered as potential VASP services: order-book exchange services bringing together buyers' and sellers' orders (potentially through a matching engine), and advanced trading services such as margin or algorithm-based trading. A platform that only allows buyers and sellers of VAs to find each other and undertakes none of the services in the VASP definition would not be a VASP. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a platform which only allows buyers and sellers of VAs to find each other"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 71: exchange and/or transfer business models include VA exchanges or transfer services facilitating exchange of VA for fiat and/or other VAs for remuneration (fee, commission, spread or other benefit); they typically accept a wide range of payment methods including cash, wires, credit cards and VAs, and can be administrator-affiliated, non-affiliated or a third-party provider. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These models typically accept a wide range of payment methods, including cash, wires, credit cards, and VAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Providers of kiosks, often called 'ATMs', 'bitcoin teller machines', 'bitcoin ATMs' or 'vending machines', may also fall into the exchange/transfer definitions because they provide or actively facilitate covered VA activities via physical electronic terminals enabling exchange of VAs for fiat or other VAs and of fiat for VAs. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also fall into the above definitions because they provide or actively facilitate covered VA activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 69 states that where no legal or natural person with control or sufficient influence over a DeFi arrangement can be identified, there may be no central owner/operator that meets the VASP definition. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there may not be a central owner/operator that meets the definition of a VASP"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In the situation where no DeFi owner/operator can be identified, paragraph 69 says countries should monitor for the emergence of risks from DeFi services and arrangements, including by engaging with representatives of their DeFi community. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should monitor for the emergence of risks posed by DeFi services and arrangements in such situations"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 69 says countries should consider, where appropriate, mitigating actions where DeFi services operating in this manner are known to them. Such actions can be taken before the service launches or while it is being offered, as necessary. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider, where appropriate, any mitigating actions, where DeFi services operating in this manner are known to them"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 69 gives an example: where no VASP is identified, a country may consider requiring that a regulated VASP take part in activities relating to the DeFi arrangement, consistent with its RBA or other mitigants. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider the option of requiring that a regulated VASP be involved in activities related to the DeFi arrangement"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 69 adds that countries could also consider the ML/TF risks and potential mitigating actions for P2P transactions set out in the Guidance (paragraphs 37-41 and 104-106, per its footnote). (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries could also consider the ML/TF risks and potential mitigating actions in relation to P2P"</sub>

## Safekeeping and/or administration

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 73: 'control' means the ability to hold, trade, transfer or spend the VA; parties that can use a VA or change its disposition have control. Control need not be unilateral and can include circumstances where keys or credentials held by others are required to change the asset's disposition, such as multi-signature processes; a multi-signature model does not mean an entity lacks control, depending on its influence over the VAs. This also applies to interpreting 'enabling control' in limb (iv). (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The term “control” should be understood as the ability to hold, trade, transfer or spend the VA"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 74: limb (iv) would include, for example, most custodial wallet service providers because they hold and/or keep VAs on behalf of another person. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most custodial wallet service providers because they hold and/or keep VAs on behalf of another person"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 75: for limb (iv), countries should account for services or business models providing the function of safeguarding VAs or the power to manage or transfer them, on the assumption that this is done only on the owner's/user's instructions. These could include persons that control the private key associated with another person's VAs or control smart contracts, to which they are not a party, involving another person's VAs. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should account for services or business models that provide the function of safeguarding the person’s VAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 76: firms merely providing ancillary infrastructure for another entity to offer the service, such as cloud data storage providers or integrity service providers verifying the accuracy of signatures, will not normally satisfy the definition. This limb also does not typically cover software developers or providers of unhosted wallets whose functions are only developing and/or selling software/hardware. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will not normally satisfy this definition"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Countries must look at individual facts and circumstances in applying the definition for specific cases. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries must look at individual facts and circumstances in applying the definition for specific cases"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote says the terms 'safekeeping', 'administration' and 'ancillary services' are used and interpreted in the context of VAs/VASPs and should not be confused with their use in other situations such as banking and traditional financial instruments or services. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They should not confused with the usage of such terms in other situations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 72 states that limb (iv) of the VASP definition should be read expansively, so any entity able to exercise control over VAs may qualify. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Limb (iv) of the VASP definition should also be read expansively."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 72 explains that, in simplest terms, safekeeping is holding a VA, or the private keys to it, for another person, while administration can include managing VAs for or on behalf of another person. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consists of the service of holding a VA or the private keys to the VA on behalf of another person"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 74 says those who may offer escrow services, such as lawyers, should consider whether they provide the service frequently as a business and whether the elements of control are offered by themselves or by a party to whom control is outsourced, such as a custodial wallet service provider. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Those who may offer escrow services, such as lawyers, should consider whether they provide this service frequently as a business"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 74 says that performing the functions in the definition, rather than being categorised as a lawyer, should be what determines whether someone is a VASP. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Providing the functions outlined in the definition should be the determining factor in identifying a VASP"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 74 says that when in doubt, the plain wording of the definitions should be interpreted flexibly so as to cover any entity that provides control of VAs for or on behalf of another person. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the plain language of the definitions should be interpreted flexibly to encompass any entity"</sub>

## Participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer and/or sale

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 78 states that merely writing VA software in order to issue a VA does not make the creator a VASP, unless the creator also carries out covered functions as a business for or on behalf of another person. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The discrete act of creating VA software to issue a VA does not make the creator a VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 77: the VASP definition is intended to cover activities related to ICOs, generally a means to raise funds for new projects from early backers. Limb (v) covers persons who participate in, or provide related financial services to, issuers' offer and/or sale of VAs, whether affiliated or unaffiliated with the issuer, across issuance, offer, sale, distribution, ongoing market circulation and trading. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Limb (v) in particular covers persons who participate in, or provide related financial services to, issuers’ offer and/or sale of VAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Limb (v) examples include businesses accepting purchase orders and funds and purchasing VAs from an issuer to resell and distribute funds or assets, as well as book building, underwriting, market making and placement agent activity. Other limbs may also be relevant to ICO businesses, and persons involved in issuance can also provide exchange, transfer or safekeeping services under limbs (i)-(iv), particularly issuers who issue and offer/sell the VA through ICOs. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "book building, underwriting, market making and placement agent activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 78: the sole act of issuing a VA, entirely on its own, is not a covered service under limb (v); a footnote defines issuance as the creation of a VA, distinct from the offer and/or sale related to it. Persons that conduct exchange and transfer of issued VAs as a business for or on behalf of another person, or participate in or provide financial services related to any associated ICO, would be covered. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the sole act of issuing a VA26 , entirely on its own, is not a covered service under limb (v)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 79: a jurisdiction's AML/CFT obligations for service providers that participate in or provide financial services relating to an issuer's issuance, offer, sale and/or distribution (such as in ICOs) may involve one or more AML/CFT and non-AML/CFT regulations, such as for money transmission, securities, commodities and/or derivatives activities. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may involve one or more of a country’s AML/CFT and non-AML/CFT regulations"</sub>

## Box 2. Example of characteristics of initial coin offerings (ICOs)

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In an ICO, an issuer or promoter typically offers a digital asset for sale in exchange for fiat currency or another VA, announced and promoted online through marketing materials. Issuers or promoters often release a 'white paper', may say capital raised will fund development of a platform or software and that the asset may later be used to access it, and may lead purchasers to expect a return on investment or a share of project returns. After issuance, assets may be resold in a secondary market (for example on trading platforms or through VASPs). (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Issuers or promoters often release a “white paper” describing the project and promoting the ICO"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: In determining how the VASP definition applies to ICO entities, the facts and circumstances underlying an asset, activity or service determine categorization, rather than labels or terminology used by market participants. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is the facts and circumstances underlying an asset, activity or service that will determine the categorization"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Example: a person creates a digital asset meeting the VA definition and sells it, using proceeds to develop the platform where it may be used. That seller is a VASP if, in addition to issuance, it conducts other activities under any limb, such as exchanging the VA for fiat or other VAs (limbs (i) and (ii)) or providing liquidity by acting as a market-maker after the ICO (limb (v)). (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "if they are providing liquidity in the VA by acting as a market-maker following the ICO"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Businesses providing related financial services to the person's sale of the VA (for example acting as broker or dealer for the person) would be a VASP under limb (v) regardless of whether formally affiliated with the person. It does not matter whether the customer intends to use the VA as an investment or as a means of payment. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regardless of whether they are formally affiliated with the person"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The digital asset in such an example may alternatively be considered a security under a country's laws, so whether the issuer is a VASP or an issuer of securities depends on the unique facts of the ICO and the country's laws. Other jurisdictions may take a different approach, which may include payment tokens, and a person may be subject to more than one regulatory framework. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether the issuer of the digital asset will be considered a VASP or an issuer of securities will depend"</sub>
