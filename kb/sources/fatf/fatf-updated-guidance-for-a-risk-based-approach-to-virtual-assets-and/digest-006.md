## PART THREE: APPLICATION OF FATF STANDARDS TO COUNTRIES AND COMPETENT AUTHORITIES

- **descriptive / context**: Section III of the FATF Guidance explains how the Recommendations on VAs and VASPs apply to countries and competent authorities. It covers identifying and mitigating risks of covered VA activities, preventive measures, licensing and registration, effective supervision on par with supervision of related financial activities of FIs, a range of effective and dissuasive sanctions, and national and international co-operation. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "implementing effective supervision on par with the supervision of related financial activities of FIs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Almost all FATF Recommendations are directly relevant to how countries should use government authorities and international co-operation to address ML/TF risks of VAs and VASPs, while other Recommendations are less directly or explicitly linked to VAs or VASPs but remain relevant and applicable. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Almost all of the FATF Recommendations are directly relevant for understanding how countries should use government authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: VAs and VASPs are subject to the full range of obligations under the FATF Recommendations as described in INR. 15, including obligations applicable to other AML/CFT-regulated entities, based on the financial activities VASPs engage in and having regard to the ML/TF risks of covered VA activities or operations. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VAs and VASPs are subject to the full range of obligations under the FATF Recommendations, as described in INR. 15"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The section also reviews how supervisors of VASPs apply the risk-based approach (RBA). (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section also reviews the application of the RBA by supervisors of VASPs."</sub>

## Risk-Based Approach and National Co-ordination

- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 1, countries should apply a RBA so that measures to prevent or mitigate ML/TF risks are commensurate with the risks identified in their jurisdictions, and should strengthen the requirements for higher-risk situations or activities involving VAs. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Under the RBA, countries should strengthen the requirements for higher-risk situations or activities involving VAs."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 98: in assessing the ML/TF risks of VAs, countries will likely keep treating as key the particular types of VA financial activity (for instance P2P transactions), VASP activities or operations, and whether a VA is centralized or decentralized and whether a regulated VASP controls it. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the distinction between centralized and decentralized VAs and whether they are subject to control by a regulated VASP"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 98: because VA financial flows can be more anonymous or obfuscated and effective supervision and CDD (including customer identification and verification) are harder, VAs and VASPs overall may be seen as higher ML/TF risk, which may call for monitoring and enhanced due diligence (EDD) where appropriate to the country's context. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VAs and VASPs in general may be regarded as posing higher ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 1 requires countries to identify, understand and assess their ML/TF risks and take action aimed at effectively mitigating them. This applies to risks of new technologies under Recommendation 15, including VAs and the risks of VASPs engaged in or providing covered VA activities, operations, products or services. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 1 requires countries to identify, understand, and assess their ML/TF risks and to take action aimed at effectively mitigating those risks."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Public-private sector co-operation may assist competent authorities in developing AML/CFT policies for covered VA activities (for example VA payments, transfers, issuance) and for innovations in related VA technologies and emerging products and services, where appropriate and applicable. It may also help countries allocate and prioritize AML/CFT resources by competent authorities. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public-private sector co-operation may assist competent authorities in developing AML/CFT policies for covered VA activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In October 2020 the FATF amended Recommendation 1 and its Interpretive Note to require countries, FIs and DNFBPs to assess proliferation financing (PF) risks. In June 2021 it amended the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 15 to clarify that these PF requirements also apply to VASPs, and released Guidance on Proliferation Financing Risk Assessment and Mitigation in June 2021. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In June 2021, the FATF amended the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 15 to clarify that these PF requirements also apply to VASPs."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should identify, assess and take effective action to mitigate the ML/TF/PF risks related to VAs. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should identify, assess and take effective action to mitigate the ML/TF/PF risks related to VAs."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: National authorities should undertake a co-ordinated risk assessment of VA activities, products and services, and of risks associated with VASPs and the overall VASP sector in their country. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "National authorities should undertake a co-ordinated risk assessment of VA activities, products, and services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In undertaking a risk assessment, it is especially important to include individuals who are knowledgeable on VAs, VASPs and their underlying technology. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including individuals who are knowledgeable on VAs, VASPs and their underlying technology is especially important"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 101: the national VA risk assessment should let all relevant authorities understand how specific VA products and services work, fit into and affect every relevant AML/CFT regulatory jurisdiction (for example money transmission and payment mechanisms, VA kiosks, commodities, securities or related issuance activities). (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable all relevant authorities to understand how specific VA products and services function, fit into, and affect"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 101: the national VA risk assessment should also encourage like AML/CFT treatment of products and services that have like risk profiles. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "promote similar AML/CFT treatment for similar products and services with similar risk profiles"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: As the VASP sector evolves, countries should consider examining the relationship between AML/CFT measures for covered VA activities and other regulatory and supervisory measures (for example consumer and investor protection, prudential safety and soundness, network IT security, tax), since measures in other fields may affect ML/TF risks. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider examining the relationship between AML/CFT measures for covered VA activities and other regulatory and supervisory measures"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider undertaking short- and longer-term policy work to develop comprehensive regulatory and supervisory frameworks for covered VA activities and VASPs (and other obliged entities operating in the VA space) as widespread adoption of VAs continues. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider undertaking short- and longer-term policy work to develop comprehensive regulatory and supervisory frameworks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should require VASPs and other obliged entities to identify, assess and take effective action to mitigate the ML/TF risks of providing or engaging in covered VA activities or offering particular VA products or services. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also require VASPs (as well as other obliged entities) to identify, assess, and take effective action to mitigate"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where VASPs are permitted under national law, countries, VASPs, FIs and DNFBPs (including those engaged in VA activities or products) must assess the associated ML/TF risks and apply a RBA to ensure appropriate measures to prevent or mitigate those risks are implemented. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must assess the associated ML/TF risks and apply a RBA to ensure that appropriate measures to prevent or mitigate those risks are implemented"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The ML/TF risks of stablecoins, particularly those with potential for mass-adoption that can be used for P2P transactions, are to be analysed in an ongoing and forward-looking manner, as with VAs. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly those with potential for mass-adoption and that can be used for P2P transactions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In developing new products, VASPs and other obliged entities should assess the ML/TF risks before bringing them to market and put mitigation measures in place before launch. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In developing new products, VASPs and other obliged entities should assess the ML/TF risks before bringing them to market"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Potential mitigation measures for new products could include limiting users' ability to transact anonymously, controlling who can access the arrangement, controlling whether and how AML/CFT preventive measures are built into the arrangement and/or ensuring that obliged entities within the arrangement fulfil AML/CFT obligations, for example by using software to monitor transactions and detect suspicious activity. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "limiting the scope of users’ ability to transact anonymously, controlling who can access the arrangement"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should look for these mitigation measures to be in place before granting registration/licensing and on an ongoing basis. The guidance notes it will be more difficult to mitigate the risks of these products once they are launched. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should look for these mitigation measures to be in place before granting registration/licensing and on an ongoing basis."</sub>

## P2P transactions

- **permissive / recommendation**: Depending on assessed P2P risks, countries may consider and implement as appropriate national-level mitigation options, including controls facilitating visibility of P2P activity and/or VA activity crossing between obliged and non-obliged entities (which could include VA equivalents to currency transaction reports or a record-keeping rule for such transfers). (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "controls that facilitate visibility of P2P activity and/or VA activity crossing between obliged entities and non-obliged entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 31: such reporting requirements are similar to those the FATF places on cross-border physical large cash transfers by individuals (Recommendation 32). (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such reporting requirements are similar to the reporting requirements the FATF places on cross-border physical large cash transfers"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Another P2P mitigation option countries may consider is ongoing risk-based enhanced supervision of VASPs and entities operating in the VA space, with a specific focus on unhosted wallet transactions (for example onsite and off-site supervision to confirm VASP compliance with regulations on these transactions). (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ongoing risk-based enhanced supervision of VASPs and entities operating in the VA space with a specific focus on unhosted wallet transactions"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may consider obliging VASPs to facilitate transactions only to/from addresses or sources deemed acceptable in line with their RBA, or only to/from VASPs and other obliged entities. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obliging VASPs to facilitate transactions only to/from addresses/sources that have been deemed acceptable in line with their RBA"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 105: countries should also try to understand the ML/TF risks tied to P2P transactions and how P2P transactions are used in their jurisdiction. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should also seek to understand the ML/TF risks related to P2P transactions"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 105: among measures countries should consider to help understand P2P risks is outreach to the private sector, including VASPs and P2P sector representatives (for example consulting on AML/CFT requirements for P2P transactions); another is training for supervisory, FIU and law enforcement personnel. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducting outreach to the private sector, including VASPs and representatives from the P2P sector"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 105: another measure countries should consider is encouraging methodologies and tools, such as blockchain analytics, for collecting and assessing P2P market metrics and risk mitigation solutions, for risk methodologies to spot suspicious behaviour, and for telling whether wallets are hosted or unhosted, including by working with programmers/developers in this space. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "encouraging the development of methodologies and tools, such as blockchain analytics, to collect and assess P2P market metrics"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 30: so far the FATF knows of no technically proven way to identify, exhaustively, precisely and accurately in all circumstances, which VASP manages the beneficiary wallet using only the VA address. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF is not aware of any technically proven means of identifying the VASP that manages the beneficiary wallet"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may consider placing additional AML/CFT requirements (for example enhanced recordkeeping or EDD requirements) on VASPs that allow transactions to/from non-obliged entities. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "placing additional AML/CFT requirements on VASPs that allow transactions to/from non-obliged entities"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may consider issuing guidance highlighting the importance of VASPs applying a RBA to customers that engage in or facilitate P2P transactions, supported by risk assessments, indicators or typologies publications where appropriate. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "guidance highlighting the importance of VASPs applying a RBA to dealing with customers that engage in, or facilitate, P2P transactions"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may consider issuing public guidance and advisories and conducting information campaigns to raise awareness of P2P risks, accounting for specific risks through assessment of specific users, patterns of observed conduct, local and regional risks, and information from regulators and law enforcement. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issuing public guidance and advisories and conducting information campaigns to raise awareness of risks posed by P2P transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Beyond P2P transactions, the FATF has identified other potential risks that may require further action: VASPs located in jurisdictions with weak or non-existent AML/CFT frameworks, and VAs with decentralised governance structures that may lack an intermediary able to apply AML/CFT measures. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VAs with decentralised governance structures (which may not include an intermediary that could apply AML/CFT measures)"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: These risks may require countries or VASPs to identify VASP- or country-specific risks and implement specific safeguards for transactions with a nexus to VASPs and countries lacking regulation, supervision or appropriate controls. The risks are particularly heightened if there is mass-adoption. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These risks are particularly heightened if there is mass-adoption."</sub>

## Prohibition or limitation of VAs/VASPs

- **permissive / recommendation**: A jurisdiction has discretion to prohibit or limit VA activities, VASPs, and VA activities carried out by non-obliged entities, based on its risk assessment and national regulatory context or to support other policy goals (for example consumer and investor protection, safety and soundness, or monetary policy). This can be a general ban or limitation, or bans or limitations on specific products or services deemed to pose an unacceptable level of ML/TF risk. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A jurisdiction has the discretion to prohibit or limit VA activities or VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such a prohibition or limitation should be in law or enforceable means. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such a prohibition or limitation should be in law or enforceable means."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In deciding whether to prohibit or limit VA activities or VASPs, countries should understand the ML/TF risks of VAs and VASPs and take into account the effect such a prohibition may have on their ML/TF risks. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also take into account the effect that such a prohibition may have on their ML/TF risks."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should periodically revisit the risk assessment underpinning a prohibition/limitation decision, as the associated ML/TF risks and the ability to enforce the prohibition/limitation may evolve rapidly. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should periodically revisit the risk assessment basis underpinning this decision"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation 2 requires national co-operation and co-ordination on AML/CFT/CPF policies, including in the VASP sector, and so applies indirectly to regulating and supervising covered VA activities. Countries should consider mechanisms such as interagency working groups or task forces so policymakers, regulators, supervisors, the FIU and law enforcement can co-operate to develop and implement effective policies and measures on ML/TF/PF risks of covered VA activities and VASPs. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider putting in place mechanisms, such as interagency working groups or task forces"</sub>

## Treatment of Virtual Assets: Interpreting the Funds- or Value-Based Terms

- **expected / expectation**: For applying the FATF Recommendations, countries should consider all funds- or value-based terms (such as property, proceeds, funds, funds or other assets, and other corresponding value) as including VAs. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider all funds- or value-based terms in the Recommendations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should apply Recommendations 3 through 8, 30, 33, 35 and 38 in the context of VAs to prevent misuse of VAs in ML, TF and PF and to act against all proceeds of crime involving VAs. These cover the ML offence, confiscation and provisional measures, TF offence, targeted financial sanctions, non-profit organisations, law enforcement powers, sanctions and international co-operation, and apply in this space even where not obviously applicable to VASPs. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should apply the relevant measures under Recommendations 3 through 8, 30, 33, 35, and 38"</sub>

## Prohibition or limitation of VAs/VASPs

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 109: if a country bans VA activities and VASPs, its mitigation measures should include finding VASPs (or other obliged entities possibly engaged in VA activities) operating illegally in the jurisdiction, imposing proportionate and dissuasive sanctions on them, and assessing the risk of services being offered there by a VASP based abroad. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "mitigation measures should include identifying VASPs (or other obliged entities that may engage in VA activities) that operate illegally"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 109: depending on the country's risk profile, a prohibition or limitation should still call for outreach and enforcement action by the country, along with risk mitigation strategies that allow for the cross-border side of VA activities (for example cross-border VA payments or transfers) and VASP operations. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Based on the country’s risk profile, prohibition/limitation should still require outreach and enforcement actions by the"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 110: national co-operation and co-ordination on VAs should include co-ordination among relevant authorities so that AML/CFT requirements are compatible with data protection, privacy and similar rules (for example data security/localisation). (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This should include co-operation and co-ordination between relevant authorities to ensure the compatibility of AML/CFT requirements"</sub>

## Treatment of Virtual Assets: Interpreting the Funds- or Value-Based Terms

- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 3, the ML offence should extend to any type of property, regardless of its value, that directly represents the proceeds of crime, including VAs. Proving property is proceeds of crime should not require a conviction for a predicate offence, including for VA-related proceeds. Countries should extend their ML offence measures to proceeds of crime involving VAs. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should not be necessary that a person be convicted of a predicate offence, including in the case of VA-related proceeds"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Under Recommendation 4, the confiscation and provisional measures for property laundered, proceeds or instrumentalities of ML or predicate offences, property related to terrorist financing, and property of corresponding value also apply to VAs. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or property of corresponding value” also apply to VAs."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 6 (targeted financial sanctions related to terrorism and TF), countries should freeze without delay the funds or other assets, including VAs, of designated persons or entities and ensure no funds or other assets, including VAs, are made available to or for their benefit. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also freeze without delay the funds or other assets—including VAs—of designated persons or entities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 7 (targeted financial sanctions related to proliferation), countries should freeze without delay the funds or other assets, including VAs, of designated persons or entities and ensure no funds or other assets, including VAs, are made available to or for their benefit. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the context of targeted financial sanctions related to proliferation, countries should freeze without delay"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 8, countries should apply measures in line with the RBA to protect non-profit organisations from TF abuse, including when clandestine diversion of funds to terrorist organisations involves VAs (see Recommendation 8(c)). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to protect non-profit organisations from TF abuse, as laid out in Recommendation 8"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 30 applies to covered VA activities and VASPs. Countries should ensure competent authorities are responsible for expeditiously identifying, tracing and initiating action to freeze and seize VA-related property that is, or may become, subject to confiscation or is suspected to be proceeds of crime, and should implement Recommendation 30 regardless of how the jurisdiction classifies VAs under its property laws. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regardless of how the jurisdiction classifies VAs in its national legal framework"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 33, countries should implement it for VASPs and VA activities and maintain statistics on STRs competent authorities receive from VASPs and from other obliged entities (such as banks) that submit STRs relating to VASPs, VAs or VA activities, and on any VAs that competent authorities freeze, seize or confiscate, regardless of how VAs are categorised under national property law. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain statistics on the STRs that competent authorities receive from VASPs and from other obliged entities"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider updating their STRs and associated statistics to incorporate VA-related indicators that facilitate investigations and financial analysis. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider updating their STRs and associated statistics to incorporate VA-related indicators"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 114: as with confiscation or temporary measures for fiat currency and goods, law enforcement authorities should be able to ask for a temporary freeze of assets where there are grounds to establish, or it is established, that the assets come from criminal activity. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "law enforcement authorities should be able to request a temporary freeze of assets"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 114: to lengthen a freeze or seek confiscation of assets, law enforcement authorities should obtain a court order. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or to request the confiscation of assets, law enforcement authorities should obtain a court order"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 115: under Recommendation 5, the TF offences should cover any funds or other assets, VAs included, whether their source is legitimate or illegitimate (see INR. 5). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should extend to “any funds or other assets,” including VAs, whether from a legitimate or illegitimate source"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 35 directs countries to have a range of effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions (criminal, civil or administrative) for persons covered by Recommendations 6 and 8 to 23 that fail to comply with AML/CFT requirements. As required by paragraph 6 of INR. 15, countries should have similar sanctions for VASPs (and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities) that fail to comply. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As required by paragraph 6 of INR. 15, countries should similarly have in place sanctions to deal with VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As with FIs and DNFBPs, sanctions for non-compliance should be applicable not only to VASPs but also to their directors and senior management, where applicable. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such sanctions should be applicable not only to VASPs but also to their directors and senior management, where applicable"</sub>

## Licensing or Registration

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should designate one or more authorities responsible for licensing and/or registering VASPs. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should designate one or more authorities that have responsibility for licensing and/or registering VASPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Standards allow countries flexibility in applying licensing or registration to VASPs. Using an existing regime (such as that for MVTS) is likely to offer a quicker path to implementation and use existing compliance knowledge, to the extent the existing regime is functional and appropriate for VASPs; it is necessary to confirm in advance that the existing system can sufficiently address VASP risk. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is necessary to confirm in advance that the existing system can sufficiently address the risk of VASPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A new purpose-built regime could omit legacy aspects that do not apply to VASPs and give greater focus to technological capacity in AML/CFT analysis and to risks and mitigants of the VA sector that differ from traditional financial services. Where countries have created new laws and regulations explicitly for VAs and VASPs, a new licensing/registration system may make more sense. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a new regime could be purpose-built for VASPs and not include legacy aspects that may not apply to VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 124: jurisdictions should set the nature and stringency of requirements, and the type of licensing or registration regime they choose, according to an assessment of the different kinds of VA and VASP activity and the related ML/TF risk. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should base the nature and stringency of the requirements and the type of regime they choose"</sub>

## Which VASPs should be licensed or registered?

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 125, citing INR. 15 paragraph 3: VASPs should as a minimum be required to be licensed or registered in the jurisdiction(s) in which they are created. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "at a minimum, VASPs should be required to be licensed or registered in the jurisdiction(s) where they are"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 125: 'creating' a legal person covers incorporating companies or using any other domestic mechanism that formalises a legal entity's existence, such as entry in a public or commercial register, recognition by a notary or other public officer, filing of bylaws or articles of incorporation, or allocation of a company tax number. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "include the incorporation of companies or any other mechanism that is used domestically to formalise the existence"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The place of business of a natural person can be characterised by the primary location where business is performed or where books and records are kept, as well as where the person resides. When business is conducted from a residence, or no place of business can be identified, the primary residence may be regarded as the place of business. The location of the business's server may also be one potential factor. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the primary location where the business is performed or where the business’ books and records are kept"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Jurisdictions may also require VASPs that offer products or services to customers in, or conduct operations from, their jurisdiction to be licensed or registered there, including VASPs accessible to residents or with employees or management located there. The FATF Standards do not require this coverage, but it may help mitigate risks given the cross-border availability of VAs. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While coverage of these entities is not required by the FATF Standards, jurisdictions may find it to be useful in mitigating risks"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: When in doubt, jurisdictions may consider that broader coverage is the safer course, as VAs bring their risks to any jurisdiction in which they are accessible regardless of where they are incorporated. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When in doubt, jurisdictions may consider that broader coverage is the safer course"</sub>
- **permissive / typology or red flag**: To identify VASPs offering products or services in a jurisdiction without being incorporated there, supervisors may use criteria such as location of offices and servers (including customer-facing operations such as call centres), promotional communications targeting specific countries or markets, the language on the VASP website and/or mobile app, whether the VASP has a distribution network in a country (for example an appointed intermediary seeking or visiting clients), and specific information asked of customers revealing the targeted country. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "promotional communications targeting specific countries/markets, the language on the VASP website and/or mobile application"</sub>

## How to identify VASPs for licensing or registration

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should take action to identify natural or legal persons carrying out VA activities or operations without the requisite license or registration and apply appropriate sanctions, including traditional obliged entities that may engage in VA activities (for example a bank that provides VAs to its customers). (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should take action to identify natural or legal persons that carry out VA activities or operations without the requisite license or registration"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: National authorities should have mechanisms to monitor the VASP sector and other obliged entities that may engage in covered VA activities, and appropriate channels to inform VASPs and other obliged entities of their obligation to register or apply for a license. Countries should designate an authority responsible for identifying and sanctioning unlicensed or unregistered VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also designate an authority responsible for identifying and sanctioning unlicensed or unregistered VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: As discussed in paragraphs 108-109, even countries that prohibit VA activities or VASPs should have tools and authorities to identify and take action against natural or legal persons that fail to comply with legal obligations, as required under Recommendation 15. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "even countries that choose to prohibit VA activities or VASPs in their jurisdiction should have in place tools and authorities"</sub>

## Which VASPs should be licensed or registered?

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 126: where a VASP is a natural person, it should be required to be licensed or registered in the jurisdiction in which its place of business is located. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should be required to be licensed or registered in the jurisdiction where its place of business is located"</sub>

## How to identify VASPs for licensing or registration

- **recommended / method**: To identify persons operating without a license or registration, countries should consider the range of tools and resources available. Examples they may consider are blockchain or distributed ledger analytics tools and other investigative tools or capabilities, and web-scraping and open-source information to identify advertising, promotional communications, affiliation programs or other solicitations by an unregistered or unlicensed entity. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "web-scraping and open-source information to identify any advertising, promotional communications or affiliation programs"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Further sources countries may consider include information from the public, obliged entities and industry circles (including channels for public feedback); FIU or other reporting-institution information such as STRs or investigative leads; non-public information such as whether the entity previously applied for or had a license or registration withdrawn; and law enforcement and intelligence reports, including tips from international counterparts. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "non-publically available information, such as whether the entity previously applied for a license or registration or had its license or registration withdrawn"</sub>
