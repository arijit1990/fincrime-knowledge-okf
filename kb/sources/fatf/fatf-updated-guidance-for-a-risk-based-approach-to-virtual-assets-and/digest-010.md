## Understanding the ML/TF Risks

- **expected / expectation**: An effective risk-based regime should reflect a country's policy, legal and regulatory approach, and the broader financial sector policy objectives it pursues (financial inclusion, stability, integrity and consumer protection), and consider factors such as market competition. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An effective risk-based regime should reflect a country’s policy, legal, and regulatory approach."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The extent to which the national framework allows VASPs to apply a RBA should reflect the nature, diversity and maturity of the VASP sector and its risk profile, as well as the ML/TF risk of individual VASPs and specific VA products, services or activities. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also reflect the nature, diversity, and maturity of the VASP sector and its risk profile"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should develop a deep understanding of the VASP market, its structure and its role in the financial system and economy to inform their risk assessment of the sector. This may require investing in training, personnel or other resources to gain the skills and expertise needed to regulate and supervise the range of VA providers and activities. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should also develop a deep understanding of the VASP market, its structure, and its role in the financial system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should draw on a variety of sources to identify and assess ML/TF risks of VA products, services, activities and VASPs, including but not limited to national or sectoral risk assessments, domestic or international typologies and supervisory expertise, and FIU guidance and feedback. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should draw on a variety of sources to identify and assess the ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Where competent authorities do not adequately understand the VASP sector or broader VA ecosystem, it may be appropriate to undertake a more targeted sectoral risk assessment of the VASP sector and/or VA environment, to develop national-level understanding of ML/TF risks and inform the institutional assessments VASPs should undertake. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may be appropriate for competent authorities to undertake a more targeted sectoral risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A number of jurisdictions are using, or exploring using, blockchain analytics services in supervision, for example to pinpoint areas to focus on in assessments of individual VASPs and to categorise the highest risk VASPs by activity. There is a cost consideration and not all VAs are covered by all vendors. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A number of jurisdictions are using, or exploring using, blockchain analytics services to assist with their supervision."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Blockchain analytics are also widely used by VASPs and some FIs to monitor their own risk exposure (for example VA transfers that have passed through mixer services or come from privacy wallets). It is important to consider potential privacy and data protection implications if such tools allow transparency not otherwise available, for example on public blockchains. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VA transfers that have passed through mixer services or come from privacy wallets"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 1 (INR. 1.3) requires countries, including supervisors, to take appropriate steps to identify and assess ML/TF risks for the country on an ongoing basis, to make information available for AML/CFT risk assessments by FIs and DNFBPs, including VASPs. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires countries, including supervisors, to take appropriate steps to identify and assess ML/TF risks for the country on an ongoing basis"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries, including supervisors, should keep risk assessments up-to-date and have mechanisms to provide appropriate information on the results to all relevant competent authorities, FIs and DNFBPs, including VASPs. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should keep the risk assessments up-to-date and should have mechanisms to provide appropriate information on the results"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where parts of the VASP sector have potentially limited capacity to identify ML/TF risks of VA products, services or activities, countries, including supervisors, should work with the sector to understand its risks and help it develop its own understanding. Depending on capacity, general or more granular information and support may be required. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries, including supervisors, should work with the sector to understand its risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When looking at individual VASPs or particular VA products, services or activities, supervisors should weigh the level of risk linked to the VASP's products and services, business models, corporate governance, financial and accounting information, delivery channels, customer profiles, geographic location, countries of operation and level of AML/CFT compliance, as well as the risks of specific VA tokens or products that potentially obfuscate transactions or undermine effective AML/CFT measures. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "business models, corporate governance arrangements, financial and accounting information, delivery channels, customer profiles"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some risk information can come from prudential supervisors where VASPs or other obliged entities engaged in covered VA activities are subject to prudential regulation (traditional FIs subject to the Core Principles, such as banks, insurers, securities providers or investment companies). This involves suitable information sharing and collaboration between prudential and AML/CFT supervisors, especially where they sit in separate agencies. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "involves appropriate information sharing and collaboration between prudential and AML/CFT supervisors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In regulatory models where licensing or registration of VASPs is national but oversight and enforcement are shared at state level, information sharing should include sharing examination findings. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information sharing should include the sharing of examination findings"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should also look at controls in place in a VASP, including the quality of its risk management policy and the functioning of its internal oversight mechanisms. Other relevant information includes the fitness and propriety of the VASP's management and compliance functions. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the quality of a VASP’s risk management policy or the functioning of its internal oversight mechanisms"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 52: under the FATF Recommendations, 'core principles' refers to the Basel Committee's Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision, IOSCO's Objectives and Principles for Securities Regulation, and the IAIS Insurance Supervisory Principles. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision issued by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Where relevant, information from other stakeholders such as supervisors (including overseas supervisors and supervisors of payment systems and instruments, securities, commodities and derivatives), the FIU and law enforcement agencies may help supervisors determine how effectively a VASP manages its ML/TF risks. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also be helpful for supervisors in determining the extent to which a VASP effectively manages the ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Some regimes that only require registration, without extensive background testing, may still enable law enforcement and regulators to be aware of a VASP's existence, lines of business, particular VA products or services and/or controlling interests. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may still enable law enforcement and regulators to be aware of the existence of a VASP, its lines of business"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should review their assessment of the risk profiles of both the VASP sector and individual VASPs periodically and when VASPs' circumstances change materially or relevant new threats emerge. Country examples are in Section V of the Guidance. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should review their assessment of the risk profiles of both the VASP sector and VASPs periodically"</sub>

## Mitigating the ML/TF Risks

- **mandatory / requirement**: The FATF Recommendations require supervisors to allocate and prioritize more supervisory resources to areas of higher ML/TF risk. Supervisors should determine the frequency and intensity of periodic assessments based on the level of ML/TF risk of the sector and individual VASPs. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Recommendations require supervisors to allocate and prioritize more supervisory resources to areas of higher ML/TF risk."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should give priority to potential higher-risk areas within an individual VASP (for example particular products, services or business lines, such as AECs or mixers and tumblers that may further obfuscate transactions or undermine CDD) or to particular types of VASPs (for example those that only or predominantly facilitate virtual-to-virtual financial activities, offer VA obfuscating products or services, facilitate VA transfers for a person to individual users that are not users of another regulated entity, or operate from or in higher-risk jurisdictions). (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs that only or predominantly facilitate virtual-to-virtual financial activities or that offer particular VA obfuscating products or services"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs should understand that applying a risk-based approach flexibly does not exempt them from applying effective AML/CFT controls. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a flexible RBA does not exempt them from applying effective AML/CFT controls"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: If a jurisdiction classifies an entire sector as higher risk, countries should still understand, and be able to explain with some granularity, the categorisation of individual VASPs within the sector based on their customer base, the countries they deal with and their applicable AML/CFT controls. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should still understand and be able to provide some explanation and granularity on the categorisation of individual VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Competent authorities should acknowledge that in a risk-based regime not all VASPs will adopt identical AML/CFT controls, and that single, unwitting and isolated incidents involving transfer or exchange of illicit proceeds do not necessarily invalidate the integrity of a VASP's AML/CFT controls. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "single, unwitting and isolated incidents involving the transfer or exchange of illicit proceeds do not necessarily invalidate"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Adjusting the type of supervision: supervisors should employ both offsite and onsite access to all relevant risk and compliance information, and to the extent permitted by their regime can determine the right mix. Offsite supervision alone may not be appropriate in higher risk situations. Where earlier examination findings suggest low ML/TF risk, resources can focus on higher risk VASPs and lower risk VASPs could be supervised offsite, for example through transaction analysis and questionnaires. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Offsite supervision alone may not be appropriate in higher risk situations."</sub>
- **expected / method**: Adjusting frequency and nature of ongoing supervision: supervisors should adjust the frequency of AML/CFT examinations in line with identified risks and combine periodic reviews with ad hoc supervision as issues emerge (for example from credible whistleblowing, law enforcement information, analysis of financial reporting or other supervisory findings). (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should adjust the frequency of AML/CFT examinations in line with the risks identified"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Other risk-based supervisory factors could include geographic location, registration or licensing status, customer base, transaction type (virtual/fiat or virtual/virtual), VA type, number of accounts or wallets, revenue, products or services offered (more transparent versus obfuscating, such as AECs), prior history of noncompliance, and/or significant changes in management. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "number of accounts or wallets, revenue, products or services offered"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Adjusting intensity of supervision and reporting requirements: supervisors should decide the scope or level of assessment in line with identified risks, aiming to assess the adequacy of VASPs' policies and procedures designed to prevent abuse. More intensive supervision could include detailed testing of systems and files to verify the risk assessment, reporting and record-keeping policies, internal auditing, and interviews with operation staff, senior management and the Board, where applicable. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "detailed testing of systems and files to verify the implementation and adequacy of the VASPs’ risk assessment"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should use their findings to review and update their ML/TF risk assessments and, where necessary, consider whether their approach to AML/CFT supervision and rules and guidance remains adequate. Whenever appropriate, and in compliance with confidentiality standards, supervisors should communicate findings to VASPs to help them enhance their RBA. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should communicate their findings to VASPs to enable them to enhance the quality of their RBA"</sub>

## General Approach

- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should understand the ML/TF risks faced by VASPs or associated with the VASP sector, with a comprehensive understanding of higher and lower risk lines of business or particular VA products, services or activities, and a particularly thorough understanding of higher-risk ones. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with a particularly thorough understanding of the higher-risk products, services or activities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should ensure staff are trained and equipped to assess whether a VASP's policies, procedures and controls are appropriate and proportional given its risk assessment and risk management procedures. Countries could consider a comparative analysis of VASPs' AML/CFT programs to inform judgment of an individual VASP's controls. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries could consider conducting a comparative analysis of VASPs’ AML/CFT programs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should determine whether a VASP's AML/CFT compliance and risk management program is adequate to (i) meet regulatory requirements and (ii) appropriately and effectively mitigate and manage relevant risks, taking into account the VASP's own risk assessment. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(i) meet the regulatory requirements, and (ii) appropriately and effectively mitigate and manage the relevant risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For VASPs operating across jurisdictions on multiple licenses or registrations, the supervisor that licenses or registers the natural or legal person VASP should consider the risks to which the VASP is exposed and the extent to which they are adequately mitigated. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the supervisor that licenses or registers the natural or legal person VASP should take into consideration the risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As part of examination procedures, supervisors should communicate their findings and views on an individual VASP's AML/CFT controls and clearly communicate their expectations of the measures needed to comply with legal and regulatory frameworks. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should communicate their findings and views about an individual VASP’s AML/CFT controls"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In jurisdictions where VA financial activities may implicate multiple competent authorities, supervisory counterparts should co-ordinate where applicable to communicate expectations clearly to VASPs and other obliged entities engaged in VA activities. This is particularly important for VASPs engaged in various types of regulated VA activity (VA money or value transfer, securities, commodities or derivatives) or activities implicating various banking, securities, commodities or other regulators. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisory counterparts within the jurisdiction should also co-ordinate with one another, where applicable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where AML/CFT weaknesses are identified in VASPs, supervisors should follow up and assess the robustness of remediation actions taken to rectify the deficiencies and prevent recurrence. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should follow-up and assess the robustness of remediation actions taken to rectify the deficiencies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For regulatory breaches, supervisors should have a broad range of measures available, which could include warnings, action letters, orders, agreements, administrative sanctions, penalties and fines, and other restrictions and conditions on a VASP's activities. A full range should be applied according to the severity of breaches in the context of unmitigated risks, giving priority to deficiencies exposing the system to the greatest ML/TF risks. See the FATF's Guidance on Effective Supervision and Enforcement by AML/CFT Supervisors of the Financial Sector and Law Enforcement. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Priority should be given to those deficiencies that expose the system to the greatest ML/TF risks."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A stablecoin that qualifies as a financial asset should be supervised in line with that classification, in the same way as all other similarly categorised assets. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If a given stablecoin qualifies as a financial asset, it should be supervised according to that determination"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Because VA transfers cross borders, international co-operation among VASP supervisors is very important in the stablecoin context. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Given the cross-border nature of VA transfers, international co-operation of VASP supervisors is very important"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: VASPs or FIs involved in stablecoins should be supervised in the same manner as VAs or financial assets as appropriate. Assessment of stablecoin risks should form part of this process, and stablecoins may pose higher or lower ML/TF risks according to supervisors' judgement, with consequences for the type and intensity of supervision. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs or FIs involved in stablecoins should be supervised in the same manner as VAs or financial assets as appropriate"</sub>

## Guidance

- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should communicate their expectations of VASPs' compliance with legal and regulatory obligations and may consider engaging in a consultative process with relevant stakeholders where appropriate. Guidance may range from high-level outcome-based requirements, risk-based obligations and information on how supervisors interpret legislation, to more detailed guidance on applying particular AML/CFT controls. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should communicate their expectations of VASPs’ compliance with their legal and regulatory obligations"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Supervisors and other competent authorities may consider the guidance and input of VA technical experts to develop deeper understanding of VASP business models and operations, potential ML/TF exposure and risks of particular VA types or covered VA activities, and to make informed judgments about mitigation measures in place or needed. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may consider the guidance and input of VA technical experts"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Providing guidance for and feedback to the VASP sector is essential and is a requirement under Recommendation 34. Guidance could include best practices enabling VASPs to undertake assessments and develop risk mitigation and compliance management systems. Ongoing, effective communication between supervisors and VASPs is an essential component of successful RBA implementation. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "providing guidance for and feedback to the VASP sector is essential and is a requirement under Recommendation 34"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Supervisors of VASPs should consider liaising with other relevant domestic regulatory and supervisory authorities to secure a coherent interpretation of VASPs' legal obligations and promote a level playing field, including between VASPs and between VASPs and other obliged entities such as FIs and DNFBPs. This is particularly important where more than one supervisor is responsible (for example prudential and AML/CFT supervisors in different agencies or divisions) or VASPs fall under different authorities. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors of VASPs should also consider liaising with other relevant domestic regulatory and supervisory authorities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Multiple sources of guidance should not create opportunities for regulatory arbitrage, loopholes or unnecessary confusion among VASPs. When possible, relevant authorities in a jurisdiction should consider preparing joint guidance. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple sources of guidance should not create opportunities for regulatory arbitrage, loopholes, or unnecessary confusion among VASPs."</sub>

## Training

- **expected / expectation**: Training at all levels, from front-line supervisors to managers and board members, is important for supervision staff to understand the VASP sector and its business models. Supervisors should ensure staff are trained to assess the quality of a VASP's ML/TF risk assessment and the adequacy, proportionality, effectiveness and efficiency of its AML/CFT policies, procedures and internal controls in light of that assessment. Training on current and emerging technologies such as blockchain analytics may also be useful. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should ensure that staff are trained to assess the quality of a VASP’s ML/TF risk assessment"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider opportunities for public-private sector training and collaboration to educate and raise awareness among operational and other competent authorities and industry on issues relating to VAs and VASP activities. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider opportunities for public-private sector training and collaboration"</sub>

## Information Exchange

- **descriptive / recommendation**: Information the public and private sectors could share includes ML/TF risk assessments; typologies and methodologies of how launderers or terrorist financiers misuse VASPs, a particular VA mechanism over another (for example transfer or exchange versus issuance) or VAs generally; and general feedback on the quality and usefulness of STRs and other reports. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Typologies and methodologies of how money launderers or terrorist financiers misuse VASPs"</sub>

## Training

- **expected / expectation**: Supervisory training should cover matters such as how to interact with the entities supervised, so that supervisory staff can form sound judgements about the quality of VASPs' risk assessments and the adequacy and proportionality of their AML/CFT controls. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Training should cover issues such as how to interact with entities and allow supervisory staff to form sound judgements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Training should also aim at consistency in the supervisory approach nationally where there are multiple competent supervisory authorities or the national supervisory model is devolved or fragmented. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "achieving consistency in the supervisory approach at a national level in cases where there are multiple competent supervisory authorities"</sub>

## Information Exchange

- **expected / expectation**: Information exchange between the public and private sectors is important and should be an integral element of a country's strategy for combating ML/TF in the context of VA and VASP activities. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information exchange between the public and private sector is important and should form an integral part"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Public authorities should share risk information where possible, to help inform VASPs' risk assessments. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public authorities should share risk information, where possible, to better help inform the risk assessments of VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Further shareable information includes suspicious indicators associated with VA activities or VASP transactions; targeted unclassified intelligence, where appropriate and subject to safeguards such as confidentiality agreements; and countries, persons or organisations whose assets or transactions should be frozen under targeted financial sanctions as required by Recommendation 6. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Targeted unclassified intelligence, where appropriate and subject to the relevant safeguards such as confidentiality agreements"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider how they might share information with the private sector to help VASPs better understand the nature of law enforcement or other government information requests, or to help shape such requests, so VASPs can provide more accurate and specific information to competent authorities. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider how they might share information with the private sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: In line with Recommendation 40, cross-border information sharing by authorities and the private sector with international counterparts is critical in the VASP sector given VASPs' cross-border and multijurisdictional reach. Authorities should also consider the Principles of Information-Sharing and Co-operation amongst VASP Supervisors (see Section VI). (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cross-border information sharing by authorities and the private sector with their international counterparts is critical in the VASP sector"</sub>
