## RISK AND CONTEXT

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 11: other contextual factors that can significantly influence effectiveness include the transparency, maturity and sophistication of the criminal justice, regulatory, supervisory and administrative regime; the level of corruption and the impact of anti-corruption measures; and the level of financial exclusion. Such factors can affect ML/TF risks and increase or reduce the level of compliance or effectiveness. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the level of corruption and the impact of measures to combat corruption"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Assessors should be cautious about information used on risk and contextual factors, particularly where it materially affects conclusions. They should take the country's views into account but review them critically, refer to other credible or reliable sources (for example international institutions or major authoritative publications), preferably multiple sources, and consider whether the information is up to date and still relevant. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should review them critically and should also refer to other credible or reliable sources of information"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 13: risk, materiality and structural or contextual factors may in some cases explain why a country is compliant or non-compliant, or why effectiveness is higher or lower than its technical compliance would suggest, and can be an important part of assessors' recommendations on improving effectiveness. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may in some cases explain why a country is compliant or non-compliant"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Ratings of both technical compliance and effectiveness are judged on a universal standard applied to all countries. An unfavourable context (for example missing structural elements) may undermine compliance and effectiveness, but risks, materiality and structural or other contextual factors should not be an excuse for poor or uneven implementation of the FATF Standards. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not be an excuse for poor or uneven implementation of the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Assessors should make clear in the MER which risk, materiality and contextual factors they have taken into account, why and how they did so, and the information sources used. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should make clear in the MER which factors they have taken into account"</sub>

## SECTOR MATERIALITY AND WEIGHTING

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 14: assessors should weight all parts of the financial, DNFBP and VASP sectors as "highly important", "moderately important" or "less important", having regard to risk, materiality and context, and set out the weightings and underlying factors in the MER. When assessing systems and measures they should explain how they weighted identified strengths and deficiencies and their impact on the overall sector weighting and assessment. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "weight all parts of the financial, DNFBP and VASP sectors in the country as"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 15: when weighting sectors, assessors should consider the ML/TF risks facing each sector and its materiality and relative importance, in line with paragraphs 5 to 14 and the MER template (Annex I of the Methodology). (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should consider the ML/TF risks facing each sector and the materiality and the relative importance"</sub>

## RISK AND CONTEXT

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 12 says assessors should ensure that contextual factors, including risks, materiality, structural elements and other contextual factors, are considered so as to reach a general understanding of the context in which the country's AML/CFT/CPF system operates. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should ensure that contextual factors, including the risks, issues of materiality, structural elements and other contextual factors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 12 says these contextual factors should influence which issues assessors treat as material or higher-risk, and so help them decide where to focus attention during an assessment. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These factors should influence which issues assessors consider to be material or higher-risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 12 says that, having weighed these elements, assessors should form their own judgement of the context in which the country's AML/CFT/CPF system operates, and should state that analysis clearly and explicitly in the MER. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should make this analysis clear and explicit in the MER"</sub>

## SECTOR MATERIALITY AND WEIGHTING

- **expected / method**: When assessing the materiality of each sector, assessors should take into account at least: (a) the size, integration and make-up of the financial, DNFBP and VASP sectors (footnote 8: for example business concentration); (b) the relative importance of different types of products/services or institutions, businesses or professions; (c) sector maturity, client base type, and the amount of domestic, regional or international business; (d) assessed risks, including the extent to which the economy relies on traceable payment and exchange systems or is cash-based, and use of electronic money or other new payment methods; and (e) estimates of the informal sector and/or shadow economy. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should take into account, at least, the following factors"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 16: assessors should also take into account structural elements and other contextual factors, for example whether established supervisors with sufficient powers, independence and resources, and acknowledged accountability, integrity and transparency, are in place for each sector; the robustness of anti-corruption and transparency frameworks; and the maturity and sophistication of each sector's regulatory and supervisory regime. Footnote 9 gives special supervisory activities such as thematic reviews and targeted outreach as an example. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether established supervisors with sufficient powers, independence and resources"</sub>

## GENERAL INTERPRETATION AND GUIDANCE

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 17: the General Glossary definitions are taken from the FATF Recommendations and published in the Methodology for assessors' convenience; assessors should note the general interpretation guidance that follows to ensure consistency of approach. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These definitions are taken from the FATF Recommendations and are published in the Methodology"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 18, Financial Institutions: assessors should thoroughly understand the types of entities engaging in the financial activities in the Glossary definition of financial institutions, bearing in mind that institutions with different generic names (for example "bank") may undertake such activities in different countries; assessors should focus on the activity, not the names attached to institutions. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should focus on the activity, not the names attached to the institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 19: where the terms "criminal property" and "property of corresponding value" are used in the Methodology (footnote 10: in R.4, R.30, R.31, R.38 and R.40), they should be read to include property owned or held by third parties other than bona fide third parties. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be read to include property that is owned or held by third parties other than bona fide third parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 11 gives examples of circumstances where property is owned or held by non-bona fide third parties and could be criminal property or property of corresponding value: property under the effective control of the criminal defendant or person under investigation but held or owned by family members, associates or legal persons and arrangements; and property gifted or transferred to the third party for an amount significantly above or below market value. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "held or owned by family members, associates or legal persons and arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 20, VASPs and virtual assets: assessors should thoroughly understand the FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs engaged in covered activities under the Glossary definition of VASP, and note that FATF Standards on virtual assets and providers are applied by Recommendation 15 ("New Technologies"). INR.15 confirms that the terms property, proceeds, funds, funds or other assets, or other corresponding value include Virtual Assets, and assessors should bear this in mind when assessing any Recommendations or Immediate Outcomes using those terms; see the Note to Assessors in R.15. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR.15 explicitly confirms that the terms property, proceeds, funds, funds or other assets, or other corresponding value include Virtual Assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 12 lists where those terms appear (R.3, R.4, R.5, R.6, R.7, R.8, R.10, R.12, R.20, R.29, R.30, R.31, R.33, R.38, R.40, IO.2 and IOs 6 to 11), and states that the words virtual assets need not appear in legislation referring to or defining those terms, provided nothing on the face of the legislation or in case law precludes virtual assets from falling within the definition. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The words virtual assets need not appear or be explicitly included in legislation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 21: countries have flexibility to classify VASPs as a standalone sector or as "FIs" or "DNFBPs"; however classified, VASPs should be subject to adequate regulation and risk-based supervision or monitoring by a competent authority, consistent with R.26 and R.27. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should be subject to adequate regulation and risk-based supervision or monitoring by a competent authority"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 22, evaluating the country's assessment of risk: when assessing Recommendation 1, Immediate Outcome 1 and core issue 11.2, assessors are not expected to conduct an independent risk assessment of their own but should not necessarily accept the country's assessment as correct. They should consider the rigour of the processes and procedures used and the internal consistency of the assessment (whether conclusions are reasonable given the information and analysis), focus on high-level issues rather than fine details, and take a common-sense approach to whether results are reasonable. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider the rigour of the processes and procedures employed"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Where relevant and appropriate, assessors should also consider other credible or reliable sources on the country's risks to identify material differences that should be explored further. Where the assessment team considers the country's risk assessment reasonable, the risk-based elements of the Methodology could be considered on the basis of it. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether there might be any material differences that should be explored further"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 23, risk-based requirements: for each Recommendation where FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs should be required to take actions, assessors should normally assess compliance on the basis that all of them should have to meet all the specified requirements. However, because the Recommendations rest on the degree of ML/TF risk for particular institution types, businesses, professions, customers, products, transactions or countries, a country may take risk into account in applying the Recommendations (for example through simplified measures). (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that all financial institutions, VASPs and DNFBPs should have to meet all the specified requirements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: If the scoping exercise or a later phase of the mutual evaluation identifies unintended consequences such as unduly disrupting or discouraging legitimate NPO activities, supported by credible and reliable sources, the country should demonstrate how its relevant mitigation measures are proportionate and appropriate to the ML/TF risks. Footnote 14 notes such disruption is inconsistent with INR.8 paragraph 2(d), which says focused NPO measures should not disrupt or discourage legitimate charitable activities. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the country should demonstrate how its relevant mitigation measures are proportionate and appropriate to the ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 13: the FATF Recommendations require countries to understand their higher and lower risks and require FIs and DNFBPs to apply measures proportionate to them; where risks are higher, enhanced measures should be required; where lower, simplified measures should be allowed and encouraged; and where there is an assessed low ML/TF risk countries may (but are not obligated to) decide not to apply certain Recommendations to a particular type of FI, activity or DNFBP (see INR.1, para.2). (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "simplified measures should be allowed and encouraged"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 24, exemptions for low-risk situations: where there is a low risk of ML/TF, countries may decide not to apply some Recommendations requiring FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs to take certain actions; in such cases countries should give assessors the evidence and analysis that was the basis for the decision. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may decide not to apply some of the Recommendations requiring financial institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 25: because the Recommendations variously say FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs "should" or "should be required to" act, or that countries "should ensure" actions, the Methodology criteria use the single consistent phrase "Financial institutions should be required", with equivalent phrasing for DNFBPs, VASPs or other entities or persons. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the relevant criteria in this Methodology use the phrase"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 26, law or enforceable means: assessors should consider whether mechanisms used to implement a requirement qualify as enforceable means under the note on the Legal basis of requirements, and be aware that Recommendations 10, 11 and 20 contain requirements which must be set out in law while other requirements may be in either law or enforceable means. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendations 10, 11 and 20 contain requirements which must be set out in law"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 23 says that, when judging whether measures a country applies are adequate to mitigate risks, assessors will need to take into account the risks and the flexibility the risk-based approach allows. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors will need to take the risks and the flexibility allowed by the risk-based approach, into account"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 23 states that implementing the risk-based approach relies on assessing the full spectrum of risks, from low to high, and consequently on having appropriate mitigating measures. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the implementation of the risk-based approach relies on the assessment of the full spectrum of risks, from low to high"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 23 says that if the scoping exercise or a later phase of the mutual evaluation identifies unintended consequences, such as unduly disrupting or discouraging legitimate NPO activities, and this is supported by credible and reliable sources, the country should demonstrate how its mitigation measures are proportionate and appropriate to the ML/TF risks. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the country should demonstrate how its relevant mitigation measures are proportionate and appropriate to the ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 26 says types of documents or measures that are not enforceable means (for example voluntary codes of conduct issued by private sector bodies or non-binding guidance from a supervisory authority) may still help contribute to effectiveness and may therefore be considered in the effectiveness analysis, but without counting towards meeting technical compliance requirements. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "types of documents or measures which are not considered to be enforceable means may, nevertheless, help contribute to effectiveness"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 27, assessment for DNFBPs: under Recommendations 22, 23 and 28 (and specific elements of R.6 and R.7), DNFBPs and their supervisory or self-regulatory bodies must act; technical compliance with these should be assessed only under those Recommendations and not carried forward into other Recommendations on FIs. The effectiveness assessment should take account of both FIs and DNFBPs when examining the relevant outcomes. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not be carried forward into other Recommendations relating to financial institutions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 28, financing of proliferation: the FATF Standard requirements on proliferation financing are limited to Recommendations 7, 15, 1 and 2; in the effectiveness assessment all proliferation financing requirements are in Immediate Outcome 11. Proliferation financing issues should be considered in those places only and not elsewhere in the assessment. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requirements of the FATF Standard relating to the financing of proliferation are limited to Recommendation 7"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 29: where AML/CFT/CPF issues are addressed at supra-national, state/province or local level as well as national, assessors should take appropriate steps to consider those measures and refer to supra-national measures (including risk assessments, laws or regulations applying to the country). All relevant measures at whatever level should be considered for both technical compliance and effectiveness. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All relevant measures, at whatever level, should be taken into account both as regards technical compliance and effectiveness"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 30: countries that are members of supra-national jurisdictions should be assessed individually, and all assessors' recommendations on improving the AML/CFT/CPF system (consistent with paragraphs 73 to 77) should be directed at the assessed country. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries that are members of supra-national jurisdictions should be assessed individually"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 31: when assessing a member state of a supra-national jurisdiction, assessors should take into account (a) all relevant laws, regulations and measures imposed at supra-national level or added at national or sub-national level by the member state according to its national risk; (b) how (sub-)national and supra-national measures complement and interact; and (c) relevant (sub-)national and supra-national risk assessments, including for supra-national assessments how the country informed their development and conclusions (for example through national agencies' input), taking into account paragraph 22 guidance. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "how (sub-)national and supra-national AML/CFT/CPF measures complement and"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 32(a), supra-national technical compliance: to streamline the process and avoid duplication and inconsistency, assessment bodies should develop standardised language describing the supra-national framework elements common to all member states, at the start of the 5th round (for example in the first assessment(s) of member states or as a separate exercise). For each assessed country the language should be modified for later changes to the supra-national framework, relevant national measures and any differences in implementation. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should develop standardised language to describe the elements of the supra-national framework that are common to all member states"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 32(a), on supra-national technical compliance: to streamline the process and avoid duplication and inconsistencies, assessment bodies should develop standardised language describing the elements of the supra-national framework common to all member states, at the start of the 5th round (for example in the context of the first assessment(s) of member states, or as a separate exercise). (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should develop standardised language to describe the elements of the supra-national framework that are common to all member states"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 32(a) says that, for each assessed country, the standardised supra-national language should be modified as appropriate to reflect later changes to the supra-national framework, any relevant national measures the country has implemented domestically, and any differences in implementation. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the standardized language should be modified as appropriate to take into account subsequent changes to the supra-national framework"</sub>
