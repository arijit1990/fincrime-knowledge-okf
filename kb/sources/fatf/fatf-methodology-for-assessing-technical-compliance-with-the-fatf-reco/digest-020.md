## Immediate Outcome 6

- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 6 states the intended outcome that financial intelligence and all other relevant information are appropriately used by competent authorities for ML and TF investigations. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriately used by competent authorities for money laundering and terrorist financing investigations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.6 system, the FIU and other competent authorities access in a timely manner a broad range of relevant, accurate and up-to-date reports and data; the FIU has the resources and skills to analyse and produce financial intelligence supporting other authorities' operational needs; and other authorities have resources and skills and, where relevant, also produce financial intelligence using FIU data. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIU has the resources and skills to conduct analysis and produces financial intelligence"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.6 system, the FIU and other authorities co-operate and exchange information securely and regularly, and a wide variety of financial intelligence is used to develop evidence, identify and trace assets, criminal proceeds or instrumentalities, and investigate ML, associated predicate offences and TF. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "develop evidence, identify and trace assets, criminal proceeds or instrumentalities and investigate money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.6 relates primarily to Recommendations 29 to 32 and also to elements of Recommendations 1, 2, 4, 8, 9, 15, 34 and 40. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 29 to 32"</sub>

## Immediate Outcome 7

- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 7 states the intended outcome that ML offences and activities are investigated and offenders are prosecuted and subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money laundering offences and activities are investigated and offenders are prosecuted"</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.7 system, ML activity, particularly from major proceeds-generating offences, is investigated, offenders are successfully prosecuted, and courts apply effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, including pursuing parallel financial investigations, cases where predicate offences occur outside the country, and stand-alone ML. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pursuing parallel financial investigations and cases where the associated predicate offences occur outside the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.7 system, investigation, prosecution, conviction and sanctions function coherently to mitigate ML risks, and the prospect of detection, conviction and punishment dissuades potential criminals from proceeds-generating crimes and ML. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the prospect of detection, conviction and punishment dissuades potential criminals"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.7 relates primarily to Recommendations 3, 30 and 31 and also to elements of Recommendations 1, 2, 15, 32, 37, 39 and 40. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 3, 30 and 31"</sub>

## Note to Assessors:

- **expected / method**: For IO.7, assessors should consider relevant findings on international co-operation, including the extent to which law enforcement seeks appropriate assistance from foreign counterparts in cross-border ML cases. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seeking appropriate assistance from their foreign counterparts in cross-border money laundering cases"</sub>
- **expected / method**: When assessing IO.7 core issues, assessors should consider whether activities and measures are aligned with risk, including overall ML risk, laundering linked to high-risk predicate offences, characteristics of the ML activity or prosecution (stand-alone, third-party, self-laundering, complex ML), ML methods, techniques and trends, and the extent of ML based on foreign versus domestic predicates. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether activities and measures are aligned with risk, including but not limited to (a) overall level of ML risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote defines third party money laundering as laundering of proceeds by a person not involved in the commission of the predicate offence, and self-laundering as laundering by a person who was involved in the predicate offence. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Third party money laundering is the laundering of proceeds by a person who was not involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote states that stand-alone (autonomous) money laundering is not a type of laundering but the prosecution of ML offences independently, without necessarily prosecuting the predicate offence, which may be particularly relevant where evidence of the particular predicate is insufficient or where there is a lack of territorial jurisdiction over the predicate offence. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Stand-alone (or autonomous) money laundering is not a type of laundering"</sub>
- **expected / limitation**: A footnote says assessors should take into account the assessed country's national framework and legal system, for example whether it applies a mandatory or discretionary approach to investigations or prosecutions, in line with paragraph 74 of the Introduction. (p. 153)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether the country implements a mandatory or discretionary approach to investigations and/or prosecutions"</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 7.1 asks how well and in what circumstances ML activity is identified and investigated, including through parallel financial investigations. (p. 153)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well, and in what circumstances is ML activity identified and investigated"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 7.2 asks to what extent ML activity, including different types of ML cases, is prosecuted (meaning an indictment has been filed) and offenders convicted; assessors should consider the types of ML cases prosecuted. (p. 153)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To what extent is ML activity (including different types of ML cases) being prosecuted"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 7.3 asks to what extent sanctions applied to natural or legal persons convicted of ML offences are effective, proportionate and dissuasive. (p. 153)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To what extent are the sanctions applied against natural or legal persons convicted of ML offences effective, proportionate and dissuasive?"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Core issue 7.4 asks to what extent other criminal justice measures are applied where an ML investigation was pursued but, for justifiable reasons, no ML conviction could be secured; such alternative measures should not diminish the importance of, or substitute for, ML prosecutions and convictions. (p. 153)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such alternative measures should not diminish the importance of, or be a substitute for, prosecutions and convictions for ML offences."</sub>
