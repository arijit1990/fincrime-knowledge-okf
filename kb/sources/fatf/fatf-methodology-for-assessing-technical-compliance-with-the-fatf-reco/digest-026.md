## Notes for Assessors

- **descriptive / context**: The Key Recommended Actions (KRA) Roadmap is a separate document from the MER, developed in parallel with it (Universal Procedures, para. 100). (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Key Recommended Actions (KRA) Roadmap is a separate document that is developed in parallel with the MER"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should compile the Key Recommended Actions in a separate list for the country, the KRA Roadmap (Universal Procedures para. 89). (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should compile the Key Recommended Actions in a separate list for the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The KRA Roadmap is prepared after the on-site visit, at the same time as the first draft of the MER, and is developed in close collaboration with the assessed country throughout the mutual evaluation process. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prepared after the on-site visit at the same time as the first draft of the MER"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should review paragraphs 72 to 76 of the Methodology Introduction for guidance on developing recommended actions, deciding which are Key Recommended Actions and which are other recommended actions, and preparing the KRA Roadmap. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should review the Methodology Introduction para. 72 to 76 for guidance on developing recommended actions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Subject to Methodology Introduction para. 72, Key Recommended Actions should only relate to IOs rated ME or LE, or to Recommendations rated PC or NC where these relate to any IO rated ME or LE. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Key Recommended Actions should only relate to IOs rated ME or LE or Recommendations rated PC or NC"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Normally there should be no more than two to three KRA related to each IO rated ME or LE, including KRA for technical compliance of the Recommendations related to that IO. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Normally, there should be no more than two to three KRA related to each IO rated ME or LE"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: After the MER is adopted, the KRA Roadmap will be given to the appropriate minister of the assessed country to advise the minister of the expectations for follow-up (Universal Procedures para. 114). (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "After the MER is adopted, the KRA Roadmap will be provided to the appropriate minister of the assessed country"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The KRA Roadmap template states that the evaluation was based on the 2012 FATF Recommendations (as updated from time to time) and prepared using the 2022 Methodology. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "based on the 2012 FATF Recommendations (as updated from time to time)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The KRA Roadmap template states that the MER sets out the strengths and weaknesses of the country's AML/CFT/CPF system, covering effectiveness and technical compliance, together with recommended actions, and that the highest priority measures are the Key Recommended Actions included in the Roadmap. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The highest priority measures are identified as Key Recommended Actions (KRA) are included in this KRA Roadmap."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Per the template, the KRA Roadmap is adopted by the FATF or FSRB Plenary, records whether the country is in regular or enhanced follow-up or active ICRG review based on its effectiveness and technical compliance ratings, and serves as the basis for the country's follow-up or ICRG process. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This KRA Roadmap also serves as the basis for"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: In the Key Recommended Actions (KRA) guidance for assessors, a KRA may additionally be set for each of Recommendations 3, 5, 6, 10, 11 and 20 that is rated NC or PC, provided the Recommendation is not connected to any Immediate Outcome rated ME or LE. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In addition, there may be one KRA for each of Recommendations 3, 5, 6, 10, 11 and 20"</sub>

## MUTUAL EVALUATION REPORT

- **descriptive / context**: The MER preface states that the report summarises the AML/CFT/CPF measures in place as at the date of the on-site visit, analyses the level of compliance with the FATF 40 Recommendations and the level of effectiveness of the system, and recommends how the system could be strengthened. (p. 201)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It analyses the level of compliance with the FATF 40 Recommendations and the level of effectiveness"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The MER template states that the evaluation relied on information provided by the country and information the evaluation team obtained during its on-site visit. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The evaluation was based on information provided by the country, and information obtained by the evaluation team"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The MER template calls for a list of the assessment team (names, agencies, countries and roles of examiners, for example legal expert), the Secretariat staff supporting it, and the reviewers of the report. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "list names, agencies and countries of examiners and their role, e.g. legal expert"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: For a country evaluated before, the template records that the previous Mutual Evaluation was conducted under the 2013 FATF Methodology and summarises how many Recommendations were rated C, LC, PC and NC. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducted according to the 2013 FATF Methodology"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The template notes whether the country was rated C or LC on the five Recommendations that were triggers for enhanced follow-up in the last round: R.3, 5, 10, 11 and 20. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which were triggers for enhanced follow-up during the last round: R.3, 5, 10, 11 and 20)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 225 of the template states that a country will be placed in enhanced follow-up if it has 5 or more PC ratings for technical compliance, or 1 or more NC ratings for technical compliance. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(a) it has 5 or more PC ratings for technical compliance; or (b) 1 or more NC ratings"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 225 states that a country will also be placed in enhanced follow-up if it is rated PC on any one or more of R.3, R.5, R.6, R.10, R.11 and R.20. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(c) it is rated PC on any one or more of R.3, R.5, R.6, R.10, R.11 and R.20"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 225 states that a country will also be placed in enhanced follow-up if it has a moderate level of effectiveness for 6 or more of the 11 effectiveness outcomes, or a low level of effectiveness for 1 or more of the 11. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it has a moderate level of effectiveness for 6 or more of the 11 effectiveness outcomes"</sub>

## Introduction to Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Risks and Context

- **expected / expectation**: Assessed countries supply most of the introduction's information through the Universal Procedures questionnaire, and assessors should review that information critically so the final introduction is factually objective and balanced. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should review that information critically to ensure that the final text of the introduction is factually objective"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The introduction should begin with a very brief description of the country's general situation, including its size, territorial makeup and constitutional structure. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "size, territorial makeup and constitutional structure"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The introduction should note any territorial or jurisdictional issues affecting the evaluation, for example where the MER covers territories or regions with different AML/CFT regimes or where the country is part of a supranational jurisdiction. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section should note any territorial or jurisdictional issues affecting the evaluation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For information in the introduction's sub-sections, assessors should give a balanced picture where possible, covering for example higher and lower risk areas and strengths and weaknesses. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should provide a balanced picture where possible thus covering"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should remember that terms such as property, proceeds, funds, funds or other assets or other corresponding value include virtual assets when assessing any Recommendation or Immediate Outcome using those terms (see Methodology Introduction para. 15 and the Note to assessors at R.15). (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "include virtual assets when assessing any Recommendation or Immediate Outcome using these terms"</sub>

## Overview of ML/TF/PF Risks

- **expected / expectation**: The overview of ML/TF/PF risks should set out the money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing threats, vulnerabilities and risks faced by the country, drawing on the risk assessment and other relevant information. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section should set out the ML, TF and PF threats, vulnerabilities and risks faced by the country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The overview should cover the underlying levels of proceeds-generating crime in the country, its nature and the estimated value of proceeds, to the extent possible. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the underlying levels of proceeds generating crime in the country, its nature and the estimated value of proceeds"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The overview should cover available information on the country's exposure to terrorist financing threats, including terrorist groups active in the country or use of the country as a source of funds or recruits for groups active elsewhere, and on proliferation financing. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or the use of the country as a source of funds or recruits for terrorist groups"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The overview should cover the ML/TF/PF risks taking into account vulnerabilities, including those posed by virtual asset activity, and consequences. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ML/TF/PF risks, taking into account vulnerabilities (including vulnerabilities posed by virtual asset activity) and consequences"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In the Overview of ML/TF/PF Risks section of the report, the particular points to cover include the country's exposure to cross-border illicit flows linked to crimes committed in other countries, including any significant potential role as a transit route for illicit goods or funds. (p. 204)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exposure to cross-border illicit flows (related to crimes in other countries)"</sub>

## Country’s risk assessment & Scoping of Higher Risk Issues

- **expected / expectation**: This section should give a high-level summary of the country risk assessment, avoiding duplication of the detail in R.1 and IO.1, and should focus more on the conclusions of the enhanced risk and scoping exercise. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section should give a high-level summary of the country risk assessment and avoid duplication"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should briefly set out any additional risks or risk factors they consider significant but that were not adequately taken into account in the country's risk assessment. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should briefly set out any additional risks or risk factors which they consider significant"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: If assessors identify such additional risks, they should note the basis for their judgement and the credible or reliable sources of information supporting it. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should note the basis for their judgement and the credible or reliable sources of information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where risks were not adequately taken into account in the country's risk assessment, assessors should cross-refer to Chapter 1, where the weaknesses should be described, to avoid duplicating text. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should make a cross-reference to Chapter 1, where those weaknesses should be described"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should summarise the scoping exercise carried out before the on-site visit to identify higher and lower risk issues, give reasons for each classification, and note how additional or less attention was given to those issues during the evaluation. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should summarise the scoping exercise conducted prior to the onsite"</sub>

## Materiality

- **expected / expectation**: The materiality section should set out the size (GDP), integration and general makeup of the economy. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the size (GDP), integration and general makeup of the economy"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The materiality section should set out the amount of business that is domestic or cross-border, how cash-based the economy is (for example the percentage of all transactions that are cash), and estimates of the size of the informal sector or shadow economy. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the extent to which the economy is cash-based"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The materiality section should also note other significant factors from paragraph 9 of the Methodology introduction, such as population size, level of development, geographical factors, and trading, cultural and social links, and should be a brief summary. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the country’s population size, level of development, geographical factors and trading, cultural and social links"</sub>

## Financial sector, VASPS and DNFBPs

- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should describe the size and makeup of the financial sector (including its percentage of GDP where available), the generic types of virtual asset activities and providers mainly used, and DNFBPs, including whether the country is an international or regional financial centre. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should describe the size and makeup of the financial sector"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should summarise the types and key features of financial institutions, VASPs and DNFBPs in the country, the numbers of each type and information on the materiality of the sector and institutions, using the tables provided by the assessed country in the introduction questionnaire. (p. 205)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Tables provided by the assessed country in the introduction questionnaire should be used to summarise the information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should note, based on risk, materiality and context, the relative importance of different types of financial institutions, VASPs and DNFBPs and their products and activities, and explain how they weighed them, to encourage consistent weighting through the MER, particularly for IO.3 (FIs and VASPs) and IO.4 (DNFBPs). (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is particularly important for assessors to explain their weighing of the relative importance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Methodology gives the example that where a particular type of financial institution is as (or almost as) important as the banking sector, weak supervision or weak preventive measures in that sector would be weighted much more heavily in IO.3. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "weak supervision or weak preventive measures in that sector would be weighted much more heavily in IO.3"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: As DNFBP examples of differing importance, the Methodology cites TCSPs in a trust and company formation centre, casinos in a country with a large gaming sector, and DPMS in countries with significant trade in gold or gemstones, where weaknesses would weigh more heavily in IO.4. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casinos in a country with a large gaming sector, DPMS in countries with a significant amount of trade"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For FIs and VASPs under IO.3 and DNFBPs under IO.4, assessors should explain their sector weighting in general terms (for example most important, highly important, moderately important or less important) rather than ranking each sector individually. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "explain how they have weighted the different sectors, in general terms"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Methodology considers an individual rank for each sector (1, 2, 3, ...) overly granular and an artificial distinction given the many types of FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs covered by the FATF Recommendations. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which would be overly granular and a rather artificial distinction"</sub>

## Legal persons and legal arrangements

- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should briefly describe the types of legal persons and legal arrangements that can be established or created in the country and that are relevant from an AML/CFT perspective, with their basic characteristics. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should briefly describe the types of legal persons and legal arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The description should give, to the extent possible, the numbers of legal persons and arrangements registered annually during the review period and their significance in the country and in the financial and DNFBP sectors. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as well as their numbers registered annually during the review period"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The description should cover international elements, particularly the extent to which the country acts as an international centre for creating or administering legal persons or arrangements, even if only as a source-of-law jurisdiction. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the extent to which the country acts as an international centre for the creation or administration"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The description should also cover the extent to which legal persons and arrangements created in, or under the law of, another jurisdiction hold assets or are used in the country. (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "created in another jurisdiction (or under the law of another jurisdiction) hold assets or are used in the country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should note, based on risk, materiality and context, the relative importance of different types of legal persons and arrangements, and explain that weighting to encourage consistency, particularly when assessing IO.5, R.24 (criterion 24.1) and R.25 (criterion 25.1). (p. 206)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to encourage consistent weighting throughout the MER, particularly when assessing IO.5, R.24"</sub>
