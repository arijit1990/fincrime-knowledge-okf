## Exchange of information between financial supervisors

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.13: financial supervisors should have a legal basis for co-operating with foreign counterparts (regardless of their nature or status), consistent with applicable international supervisory standards, in particular for exchanging supervisory information related or relevant to AML/CFT. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial supervisors should have a legal basis for providing co-operation with their foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.15: financial supervisors should be able to exchange, when relevant for AML/CFT and in particular with supervisors sharing responsibility for financial institutions in the same group, regulatory information (domestic regulatory system and general information on financial sectors); prudential information, in particular for Core Principles supervisors (business activities, beneficial ownership, management, fit and properness); and AML/CFT information (internal AML/CFT procedures and policies, CDD information, customer files, samples of accounts and transaction information). (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "internal AML/CFT procedures and policies of financial institutions, customer due diligence information, customer files"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.16: financial supervisors should be able to conduct inquiries on behalf of foreign counterparts and, as appropriate, authorise or facilitate foreign counterparts conducting inquiries themselves in the country, to facilitate effective group supervision. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to facilitate effective group supervision"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.17: financial supervisors should ensure they have the requested supervisor's prior authorisation for any dissemination or use of exchanged information for supervisory or non-supervisory purposes, unless the requesting supervisor is under a legal obligation to disclose or report it, in which case it should at a minimum promptly inform the requested authority of the obligation. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unless the requesting financial supervisor is under a legal obligation to disclose or report the information"</sub>

## Exchange of information between financial supervisors

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.14: financial supervisors should be able to share with foreign counterparts the information they have domestically, including information held by financial institutions, in a way that is proportionate to their respective needs. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including information held by financial institutions, in a manner proportionate to their respective needs"</sub>

## Exchange of information between law enforcement authorities

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.19: law enforcement authorities should be able to exchange domestically available information and co-operate with foreign counterparts to identify and trace criminal property and property of corresponding value, in support of freezing, seizing and confiscation through the formal mutual legal assistance process, and, in appropriate cases, commence domestic investigations or proceedings based on information received from foreign counterparts. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "commence domestic investigations or proceedings based on such information received from foreign counterparts, in appropriate cases"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.20: law enforcement authorities should, in appropriate cases, be able to spontaneously share relevant information about criminal property and property of corresponding value with foreign counterparts without a prior request, and spontaneously identify and trace such property if they suspect property relating to a foreign investigation may be in their jurisdiction. Footnote 180: LEAs should have discretion on when and under what conditions to share, for example so as not to prejudice domestic investigations. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have a discretion on when and under what conditions to share such information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.21: law enforcement authorities should be able to use their powers, including investigative techniques available under domestic law, to conduct inquiries and obtain information on behalf of foreign counterparts; regimes or practices governing such co-operation, such as agreements between Interpol, Europol or Eurojust and individual countries, should govern restrictions on use imposed by the requested authority. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "agreements between Interpol, Europol or Eurojust and individual countries"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.22: law enforcement authorities should be able to form joint investigative teams for cooperative investigations and, when necessary, establish bilateral or multilateral arrangements to enable them. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "form joint investigative teams to conduct cooperative investigations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.23: countries should take part in multilateral networks to facilitate rapid and constructive international co-operation in asset recovery, and apply for membership in a relevant Asset Recovery Inter-agency Network (ARIN) or other body supporting international co-operation in asset recovery. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply for membership in a relevant Asset Recovery Inter-agency Network (ARIN)"</sub>

## Exchange of information between non-counterparts

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.24: countries should permit competent authorities to exchange information indirectly with non-counterparts, applying the relevant principles above, and should ensure the authority requesting information indirectly always makes clear for what purpose and on whose behalf the request is made. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "always makes it clear for what purpose and on whose behalf the request is made"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 181: indirect exchange of information refers to requested information passing from the requested authority through one or more domestic or foreign authorities before reaching the requesting authority; such exchange and its use may be subject to authorisation by one or more competent authorities of the requested country. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "passing from the requested authority through one or more domestic or foreign authorities"</sub>
- **expected / context**: Footnote 179: competent authorities should be able to co-operate diagonally, meaning with non-counterparts, on an indirect basis. If the authorities responsible for the suspension power are of different types in the requesting and requested countries (for example law enforcement and FIU), the FIU should be able to send and receive counterparts' requests for assistance to allow indirect diagonal co-operation, and any related domestic information exchange with other competent authorities needed to facilitate it should be permitted. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Competent authorities should be able to co-operate diagonally (i.e. with non-counterparts) on an indirect basis"</sub>

## Immediate Outcome 1

- **descriptive / definition**: Immediate Outcome 1 is that ML and TF risks are identified, assessed and understood, and policies are co-operatively developed and, where appropriate, actions co-ordinated domestically to combat money laundering and the financing of terrorism. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money laundering and terrorist financing risks are identified, assessed and understood"</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **descriptive / context**: In an effective system for Immediate Outcome 1, a country properly identifies, assesses and understands its ML and TF risks with the involvement of competent and other relevant authorities and a wide range of reliable information sources, and uses the risk assessment(s) as a basis for developing and prioritising AML/CFT policies and effectively mitigating identified risks through proportionate measures, including enhanced measures where risks are higher and simplified measures where lower. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including enhanced measures where the risks are assessed to be higher and simplified measures where the risks are assessed to be lower"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An effective system also involves domestic co-operation and co-ordination to develop AML/CFT policies, communicating and implementing them in a co-ordinated way, with effective co-operation and, where appropriate, co-ordination and timely information sharing between competent authorities for operational purposes; over time this results in substantial mitigation of ML and TF risks. The outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 1, 2, 33 and 34 and elements of R.15. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 1, 2, 33 and 34 and elements of R.15."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors are not expected to re-assess the country's risk assessment(s); based on their view of its reasonableness and the country's context, they should focus on how well competent authorities have identified, assessed and understood ML/TF risks and use that understanding in practice to inform policy development and proportionate mitigating actions. Assessors should also take their IO.1 findings into account in assessing the other IOs. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors are not expected to re-assess the country’s assessment(s) of risks."</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issues for Immediate Outcome 1: how well the country identifies, assesses and understands its ML/TF risks (1.1); how well national AML/CFT policies and activities address identified risks (1.2); the extent to which risk assessment results justify exemptions and support enhanced measures for higher risk or simplified measures for lower risk (1.3); the extent to which objectives and activities of competent authorities and SRBs are consistent with evolving national policies and identified risks (1.4); the extent to which competent authorities and SRBs co-operate and co-ordinate policy development and implementation (1.5); and the extent to which competent authorities co-operate and, where appropriate, co-ordinate for operational purposes (1.6). (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well does the country identify, assess and understand its ML/TF risks?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 183: because forms of co-operation and co-ordination between authorities differ, core issues 1.5 and 1.6 do not prejudge a country's choice of a particular form and apply equally to all. Footnote 182: policy co-operation should have regard to AML/CFT requirements and data protection and privacy rules and similar provisions (e.g. data security/localisation) as needed. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "core issues 1.5 and 1.6 do not prejudge a country’s choice for a particular form"</sub>

## Examples of Information that could support the conclusions on Core Issues

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Examples of information supporting IO.1 conclusions include the country's assessment(s) of its ML/TF risks (types produced; types published or communicated) and AML/CFT policies and strategies (policies, strategies and statements communicated or published; engagement and commitment at senior official and political level). (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "types of assessment(s) produced; types of assessment(s) published / communicated"</sub>

## Examples of Specific Factors that could support the conclusions on Core Issues

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Specific factors for IO.1 include engagement of relevant authorities at policy and operational levels (frequency and relevancy of engagement, formal and informal channels, cases of successful inter-agency coordination); the methods, tools and information used to develop and review risk assessment conclusions and how comprehensive they are; the usefulness of strategic financial intelligence, analysis, typologies and guidance; and which authorities and stakeholders (including financial institutions and DNFBPs) provide input to national assessments, and at what stage. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How useful are strategic financial intelligence, analysis, typologies and guidance?"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: A further specific factor for IO.1 is whether the risk assessment(s) are kept up-to-date, reviewed regularly and responsive to significant events or developments, including new threats and trends. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "kept up-to-date, reviewed regularly and responsive to significant events or developments"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Further IO.1 specific factors: whether the risk assessment(s) are reasonable and consistent with ML/TF threats, vulnerabilities and specificities, including structural and contextual factors such as stable institutions, rule of law and level of corruption, and take account of risks identified by other credible sources where appropriate; and whether authorities' policies support proportionate measures and respond to changing ML/TF risks. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as stable institutions, the rule of law and the level of corruption"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Further IO.1 specific factors: the framework or body used to ensure regular co-operation and co-ordination at policymaking level and whether it includes all relevant authorities; the mechanisms for operational-level co-operation, clarity of authorities' roles and how interagency work is facilitated (e.g. joint teams or shared data platforms); whether interagency information sharing is timely and information needs and sources are clear, including measures such as standard formats and secure channels; and whether resources and expertise are adequate for risk assessments and domestic co-operation. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are there joint teams or shared data platforms"</sub>

## Immediate Outcome 2

- **descriptive / definition**: Immediate Outcome 2 is that international co-operation delivers appropriate information, financial intelligence and evidence, and facilitates action against criminals and their property. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International co-operation delivers appropriate information, financial intelligence and evidence"</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **descriptive / context**: In an effective system for Immediate Outcome 2, the country provides constructive and timely information or assistance when requested, helping to locate and extradite criminals and to identify, freeze, seize, confiscate and share criminal property and property of corresponding value and provide information (including evidence, financial intelligence, supervisory and beneficial ownership information); competent authorities also seek international co-operation to pursue criminals and criminal property, which over time makes the country an unattractive location for criminals, including terrorists. The outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 36 to 40 and elements of Recommendations 9, 15, 24, 25 and 32. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "makes the country an unattractive location for criminals (including terrorists) to operate in"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For Immediate Outcome 2, assessors should consider how their findings on competent authorities' role in seeking and delivering international co-operation affect other IOs, including how the country seeks co-operation on domestic cases, and how findings under other IOs may affect their view of IO.2 (while avoiding duplication); the international co-operation section should begin with an introductory paragraph on the overall importance of international co-operation given the country's risk and context. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an introductory paragraph identifying and explaining the team’s findings on the overall importance of international co-operation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should consider whether international co-operation efforts are aligned with risk, taking into account the overall extent, timeliness and prioritisation of co-operation on AML/CFT activities, the nature or type of co-operation, the offences or matters to which assistance or requests relate, and the countries to or from which requests were made or received. Footnote 184 notes that countries have little control over the number or type of requests received. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the overall extent, timeliness and prioritisation of co-operation on AML/CFT activities"</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 2.1 asks to what extent the country has provided constructive and timely mutual legal assistance and extradition in response to requests (e.g. evidence, locating and extraditing criminals for ML, associated predicate offences and TF) and to facilitate asset recovery including enforcement of foreign freezing, seizing and confiscation orders, and what the quality of that assistance is. Core issue 2.2 asks to what extent the country has sought MLA and extradition in an appropriate and timely manner for the same purposes, including foreign enforcement of orders. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To what extent has the country provided constructive and timely mutual legal assistance and extradition to respond to requests"</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **expected / expectation**: Under IO.2, assessors should give appropriate weight both to the quality and impact of international co-operation and to the number of co-operation requests made, in light of the country's risk profile. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should give appropriate weight both to the quality and impact185 of international co-operation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under IO.2, processes and procedures for seeking or providing co-operation may matter to the extent they affect effectiveness, but assessors should avoid focusing excessively on them or repeating information covered in the technical compliance Annex. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should avoid focusing excessively on these factors or repeating information covered in the technical compliance Annex"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The IO.2 core issues divide into 'formal' international co-operation (mutual legal assistance and extradition; core issues 2.1 and 2.2) and other, more 'informal' co-operation (for example direct or indirect communication between counterpart authorities and assistance via regional or international mechanisms; core issues 2.3 and 2.4). (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "mutual legal assistance and extradition; core issues 2.1 and 2.2"</sub>
- **recommended / expectation**: Assessors should consider the links between formal and informal international co-operation in the assessed country and how informal co-operation is used to support formal co-operation. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should consider the links between these two types of co-operation in the assessed country"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Methodology states that in practice, informal co-operation will often be an essential element underpinning successful formal co-operation. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "informal co-operation will often be an essential element that underpins successful formal co-operation"</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 2.3 asks to what extent the different competent authorities use other forms of international co-operation to seek information or assistance from foreign authorities, appropriately and in a timely manner, for AML/CFT purposes including asset recovery. It is to include all relevant types of information (criminal records and intelligence and other suspect identity information, financial information, financial intelligence, and basic and beneficial ownership information) and covers information and assistance from relevant competent authorities such as supervisors, FIUs, law enforcement agencies, asset recovery or asset management authorities, and customs and tax authorities. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to seek information or assistance from foreign authorities in an appropriate and timely manner"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 2.4 asks to what extent competent authorities use other forms of international co-operation to provide information or assistance to foreign authorities in a constructive and timely manner (including spontaneously) for AML/CFT purposes, including asset recovery, covering the same types of information and authorities as core issue 2.3. (p. 128)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to provide information or assistance to foreign authorities in a constructive and timely manner (including spontaneously)"</sub>
