## RECOMMENDATION 4 CONFISCATION AND PROVISIONAL MEASURES

- **descriptive / context**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 4: assessors should refer to listed Glossary definitions (including asset recovery, confiscation, criminal property, ex parte, freeze, non-conviction based confiscation, proceeds, property and seize) and paragraph 19 of the Introduction. For all criteria with requirements on criminal property and/or property of corresponding value, these apply whether the property is owned or held by a criminal defendant or a third party, without prejudicing the rights of bona fide third parties. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without prejudicing the rights of bona fide third parties"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.1 (general principles): countries should (a) have policies and operational frameworks that prioritise asset recovery domestically and internationally; (b) periodically review their asset recovery regime for ongoing effectiveness; (c) provide sufficient resources to pursue asset recovery effectively; and (d) consistent with Recommendation 2, ensure domestic co-operation and co-ordination frameworks and agency structures enabling effective use of the regime. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have policies and operational frameworks that prioritise asset recovery in both the domestic and international context"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.2 (investigative measures): countries should have measures, including legislative measures, enabling competent authorities to identify, trace and evaluate criminal property and property of corresponding value, and to take any appropriate investigative measures. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify, trace and evaluate criminal property and property of corresponding value"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.3(a): countries should have measures, including legislative ones, allowing the FIU or another competent authority, in response to relevant information, to act immediately, directly or indirectly, by withholding consent to or suspending a transaction suspected of involving money laundering, a predicate offence or terrorist financing. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "withhold consent to or suspend a transaction suspected of being related to money laundering"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.3(b): countries should specify the maximum duration of this suspension measure, which should allow sufficient time to analyse the transaction and for competent authorities to initiate, where appropriate, action to freeze or seize. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ensure that the maximum duration of this measure"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.4: countries should have measures, including legislative, enabling competent authorities to carry out provisional measures expeditiously, including (a) freezing and seizing to prevent any dealing, transfer or disposal of criminal property and property of corresponding value; (b) allowing the initial application to freeze or seize to be made ex parte or without prior notice; and (c) ensuring provisional measures do not have unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions, such as in relation to demonstrating the risk of dissipation. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not have unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions for effective action"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 42: ex parte proceedings may be subject to appropriate safeguards under domestic law, including triggering notice or inter partes review after the provisional measure is implemented. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including triggering notice or inter partes review after the implementation of the provisional measure"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.5: countries should enable competent authorities to freeze and seize criminal property and property of corresponding value without a court order when necessary to act as expeditiously as possible, with such actions reviewable through judicial proceedings within a period of time. If this is inconsistent with fundamental principles of domestic law, a country should have an alternative mechanism enabling authorities to systematically act quickly enough to prevent dissipation. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without a court order when it is necessary to act as expeditiously as possible"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.6: countries should have measures, including legislative, enabling competent authorities to take steps to prevent or void actions that prejudice the country's ability to freeze, seize or confiscate criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "take steps that will prevent or void actions that prejudice"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.7 (confiscation): countries should have measures, including legislative, enabling confiscation of criminal property and property of corresponding value after a person is convicted. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable the confiscation of criminal property and property of corresponding value after a person is convicted"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Footnote 43: in assessing criteria 4.7 to 4.9, assessors should consider whether the measures, including legislative measures, are comprehensive, for example as regards the crimes covered, application to natural and legal persons and to all types of property, and whether there are monetary thresholds. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should consider whether the measures, including legislative measures, are comprehensive in nature"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.8: countries should have measures enabling confiscation to be extended to other property of a person convicted of ML, predicate offences or TF where the court is satisfied that such property is derived from criminal conduct, to the extent consistent with fundamental principles of domestic law. Footnote 44: countries may limit extended confiscation to serious offences consistent with R.3. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to enable confiscation to be extended to other property of a person convicted of money laundering"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.9: countries should have measures enabling confiscation of criminal property without requiring a criminal conviction (non-conviction based confiscation) in cases involving ML, predicate offences or TF, to the extent consistent with fundamental principles of domestic law. Footnote 46: countries have flexibility in how they implement it; footnote 47: they may limit it to serious offences consistent with R.3. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without requiring a criminal conviction (non-conviction based confiscation)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.10 (asset recovery and tax authorities): to enhance asset recovery and support identification of criminal property, countries should enable competent authorities and tax authorities to co-operate and, where appropriate, co-ordinate and share information domestically. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable their competent authorities and tax authorities to co-operate and, where appropriate, co-ordinate and share information domestically"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.11 (asset management): countries should have mechanisms for managing, preserving and, when necessary, disposing of frozen, seized or confiscated property, including, where appropriate, pre-confiscation sale of property. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "managing, preserving and, when necessary, disposing of, frozen, seized, or confiscated property"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.12: countries should have measures enabling them to enforce a confiscation order and realise the property or value subject to it, leading to permanent deprivation of that property or value. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leading to the permanent deprivation of the property or value subject to the order"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.13: countries should have mechanisms to return confiscated property to its prior legitimate owners and to use confiscated property to compensate victims of crime. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use confiscated property to compensate victims of crime"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 4.9: countries should have measures, including legislative ones, enabling confiscation of criminal property without a criminal conviction (non-conviction based confiscation) in cases involving money laundering, predicate offences or terrorist financing, to the extent consistent with fundamental principles of domestic law. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without requiring a criminal conviction (non-conviction based confiscation)"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 45: in deciding whether property derives from criminal conduct, considerations could include whether its value reflects the proceeds of a criminal lifestyle, is disproportionate to the convicted person's lawful income, or whether the offender can show the property has a lawful origin. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "considerations could include, for example, whether the value of the property represents the proceeds of a criminal lifestyle"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 46: countries have flexibility in how they implement non-conviction based confiscation. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries have flexibility in how they implement non-conviction based confiscation."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 47: countries may limit non-conviction based confiscation to serious offences consistent with R.3. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may limit the application of non-conviction based confiscation to serious offences consistent with R.3."</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 5 TERRORIST FINANCING OFFENCE

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.1: countries should criminalise TF on the basis of the Terrorist Financing Convention; footnote 48 says criminalisation should be consistent with Article 2 of the International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should criminalise TF on the basis of the Terrorist Financing Convention."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.3: TF offences should include financing the travel of individuals who travel to a State other than their States of residence or nationality to perpetrate, plan, prepare or participate in terrorist acts, or to provide or receive terrorist training. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TF offences should include financing the travel of individuals who travel to a State other than their States of residence or nationality"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.4: TF offences should extend to any funds or other assets, whether from a legitimate or illegitimate source. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TF offences should extend to any funds or other assets whether from a legitimate or illegitimate source"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.5: TF offences should not require that the funds or other assets were actually used to carry out or attempt a terrorist act(s), or be linked to a specific terrorist act(s). (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "were actually used to carry out or attempt a terrorist act(s)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.6: it should be possible for the intent and knowledge required to prove the TF offence to be inferred from objective factual circumstances. Criterion 5.7: proportionate and dissuasive criminal sanctions should apply to natural persons convicted of TF. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Proportionate and dissuasive criminal sanctions should apply to natural persons convicted of TF."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.8: criminal liability and sanctions should apply to legal persons and, where not possible due to fundamental principles of domestic law, civil or administrative liability and sanctions; this should not preclude parallel proceedings where more than one form of liability is available, is without prejudice to natural persons' criminal liability, and all sanctions should be proportionate and dissuasive. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All sanctions should be proportionate and dissuasive."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.2: TF offences should cover any person who wilfully provides or collects funds or other assets by any means, directly or indirectly, intending or knowing that they will be used in full or in part (a) to carry out a terrorist act or acts, or (b) by a terrorist organisation or an individual terrorist, including where no link to a specific terrorist act exists. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "even in the absence of a link to a specific terrorist act or acts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 49: criminalising TF only on the basis of aiding and abetting, attempt or conspiracy is not enough to comply with the Recommendation. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Criminalising TF solely on the basis of aiding and abetting, attempt, or conspiracy is not sufficient"</sub>
- **mandatory / context**: Footnote 50: contribution by a group under criterion 5.9(d) shall be intentional and shall either be made with the aim of furthering the criminal activity or purpose of the group where that involves a TF offence, or be made in the knowledge of the group's intention to commit a TF offence. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such contribution shall be intentional and shall either"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.10: TF offences should be designated as ML predicate offences. Criterion 5.11: TF offences should apply regardless of whether the alleged offender is in the same or a different country from where the terrorist(s) or terrorist organisation(s) are located or the terrorist act(s) occurred or will occur. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TF offences should be designated as ML predicate offences."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 5.9: it should also be an offence to attempt the TF offence; to take part as an accomplice in a TF offence or attempt; to organise or direct others to commit one; and to contribute to one or more TF offences or attempts by a group acting with a common purpose. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "organise or direct others to commit a TF offence or attempted offence"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 6 TARGETED FINANCIAL SANCTIONS RELATED TO TERRORISM AND TERRORIST FINANCING

- **descriptive / context**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 6: assessors should refer to Glossary definitions including designated person or entity, designation, ex parte, freeze, funds or other assets, targeted financial sanctions, third parties and without delay. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designated person or entity, designation, ex parte, financial institutions, freeze, funds or other assets"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 6.1 (UN 1267/1989 and 1988 regimes): countries should (a) identify a competent authority or court responsible for proposing persons or entities to the 1267/1989 Committee and to the 1988 Committee for designation; (b) have mechanism(s) for identifying designation targets based on the criteria in the relevant UNSCRs; (c) apply an evidentiary standard of "reasonable grounds" or "reasonable basis" when deciding whether to propose a designation, which should not be conditional on a criminal proceeding; (d) follow the relevant committee's procedures and standard forms for listing; and (e) provide as much relevant information as possible on the proposed name. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply an evidentiary standard of proof of"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 6.1(e) continued: proposals should include a statement of case containing as much detail as possible on the basis for listing and, when proposing names to the 1267/1989 Committee, specify whether their status as a designating state may be made known. Footnote 51: identifying information should allow accurate and positive identification and, to the extent possible, include information Interpol requires for a Special Notice; footnote 52: the statement of case should be releasable on request except parts a Member State identifies as confidential to the committee; footnote 53 lists supporting detail to include. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "specify whether their status as a designating state may be made known"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 6.2 (UNSCR 1373): countries should (a) identify a competent authority or court responsible for designating persons or entities meeting UNSCR 1373 criteria, on the country's own motion or after examining and giving effect to, if appropriate, another country's request; (b) have mechanism(s) for identifying designation targets based on UNSCR 1373 criteria; and (c) on receiving a request, promptly determine whether they are satisfied, per applicable (supra-)national principles, that it is supported by reasonable grounds or a reasonable basis to suspect or believe the proposed designee meets the criteria. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "make a prompt determination of whether they are satisfied"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 6.2(d)-(e): countries should apply an evidentiary standard of "reasonable grounds" or "reasonable basis" when deciding whether to make a designation, with (proposals for) designations not conditional on a criminal proceeding; and, when asking another country to give effect to freezing actions, provide as much identifying and specific supporting information as possible. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide as much identifying information and specific information supporting the designation, as possible"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 6.3(a): the competent authority(ies) should have legal authorities and procedures or mechanisms to collect or solicit information to identify persons and entities who, on reasonable grounds or a reasonable basis to suspect or believe, meet the designation criteria. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collect or solicit information to identify persons and entities that, based on reasonable grounds"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 6.3(b): the competent authority(ies) should have legal authorities and procedures or mechanisms to act ex parte against an identified person or entity whose designation (or proposed designation) is being considered. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "operate ex parte against a person or entity who has been identified"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 55: a country should apply the standard of its own legal system on the kind and quantum of evidence needed to find that "reasonable grounds" or "reasonable basis" exist for a designation, whether the proposal is the country's own motion or at another country's request. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A country should apply the legal standard of its own legal system regarding the kind and quantum of evidence"</sub>
