## Structural Elements

- **expected / expectation**: If assessors have serious concerns that any structural element underpinning an effective AML/CFT/CPF system is weak or absent, they should highlight those concerns in this section. Assessors are not expected to reach a general conclusion on the extent to which such factors are present. (p. 207)[^original]

## Background and other Contextual Factors

- **descriptive / context**: Examples of contextual factors given include the maturity and sophistication of the AML/CFT/CPF regime and its institutions; the maturity of the criminal justice, regulatory, supervisory and administrative regime; the level of corruption and the impact of measures against it; the level of financial exclusion; exposure to organised crime risks; and regional instability, including armed conflict, climate-related events, natural disasters or irregular migration flows (domestic or in neighbouring countries). (p. 207)[^original]

## AML/CFT/CPF strategy

- **expected / expectation**: The AML/CFT/CPF strategy section of the MER should set out the Government's main policies and objectives for combating money laundering and terrorist financing, including its priorities and any wider policy objectives (such as financial inclusion) that affect the strategy. (p. 207)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "set out the main policies and objectives of the Government for combating money laundering and terrorist financing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Relevant policies and objectives for combating the financing of proliferation should also be set out in the strategy section. (p. 207)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any relevant policies and objectives for combating the financing of proliferation should also be set out in this section."</sub>

## Legal & institutional framework

- **expected / expectation**: MER paragraph 22 says assessors should give a brief overview of the ministries, agencies and authorities responsible for formulating and implementing the government's AML/CFT and proliferation financing policies, including relevant supra-national or sub-national (state, province or local) authorities, with the principal role of each body and the bodies responsible for combating proliferation financing. (p. 207)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should give a brief overview of which ministries, agencies and authorities are responsible"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should indicate any significant changes to the institutional framework since the last MER, including the rationale for those changes. (p. 207)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should indicate any significant changes since the last MER to the institutional framework"</sub>

## Background and other Contextual Factors

- **expected / expectation**: Under paragraph 20 of the MER template, assessors should note domestic and international contextual factors that might significantly influence how effective the country's AML/CFT/CPF measures are, as described in paragraph 11 of the Methodology. (p. 207)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should note domestic and international contextual factors that might significantly influence the effectiveness"</sub>

## Legal & institutional framework

- **expected / expectation**: The country's AML/CFT/CPF legal framework should be summarised only briefly in this section; detailed description and analysis of each element belongs in the technical annex. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Detailed description and analysis of each element is not necessary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should describe the co-operation and coordination mechanisms the country uses to help develop and implement AML/CFT policies and policies for combating the financing of proliferation. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should describe the co-operation and coordination mechanisms used by the country"</sub>

## Preventive measures

- **expected / expectation**: The preventive measures section (paragraph 23) should set out the legal or other enforceable instruments through which preventive measures are applied and the scope of the obligations, and should briefly identify any problems with the scope of AML/CFT obligations. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section should set out the legal (or other enforceable) instruments through which they are applied"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where a country has exempted specific sectors or activities from the requirements, the exemptions should be noted. Assessors should indicate whether the exemptions meet the criteria in R.1 and whether they consider them justified based on the country's ML/TF risk assessment(s). (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should indicate whether such exemptions meet the criteria set out in R.1"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: This section should also note cases where a country has decided, based on risk, to require AML/CFT preventive measures to be applied by additional sectors that are normally outside the scope of the FATF Recommendations. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by additional sectors which are normally outside the scope of the FATF Recommendations"</sub>

## Supervisory arrangements

  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should set out the institutional arrangements for supervision and oversight of financial institutions, VASPs and DNFBPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The section should also note the institutional framework for legal persons and arrangements, including any authorities responsible for their creation, registration and supervision. (p. 208)[^original]

## Chapter 1. Assessment of Risks, Co-ordination and Policy Setting

## Key Findings

- **expected / expectation**: In the Key Findings, assessors should briefly summarise their conclusions for the chapter and highlight the most significant findings.   <sub>anchor: "Key findings and key recommended actions should be consistent on the substance without a need to strictly mirror each other."</sub>

## Key Recommended Actions (KRA)

- **expected / expectation**: The Key Recommended Actions section should set out a targeted and prioritised set of recommendations on how the country should improve its level of effectiveness and its compliance with the FATF Recommendations, covering the Immediate Outcomes and Recommendations in the chapter and addressing both technical deficiencies and practical implementation or effectiveness issues. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section should set out a targeted and prioritised set of recommendations on"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should clearly indicate which Immediate Outcome(s) or Recommendation(s) each recommended action is intended to address, and follow the same general approach in other chapters of the MER. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should clearly indicate which Immediate Outcome(s) or"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Key Recommended Actions (KRA) should be listed separately from Other Recommended Actions. If IO.1 is rated HE or SE, the KRA section is deleted and all recommended actions for the chapter go in the Other Recommended Actions section. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If IO.1 is rated HE or SE, delete this section and reflect all recommended actions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Key Recommended Actions only relate to Immediate Outcomes rated ME or LE, or to Recommendations rated PC or NC where these relate to an IO rated ME or LE. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Key Recommended Actions only relate to Immediate Outcomes rated ME or LE"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: There should not normally be more than 2-3 KRAs per Immediate Outcome, including any KRA that concerns a related Recommendation. In exceptional cases assessors may also set out a limited number of KRAs on contextual factors, which should be linked to an explanation in the MER of the grounds for the action and its intended impact on the country's effective compliance with the FATF Standards. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There should not normally be more than 2-3 KRAs per Immediate Outcome, including any KRA that concerns a related Recommendation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The report should prioritise KRAs for remedial measures, taking into account the country's risks and context, its level of effectiveness, and the weaknesses and problems identified. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report should prioritise KRA for remedial measures, taking into account the"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors' recommendations should not simply address each deficiency or weakness identified, but should add value by identifying and prioritising specific, targeted measures that most effectively mitigate the country's risks and deficiencies, taking contextual factors into account. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but should add value by identifying and prioritising specific and targeted measures in order to most effectively mitigate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should consider the country's circumstances, context and legal and institutional system when making recommendations, noting that there are several different ways to achieve an effective AML/CFT/CPF system and that the assessors' own preferred model may not suit the country assessed. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "noting that there are several different ways to achieve an effective AML/CFT/CPF system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should work together with the country to identify the measures needed so that meaningful recommendations can be made. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should work together with the country to identify the measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendations, particularly KRAs, should be practical, achievable, precise and clear without being overly prescriptive; they should also be measurable and time-bound so progress can be benchmarked, and outcome oriented and targeted so that they increase effectiveness. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They also should be measurable and time-bound, so that the progress achieved can be benchmarked"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: To help the assessed country develop an action plan, assessors should clearly indicate where a specific action is required and where there is some flexibility in how a priority objective is achieved. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should clearly indicate in their recommendations where a specific action is required and where there may be some flexibility"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should avoid unnecessarily rigid or overly detailed recommendations (for example on the scheduling of certain measures or the prosecution of specific persons) so as not to hinder countries from adapting recommendations to local circumstances. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should avoid making unnecessarily rigid or overly detailed recommendations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The numbering of the KRAs should correspond to the numbering used in the KRA Roadmap. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The numbering of the KRAs should correspond to the numbering used in the"</sub>

## Other Recommended Actions

- **expected / expectation**: If IO.1 is rated HE or SE, all recommended actions for the chapter should appear in the Other Recommended Actions section. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If IO.1 is rated HE or SE, all recommended actions for this chapter should appear"</sub>

## Key Recommended Actions (KRA)

- **expected / expectation**: The report should prioritise Key Recommended Actions for remedial measures, taking into account the country's risks and context, its level of effectiveness, and the weaknesses and problems identified. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report should prioritise KRA for remedial measures, taking into account the"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Prioritisation of measures could be on the basis that they offer the greatest and most rapid practical improvements, have the widest-reaching effects, or are easiest to achieve. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This could be on the basis that they offer the greatest and most rapid practical improvements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When making recommendations, assessors should be careful to consider the country's circumstances, context and legal and institutional system, noting that there are several different ways to achieve an effective AML/CFT/CPF system and that the assessors' own preferred model may not be appropriate for the country assessed. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "noting that there are several different ways to achieve an effective AML/CFT/CPF system"</sub>

If assessors can identify further actions in areas of high effectiveness, they should include them in their recommendations. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Even if a country has a high level of effectiveness, this does not imply that there"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Ordinarily, there should be no more than five recommended actions per Immediate Outcome. (p. 212)[^original]

## Overall Conclusions on IO.1

- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should indicate the effectiveness rating for the Immediate Outcome. In deciding the overall level of effectiveness they should take into account (a) the core issues, (b) relevant technical compliance issues/deficiencies significantly impacting effectiveness, (c) risks and contextual factors, and (d) the level of effectiveness in other relevant Immediate Outcomes that significantly impact effectiveness in this area. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When deciding on the overall level of effectiveness, assessors should take into account"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should briefly explain their conclusion on the effectiveness rating and be explicit about the weight and importance attached to the elements taken into account. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They should be explicit about the weight and importance they attach to the elements taken into account."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The overall conclusion should not duplicate the Key Findings section at the start of each chapter and should ideally be no more than one or two paragraphs long. The same approach applies to all other Immediate Outcomes. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The conclusion should not duplicate the Key Findings section at the beginning of each chapter"</sub>

## Chapter 1. Assessment of Risks, Co-ordination and Policy Setting

- **expected / expectation**: The IO.1 analysis section should open with any general considerations about the country's risks and context that affect the assessment, and should summarise assessors' general impression of whether the country appears to exhibit the characteristics of an effective system. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The first paragraph(s) should note any general considerations regarding the"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should cover each of the Core Issues in their analysis and are strongly encouraged to use the template sub-headings to structure it. Additional sub-headings may be used where necessary, or to indicate that a Core Issue is not applicable in a particular country and why. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should cover each of the Core Issues in their analysis."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors retain flexibility to amend, add or delete sub-headings for other IOs, but in all cases sub-headings should be neutral and not give any qualitative comment on how the country is performing on a given IO. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In all cases sub- headings should be neutral and not provide any qualitative comment"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should note the main sources of information and evidence used. They are not required to use all the information listed in the Methodology but should set out the information and evidence that materially influences their conclusion, and should note any technical compliance issues that influence the level of effectiveness. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors are not required to use all the information noted in the Methodology"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Factors assessed under IO.1 on the country's risk assessment and implementation of the risk-based approach may have far-reaching effects on other outcomes; for example, risk assessment affects risk-based measures under IO.3 and IO.4 and the deployment of competent authorities' resources across all outcomes. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may have far-reaching effects on other outcomes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where possible assessors should avoid duplication: they should present the analysis of a particular issue once, in the most relevant section of the MER, and cross-reference it elsewhere where the issue is relevant. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should present their analysis of a particular issue once, in what they consider"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The IO.1 template structure comprises: 1.1 the country's identification, assessment and understanding of its ML/TF risks (ML risks, TF risks); 1.2 national policies and activities to address identified ML/TF risks; 1.3 exemptions, enhanced and simplified ML/TF measures; 1.4 objectives and activities of competent authorities and SRBs; 1.5 national coordination and cooperation to develop and implement policy; and 1.6 national coordination and cooperation for operational purposes. (p. 213)[^original]
