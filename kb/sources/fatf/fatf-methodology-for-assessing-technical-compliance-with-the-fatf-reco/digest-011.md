## (DNFBPS): CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 22.1: DNFBPs should be required to comply with the Recommendation 10 CDD requirements in the situations set out in sub-criteria (a) to (e). (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DNFBPs should be required to comply with the CDD requirements set out in Recommendation 10 in the following situations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 22.1(a): casinos should apply CDD when customers engage in financial transactions equal to or above USD/EUR 3 000. Footnote 105: customer identification at casino entry could be, but is not necessarily, sufficient; countries must require casinos to be able to link CDD information on a customer to the transactions the customer conducts in the casino; "financial transactions" does not refer to gambling transactions involving only casino chips or tokens. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when customers engage in financial transactions105 equal to or above USD/EUR 3 000"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 22.1(b): real estate agents should apply CDD when involved in transactions for a client concerning buying and selling real estate. Footnote 106 says this means CDD with respect to both the purchasers and the vendors of the property. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when they are involved in transactions for a client concerning the buying and selling of real estate"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 22.1(c): dealers in precious metals and dealers in precious stones should apply CDD when they engage in any cash transaction with a customer equal to or above USD/EUR 15,000. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when they engage in any cash transaction with a customer equal to or above USD/EUR 15,000"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 22.1(d): lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants should apply CDD when they prepare for, or carry out, transactions for clients concerning buying and selling of real estate; managing client money, securities or other assets; management of bank, savings or securities accounts; organising contributions for the creation, operation or management of companies; and creating, operating or managing legal persons or arrangements and buying and selling business entities. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when they prepare for, or carry out, transactions for their client concerning the following activities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 22.1(e): trust and company service providers should apply CDD when they prepare for or carry out transactions for a client concerning acting as a formation agent of legal persons; acting as (or arranging for another person to act as) a director or secretary of a company, partner of a partnership or similar position; providing a registered office, business address, accommodation, correspondence or administrative address; acting as (or arranging for another to act as) a trustee of an express trust or equivalent; or acting as (or arranging for another to act as) a nominee shareholder. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "acting as (or arranging for another person to act as) a nominee shareholder for another person"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criteria 22.2 to 22.5: in the situations in criterion 22.1, DNFBPs should be required to comply with the record-keeping requirements of Recommendation 11, the PEP requirements of Recommendation 12, the new technologies requirements of Recommendation 15, and the reliance on third parties requirements of Recommendation 17. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DNFBPs should be required to comply with the reliance on third-parties requirements set out in Recommendation 17"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 23 DNFBPS: OTHER MEASURES

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 23.1: the Recommendation 20 suspicious transaction reporting requirements should apply to all DNFBPs, with qualifications: lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants when, on behalf of or for a client, they engage in a financial transaction relating to the activities in criterion 22.1(d); dealers in precious metals or stones when they engage in a cash transaction with a customer equal to or above USD/EUR 15,000; and trust and company service providers when they engage in a transaction for a client relating to the activities in criterion 22.1(e). (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The requirements to report suspicious transactions set out in Recommendation 20 should apply to all DNFBPs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criteria 23.2 to 23.4: in the situations in criterion 23.1, DNFBPs should be required to comply with the internal controls requirements of Recommendation 18, the higher-risk countries requirements of Recommendation 19, and the tipping-off and confidentiality requirements of Recommendation 21. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DNFBPs should be required to comply with the higher-risk countries requirements set out in Recommendation 19"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 107: lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants acting as independent legal professionals are not required to report suspicious transactions if the information was obtained in circumstances subject to professional secrecy or legal professional privilege; each country determines what matters fall under privilege or secrecy, which would normally cover information received from or obtained through a client in ascertaining the client's legal position, or in defending or representing the client in judicial, administrative, arbitration or mediation proceedings. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not required to report suspicious transactions if the relevant information was obtained in circumstances"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 108: where countries allow lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants to send STRs to their appropriate self-regulatory bodies (SRBs), there should be forms of co-operation between those bodies and the FIU. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there should be forms of co-operation between these bodies and the FIU"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The Note to Assessors for Recommendation 23 says that, when assessing criterion 23.2, assessors should consider whether DNFBPs are required to comply with the internal control requirements in criteria 18.1, 18.2 and 18.3. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether DNFBPs are required to comply with the internal control requirements set out in criteria 18.1, 18.2 and 18.3"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 109 states that when lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals, or accountants acting as independent legal professionals try to discourage a client from illegal activity, that does not count as tipping-off. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seek to dissuade a client from engaging in illegal activity, this does not amount to tipping-off"</sub>

## PERSONS

- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 24: if assessors identify scope deficiencies (for example, some types of legal person not covered by R.24 requirements), they should assess them only in criterion 24.1 and not cascade them into other criteria that address the presence and adequacy of specific R.24 requirements. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should assess this only in criterion 24.1 and not cascade the deficiency(ies) into other criteria"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In weighting criterion 24.1, individual criteria do not have equal importance and the number of criteria met is not always an indication of overall compliance with R.24; the importance of a scope deficiency depends on the materiality of each type of legal person created in the country, the extent to which each type is covered by R.24, and the significance of the deficiency given the country's risk profile and other structural and contextual information, including whether it is a company formation centre. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "individual criteria do not have equal importance and the number of criteria met is not always an indication"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 110 notes that for companies, the measures required by R.24 are set out with specific reference to companies. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The measures required by R.24 are set out with specific reference to companies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 110 says that for foundations, Anstalt and limited liability partnerships, countries should take measures and impose requirements similar to those for companies, allowing for the different forms and structures of these entities. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Foundations, Anstalt, and limited liability partnerships - countries should take similar measures and impose similar requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 110 says that for other types of legal persons, countries should take into account their different forms and structures and the ML/TF risk levels of each type, aiming for appropriate transparency. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should take into account the different forms and structures of those other legal persons"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 110 adds that, as a minimum, all legal persons should ensure that similar types of basic information are recorded. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At a minimum, all legal persons should ensure that similar types of basic information are recorded"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 2: if assessors find a scope deficiency, they should assess it only under criterion 24.1 and should not carry it over into other criteria that look at whether the specific R.24 requirements are present and adequate. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should assess this only in criterion 24.1 and not cascade the deficiency(ies) into other criteria"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Criterion 24.6 assessment should focus on the requirements and mechanisms a country has implemented for beneficial ownership information, whereas criterion 24.8 focuses on whether the information collected is adequate, accurate and up-to-date; any deficiency in adequacy, accuracy or currency should be noted under 24.8 and not elsewhere. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such deficiencies should be noted under criterion 24.8 (not elsewhere in other criteria)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 2(c): assessors should explain the basis on which they weight a scope deficiency under criterion 24.1. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should explain the basis for their weighting"</sub>
- **permissive / finding or result**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 2(c): a particularly serious scope deficiency could lead to an NC or PC rating even when all the other criteria are met. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a particularly serious scope deficiency(ies) could result in a NC or PC rating even if all other criteria are met"</sub>
- **permissive / finding or result**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 2(c): several scope deficiencies that are each relatively minor could lead to an LC rating. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "multiple (but relatively minor) scope deficiencies could result in an LC rating"</sub>
- **permissive / case example**: Footnote 113 gives an example: an NC or PC rating could be justified if companies, normally the most materially important type of legal person, are not subject to the basic R.24 requirements while all other types are fully covered, depending on the relative material importance and risk of those other types. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NC or PC rating could be justified if companies (which are normally the most materially important type of legal person"</sub>
- **permissive / case example**: Footnote 113 gives a contrasting example: an LC rating could be justified if companies and other materially important types of legal person are subject to most R.24 requirements, but some other types that are neither materially important nor high risk are completely outside the scope of R.24. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an LC rating could be justified if companies and other types of legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 3: sub-criterion 24.1(d) does not require countries to apply measures to individual foreign-created legal persons. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not require countries to apply measures to individual foreign-created legal persons"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 5: when assessing criterion 24.6, assessors should confirm that the country has the compulsory company approach described in sub-criterion 24.6(a). (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the compulsory company approach described in sub-criterion 24.6(a)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 5(b): assessors should also confirm that the country requires either a public authority or body to hold beneficial ownership information (24.6(b)(i)) or an alternative mechanism (24.6(b)(ii)); a country that chooses an alternative mechanism should demonstrate that it gives efficient access to BO information. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should demonstrate that the alternative provides efficient access to BO information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for R.24, paragraph 5(c): assessors should confirm that the country also has additional supplementary measures, as necessary, to ensure that the beneficial ownership of a company can be determined. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "additional supplementary measures as necessary to ensure the beneficial ownership of a company can be determined"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 114 states that relying on basic information or existing information alone, such as BO information held by financial institutions and DNFBPs under R.10 and R.22, is not enough to qualify as an alternative mechanism. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is not sufficient to qualify as an alternative mechanism"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 114 adds that countries may consider using that existing information to develop an alternative mechanism that gives competent authorities efficient access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider utilising this information to develop an alternative mechanism"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 114 states that identifying, and taking reasonable measures to verify the identity of, the natural person who is the senior managing official in the circumstances of paragraph 5.b(i.iii) of INR.10 does not amount to collecting beneficial ownership information as the Glossary defines it. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not constitute collecting beneficial ownership information as that term is defined in the Glossary"</sub>

## Scope extends to companies and other legal persons

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.1(c): for other types of legal persons, countries should consider their forms, structures and associated ML/TF risk levels with a view to appropriate transparency and, at a minimum, ensure similar types of basic information are recorded and kept accurate and up-to-date by such legal persons and are accessible in a timely way by competent authorities. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accessible in a timely way by competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.1(d): for foreign-created legal persons, countries should ensure that criteria 24.3(b) and 24.10 are applied by relevant authorities to types of foreign-created legal persons that present ML/TF risks and have sufficient links with the country. Footnote 116: countries may determine sufficient link on the basis of risk, with example tests such as permanent establishment, branch or agency, significant business activity, significant and ongoing relations with AML/CFT-regulated financial institutions or DNFBPs, significant real estate or other local investment, employing staff, or tax residency. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "types of foreign-created legal persons that present ML/TF risks and have sufficient links116 with the country"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.2: countries should have mechanisms that identify, describe and make publicly available information on the different types, forms and basic features of legal persons in the country; the processes for creating legal persons; and the processes for obtaining and recording basic and beneficial ownership information on legal persons. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "mechanisms that identify, describe and make publicly available the information regarding"</sub>

## Risk assessment and risk mitigation

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.3(a): countries should assess the ML/TF risks associated with the different types of legal persons created in the country and take appropriate steps to manage and mitigate the risks identified; for the other types of legal persons in 24.1(c), this means reviewing their ML/TF risks and, based on the level of risk, determining the measures needed to ensure competent authorities have timely access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should assess the ML/TF risks"</sub>

## Scope extends to companies and other legal persons

- **expected / requirement**: Under criterion 24.1(b), countries should take measures and impose requirements similar to those for companies in respect of foundations, Anstalt, Waqf and limited liability partnerships, taking into account their different forms and structures; footnote 115 excludes Waqf in countries where Waqf are legal arrangements under R.25. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should take similar measures and impose similar requirements as those requirements for companies"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under criterion 24.1(c), for other types of legal persons countries should take into account their different forms and structures and their ML/TF risk levels, aiming for appropriate transparency, and as a minimum should ensure that similar basic information is recorded and kept accurate and up to date by such legal persons and is accessible to competent authorities in a timely way. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At a minimum, countries should ensure that similar types of basic information should be recorded and kept accurate and up-to-date"</sub>

## Risk assessment and risk mitigation

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.3(b): countries should assess the ML/TF risks to which they are exposed that are associated with different types of foreign-created legal persons and take appropriate steps to manage and mitigate them. Footnote 119: this could be done through national and/or supranational measures, which could include requiring BO information on some types of foreign-created legal persons to be held as set out under 24.6. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "associated with different types of foreign- created legal persons, and take appropriate steps to manage and mitigate the risks"</sub>

## Basic information

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.4: countries should require all companies created in the country to be registered in a company registry, which should record and make public all the basic information set out in criterion 24.5(a). Footnote 120: company registry means a register in the country of companies incorporated or licensed there, normally maintained by or for the incorporating authority, and not information held by or for the company itself. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "registered in a company registry,120 which should record and make public all the basic information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.5(a): countries should require all companies created in their country to obtain and record minimum basic information: company name, proof of incorporation, legal form and status, address of the registered office, basic regulating powers (e.g. memorandum and articles of association), a list of directors, and a unique identifier such as a tax identification number or equivalent (where this exists). (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "company name, proof of incorporation, legal form and status, the address of the registered office"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.5(b): companies should keep a register of shareholders or members containing their names, the number of shares each shareholder holds and the categories of shares (including the nature of the associated voting rights). Footnote 123: this applies to the nominal owner of all registered shares. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a register of their shareholders or members, containing the names of the shareholders and members and number of shares held"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnotes 121 and 122: the basic information can be recorded by the company itself or by a third person under the company's responsibility; if the unique identifier used is a tax identification number, it should be held by the company registry or another public body. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The information can be recorded by the company itself or by a third person under the company’s responsibility."</sub>
