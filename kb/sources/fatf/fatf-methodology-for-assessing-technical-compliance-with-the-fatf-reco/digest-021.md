## Immediate Outcome 8

- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 8 states the intended outcome that asset recovery processes lead to confiscation and permanent deprivation of criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Asset recovery processes lead to confiscation and permanent deprivation of criminal property and property of corresponding value."</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.8 system, criminals are deprived of criminal property and property of corresponding value, domestic or abroad, through timely use of a comprehensive range of asset recovery measures; asset recovery is prioritised and the legal and operational frameworks are reviewed periodically. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Asset recovery is prioritised by the country and the relevant legal and operational frameworks are reviewed periodically"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.8 system, pursuit of criminal property is prioritised and integrated into the objectives of key stakeholders, particularly LEAs, prosecutors and FIUs, with asset recovery strategies developed at the outset of investigations and updated throughout. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "asset recovery strategies are developed at the outset of investigations and updated throughout"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.8 system, operational and strategic co-operation is effective, relevant information is easily accessible and shared rapidly, and appropriate skills and sufficient resources are available and used effectively relative to the risks. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "relevant information is easily accessible and shared rapidly"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.8 system, criminal property and property of corresponding value is identified rapidly (including through early parallel financial investigations), secured to prevent dissipation, and preserved in value through effective asset management. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including through early use of parallel financial investigations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.8 system, criminal property is confiscated, confiscation orders are enforced, and where appropriate criminal property is returned to or used to compensate victims, ultimately making crime unprofitable and disrupting ML, predicate crimes and TF. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where appropriate, criminal property is returned to or used to compensate victims"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.8 relates primarily to Recommendations 1, 4 and 32 and also to elements of Recommendations 15, 30, 31, 37, 38 and 40; targeted financial sanctions linked to TF are excluded from IO.8 and covered in IO.10. (p. 155)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Targeted financial sanctions linked to terrorist financing are excluded from this Immediate Outcome and covered in Immediate Outcome 10."</sub>

## Note to Assessors:

- **expected / method**: For IO.8, assessors should refer to listed Glossary definitions including asset recovery, confiscation, criminal property, freeze, non-conviction based confiscation and seize, and note that criminal property and property of corresponding value apply whether held by a criminal defendant or a third party, without prejudicing the rights of bona fide third parties. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without prejudicing the rights of bona fide third parties"</sub>
- **expected / method**: For IO.8, assessors should consider results on identification and tracing, freezing and seizing, confiscation and enforcement of orders, domestic and abroad, by conviction based or non-conviction based procedures, but should not count fines or other monetary penalties that form part of the sentence or sanction as confiscated property. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should not take into account amounts, such as fines or other monetary penalties"</sub>
- **expected / method**: For IO.8, assessors should consider relevant findings on international co-operation in IO.2, including whether law enforcement and prosecutors seek appropriate asset recovery assistance from foreign counterparts. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seeking appropriate assistance in asset recovery matters from their foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / method**: When assessing IO.8 core issues, assessors should consider whether activities are aligned with risk, including overall ML risk, higher-risk predicate offences, cross-border risks, and estimates of criminal proceeds generated in, transferred to or laundered in the country. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "estimates of criminal proceeds generated in, transferred to, and/or laundered in the country"</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.1 asks to what extent the country prioritises asset recovery as a policy objective, periodically reviews its asset recovery regime, and uses effective agency structures with adequate resources and co-operation frameworks. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prioritising the pursuit of asset recovery as a policy objective"</sub>
- **expected / method**: A footnote to core issue 8.1 says that in assessing co-operation frameworks assessors should also assess co-operation between tax authorities and competent authorities and any results, which in appropriate cases may have deprived criminals of criminal proceeds or property of corresponding value. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should also assess cooperation between tax authorities and competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.2 asks how well competent authorities identify and trace criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 156)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well are the competent authorities identifying and tracing criminal property and property of corresponding value?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.3 asks how well authorities freeze or seize criminal property, whether provisional measures are actively pursued from financial and parallel investigations including in complex or significant cases, and whether swift action is taken when needed, for example by suspending transactions or withholding consent. (p. 157)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Are provisional measures actively pursued as a result of financial (and parallel) investigations"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.4 asks how well authorities manage frozen or seized property to preserve its value, including through pre-confiscation sale or disposal where appropriate. (p. 157)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "managing frozen or seized property to preserve its value including through pre-confiscation sale or disposal"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.5 asks how well authorities confiscate, through conviction based or non-conviction based procedures, and enforce confiscation orders for criminal property and property of corresponding value, domestic or abroad. (p. 157)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confiscating (whether through conviction based or non-conviction based procedures), and enforcing confiscation orders"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.6 asks to what extent the country returns confiscated property to victims through restitution, compensation or other measures; a footnote adds that in appropriate cases frozen or seized property may be returned to victims. (p. 157)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "return confiscated200 property to victims through restitution, compensation or other measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 8.7 asks how well the declaration or disclosure system identifies and seizes non-declared or falsely-declared cross-border movements of currency and BNIs, whether border and customs authorities apply effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, and whether the system leads to confiscation of currency or BNIs linked to ML/TF or predicate offences. (p. 157)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identifying and seizing non-declared or falsely-declared cross border movements of currency and bearer negotiable instruments"</sub>

## Immediate Outcome 9

- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 9 states the intended outcome that TF offences and activities are investigated and persons who finance terrorism are prosecuted and subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Terrorist financing offences and activities are investigated and persons who finance terrorism are prosecuted"</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.9 system, TF is investigated, offenders prosecuted and sanctioned by courts; when appropriate TF is pursued as a distinct criminal activity and financial investigations support counter-terrorism investigations with good co-ordination; the components function coherently and the prospect of detection, conviction and punishment deters TF. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When appropriate, terrorist financing is pursued as a distinct criminal activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.9 relates primarily to Recommendations 5, 30, 31 and 39 and also to elements of Recommendations 1, 2, 15, 32, 37 and 40. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 5, 30, 31 and 39"</sub>

## Note to Assessors:

- **expected / method**: For IO.9, assessors should consider relevant findings on international co-operation, including whether law enforcement and prosecutors seek appropriate assistance from foreign counterparts in cross-border TF cases. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seeking appropriate assistance from their foreign counterparts in cross-border terrorist financing cases"</sub>
- **expected / method**: When assessing IO.9 core issues, assessors should consider whether activities and measures are aligned with risk, including the overall level of TF risk, characteristics of domestic and cross-border TF activity (collection, movement and use of funds or other assets), and prevailing TF methods, techniques and trends. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the characteristics of the domestic and cross-border TF activity (e.g. collection, movement and use of funds or other assets)"</sub>
- **expected / limitation**: A footnote says assessors should take into account the assessed country's national framework and legal system, such as whether it has a mandatory or discretionary approach to investigations or prosecutions. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including, e.g. whether the country implements a mandatory or discretionary approach to investigations and/or prosecutions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For Immediate Outcome 9, assessors should be aware that some elements of the outcome may involve sensitive material, for example information gathered for national security purposes, which countries may be reluctant or unable to make available to assessors. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should be aware that some elements of this outcome may involve material of a sensitive nature"</sub>
- **recommended / method**: When assessing IO.9, assessors should also consider relevant findings on the level of international co-operation in which competent authorities participate; this involves considering the extent to which law enforcement and prosecutorial agencies seek appropriate assistance from their foreign counterparts in cross-border terrorist financing cases. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seeking appropriate assistance from their foreign counterparts in cross-border terrorist financing cases"</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 9.1 asks how well and in what circumstances TF activity is identified and investigated, and to what extent investigations identify the specific role played by the terrorist financier. (p. 161)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To what extent do the investigations identify the specific role played by the terrorist financier?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 9.2 asks to what extent TF activity, including different types of TF cases, is prosecuted and offenders convicted; a footnote says its focus is prosecution and conviction of R.5 offences, use of non-TF offences falls under 9.5, and assessors should consider the types of TF cases prosecuted. (p. 161)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The focus of this core issue is on the prosecution and conviction of offences covered under R.5."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 9.3 asks to what extent sanctions or measures applied to natural and legal persons convicted of TF offences are effective, proportionate and dissuasive. (p. 161)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sanctions or measures applied against natural and legal persons convicted of TF offences effective, proportionate and dissuasive"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 9.4 asks to what extent TF investigation, prosecution and conviction is considered in formulating national counter-terrorism strategies, and how well information and intelligence from these are shared and used for national counter-terrorism purposes such as identification and designation of terrorists, organisations and support networks. (p. 161)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "used, in the formulation of national counter-terrorism strategies"</sub>
