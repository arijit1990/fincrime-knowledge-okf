## Immediate Outcome 3

- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 3 states the intended outcome that supervisors appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions and VASPs for AML/CFT compliance, that those institutions adequately apply AML/CFT preventive measures and report suspicious transactions, and that actions taken by supervisors, financial institutions and VASPs are proportionate to risks. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors186 appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions and VASPs for compliance with AML/CFT requirements"</sub>

## Characteristics of an effective system

- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.3 system, risk based supervision and monitoring identifies, assesses and mitigates ML/TF risks in the financial and VASP sectors by preventing criminals and their associates from holding or being beneficial owners of significant or controlling interests or management functions in financial institutions and VASPs. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "preventing criminals and their associates from holding"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.3 system, supervision also guides, monitors and enforces compliance by financial institutions and VASPs so that they have effective AML/CFT policies in place, and where issues are identified, appropriate risk-based measures are taken to address them. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "guiding, monitoring and enforcing compliance by financial institutions and VASPs to ensure"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Over time, supervision and monitoring are described as improving AML/CFT compliance and discouraging criminal abuse of the financial and VASP sectors, particularly those most exposed to ML/TF risk. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Over time, supervision and monitoring improve the level of AML/CFT compliance and discourage attempts by criminals to abuse"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In an effective IO.3 system, financial institutions and VASPs understand their ML/TF risks, develop and apply risk-based AML/CFT policies (including group-wide policies), internal controls and programmes, apply appropriate CDD including for beneficial owners, conduct ongoing monitoring, detect and report suspicious transactions, and comply with other AML/CFT requirements, ultimately reducing ML/TF activity within these entities. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "develop and apply risk-based AML/CFT policies (including group-wide policies), internal controls and programmes to adequately mitigate those risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.3 relates primarily to Recommendations 9-21, 26, 27, 34 and 35, and also to elements of Recommendations 1, 29 and 40. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This outcome relates primarily to Recommendations 9-21, 26, 27, 34 and 35, and also elements"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote to IO.3 explains that supervisors is defined in the Glossary and covers supervision of financial institutions, that R.15 extends this to VASPs, and that VASPs should be supervised by a competent authority and not by an SRB. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should be supervised by a competent authority (not an SRB)."</sub>

## Note to Assessors:

- **expected / method**: For IO.3, assessors should refer to a listed set of Glossary definitions, including accounts, beneficial owner, competent authorities, correspondent banking, financial group, MVTS, PEPs, proportionate, reasonable measures, shell bank, supervisors, virtual asset and VASPs. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should refer to the following Glossary definitions when assessing this Immediate Outcome: accounts, beneficial owner, competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / method**: For IO.3, assessors should take into account the country's background, context, materiality and identified ML/TF risks, reflecting on the core issues in line with the size, complexity and risk profiles of the sectors and on whether mitigating activities align with identified risks; in addressing deficiencies, extra focus should go to how they are weighted and their systemic impact, to stay consistent with the risk-based approach. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reflect on the core issues in line with the size, complexity and risk profiles of the sectors under analysis"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Regardless of how a country classifies VASPs, they should be subject to adequate regulation and risk-based supervision or monitoring by a competent authority consistent with R.26 and R.27, so assessors should always assess the effectiveness of VASPs under IO.3. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subject to adequate regulation and risk-based supervision or monitoring by a competent authority, consistent with R.26 and R.27"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Evidence for IO.3 and IO.4 can include responses to questionnaires from assessed countries and from financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs, case studies and the listed examples of information or factors, and assessors may request additional information, including during the onsite visit, to corroborate findings on how supervision has improved compliance and discouraged criminal abuse. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Evidence can include responses to questionnaires by assessed countries and financial institutions/DNFBP and VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote states that the whole Note to Assessors applies when assessing both IO.3 and IO.4. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Note to Assessors in its entirety is applicable when assessing both IO.3 and IO.4."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Where a country decides to prohibit VASPs, the IO.3 effectiveness assessment will focus primarily on detection and enforcement of the prohibition (core issue 3.6) and on how well the country understands VASP-related ML/TF risks (core issue 3.2). (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where a country decides to prohibit VASPs, the effectiveness assessment will focus primarily on the detection"</sub>
- **recommended / method**: When assessing IO.3 and IO.4, assessors should also consider relevant findings, including at group level, on the level of international co-operation in which supervisors participate. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should also consider the relevant findings (including at the group level) on the level of international co-operation"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Assessors are not expected to conduct an in-depth review of the operations of financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors are not expected to conduct an in-depth review of the operations of financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs"</sub>
- **recommended / method**: Instead of an in-depth operational review, assessors should consider, on the basis of evidence and interviews with supervisors, FIUs, other competent authorities and the private sector, whether financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs have adequately assessed and understood their ML/TF risk exposure, whether their policies, procedures and internal controls adequately address and mitigate these risks, and whether regulatory requirements (including STR reporting) are properly implemented. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but should consider, on the basis of evidence and interviews with supervisors, FIUs and other competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Assessors should assess regulation, supervision and monitoring by supervisors and private sector implementation of preventive measures in a coherent manner, since effective supervision over time is positively correlated with more effective implementation of preventive measures. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "implementation of preventive measures by the private sector in a coherent manner."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Poor private-sector implementation of preventive measures can suggest ineffective supervision or monitoring, except where significant legal deficiencies may have undermined effectiveness of preventive measures; the overall assessment of IO.3 and IO.4 should equally combine the two elements. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Poor implementation of preventive measures by the private sector can suggest ineffective supervision or monitoring"</sub>

## Core Issues to be considered in determining if the Outcome is being achieved

- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.1 asks how well licensing, registration or other controls prevent criminals and their associates from holding or being beneficial owners of significant or controlling interests or management functions in financial institutions and VASPs, and how well breaches of those licensing or registration requirements are detected and addressed. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well does licensing, registration or other controls implemented by supervisors or other authorities prevent, criminals and their associates"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.2 asks how well supervisors identify, understand and promote understanding of ML/TF risks and AML/CFT obligations by financial institutions and VASPs, including maintaining over time an understanding of risks in different sectors and types of institutions and in individual institutions and VASPs. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well do the supervisors identify, understand, and promote financial institutions and VASPs understanding of ML/TF risks and AML/CFT obligations?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.3 asks how well financial institutions and VASPs understand the level and nature of their ML/TF risks, including how those risks evolve over time. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well do financial institutions and VASPs understand the level and the nature of their ML/TF risks?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.4 asks how well financial institutions and VASPs understand and apply AML/CFT obligations and mitigating measures appropriate to their business, covering (a) CDD and record-keeping including beneficial ownership information and ongoing monitoring. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(a) the CDD and record-keeping measures (including in relation to beneficial ownership information and ongoing monitoring)?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.4(b) covers enhanced or specific measures for PEPs, correspondent banking, new technologies, wire and virtual asset transfer rules, and high-risk countries identified by the FATF. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(iv) wire and virtual asset transfer rules, and"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.4(c) covers AML/CFT reporting obligations and the practical measures to prevent tipping off. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their AML/CFT reporting obligations? What are the practical measures to prevent tipping off?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.4(d) covers internal controls, procedures and audit requirements, including at group level where applicable, to ensure AML/CFT compliance. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "internal controls and procedures and audit requirements (including at group level where applicable)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.4(e) asks to what extent legal or regulatory requirements, for example financial secrecy, impede implementation of AML/CFT obligations and mitigating measures. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to what extent are there legal or regulatory requirements (e.g. financial secrecy) impeding implementation of AML/CFT obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.5 asks how well supervisors monitor or supervise the extent to which financial institutions and VASPs comply with AML/CFT requirements, with a view to mitigating risks. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "monitor and/or supervise the extent to which financial institutions and VASPs are complying with their AML/CFT requirements?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Core issue 3.6 asks to what extent monitoring or supervision, including outreach, training, remedial actions and effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions where appropriate, has had a demonstrable positive impact on compliance by financial institutions and VASPs over time. (p. 134)[^original]
