## Risk assessment and risk mitigation

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.3: countries should assess the ML/TF risks associated with trusts and similar legal arrangements governed under their law; administered in their country or whose trustee or equivalent resides there; and types of foreign legal arrangements that have sufficient links with their country, and should take appropriate steps to manage and mitigate the identified risks. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should assess the money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with the following different types of trusts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 147: countries may determine a sufficient link on the basis of risk; example sufficiency tests include significant and ongoing business relations with financial institutions or DNFBPs, significant real estate or other local investment, or tax residency in the country. Footnote 146: this could be done through national and/or supranational measures. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has significant and ongoing business relations with financial institutions or DNFBPs"</sub>

## Basic and Beneficial ownership information

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.4(a): countries should require trustees of any express trust, and persons in equivalent positions in similar arrangements, who are resident in the country or administer such arrangements there, to obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information on the arrangement, including the identity of the settlor(s), trustee(s), protectors (if any), each beneficiary or, where applicable, the class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other natural person exercising ultimate effective control over the trust (equivalent positions for similar arrangements). (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date149 beneficial ownership information150 151 regarding the trust"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.4(b) and (c): where parties to a trust or similar arrangement are legal persons or arrangements, trustees should also obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and beneficial ownership information on them; and they should hold basic information on other regulated agents of, and service providers to, the arrangement, including but not limited to investment advisors or managers, accountants and tax advisors. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "basic information on other regulated agents of, and service providers to, the trust and similar legal arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.5: trustees and persons holding equivalent positions should be required to maintain the criterion 25.4 information for at least five years after their involvement with the trust or similar arrangement ceases. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for at least five years after their involvement with the trust or similar legal arrangement ceases"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.6: countries should require that information held under criterion 25.4 be kept accurate and up-to-date and be updated within a reasonable period following any change. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "kept accurate and up-to-date and the information should be updated within a reasonable period following any change"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 152 to the beneficiary-identification element of the trust criteria says that when a trust is set up with no ascertainable beneficiaries, the trustee should obtain and hold information on the class of beneficiaries, its characteristics and the objects of a power. It adds that, following a risk-based approach, countries may judge that individual beneficiaries need not be identified for certain charitable trusts or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Following a risk-based approach, countries may decide that it is not necessary to identify the individual beneficiaries"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.7(a): countries should take measures to ensure that trustees, or persons in equivalent positions in similar legal arrangements, disclose their status to financial institutions and DNFBPs when, in that function, they form a business relationship or carry out an occasional transaction above the threshold. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "disclose their status to financial institutions and DNFBPs when, in their function, forming a business relationship"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.7(c): countries should take measures to ensure that trustees, or persons in equivalent positions in similar legal arrangements, are not prevented by law or enforceable means from giving financial institutions and DNFBPs, upon request, information on the beneficial ownership of the trust or similar arrangement and on any of its assets to be held or managed under the terms of the business relationship. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not prevented by law or enforceable means from providing financial institutions and DNFBPs, upon request"</sub>

## Timely access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date information

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.8: countries should have mechanisms ensuring that information on trusts and similar legal arrangements, including information provided under criteria 25.7 and 25.9, is adequate, accurate and up-to-date. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is adequate,155 accurate156 and up-to-date.157 158"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 155: adequate information is information sufficient to identify the natural persons who are the beneficial owner(s) and their role in the arrangement (settlor(s), trustee(s), protector(s), beneficiary(ies) or class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other person with ultimate effective control); where the trustee or another party is a legal person, that legal person's beneficial owner should be identified. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where the trustee and any other party to the legal arrangement is a legal person, the beneficial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 156: accurate information has been verified by checking the identity and status of the beneficial owner using reliable documents, data or information, and the extent of verification may vary with the level of risk. Footnote 157: up-to-date information is as current as possible and updated within a reasonable period following any change; for beneficiaries designated by characteristics or class, trustees are not expected to obtain fully adequate and accurate information until the person becomes entitled at payout or intends to exercise vested rights, as per the risk-based approach. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not expected to obtain fully adequate and accurate information until the person becomes entitled as beneficiary"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Criterion 25.9: to ensure competent authorities can efficiently and promptly access adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and beneficial ownership information on trusts and similar arrangements, trustees and trust assets other than through trustees, countries should consider using, on the basis of risk, context and materiality and as necessary, any of: a public authority or body holding such information (e.g. a central registry of trusts, or asset registries for land, property, vehicles, shares or other assets), which need not be a single body; other competent authorities such as tax authorities; and other agents or service providers such as trust and company service providers, investment advisors or managers, accountants, lawyers or financial institutions. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider using any of the following sources of information as necessary"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 160: a body could record beneficial ownership information alongside other information (e.g. tax information), the source could be multiple registries (e.g. by province or district, sector, or type of legal arrangement), or a private body entrusted by the public authority. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or of a private body entrusted with this task by the public authority"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.10: countries should ensure competent authorities, in particular law enforcement authorities and FIUs, have all powers necessary to obtain timely access to information held by trustees, equivalent persons and other parties, in particular information held by financial institutions and DNFBPs on the basic and beneficial ownership of the legal arrangement, the residence of the trustees and equivalents, and any assets held or managed by the financial institution or DNFBP for trustees or equivalents with which they have a business relationship or for which they undertake an occasional transaction. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have all the powers necessary to obtain timely access to the information held by trustees"</sub>

## Liability and sanctions

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.11: countries should ensure there are clear responsibilities to comply with the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 25; that trustees or equivalent persons are either legally liable for failures to perform duties relevant to criteria 25.4 to 25.7 or subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal, civil or administrative sanctions for failing to comply; and that there are effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions for failing to grant competent authorities timely access to information on the trust under criteria 25.4 and 25.5. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for failing to grant to competent authorities timely access to information regarding the trust"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 161: countries need not include the requirements of criteria 25.4 to 25.7 and 25.11 in legislation, provided that appropriate obligations to that effect exist for trustees (e.g. through common law or case law). (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided that appropriate obligations to such effect exist for trustees"</sub>

## International cooperation

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.12: countries should rapidly, constructively and effectively provide international cooperation on information, including beneficial ownership information, on trusts and other legal arrangements on the basis of Recommendations 37 and 40, including not placing unduly restrictive conditions on exchange or assistance (e.g. refusing a request because it involves fiscal matters or bank secrecy), and facilitating foreign competent authorities' access to information held by registries or other domestic authorities. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitating access by foreign competent authorities to any information held by registries or other domestic authorities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.12(c) and (d): countries should exchange domestically available information on trusts or other legal arrangements, and use their competent authorities' powers, in accordance with domestic law, to obtain beneficial ownership information on behalf of foreign counterparts. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exchanging domestically available information on the trusts or other legal arrangement"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.12(e): to facilitate rapid, constructive and effective international cooperation, where possible countries should designate and make publicly known the agency(ies) responsible for responding to international requests for beneficial ownership information, consistent with their approach to access to such information; to this end, countries should consider keeping information held or obtained for identifying beneficial ownership in a readily accessible manner. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider keeping information held or obtained for the purpose of identifying beneficial ownership in a readily accessible manner"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 26 REGULATION AND SUPERVISION OF FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 26.1: countries should designate one or more supervisors responsible for regulating and supervising (or monitoring) financial institutions' compliance with AML/CFT requirements. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should designate one or more supervisors that have responsibility for regulating and supervising"</sub>

## Market Entry

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 26.2: Core Principles financial institutions should be required to be licensed; other financial institutions, including those providing money or value transfer or money or currency changing services, should be licensed or registered. Countries should not approve the establishment, or continued operation, of shell banks. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should not approve the establishment, or continued operation, of shell banks."</sub>

## Risk-based approach to supervision and monitoring

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 26.4: Core Principles institutions should be subject to regulation and supervision in line with the core principles where relevant for AML/CFT, including consolidated group supervision for AML/CFT purposes; all other financial institutions should be subject to regulation and supervision or monitoring having regard to sector ML/TF risks and, at a minimum for MVTS and money or currency changing providers, systems for monitoring and ensuring compliance with national AML/CFT requirements. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the application of consolidated group supervision for AML/CFT purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 163: the Core Principles relevant to AML/CFT include BCBS Principles 1-3, 5-9, 11-15, 26 and 29; IAIS Principles 1, 3-10, 18, 21-23 and 25; and IOSCO Principles 24, 28, 29 and 31 and Responsibilities A, B, C and D; assessors may refer to existing assessments of compliance with these Core Principles where available. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) Principles 1-3, 5-9, 11-15, 26, and 29"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 26.5: how often and how intensively on-site and off-site AML/CFT supervision of financial institutions or groups is carried out should be set on the basis of three things: (a) the institution's or group's ML/TF risks, policies, internal controls and procedures as identified in the supervisor's risk-profile assessment; (b) the ML/TF risks present in the country; and (c) the characteristics of the institutions or groups, notably their diversity and number and the discretion the risk-based approach gives them. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the diversity and number of financial institutions and the degree of discretion allowed to them under the risk-based approach"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 26.6: the supervisor should review its assessment of a financial institution's or group's ML/TF risk profile, including the risks of non-compliance, at regular intervals and also when major events or developments occur in the institution's or group's management and operations. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor should review the assessment of the ML/TF risk profile of a financial institution or group"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 27 POWERS OF SUPERVISORS

- **expected / requirement**: Criteria 27.1 and 27.2: supervisors should have powers to supervise or monitor and ensure financial institutions' compliance with AML/CFT requirements, and the authority to conduct inspections of financial institutions. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should have the authority to conduct inspections of financial institutions."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 27.3: supervisors should be authorised to compel production of any information relevant to monitoring compliance with AML/CFT requirements; footnote 164 says this power should not be predicated on the need to require a court order. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not be predicated on the need to require a court order"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 27.4: supervisors should be authorised to impose sanctions in line with Recommendation 35 for failure to comply with AML/CFT requirements, including a range of disciplinary and financial sanctions and the power to withdraw, restrict or suspend the financial institution's licence. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the power to withdraw, restrict or suspend the financial institution’s licence"</sub>
