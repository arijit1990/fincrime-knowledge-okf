## GENERAL GLOSSARY 177

- **descriptive / definition**: Financial group means a group consisting of a parent company or other legal person exercising control and coordinating functions over the rest of the group for group supervision under the Core Principles, together with branches and/or subsidiaries subject to AML/CFT policies and procedures at group level. (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any other type of legal person exercising control and coordinating functions over the rest of the group"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Financial institutions means any natural or legal person who conducts as a business, for or on behalf of a customer, activities including accepting deposits and other repayable funds from the public (which also captures private banking), lending (including consumer credit, mortgage credit, factoring and trade finance), and financial leasing (not extending to consumer product leasing). (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Acceptance of deposits and other repayable funds from the public.217"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The financial institution activities also include money or value transfer services (not applying to those providing financial institutions solely with message or other support systems for transmitting funds), issuing and managing means of payment such as cards, cheques, traveller's cheques, money orders, bankers' drafts and electronic money, and financial guarantees and commitments. (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Issuing and managing means of payment (e.g. credit and debit cards, cheques, traveller's cheques"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The financial institution activities also include trading in money market instruments, foreign exchange, exchange, interest rate and index instruments, transferable securities and commodity futures, and participation in securities issues and related financial services. (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Participation in securities issues and the provision of financial services related to such issues."</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 178

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 181

  <sub>anchor: "issues instructions (directly or indirectly) to a nominee to act on their behalf"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A nominee is an individual or legal person instructed by a nominator to act on their behalf in a certain capacity regarding a legal person; a nominee director (resident director) routinely exercises director functions on the nominator's instructions and is never the beneficial owner of the legal person. (p. 184)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A Nominee Director is never the beneficial owner of a legal person."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A nominee shareholder exercises voting rights according to the nominator's instructions and/or receives dividends on the nominator's behalf, and is never the beneficial owner of a legal person based on the shares it holds as nominee. (p. 184)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exercises the associated voting rights according to the instructions of the nominator"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 183

- **mandatory / definition**: Where the Recommendations refer to a financial institution being satisfied as to a matter, the institution must be able to justify its assessment to competent authorities. (p. 186)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that institution must be able to justify its assessment to competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Seize means prohibiting transfer, conversion, disposition or movement of property on the basis of an action by a competent authority or court under a freezing mechanism; unlike freezing, seizure uses a mechanism letting the authority or court take control of the property, which remains the property of the holder of the interest at the time of seizure although the authority will often take possession, administration or management. (p. 186)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a seizure is effected by a mechanism that allows the competent authority or court to take control of specified property"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A self-regulatory body (SRB) represents a profession such as lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals or accountants, is made up of members of the profession, has a role in regulating who may enter and practise, and performs certain supervisory or monitoring functions; such bodies should enforce rules to maintain high ethical and moral standards. (p. 186)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An SRB is a body that represents a profession"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Settlors are natural or legal persons who transfer ownership of their assets to trustees by a trust deed or similar arrangement. (p. 186)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Settlors are natural or legal persons who transfer ownership of their assets to trustees"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A shell bank is a bank with no physical presence in the country where it is incorporated and licensed and unaffiliated with a regulated financial group subject to effective consolidated supervision; physical presence means meaningful mind and management in the country, and a local agent or low-level staff does not constitute physical presence. (p. 186)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The existence simply of a local agent or low-level staff does not constitute physical presence."</sub>
- **mandatory / definition**: For assessing compliance with the FATF Recommendations, the word should has the same meaning as must. (p. 186)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the word should has the same meaning as must"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 184

- **expected / definition**: Supervisors are designated competent authorities or non-public bodies responsible for ensuring compliance by financial institutions (financial supervisors) and/or DNFBPs with ML/TF requirements; non-public bodies, which could include certain SRBs, should have power to supervise and sanction, be empowered by law for their functions, and be supervised by a competent authority in that respect. (p. 187)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also be empowered by law to exercise the functions they perform, and be supervised by a competent authority"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote adds that financial supervisors include Core Principles supervisors who carry out supervisory functions related to implementing the FATF Recommendations. (p. 187)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core Principles supervisors who carry out supervisory functions that are related to the implementation of the FATF Recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Targeted financial sanctions means both asset freezing and prohibitions to prevent funds or other assets being made available, directly or indirectly, for the benefit of designated persons and entities. (p. 187)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both asset freezing and prohibitions to prevent funds or other assets from being made available"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A terrorist is any natural person who commits or attempts to commit terrorist acts unlawfully and wilfully, participates as an accomplice, organises or directs others to commit terrorist acts, or intentionally contributes to terrorist acts by a group acting with a common purpose, with the aim of furthering the act or knowing the group's intention. (p. 187)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "participates as an accomplice in terrorist acts"</sub>

- **descriptive / definition**: A terrorist act also includes any other act intended to cause death or serious bodily injury to a civilian or other person not taking an active part in hostilities in armed conflict, when its purpose by nature or context is to intimidate a population or compel a government or international organisation to do or abstain from any act.   <sub>anchor: "to intimidate a population, or to compel a Government or an international organisation to do or to abstain"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY

- **descriptive / definition**: In the FATF Glossary, a terrorist act includes an act that is an offence within the scope of, and as defined in, one of ten listed treaties: the Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Seizure of Aircraft (1970); the Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against the Safety of Civil Aviation (1971); the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of Crimes against Internationally Protected Persons, including Diplomatic Agents (1973); the International Convention against the Taking of Hostages (1979); the Convention on the Physical Protection of Nuclear Material (1980); the 1988 Protocol on unlawful acts of violence at airports serving international civil aviation; the Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against the Safety of Maritime Navigation (2005); the 2005 Protocol on the Safety of Fixed Platforms located on the Continental Shelf; the International Convention for the Suppression of Terrorist Bombings (1997); and the International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism (1999). (p. 188)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism (1999)"</sub>

(p. 188)[^original]
