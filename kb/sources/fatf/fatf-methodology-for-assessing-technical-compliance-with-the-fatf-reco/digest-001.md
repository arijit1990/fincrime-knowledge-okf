## INTRODUCTION

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 1: the Methodology provides the basis for assessing technical compliance with the FATF Recommendations as adopted in February 2012 (and updated from time to time) and for reviewing the effectiveness of a country's AML/CFT/CPF system. Its three sections are an introduction, the criteria for technical compliance with each Recommendation, and the outcomes, indicators, data and other factors used to assess effectiveness. Mutual evaluation processes and procedures are in a separate document. Footnote 1: assessment and evaluation cover mutual evaluations by the FATF and FSRBs and third-party assessments by the IMF and World Bank. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The processes and procedures for mutual evaluations are set out in a separate document."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 2: for the 5th round the FATF continues the 4th Round approach of complementary assessments of technical compliance and of whether and how the AML/CFT/CPF system is effective, so the Methodology has two components. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using complementary approaches for assessing technical compliance with the FATF Recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The technical compliance assessment addresses the specific requirements of the FATF Recommendations, principally as they relate to the country's legal and institutional framework and the powers and procedures of competent authorities, which are the fundamental building blocks of an AML/CFT/CPF system. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These represent the fundamental building blocks of an AML/CFT/CPF system."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The effectiveness assessment differs fundamentally from technical compliance: it assesses the adequacy of implementation of the FATF Recommendations and the extent to which a country achieves a defined set of outcomes central to a robust AML/CFT/CPF system, focusing on whether the legal and institutional framework is producing the expected results. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "extent to which the legal and institutional framework is producing the expected results"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 3: together the technical compliance and effectiveness assessments present an integrated analysis of how compliant the country is with the FATF Standards and how successful it is in maintaining a strong AML/CFT/CPF system. Footnote 2: the FATF Standards comprise the Recommendations, their Interpretive Notes and the applicable Glossary definitions. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "present an integrated analysis of the extent to which the country is compliant"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4: the Methodology is designed to assist assessors, reflects the requirements in the FATF Recommendations and Interpretive Notes but does not amend or override them, helps assessors identify systems developed by countries with diverse legal, regulatory and financial frameworks, and is also useful for countries reviewing their own systems, including technical assistance needs. It is informed by assessment experience of the FATF, FSRBs, IMF and World Bank under earlier versions of the Recommendations. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but does not amend or override them"</sub>

## RISK AND CONTEXT

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 5: the starting point of every assessment is the assessors' initial understanding of the country's risks and context, including (a) the nature and extent of ML and TF risks, (b) country circumstances affecting the materiality of factors for different Recommendations (for example the makeup of its economy and financial sector), (c) structural elements underpinning the AML/CFT/CPF system, and (d) other contextual factors that could influence how measures are implemented and how effective they are. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The starting point for every assessment is the assessors"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 6: ML/TF risks are critically relevant to evaluating technical compliance with Recommendation 1 and the risk-based elements of other Recommendations, and to assessing effectiveness; assessors should consider the nature and extent of ML/TF risk at the outset and throughout the assessment. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consider the nature and extent of the money laundering and terrorist financing risk for the country at the outset"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: PF risks are also critical to evaluating technical compliance with Recommendation 1, but unlike ML/TF risks, consideration of PF risks is strictly limited to specific elements of the assessment. Footnote 3: for R.1, proliferation financing risk refers strictly and only to the potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of the targeted financial sanction obligations in R.7. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consideration of PF risks is strictly limited to specific elements of the assessment"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 7: assessors should ensure the risk scoping exercise takes account of the whole range of relevant risk factors and use its outcome to guide and focus the assessment on higher risk areas. Example factors are the level and type of proceeds-generating crime, terrorist groups active or raising funds in the country, exposure to cross-border flows of criminal or illicit assets, significant financial activity in unregulated sectors, and high rates of financial exclusion. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use the outcome of the risk scoping to guide and focus the assessment on the higher risk areas"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 4: because financial activity in unregulated sectors and financial exclusion can be driven by factors unrelated to ML/TF, assessors taking them into account should also consider the broader context of the country's level of economic development, availability of financial services, rates of financial inclusion and the level of risk the country poses to the international financial system. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "driven by factors unrelated to ML/TF"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 8: assessors should use the country's own risk assessment(s) as an initial basis for understanding risks but should not uncritically accept it as correct and need not follow all its conclusions. They should give due consideration to information provided by the country (for example national, threat, sectoral or thematic assessments) and indicate the key credible and reliable sources they relied on in judging the reasonableness of the country's assessment. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not uncritically accept a country"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Where assessors cannot conclude that the country's risk assessment is reasonable, or it is insufficient or non-existent, they should consult closely with national authorities to try to reach a common understanding of the key risks. If there is no agreement, or they cannot conclude the assessment is reasonable, they should clearly explain differences of understanding and their reasoning in the Mutual Evaluation Report (MER), and use their own understanding of the risks as the basis for assessing other risk-based elements (for example risk-based supervision). Assessors should also note the guidance in paragraph 22 on evaluating risk assessments for R.1 and Immediate Outcome 1. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should consult closely with the national authorities to try to reach a common understanding"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 9: assessors should consider materiality, including the relative importance of different parts of the financial sector, VASPs and DNFBPs; the size, integration and make-up of those sectors; the relative importance of financial products or institutions; the amount of domestic versus cross-border business; the extent to which the economy is cash-based; and estimates of the informal sector and/or shadow economy. They should also be aware of population size, level of development, geographical factors, and trading, cultural and social links. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should also consider issues of materiality"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Assessors should consider the relative importance of different sectors and issues in assessing both technical compliance and effectiveness; the most important and relevant issues for the country, including areas of higher ML/TF risk, should be given more weight in technical compliance ratings and more attention when assessing and rating effectiveness. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be given more weight when determining ratings for technical compliance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 10 says the examination of structural elements should be informed by factors including, but not limited to, the level of compliance with relevant international obligations and with fundamental principles of domestic law. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The examination of structural elements should be informed by factors, including but not limited to"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 10 says a lack of the structural elements, or significant weaknesses and shortcomings in the general framework, may significantly hinder the implementation of an effective AML/CFT/CPF framework. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The lack of such structural elements, or significant weaknesses and shortcomings in the general framework"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where assessors identify a lack of compliance or effectiveness, paragraph 10 says missing structural elements may be a reason for it and should be identified in the MER, where relevant. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "missing structural elements may be a reason for this and should be identified in the MER"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 5 says the relevant international obligations, consistent with the FATF Mandate and Recommendations, include those of the United Nations, its Security Council and relevant Committees; the Egmont Group of FIUs (see INR.29, para.13); the Basel Committee, IOSCO and IAIS (see INR.40 para.13, footnote 92 and the Glossary definition of core principles); and the Council of Europe and the Organization of American States (see R.36). (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Egmont Group of Financial Intelligence Units (see INR.29, para.13)"</sub>
