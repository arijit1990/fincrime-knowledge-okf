## Card payments

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 16.25: for transfers flowing from a credit, debit or prepaid card transaction for the purchase of goods or services, financial institutions should be required to ensure that all transfers are accompanied by the card number, and that the name and location of the card issuing and merchant acquiring financial institutions are made available upon request in a timely manner. (p. 269)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all transfers are accompanied by the credit or debit or prepaid card number"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnotes to criterion 16.25 explain that purchases of goods or services means purchases from individuals or entities onboarded by the financial institution to accept card payments following required CDD; that when a card is used for other payments (such as person-to-person transfers) the domestic or cross-border payment criteria apply; and that card issuer and acquirer information should let institutions and authorities identify which financial institutions hold full cardholder and merchant information and in which countries. (p. 269)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When a credit or debit or prepaid card is used to effect other types of payment or value transfer"</sub>

## Cash withdrawals

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 16.26: for cross-border cash withdrawals using a credit, debit or prepaid card through a different financial institution, financial institutions should be required to ensure that the card number accompanies the withdrawal and that the cardholder's name is sent to the acquiring financial institution upon request within three business days of receiving the request. (p. 270)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the name of the cardholder is sent to the acquiring financial institution upon request, within three business days"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 16.27: financial institutions should be required to ensure that domestic cash withdrawals are accompanied by the account number or card number; no information beyond this is required to accompany domestic cash withdrawals. (p. 270)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions should be required to ensure that domestic cash withdrawals are accompanied by the account number or card number."</sub>

## Implementation of Targeted Financial Sanctions

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 16.28: countries should ensure that, when processing payments or value transfers, financial institutions take freezing action and comply with prohibitions on transactions with designated persons and entities under the relevant UNSCRs on preventing and suppressing terrorism and terrorist financing (such as UNSCRs 1267 and 1373 and successors) and on proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and its financing. (p. 270)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial institutions take freezing action and comply with prohibitions from conducting transactions with designated persons and entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Recommendation 16 does not specify whether or how transmitted information should be screened against sanctions lists, as various mechanisms may ensure compliance with applicable targeted financial sanctions. (p. 270)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 16 does not specify whether or how the information transmitted should be screened against sanction lists"</sub>

## Cash withdrawals

- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 254: the criterion 16.26 requirements do not cover cash withdrawals from ATMs run by the same institution that holds the account, provided the information described in 16.26 can be obtained through other means under Recommendation 18. (p. 270)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These requirements do not apply to cash withdrawals from ATMs operated by the same institution where the account is held"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 10 CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 10.1: financial institutions should be prohibited from keeping anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names. (p. 271)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions should be prohibited from keeping anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 10.2: financial institutions should be required to undertake CDD when establishing business relations; when carrying out occasional transactions above the applicable designated threshold (USD/EUR 15 000), including linked transactions in several operations; when carrying out occasional transactions that are payments or value transfers in the circumstances covered by Recommendation 16 and its Interpretive Note; when there is a suspicion of ML/TF regardless of any exemptions or thresholds; or when there are doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained customer identification data. (p. 271)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "carrying out occasional transactions above the applicable designated threshold (USD/EUR 15 000)"</sub>
- **expected / context**: Footnote 257 to Recommendation 10: the principle that financial institutions conduct CDD should be set out in law, though specific requirements may be set out in enforceable means. (p. 271)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The principle that financial institutions conduct CDD should be set out in law"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 15 NEW TECHNOLOGIES

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 15.9: VASPs should be required to comply with the requirements in Recommendations 10 to 21, with qualifications. Under R.10, the occasional transactions designated threshold above which VASPs must conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000. (p. 272)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The occasional transactions designated threshold above which VASPs are required to conduct CDD is USD/EUR 1 000."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 15.9(b), R.16 for virtual asset transfers: originating VASPs should obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information, submit it immediately and securely to the beneficiary VASP or financial institution (if any), and make it available on request to appropriate authorities. Beneficiary VASPs should obtain and hold required originator information and required and accurate beneficiary information and make it available on request to appropriate authorities. (p. 272)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "originating VASPs obtain and hold required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under criterion 15.9(b), the other requirements of R.16 (including monitoring of availability of information, taking freezing action and prohibiting transactions with designated persons and entities) apply to VASPs on the same basis as in R.16, and the same obligations apply to financial institutions when sending or receiving virtual asset transfers on behalf of a customer. (p. 272)[^original]

## Immediate Outcome 3

- **descriptive / context**: Revised Immediate Outcome 3 states that supervisors appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions and VASPs for AML/CFT compliance, and that financial institutions and VASPs adequately apply AML/CFT preventive measures and report suspicious transactions, with actions by supervisors, FIs and VASPs proportionate to the risks. (p. 273)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors262 appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions and VASPs for compliance with AML/CFT requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Core issue 3.1 asks how well licensing, registration or other controls prevent criminals and their associates from holding, or being the beneficial owner of, a significant or controlling interest or a management function in financial institutions and VASPs, and how well breaches are detected and addressed as appropriate. (p. 273)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well are breaches of such licensing or registration requirements detected and addressed as appropriate?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Core issue 3.2 asks how well supervisors identify, understand and promote FI and VASP understanding of ML/TF risks and AML/CFT obligations, including identifying and maintaining an understanding of risks across sectors, types of institutions and individual institutions over time. Core issue 3.3 asks how well FIs and VASPs understand the level and nature of their ML/TF risks, including how the risks evolve over time. (p. 273)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How well do financial institutions and VASPs understand the level and the nature of their ML/TF risks?"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 262 states that Supervisors is defined in the Glossary and covers supervision of financial institutions; R.15 extends this to VASPs, which should be supervised by a competent authority (not an SRB). For FIs and VASPs, the definition refers to designated competent authorities or non-public bodies. (p. 273)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should be supervised by a competent authority (not an SRB)."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Revised Immediate Outcome 3 describes the intended outcome as supervisors appropriately supervising, monitoring and regulating financial institutions and VASPs for compliance with AML/CFT requirements, and those institutions and VASPs adequately applying AML/CFT preventive measures and reporting suspicious transactions. It adds that the actions of supervisors, financial institutions and VASPs are proportionate to the risks. (p. 273)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors262 appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions and VASPs for compliance with AML/CFT requirements"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: Footnote 262: 'Supervisors' is defined in the Glossary and covers the supervision of financial institutions; R.15 extends this to VASPs, which should be supervised by a competent authority and not by an SRB. For financial institutions and VASPs, the definition of supervisor refers to designated competent authorities or non-public bodies. (p. 273)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VASPs should be supervised by a competent authority (not an SRB)."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Core issue 3.4 also covers FIs' and VASPs' AML/CFT reporting obligations and practical measures to prevent tipping off; internal controls, procedures and audit requirements (including at group level where applicable); and the extent to which legal or regulatory requirements (e.g. financial secrecy) impede implementation of AML/CFT obligations and mitigating measures. (p. 274)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "What are the practical measures to prevent tipping off?"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Core issue 3.5 asks, with a view to mitigating risks, how well supervisors monitor and/or supervise the extent to which FIs and VASPs comply with AML/CFT requirements. Core issue 3.6 asks to what extent monitoring and/or supervision, including outreach, training and remedial actions and/or effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions where appropriate, has had a demonstrable positive impact on compliance over time. (p. 274)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had a demonstrable positive impact on compliance by financial institutions and VASPs over time?"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: IO.3 examples of specific factors include information on FI and VASP compliance, for example frequency of internal AML/CFT compliance review proportionate to risks, frequency and quality of AML/CFT training, time taken to give competent authorities accurate and complete CDD information on request, accounts or relationships rejected for incomplete CDD information, payments, value transfers and VA transfers rejected for insufficient requisite information, and trends identified from transaction monitoring and reporting. (p. 274)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accounts/relationships rejected due to incomplete CDD information; payments and value transfers and VA transfers rejected due to insufficient requisite information"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Another IO.3 specific factor asks whether internal policies and controls of FIs and VASPs (including in a group context where appropriate) enable timely review of complex or unusual transactions, potential STRs for reporting to the FIU, and potential false-positives, and to what extent reported STRs contain complete, accurate and adequate information about the suspicious transaction. (p. 274)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable timely review of: (a) complex or unusual transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: IO.3 specific factors also ask to what extent financial institutions take appropriate measures to detect and mitigate the risk of misdirected payments, e.g. due to possible ML, fraud or error; how AML/CFT policies and controls are communicated to senior management and staff; and what remedial actions and sanctions FIs and VASPs take when AML/CFT obligations are breached. (p. 274)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To what extent do financial institutions take appropriate measures to detect and mitigate the risk of misdirected payments"</sub>

## METHODOLOGY FOR ASSESSING TECHNICAL COMPLIANCE

- **descriptive / context**: Mutual evaluations assess what a country is doing to tackle money laundering, terrorist financing and the financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction. The FATF Methodology is a guide for assessors carrying out a mutual evaluation, and it offers a structured analytical framework that supports consistency and high quality in the resulting reports. (p. 277)[^original]
