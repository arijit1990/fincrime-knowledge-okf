## Beneficial ownership information

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.6: countries should follow a multi-pronged approach to ensure that the beneficial ownership of a company can be determined in a timely manner by a competent authority. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should follow a multi-pronged approach in order to ensure that the beneficial ownership of a company can be determined"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.6(a): countries should require companies to obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on their own beneficial ownership; to co-operate with competent authorities to the fullest extent possible in determining the beneficial owner, including making the information available in a timely manner; and to co-operate with financial institutions and DNFBPs in providing adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to co-operate with competent authorities to the fullest extent possible in determining the beneficial owner"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.6(b)(i): countries should require adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information on legal persons to be held by a public authority or body (for example a tax authority, FIU, company registry or beneficial ownership registry), although the information need not be held by a single body only. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "although information need not be held by a single body only"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 127: a body could record beneficial ownership information alongside other information (such as basic ownership, incorporation or tax information), the source could take the form of multiple registries (for example by province or district, sector, or type of legal person such as NPOs), or it could be a private body entrusted with the task by the public authority. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or of a private body entrusted with this task by the public authority"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Criterion 24.6(b)(ii): countries may decide to use an alternative mechanism instead of 24.6(b)(i) if it also gives authorities efficient access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information; reliance on basic information or existing information alone is insufficient, and there must be some specific mechanism providing efficient access. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reliance on basic information or existing information alone is insufficient"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.6(b): countries should choose, based on risk, context and materiality, which form of registry or alternative mechanism they will use so that competent authorities can access beneficial ownership information efficiently, and should record that decision in writing. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should decide, on the basis of risk, context and materiality, what form of registry"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.6(b)(iii): countries should use any additional supplementary measures necessary to ensure the beneficial ownership of a company can be determined, for example information held by regulators or stock exchanges, or information obtained by financial institutions and/or DNFBPs under Recommendations 10 and 22. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information obtained by financial institutions and/or DNFBPs in accordance with Recommendations 10128 and 22.129"</sub>

## Timely access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date information

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.7: all persons, authorities and entities mentioned in criterion 24.6, and the company itself (or its administrators, liquidators or others involved in its dissolution), should maintain the information and records for at least five years after the company is dissolved or otherwise ceases to exist, or five years after it ceases to be a customer of the professional intermediary or financial institution. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "at least five years after the date on which the company is dissolved or otherwise ceases to exist"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.8: countries should have mechanisms ensuring that basic and beneficial ownership information, including information provided to the company registry and any information referred to in criterion 24.6, is adequate, accurate and up to date. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is adequate,130 accurate131 and up to date.132"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 130: adequate information is information sufficient to identify the natural person(s) who are the beneficial owner(s) and the means and mechanisms through which they exercise ownership or control; identifying examples include full name, nationality(ies), full date and place of birth, residential address, national identification number and document type, and tax identification number or equivalent in the country of residence. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sufficient to identify the natural person(s) who are the beneficial owner(s), and the means and mechanisms"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 131: accurate information has been verified by checking the identity and status of the beneficial owner using reliable, independently sourced documents, data or information; the extent of verification may vary with the level of risk, and countries should consider complementary measures as necessary to support accuracy, e.g. discrepancy reporting. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The extent of verification measures may vary according to the specific level of risk."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 132: up-to-date information is as current as possible and is updated within a reasonable period (e.g. within one month) following any change. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "updated within a reasonable period (e.g. within one month) following any change"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.9: competent authorities, in particular law enforcement authorities and FIUs, should have all powers necessary to obtain timely access to basic and beneficial ownership information held by relevant parties, including rapid and efficient access to information held by a public authority or other competent authority, and/or on the financial institutions or DNFBPs that hold it. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have all the powers necessary to be able to obtain timely access to the basic and beneficial ownership information"</sub>

## Beneficial ownership information

- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 128: beneficial ownership information for legal persons is the information referred to in INR.10, paragraph 5(b)(i); controlling shareholders under that paragraph may be defined by a threshold, such as any person owning more than a set percentage of the company, determined from the jurisdiction's risk assessment and capped at a maximum of 25%. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "determined based on the jurisdiction’s assessment of risk, with a maximum of 25%"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 128: identifying, and taking reasonable measures to verify the identity of, the natural person who holds the position of senior managing official in the circumstances in paragraph 5.b(i.iii) of INR.10 does not amount to collecting beneficial ownership information as the Glossary defines that term. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not constitute collecting beneficial ownership information as that term is defined in the Glossary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 129: countries should have the ability to establish in a timely way whether a company holds or controls an account with a financial institution in the country. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should be able to determine in a timely manner whether a company has or controls an account"</sub>

## Timely access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date information

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.10: countries should have a combination of mechanisms enabling competent authorities to obtain, or have timely access to, adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on the beneficial ownership and control of foreign-created companies and other legal persons that present ML/TF risks and have a sufficient link with the country. Footnote 135: countries may choose the mechanisms but should also comply with the minimum requirements of criteria 24.3(b). (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "beneficial ownership and control of foreign- created companies and other legal persons that present ML/TF risks"</sub>

## Obstacles to transparency

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.11: countries should require their company registry to facilitate timely access by financial institutions, DNFBPs and other countries' competent authorities to the public information it holds, and at a minimum to the information in criterion 24.5(a). (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitate timely access by financial institutions, DNFBPs and other countries’ competent authorities to the public information they hold"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.12: to prevent and mitigate misuse of bearer shares and bearer share warrants (or similar untraceable instruments), countries should prohibit the issuance of new bearer shares and bearer share warrants, and for existing ones apply within a reasonable timeframe one or more of: conversion into registered form; immobilisation by requiring them to be held with a regulated financial institution or professional intermediary with timely access by competent authorities; and, until conversion or immobilisation is completed, requiring holders to notify the company and the company to record their identity before any rights can be exercised. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prohibiting the issuance of new bearer shares and bearer share warrants"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 136: the bearer share requirements do not apply to newly issued and existing bearer shares or warrants of a company listed on a stock exchange and subject to disclosure requirements (by exchange rules, law or enforceable means) ensuring adequate transparency of beneficial ownership. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These requirements do not apply to newly issued and existing bearer shares or bearer share warrants of a company"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.13: to prevent and mitigate misuse of nominee shareholding and nominee directors, countries should apply one or more of: requiring nominees to disclose their nominee status and nominator's identity to the company and any relevant registry, with that information recorded and nominee status included in public information; requiring nominees to be licensed, with nominee status and nominator identity held by the public authority or alternative mechanism and nominees maintaining information identifying their nominator and the natural person on whose behalf they ultimately act, available to competent authorities on request; or enforcing a prohibition on nominee shareholders or directors. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring nominee shareholders and directors to disclose their nominee status and the identity of their nominator"</sub>

- **expected / requirement**: Under criterion 24.9, in addition to powers for competent authorities, countries should ensure that national-level public authorities, and others as appropriate, can obtain timely access to basic and beneficial ownership information on legal persons during public procurement. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have timely access to basic and beneficial ownership information on legal persons in the course of public procurement"</sub>

## Obstacles to transparency

- **descriptive / context**: Footnotes 137 to 139: a country need not impose a separate licensing or registration system on persons already licensed or registered as financial institutions or DNFBPs that may perform nominee activities and are subject to the full range of FATF obligations; identifying the beneficial owner where a nominee holds a controlling interest requires establishing the natural person on whose behalf the nominee ultimately acts; for intermediaries in nominee activities, R.22 and R.28 should be referred to. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires establishing the identity of the natural person on whose behalf the nominee is ultimately"</sub>

## International cooperation

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.15: countries should rapidly, constructively and effectively provide the widest possible international cooperation on basic and beneficial ownership information, on the basis of Recommendations 37 and 40, including not placing unduly restrictive conditions on information exchange or assistance (e.g. refusing a request because it involves fiscal matters or bank secrecy), facilitating foreign competent authorities' access to basic information held by company registries, and exchanging information on shareholders. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not placing unduly restrictive conditions on the exchange of information or assistance"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.15(d) and (e): countries should use their powers, in accordance with domestic law, to obtain beneficial ownership information on behalf of foreign counterparts, and monitor the quality of assistance received from other countries on requests for basic and beneficial ownership information or for locating beneficial owners residing abroad. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "monitoring the quality of assistance they receive from other countries"</sub>

## Liability and sanctions

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.14: there should be an explicit responsibility to comply with the requirements of the interpretive note to Recommendation 24, together with liability and, as appropriate, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions for any legal or natural person that fails to comply properly. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There should be a clearly stated responsibility to comply with the requirements in the interpretive note"</sub>

## International cooperation

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 24.15(f) and (g): countries should keep information held or obtained for identifying beneficial ownership in a readily accessible manner, and designate and make publicly known the agency(ies) responsible for responding to all international requests for beneficial ownership information. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "keeping in a readily accessible manner information held or obtained for the purpose of identifying beneficial ownership"</sub>

## ARRANGEMENTS

- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 25: scope deficiencies (e.g. trusts covered but other legal arrangements not, or covered only to varying degrees) should be assessed only in criterion 25.1 and not cascaded into other criteria on the presence and adequacy of specific R.25 requirements. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should assess this only in criterion 25.1 and not cascade the deficiency(ies) into other criteria"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In weighting criterion 25.1, the importance of a scope deficiency depends on the materiality of each type of legal arrangement set up or administered in the country (or whose trustees are resident there), the extent to which each type is covered by R.25, and the significance of the deficiency given the country's risk profile and structural and contextual information, including whether it is a trust formation centre. Assessors should explain the basis for their weighting. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including if it is a trust formation centre"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 142: an NC or PC rating could be justified if the country is a trust formation centre that does not apply the basic R.25 requirements to express trusts but fully covers all other types of legal arrangements, depending on the relative material importance and risk of those other types. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an NC or PC rating could be justified if the country is a trust formation centre"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 142: conversely, an LC rating could be justified if trusts or other materially important types of legal arrangements are subject to most R.25 requirements, while other types that are neither materially important nor high risk fall entirely outside R.25. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(which are not materially important or high risk) are completely outside the scope of R.25"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Criterion 25.4 assessment should focus on the requirements a country has implemented for beneficial ownership information, whereas criterion 25.8 focuses on whether the information collected is adequate, accurate and up-to-date; deficiencies in adequacy, accuracy or currency should be noted under 25.8 and not elsewhere. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such deficiencies should be noted under criterion 25.8 (not elsewhere in other criteria)"</sub>

## Scope extends to express trusts and other similar arrangements

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 25.2: countries with express trusts and similar legal arrangements governed under their law should have mechanisms that identify the different types, forms and basic features of such arrangements; identify and describe the processes for setting them up and for obtaining basic and beneficial ownership information; and make that information publicly available. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify the different types, forms and basic features of express trusts and/or other similar legal arrangements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 144: criterion 25.2 covers arrangements set up under the law of the assessed country, not those set up under another country's law even if administered in the assessed country. Footnote 145: for a legal arrangement, basic information means the identifier (e.g. name, unique identifier such as a tax identification number where it exists), the trust deed (or equivalent) and purposes if any, and the residence of the trustee/equivalent or the place from which the arrangement is administered. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the trust deed (or equivalent) and purposes, if any, the residence of the trustee/equivalent"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 143: Waqf are not treated as similar arrangements in countries where Waqf are legal persons under R.24. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Except in countries where Waqf are legal persons under R.24."</sub>
