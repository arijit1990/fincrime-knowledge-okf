## GENERAL INTERPRETATION AND GUIDANCE

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 32(b): in addition to the Methodology's other requirements, when assessing technical compliance in the supra-national context assessors should describe in the MER (i) supra-national measures directly applicable to the assessed country (for example laws and regulations applying equally to all member states) and (ii) whether there are gaps in the combined framework of national, sub-national and supra-national measures. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether there are any gaps in the combined framework of national, sub- national and supra-national measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 33(b): when assessing the effectiveness of an Immediate Outcome in the supra-national context, assessors should take into account and describe in the MER the extent to which supra-national measures are implemented in the assessed country, the interplay between supra-national and national measures in that area, and how and to what extent supra-national AML/CFT/CPF measures are enforced. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the extent to which supra-national measures are implemented in the assessed country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 34, financial supervision: laws and enforceable means imposing preventive AML/CFT/CPF requirements on the banking, insurance and securities sectors should be implemented and enforced through the supervisory process, and the relevant core supervisory principles of the Basel Committee, IAIS and IOSCO should also be adhered to (see criterion 26.4). These principles may overlap with or complement FATF Standards, and assessors should have regard to any assessments or findings on the Core Principles or other relevant supervisory standard-setter principles. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "relevant core supervisory principles issued by the Basel Committee, IAIS and IOSCO should also be adhered to"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 33(a), supra-national context: implementation of AML/CFT measures may differ among member states of a supra-national jurisdiction, depending on the assessed country's risk and context, how its national framework and other AML/CFT/CPF measures interact with supra-national ones, and any gaps against the FATF Standards. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and other AML/CFT/CPF measures interact with those at the supra-national level and any gaps in these frameworks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 33(a): assessors should explore these supra-national and national interaction issues with both the relevant supra-national authorities and the national authorities. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should explore these issues with both the relevant supra-national and the national authorities."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: For other types of financial institutions and VASPs, how (based on risk) AML/CFT laws and enforceable means are implemented and enforced, whether through a supervisory or monitoring framework, varies from country to country. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it will vary from country to country as to how, based on risk, these"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 35, sanctions: several Recommendations require "effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions". In technical compliance, assessors should consider whether the framework of laws and enforceable means includes a sufficient range of sanctions that can be applied proportionately to greater or lesser breaches. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "includes a sufficient range of sanctions that they can be applied proportionately to greater or lesser breaches"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 16 lists examples of sanction types: written warnings; orders to comply with specific instructions (possibly with daily fines); ordering regular reports on measures being taken; fines; barring individuals from employment in the sector; replacing or restricting powers of managers, directors and controlling owners; conservatorship, or suspension or withdrawal of licence; and criminal penalties where permitted. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "barring individuals from employment within that sector"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 36, international co-operation: it is assessed mainly through Recommendations 36-40 and Immediate Outcome 2, but assessors should also be aware of its impact on other Recommendations and Immediate Outcomes (for example investigation of cross-border crimes or supervision of international groups) and set out clearly any instances where compliance or effectiveness is positively or negatively affected by international co-operation. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "principally Recommendations 36-40 and Immediate Outcome 2"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 37, draft legislation: assessors should only take into account laws, regulations or other measures in force and effect by the end of the on-site visit. Bills or other proposals made available may be referred to in the report but should not be taken into account in conclusions or for ratings. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in force and effect by the end of the on-site visit to the country"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Paragraph 38: assessors may consider FATF Guidance as background information on how countries could effectively implement requirements and as examples of effective practices, but the guidance should not form part of the assessment. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the guidance should not form part of the assessment"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 35: in the effectiveness assessment, assessors should consider whether sanctions applied in practice secure future compliance by the sanctioned institution or person, match the seriousness of the breach or offence, and deter others from non-compliance, taking into account the country's context and legal system. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider whether the sanctions applied in practice are effective at ensuring future compliance"</sub>

## TECHNICAL COMPLIANCE

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 40: the Recommendations apply to all countries, but legislative, institutional and supervisory frameworks differ. Provided the Recommendations are complied with, countries are entitled to implement the Standards consistently with their national legislative and institutional systems even though methods differ, and assessors should take into account the country's risks and structural or contextual factors. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries are entitled to implement the FATF Standards in a manner consistent with their national legislative and institutional systems"</sub>

## COMPLIANCE RATINGS

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 42: for each Recommendation assessors reach a conclusion on the extent of compliance using four levels: compliant, largely compliant, partially compliant and non-compliant; in exceptional circumstances a Recommendation may be rated not applicable. Ratings are based only on the criteria in the technical compliance assessment. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "compliant, largely compliant, partially compliant and non-compliant"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Technical compliance ratings: Compliant (C) means there are no shortcomings; Largely compliant (LC) means only minor shortcomings; Partially compliant (PC) means moderate shortcomings; Non-compliant (NC) means major shortcomings. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Largely compliant LC There are only minor shortcomings."</sub>

## TECHNICAL COMPLIANCE

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 39: for the most part, the technical compliance component leaves out requirements of the Standards that relate mainly to effectiveness, which are assessed separately in the effectiveness component. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For the most part, it does not include the specific requirements of the Standards that relate principally to effectiveness"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 41: each Recommendation's requirements are set out as numbered criteria that represent the elements needed to demonstrate full compliance with its mandatory elements; the numbering order carries no priority or ranking of importance. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the sequence of criteria does not represent any priority or order of importance"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 41: for criteria that have indented elaboration beneath them, assessors should review whether each of the elements is present in order to judge whether the criterion as a whole is met. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should review whether each of the elements is present, in order to judge"</sub>

## COMPLIANCE RATINGS

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 43: it is the responsibility of the assessed country to demonstrate that its AML/CFT/CPF system is compliant with the Recommendations. In determining compliance, assessors should assess not only whether laws and enforceable means comply with the Recommendations but also whether the institutional framework is in place. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is the responsibility of the assessed country to demonstrate that its AML/CFT/CPF system is compliant"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 44, weighting of criteria: criteria do not all have equal importance and the number met is not always an indication of overall compliance. Assessors should consider the relative importance of criteria and how significant any deficiencies are given the country's risk profile and structural and contextual information (for example a higher risk area or a large part of the financial sector). (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not all have equal importance and the number of criteria met is not always an indication"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In some cases a single deficiency may be sufficiently important to justify an NC rating even if other criteria are met, whereas a deficiency relating to a low risk or little used type of financial activity may have only a minor effect on the overall rating. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a single deficiency may be sufficiently important to justify an NC rating, even if other criteria are met"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Paragraph 45, overlaps between Recommendations: the same underlying deficiency can cascade across the assessment of several Recommendations (for example a deficient risk assessment could undermine risk-based measures throughout the system, or failure to apply AML/CFT regulations to a type of FI or DNFBP could affect all Recommendations applying to FIs or DNFBPs). Assessors should reflect the deficiency in the factors underlying each applicable rating and, if appropriate, mark the rating accordingly. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the same underlying deficiency will have a cascading effect on the assessment of several different Recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The rating "Not applicable" (NA) is used where a requirement does not apply because of structural, legal or institutional features of the country. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A requirement does not apply, due to the structural, legal or institutional features of a country."</sub>
- **expected / method**: When deciding the level of shortcomings for any Recommendation, assessors should consider, having regard to country context, the number and relative importance of the criteria met, mostly met, partly met or not met. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessors should consider, having regard to the country context, the number and the relative importance of the criteria"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: In cascading cases assessors should also clearly indicate in the MER that the same underlying cause is involved in all relevant Recommendations. Footnote 17: PF issues are assessed exclusively under R.7, IO.11 and specifically identified elements of R.1, R.2 and R.15, and any underlying CPF deficiency should not have a cascading effect. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also clearly indicate in the MER that the same underlying cause is involved in all relevant Recommendations."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 46: assessing effectiveness is equally as important as assessing technical compliance, and is intended to improve the FATF's focus on outcomes, identify the extent to which the national system achieves the objectives of the Standards and any systemic weaknesses, and enable countries to prioritise measures. Effectiveness is defined as "The extent to which the defined outcomes are achieved". (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The extent to which the defined outcomes are achieved"</sub>

## EFFECTIVENESS

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 48: the goal of an effectiveness assessment is an appreciation of the whole AML/CFT/CPF system and how well it works. It does not check whether specific requirements are met or all elements of a Recommendation are in place; it requires a judgement as to whether, or to what extent, defined outcomes are being achieved in practice, relying on assessors' judgement in consultation with the assessed country. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It does not involve checking whether specific requirements are met"</sub>

## THE FRAMEWORK FOR ASSESSING EFFECTIVENESS

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 50: the FATF's effectiveness assessment uses a hierarchy of defined outcomes. The high-level objective is that financial systems and the broader economy are protected from the threats of money laundering and the financing of terrorism and proliferation, strengthening financial sector integrity and contributing to safety and security. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "focusing on a hierarchy of defined outcomes"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Effectiveness is assessed primarily on the basis of eleven Immediate Outcomes, each a key goal of an effective system, feeding into three Intermediate Outcomes (the major thematic goals). The approach does not directly assess the effectiveness of implementing individual Recommendations or the performance of specific organisations or institutions, and assessors are not expected to evaluate the High-Level Objective or Intermediate Outcomes directly, though they may be relevant in the MER's overall summary. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF assesses effectiveness primarily on the basis of eleven Immediate Outcomes"</sub>

## EFFECTIVENESS

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 47: in the AML/CFT/CPF context, effectiveness is how far financial systems and economies mitigate the risks and threats of money laundering and of financing of terrorism and proliferation. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the extent to which financial systems and economies mitigate the risks and threats of money laundering"</sub>
- **permissive / definition**: Paragraph 47: effectiveness could relate to the intended result of a policy, law or enforceable means; a programme of law enforcement, supervision or intelligence activity; or implementation of a specific set of measures to mitigate ML/TF risks and combat proliferation financing. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(b) programme of law enforcement, supervision, or intelligence activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 49: the assessed country is responsible for demonstrating that its AML/CFT/CPF system is effective; if evidence is not made available, assessors can only conclude that the system is not effective. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If the evidence is not made available, assessors can only conclude that the system is not effective."</sub>

## THE FRAMEWORK FOR ASSESSING EFFECTIVENESS

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 51 diagram: Immediate Outcome 1 is that ML and TF risks are identified, assessed and understood, policies are co-operatively developed and, where appropriate, actions coordinated domestically. It contributes to the Intermediate Outcome that policy, co-ordination and co-operation mitigate ML and TF risks. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "risks are identified, assessed and understood, policies are co-operatively developed"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 2 is that international co-operation delivers appropriate information, financial intelligence and evidence and facilitates action against criminals and their property. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International co-operation delivers appropriate information, financial intelligence, and evidence"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 5 is that legal persons and arrangements are prevented from misuse for ML or TF and information on their beneficial ownership is available to competent authorities without impediments. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on their beneficial ownership is available to competent authorities without impediments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 6 is that financial intelligence and all other relevant information are appropriately used by competent authorities for ML and TF investigations. Immediate Outcome 7 begins that ML offences and activities are investigated and offenders prosecuted (the text continues in the next excerpt). IOs 6 onward serve the Intermediate Outcome that ML threats are detected and disrupted and criminals sanctioned and deprived of illicit proceeds, and that TF threats are detected and disrupted, terrorists deprived of resources and financiers sanctioned. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial intelligence and all other relevant information are appropriately used by competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 3 states that supervisors appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate financial institutions and VASPs for AML/CFT compliance, that FIs and VASPs adequately apply preventive measures and report suspicious transactions, and that actions taken by supervisors and by FIs and VASPs are proportionate to the risks. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The actions taken by supervisors and by financial institutions and VASPs are proportionate to the risks."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Immediate Outcome 4 states that supervisors appropriately supervise, monitor and regulate DNFBPs for AML/CFT compliance, and that DNFBPs adequately apply AML/CFT preventive measures proportionate to the risks and report suspicious transactions. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DNFBPs adequately apply AML/CFT preventive measures proportionate to the risks, and report suspicious transactions"</sub>
