## GENERAL GLOSSARY

(p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "incomplete instruments (including cheques, promissory notes and money orders) signed, but with the payee’s name omitted"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The Glossary defines bearer shares as negotiable instruments according ownership in a legal person to whoever possesses the physical certificate, and similar instruments without traceability; dematerialised or registered forms whose owner can be identified are excluded. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accord ownership in a legal person to the person who possesses the physical bearer share certificate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Bearer share warrants are defined as negotiable instruments entitling the possessor of the physical warrant certificate to ownership in a legal person, and similar untraceable instruments; the definition excludes dematerialised or registered forms and instruments that only confer a right to subscribe for ownership on specified conditions until exercised. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any other instruments that only confers a right to subscribe for ownership in a legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF Glossary defines asset recovery as a process covering the identification, tracing, evaluation, freezing, seizure and confiscation of criminal property and property of corresponding value, enforcing the resulting order, and managing and disposing of that property (including returning or sharing it). (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The term asset recovery refers to the process of identifying, tracing, evaluating, freezing"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 169

- **descriptive / definition**: For legal persons, the Glossary defines beneficial owner as the natural person(s) who ultimately owns or controls a customer and/or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, including those exercising ultimate effective control; only a natural person can be an ultimate beneficial owner and more than one can exist for a legal person. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only a natural person can be an ultimate beneficial owner, and more than one natural person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For legal arrangements, beneficial owner includes the settlor(s), trustee(s), protector(s) if any, each beneficiary or class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other natural person exercising ultimate effective control; for arrangements similar to an express trust it means those holding equivalent positions, and where the trustee or another party is a legal person, that legal person's beneficial owner should be identified. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the beneficial owner of that legal person should be identified"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote explains that ultimately owns or controls and ultimate effective control refer to situations where ownership or control is exercised through a chain of ownership or by means other than direct control, and that the beneficial owner definition also applies to the beneficial owner of a beneficiary under a life or other investment linked insurance policy. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "situations in which ownership/control is exercised through a chain of ownership or by means of control other than direct control"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 170

- **descriptive / definition**: In trust law, a beneficiary is a person entitled to the benefit of a trust, and may be a natural or legal person or arrangement; all trusts other than charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts must have ascertainable beneficiaries, though some may have only objects of a power until someone becomes entitled after the accumulation period. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All trusts (other than charitable or statutory permitted non- charitable trusts) are required to have ascertainable beneficiaries."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In life insurance or other investment linked insurance, a beneficiary is the natural or legal person, legal arrangement or category of persons who will be paid the policy proceeds if an insured event covered by the policy occurs. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who will be paid the policy proceeds when/if an insured event occurs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The Glossary defines competent authorities as all public authorities with designated AML/CFT responsibilities, including the FIU, authorities investigating or prosecuting ML, predicate offences and TF or seizing, freezing and confiscating criminal assets, authorities receiving cross-border currency and BNI reports, and AML/CFT supervisors; SRBs are not to be regarded as competent authorities. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SRBs are not to be regarded as competent authorities."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A footnote clarifies that public authorities include financial supervisors established as independent non-governmental authorities with statutory powers. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial supervisors established as independent non-governmental authorities with statutory powers"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY

- **descriptive / definition**: In trust law, a beneficiary is the person or persons entitled to the benefit of a trust arrangement, and a beneficiary can be a natural or legal person or arrangement. (p. 173)[^original]

## GENERAL GLOSSARY 171

(p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "means the permanent deprivation of funds or other assets by order of a competent authority or a court"</sub>

(p. 174)[^original]
