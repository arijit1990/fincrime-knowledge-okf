## RECOMMENDATION 33 STATISTICS

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 33.1: countries should maintain comprehensive statistics on matters relevant to the effectiveness and efficiency of their AML/CFT systems, including statistics on STRs received and disseminated; ML/TF investigations, prosecutions and convictions; property frozen, seized and confiscated; and mutual legal assistance or other international cooperation requests made and received. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain comprehensive statistics on matters relevant to the effectiveness and efficiency of their AML/CFT systems"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 171: for purposes of technical compliance, the assessment of criterion 33.1 is limited to the four listed areas (a) to (d). (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the assessment should be limited to the four areas listed in paragraphs (a) to (d)"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 34 GUIDANCE AND FEEDBACK

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 34.1: competent authorities, supervisors and SRBs should establish guidelines and provide feedback to assist financial institutions and DNFBPs in applying national AML/CFT measures, in particular in detecting and reporting suspicious transactions. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Competent authorities, supervisors and SRBs should establish guidelines and provide feedback"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 35 SANCTIONS

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 35.1: countries should ensure a range of proportionate and dissuasive criminal, civil or administrative sanctions is available for natural or legal persons that fail to comply with the AML/CFT requirements of Recommendations 6 and 8 to 23. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "range of proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, whether criminal, civil or administrative, available to deal with natural or legal persons"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 35.2: sanctions should be applicable not only to financial institutions and DNFBPs but also to their directors and senior management. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sanctions should be applicable not only to financial institutions and DNFBPs but also to their directors and senior management"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 172: sanctions should be directly or indirectly applicable for a failure to comply; they need not be in the same document that imposes the requirement and can be in another document, provided there are clear links between the requirement and the available sanctions. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided there are clear links between the requirement and the available sanctions"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 36 INTERNATIONAL INSTRUMENTS

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 36.2: countries should fully implement the Vienna, Palermo, Merida and Terrorist Financing Conventions. Footnote 173 lists the relevant articles: Vienna Convention Articles 3-11, 15, 17 and 19; Palermo Convention Articles 5-7, 10-16, 18-20, 24-27, 29-31 and 34; Merida Convention Articles 14-17, 23-24, 26-31, 38, 40, 43-44, 46, 48, 50-55, 57-58; and Terrorist Financing Convention Articles 2-18. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Vienna Convention (Articles 3-11, 15, 17 and 19)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 174: the UNCAC Implementation Review Mechanism, with UNODC as secretariat, assesses implementation of the UNCAC, whereas the FATF assesses compliance with R.36, which has a narrower scope and focus in relation to UNCAC; findings may differ because of differences in methodologies, objectives and scope of the standards. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has a narrower scope and focus"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 36.1: countries should become parties to four instruments: the Vienna Convention, the Palermo Convention, the United Nations Convention against Corruption (the Merida Convention) and the Terrorist Financing Convention. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should become a party to the Vienna Convention, the Palermo Convention"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 37 MUTUAL LEGAL ASSISTANCE

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.1: countries should have a legal basis allowing them to rapidly provide the widest possible range of mutual legal assistance in ML, associated predicate offence and TF investigations, prosecutions and related proceedings. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a legal basis that allows them to rapidly provide the widest possible range of mutual legal assistance"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.2: countries should use a central authority or another established official mechanism for transmission and execution of requests, there should be clear processes for timely prioritisation and execution of mutual legal assistance requests, and a case management system should be maintained to monitor progress. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a case management system should be maintained"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.3: mutual legal assistance should not be prohibited or made subject to unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not be prohibited or made subject to unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.4: countries should not refuse a mutual legal assistance request on the sole ground that the offence also involves fiscal matters, or on grounds of secrecy or confidentiality requirements on financial institutions or DNFBPs, except where the information sought is held in circumstances where legal professional privilege or legal professional secrecy applies. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "on the sole ground that the offence is also considered to involve fiscal matters"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.5: countries should maintain the confidentiality of mutual legal assistance requests received and the information in them, subject to fundamental principles of domestic law, to protect the integrity of the investigation or inquiry. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain the confidentiality of mutual legal assistance requests that they receive"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.6: where mutual legal assistance requests do not involve coercive actions, countries should not make dual criminality a condition for rendering assistance. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not make dual criminality a condition for rendering assistance"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.7: where dual criminality is required for mutual legal assistance, it should be deemed satisfied regardless of whether both countries place the offence in the same category or use the same terminology, provided both criminalise the conduct underlying the offence. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided that both countries criminalise the conduct underlying the offence"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 37.8: powers and investigative techniques required under Recommendation 31 or otherwise available to domestic competent authorities should also be available in response to mutual legal assistance requests and, if consistent with the domestic framework, in response to direct requests from foreign judicial or law enforcement authorities to domestic counterparts; this includes the Recommendation 31 powers on production, search and seizure of information, documents or evidence (including financial records) and taking witness statements, and a broad range of other powers and investigative techniques. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in response to a direct request from foreign judicial or law enforcement authorities to domestic counterparts"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 38 MUTUAL LEGAL ASSISTANCE: FREEZING AND CONFISCATION

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.1: countries should have measures, including legislative measures, to take expeditious action in the widest possible range of circumstances in response to foreign requests for assistance to identify, trace, evaluate, investigate, freeze, seize and confiscate criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to take expeditious action in the widest possible range of circumstances in response to requests for cooperation by foreign countries"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.2: the measures in 38.1 should enable countries to recognise and enforce foreign freezing, seizing and confiscation orders, including orders based on conviction and non-conviction based confiscation proceedings and related provisional measures, as set out in Recommendation 4. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recognise and enforce foreign freezing, seizing and confiscation orders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 176: for requests based on non-conviction based confiscation proceedings, countries should have authority to provide assistance, at a minimum, where a perpetrator is unavailable by reason of death, flight or absence, or the perpetrator is unknown, to the furthest extent consistent with fundamental principles of domestic law. Footnote 175: countries need not require a court to be involved in enforcing foreign orders, but where courts decide enforceability they should have discretion and flexibility to order practical steps to secure the asset. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unavailable by reason of death, flight, absence, or the perpetrator is unknown"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 38: assessors should also consult paragraph 19 of the Introduction to the Methodology. Criteria with requirements on criminal property and property of corresponding value apply whether a criminal defendant or a third party owns or holds the property, and the rights of bona fide third parties are not to be prejudiced. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without prejudicing the rights of bona fide third parties"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.3: when requested countries recognise and enforce foreign freezing, seizing and confiscation orders, they should be able to rely on the foreign order's findings of fact, and enforcement should not depend on a domestic investigation being conducted. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requested countries should be able to rely on the findings of fact in the foreign order"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.4: where the requested country needs a court order before assisting, because of fundamental principles of domestic law or other considerations, requesting countries should make sure their own courts can issue freezing, seizing and confiscation orders for property abroad or, if applicable, have mechanisms for domestic judicial review and validation of orders submitted for enforcement. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requesting countries should ensure that their courts have authority to issue freezing, seizing and confiscation orders"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.6: countries should have measures enabling informal communication with other countries in asset recovery cases, including facilitating assistance before a request is made and updating countries on request status, and the authority to provide further related assistance on an initial request without requiring a supplemental request in appropriate cases. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without requiring a supplemental request, in appropriate cases"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.7: countries should be able to share confiscated property with other countries, in particular when confiscation results directly or indirectly from co-ordinated law enforcement actions, and make arrangements, where appropriate, to deduct or share substantial or extraordinary costs of enforcing a freezing, seizing or confiscation order. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "share confiscated property with other countries, in particular when confiscation is directly or indirectly a result of co-ordinated"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.8: countries should have in place the widest possible range of treaties, arrangements or other mechanisms to enhance co-operation in asset recovery. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "widest possible range of treaties, arrangements, or other mechanisms to enhance co-operation in asset recovery"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 38.5: countries should have mechanisms for managing and preserving frozen, seized or confiscated property and, when necessary, disposing of it, at every stage of the cross-border asset recovery process, as set out in Recommendation 4. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "mechanisms to manage, preserve and, when necessary, dispose of frozen, seized or confiscated property, at all stages"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 39 EXTRADITION

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 39.1: countries should be able to execute extradition requests in relation to ML/TF without undue delay; in particular they should ensure ML and TF are extraditable offences, have a case management system and clear processes for timely execution including prioritisation where appropriate, and not place unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions on executing requests. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "execute extradition requests in relation to ML/TF without undue delay"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 39.2: countries should either extradite their own nationals or, where they do not do so solely on grounds of nationality, at the requesting country's request submit the case without undue delay to their competent authorities for prosecution of the offences set out in the request. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "submit the case without undue delay to its competent authorities for the purpose of prosecution"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 39.3: where dual criminality is required for extradition, it should be deemed satisfied regardless of whether both countries place the offence in the same category or use the same terminology, provided both criminalise the conduct underlying the offence. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where dual criminality is required for extradition, that requirement should be deemed to be satisfied"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 39.4: consistent with fundamental principles of domestic law, countries should have simplified extradition mechanisms in place; footnote 177 gives examples: direct transmission of provisional arrest requests between appropriate authorities, extraditing persons based only on warrants of arrest or judgments, or simplified extradition of consenting persons who waive formal proceedings. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should have simplified extradition mechanisms177 in place"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 40 OTHER FORMS OF INTERNATIONAL CO-OPERATION

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.1: countries should ensure their competent authorities can rapidly provide the widest range of international co-operation on ML, predicate offences and TF, with exchanges possible both spontaneously and upon request. (p. 118)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such exchanges of information should be possible both spontaneously and upon request."</sub>

## General Principles

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.2: competent authorities should have a lawful basis for providing co-operation; be authorised to use the most efficient means; have clear and secure gateways, mechanisms or channels for transmission and execution of requests; have clear processes for prioritisation and timely execution; and have clear processes for safeguarding information received. (p. 118)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have clear and secure gateways, mechanisms or channels that will facilitate and allow for the transmission and execution of requests"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.4: on request, requesting competent authorities should provide timely feedback to authorities from which they received assistance on the use and usefulness of the information obtained. (p. 118)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requesting competent authorities should provide feedback in a timely manner"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.5: countries should not prohibit, or place unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions on, information exchange or assistance; in particular competent authorities should not refuse a request because it also involves fiscal matters; because laws require financial institutions or DNFBPs to maintain secrecy or confidentiality (except where the information is held under legal professional privilege or secrecy); because an inquiry, investigation or proceeding is underway in the requested country, unless assistance would impede it; or because the requesting authority's nature or status (civil, administrative, law enforcement, etc.) differs from that of its foreign counterpart. (p. 118)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unless the assistance would impede that inquiry, investigation or proceeding"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.3: where competent authorities need bilateral or multilateral agreements or arrangements in order to co-operate, these should be negotiated and signed promptly and with as wide a range of foreign counterparts as possible. (p. 118)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these should be negotiated and signed in a timely way and with the widest range of foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.6: countries should establish controls and safeguards to ensure information exchanged by competent authorities is used only for the purpose, and by the authorities, for which it was sought or provided, unless prior authorisation has been given by the requested competent authority. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "used only for the purpose, and by the authorities, for which the information was sought or provided"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.7: competent authorities should maintain appropriate confidentiality for requests and exchanged information, consistent with privacy and data protection obligations, protecting exchanged information at a minimum as they would similar domestically received information, and should be able to refuse to provide information if the requesting authority cannot protect it effectively. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "if the requesting competent authority cannot protect the information effectively"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.8: competent authorities should be able to conduct inquiries on behalf of foreign counterparts and exchange with them all information that would be obtainable by them if the inquiries were carried out domestically. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conduct inquiries on behalf of foreign counterparts and exchange with their foreign counterparts all information"</sub>

## Exchange of Information between FIUs

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.9: FIUs should have an adequate legal basis for providing co-operation on ML, predicate offences and TF. Footnote 178: FIUs should be able to co-operate regardless of whether the counterpart FIU is administrative, law enforcement, judicial or other in nature. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should have an adequate legal basis for providing co-operation on money laundering"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.10: FIUs should provide feedback to foreign counterparts, on request and whenever possible, on the use of the information provided and on the outcome of the analysis conducted based on it. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as well as on the outcome of the analysis conducted, based on the information provided"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 40.11: FIUs should have the power to exchange all information required to be accessible or obtainable directly or indirectly by the FIU, in particular under Recommendation 29, and any other information they have the power to obtain or access domestically, subject to the principle of reciprocity. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subject to the principle of reciprocity"</sub>
