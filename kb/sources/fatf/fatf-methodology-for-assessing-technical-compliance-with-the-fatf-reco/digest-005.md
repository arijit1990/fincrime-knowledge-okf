## RECOMMENDATION 1 ASSESSING RISKS AND APPLYING A RISK-BASED APPROACH

- **descriptive / context**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 1: assessors should refer to Glossary definitions of competent authorities, country, DNFBP, financial institutions, proportionate risk, SRB, should, supervisors, targeted financial sanctions and terrorist financing when assessing this Recommendation. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessors should refer to the following Glossary definitions when assessing this Recommendation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.1: countries should identify and assess the ML/TF risks for the country (footnote 21: supra-national ML/TF risk assessments should be taken into account where appropriate in considering whether the obligation is satisfied). Criterion 1.2: countries should designate an authority or mechanism to co-ordinate actions to assess risks. Criterion 1.3: countries should keep risk assessments up to date. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should designate an authority or mechanism to co-ordinate actions to assess risks."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 20: R.1 requirements should be assessed taking into account the more specific risk-based requirements in other Recommendations; under R.1 assessors form an overall view of risk assessment and mitigation by countries and FIs/DNFBPs without duplicating detailed assessments elsewhere. Assessors are not expected to conduct an in-depth review of the country's risk assessment(s) but should focus on the process, mechanism, information sources and contextual factors, and consider the reasonableness of the conclusions. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not duplicate the detailed assessments of risk-based measures required under other Recommendations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.5 (PF risk assessment): countries should (a) identify and assess PF risks for the country; (b) designate an authority or mechanism to co-ordinate PF risk assessment; (c) keep PF risk assessments up to date; and (d) have mechanisms to provide appropriate PF risk assessment results to all relevant competent authorities, SRBs, financial institutions and DNFBPs. Footnote 22: for R.1, PF risk refers strictly and only to potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of the targeted financial sanctions obligations in R.7; footnote 23: supra-national PF risk assessments should be taken into account where appropriate. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designate an authority or mechanism to co-ordinate actions to assess PF risks;"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.4: countries should have mechanisms for sharing the results of their risk assessment(s) with all relevant competent authorities and self-regulatory bodies (SRBs), financial institutions and DNFBPs. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have mechanisms to provide information on the results of the risk assessment(s)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.8: where countries identify higher risks, they should ensure their AML/CFT regime addresses them, including by requiring FIs and DNFBPs to take enhanced measures to manage and mitigate the risks, or to ensure this information is incorporated into their risk assessments. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring financial institutions and DNFBPs to take enhanced measures to manage and mitigate the risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.9: countries should (a) identify areas of lower risk (footnote 24: for example through national or sub-national risk assessments) to support FIs and DNFBPs in applying proportionate measures; (b) allow and encourage simplified measures for risk-based FATF Recommendations where a lower risk has been identified, with due regard to the country's ML/TF risk assessment, and not permit simplified measures whenever there is a suspicion of ML/TF; and (c) give FIs and DNFBPs guidance or information on possible approaches to implementing simplified measures where risks are lower. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not permit simplified measures whenever there is a suspicion of ML/TF"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 25: encouragement of simplified measures can take the form of government, supervisor or competent authority guidance improving understanding of when simplified measures may be appropriate and their form, or outreach or other engagement with FIs and DNFBPs. Footnote 26: where the Recommendations identify higher risk activities requiring enhanced or specific measures, countries should ensure all such measures are applied, although their extent may vary with the specific level of risk. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "encouragement can take the form of guidance issued by the government, supervisor or other competent authority"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.6: on the basis of their understanding of their risks, countries should use a risk-based approach when allocating resources and putting in place measures to prevent or mitigate ML/TF. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Based on their understanding of their risks, countries should apply a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.7(a): countries that decide not to apply some FATF Recommendations requiring FIs or DNFBPs to take certain actions should demonstrate an assessed low ML/TF risk, that the exemption arises in limited and justified circumstances, and that it relates to a particular type of FI, activity or DNFBP. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is an assessed low risk of ML/TF; the exemption occurs in limited and justified circumstances"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.7(b): alternatively, countries that decide not to apply some such Recommendations should demonstrate that a financial activity, other than transferring money or value, is carried out by a natural or legal person only occasionally or on a very limited basis (having regard to quantitative and absolute criteria), so that ML/TF risk is low. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "carried out by a natural or legal person on an occasional or very limited basis"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.11: based on their understanding of PF risks, countries should implement risk-based measures proportionate to the risks identified and allocate resources efficiently to mitigate PF risks. Countries exempting FIs or DNFBPs from requirements to identify, assess, monitor, manage or mitigate PF risks should demonstrate that the exemption relates to a particular type of FI, activity or DNFBP and that there is an assessed low PF risk for them. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries which decide to exempt financial institutions or DNFBPs from requirements to identify, assess, monitor, manage or mitigate PF risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.11(b)-(d): where countries identify higher PF risks they should ensure their regime addresses them, including by requiring FIs and DNFBPs to take proportionate measures to manage and mitigate the risks; where they identify lower risks, measures should be proportionate to the PF risk while still ensuring full implementation of targeted financial sanctions under Recommendation 7; and supervisors and SRBs should ensure FIs and DNFBPs implement their PF risk obligations under R.1. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while still ensuring full implementation of targeted financial sanctions as required in Recommendation 7"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Footnotes 28 and 29: regardless of any exemption from PF risk requirements, full implementation of targeted financial sanctions as required by R.7 is mandatory in all cases, and R.7 obligations place strict requirements on all natural and legal persons that are not risk-based. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "full implementation of targeted financial sanctions as required by R.7 is mandatory in all cases"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.11: on the basis of their understanding of their PF risks, countries should implement risk-based measures proportionate to the identified risks and allocate resources efficiently to mitigate PF risks. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should implement risk-based measures, proportionate to the risks identified and allocate resources efficiently"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.11(a): countries deciding to exempt FIs or DNFBPs from requirements to identify, assess, monitor, manage or mitigate PF risks should demonstrate that the exemption relates to a particular type of FI, activity or DNFBP and that there is an assessed low PF risk for those FIs, activities or DNFBPs. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries which decide to exempt financial institutions or DNFBPs from requirements to identify, assess, monitor, manage or mitigate PF risks"</sub>

## OBLIGATIONS AND DECISIONS FOR FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS AND DNFBPS

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.12: FIs and DNFBPs should be required to take appropriate steps to identify, assess and understand their ML/TF risks, covering customers, countries or geographic areas, and products, services, transactions or delivery channels. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions and DNFBPs should be required to take appropriate steps to identify, assess and understand"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 30: the nature and extent of any ML/TF risk assessment should be appropriate and proportionate to the nature and size of the business. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be appropriate and proportionate to the nature and size of the business"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Footnote 30: competent authorities or SRBs may decide that individual documented risk assessments are not needed, provided the specific risks inherent to the sector are clearly identified and understood and individual FIs and DNFBPs understand their ML/TF risks. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Competent authorities or SRBs may determine that individual documented risk assessments are not required"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.13: FIs and DNFBPs should be required to have policies, controls and procedures approved by senior management to manage and mitigate risks identified (by the country or by the FI or DNFBP), and to monitor their implementation. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have policies, controls and procedures, which are approved by senior management"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.14: FIs and DNFBPs should be required to apply proportionate measures in line with risks, take enhanced measures where higher risks are identified, and be allowed and encouraged to take simplified measures where lower risks are identified and criteria 1.10, 1.12 and 1.13 are met. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "be allowed and encouraged to take simplified measures to manage and mitigate"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 1.15 (PF risk assessment and mitigation): FIs and DNFBPs should be required to identify and assess their PF risks, including documenting PF risk assessments, keeping them up to date, and having mechanisms to provide PF risk assessment information to competent authorities and SRBs; and to have policies, controls and procedures approved by senior management and consistent with national requirements and guidance from competent authorities and SRBs to manage and mitigate identified PF risks. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify and assess, their PF risks.32 This includes being required to:"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 31: FIs' and DNFBPs' processes to identify, assess, monitor, manage and mitigate PF risks may be carried out within their existing targeted financial sanctions and/or compliance programmes. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be done within the framework of their existing targeted financial sanctions and/or compliance programmes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 32: the nature and extent of any PF risk assessment should be appropriate to the nature and size of the business, and FIs and DNFBPs should always understand their proliferation financing risks. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions and DNFBPs should always understand their proliferation financing risks"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Footnote 32: competent authorities or SRBs may decide that individual documented PF risk assessments are not required, provided the specific risks inherent to the sector are clearly identified and understood. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but competent authorities or SRBs may determine that individual documented risk assessments are not required"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 2 NATIONAL CO-OPERATION AND CO-ORDINATION

- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 2.1: countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies that are informed by the risks identified and regularly reviewed (footnote 34: for R.2, PF risk refers strictly and only to potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of the R.7 targeted financial sanctions obligations). (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies which are informed by the risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 2.2: countries should have inter-agency frameworks enabling policy makers, the FIU, law enforcement authorities, supervisors and other relevant competent authorities to co-operate and, where appropriate, co-ordinate and exchange information domestically on the development and implementation of AML/CFT/CPF policies. Footnotes 35 and 36: INR.2 paragraph 3 examples of relevant authorities are not definitive, and there may be a single framework or separate frameworks for ML, TF and PF. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have inter-agency frameworks in place to enable policy makers"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 2.3: to lead such frameworks, countries should designate one or more authorities or have a co-ordination or other mechanism responsible for setting national AML/CFT/CPF policies and ensuring co-operation and co-ordination among all relevant agencies. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "responsible for setting national AML/CFT/CPF policies and ensuring co-operation and co-ordination among all relevant agencies"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 2.4: countries should have mechanisms permitting effective operational co-operation and, where appropriate, co-ordination and timely sharing of relevant information domestically between competent authorities, both proactively and on request, for operational purposes related to AML, CFT and CPF. Footnote 37: INR.2 paragraph 4 examples of mechanisms are neither definitive nor mandatory. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both proactively and on request, for operational purposes related to AML, CFT and CPF"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 2.5: countries should have co-operation and co-ordination between relevant authorities to ensure compatibility of AML/CFT/CPF requirements with data protection and privacy rules and similar provisions (for example data security/localisation). Footnote 38: for technical compliance the assessment is limited to whether there is co-operation and, where appropriate, co-ordination, formal or informal, between the relevant authorities. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "compatibility of AML/CFT/CPF requirements with Data Protection and Privacy rules"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 2.1: countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies that are informed by the identified risks and are regularly reviewed. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies which are informed by the risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 34: in the context of R.2, risk in relation to PF refers strictly and only to the potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of the targeted financial sanctions obligations in R.7. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers strictly and only to the potential breach, non- implementation or evasion"</sub>

## RECOMMENDATION 3 MONEY LAUNDERING OFFENCE

- **descriptive / context**: Note to Assessors for Recommendation 3: assessors should refer to Glossary definitions of country, designated categories of offences, fundamental principles of domestic law, law, legal persons, money laundering offence, property and should. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designated categories of offences, fundamental principles of domestic law, law, legal persons, money laundering offence, property and should"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 3.1: ML should be criminalised on the basis of the Vienna Convention (Article 3(1)(b) and (c)) and the Palermo Convention (Article 6(1)); footnote 39 notes in particular the physical and material elements of the offence. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ML should be criminalised on the basis of the Vienna Convention and the Palermo Convention"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 3.2: predicate offences for ML should cover all serious offences, with a view to including the widest range of predicate offences, and at a minimum should include a range of offences in each of the designated categories of offences. Footnote 40: R.3 does not require a separate offence of participation in an organised criminal group and racketeering; meeting either Palermo Convention option (a separate offence or an offence based on conspiracy) is sufficient. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "predicate offences for ML should cover all serious offences, with a view to including the widest range of predicate offences"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 3.4: the ML offence should extend to any type of property, regardless of its value, that directly or indirectly represents the proceeds of crime. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "extend to any type of property, regardless of its value, that directly or indirectly represents the proceeds of crime"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 3.5: when proving that property is the proceeds of crime, it should not be necessary that a person be convicted of a predicate offence. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should not be necessary that a person be convicted of a predicate offence"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 41: countries determine the underlying predicate offences for ML by reference to (a) all offences; (b) a threshold linked either to a category of serious offences or to the imprisonment penalty applicable (threshold approach); (c) a list of predicate offences; or (d) a combination of these approaches. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(threshold approach); or (c) to a list of predicate offences; or (d) a combination of these approaches"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 3.3: where countries use a threshold approach, or a combined approach including one, predicate offences should at a minimum cover all offences that fall within the category of serious offences under national law, or that carry a maximum penalty of more than one year's imprisonment, or (for countries with a minimum threshold in their legal system) a minimum penalty of more than six months' imprisonment. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "predicate offences should, at a minimum, comprise all offences that:"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Criterion 3.6: predicate offences for money laundering should cover conduct that took place in another country, where that conduct is an offence there and would have been a predicate offence had it occurred domestically. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which constitutes an offence in that country and which would have constituted a predicate offence"</sub>
