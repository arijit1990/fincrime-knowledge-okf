## C. TIMELY ACCESS TO ADEQUATE, ACCURATE AND UP-TO-DATE INFORMATION

- **descriptive / context**: INR.24 footnote 74: a body could record BO information alongside other information (e.g. basic ownership, incorporation or tax information), or the source could be multiple registries (e.g. by province, district, sector or legal person type such as NPOs) or a private body entrusted by the public authority. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or of a private body entrusted with this task by the public authority"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: INR.24 footnote 75: countries should be able to determine in a timely manner whether a company has or controls an account with a financial institution in the country. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should be able to determine in a timely manner whether a company has or controls an account"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For legal persons under INR.24, "adequate" BO information is information sufficient to identify the natural person(s) who are beneficial owners and the means and mechanisms by which they exercise ownership or control. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Adequate information is information that is sufficient to identify76 the natural person(s) who are the beneficial owner(s)"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: INR.24 footnote 76: examples of information identifying beneficial owners include full name, nationality(ies), full date and place of birth, residential address, national identification number and document type, and tax identification number or equivalent in the country of residence. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "include the full name, nationality(ies), the full date and place of birth, residential address"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For legal persons, "accurate" BO information is information verified by checking the beneficial owner's identity and status using reliable, independently sourced documents, data or information; the extent of verification may vary with risk. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using reliable, independently sourced/obtained documents, data or information"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider complementary measures, such as discrepancy reporting, to support the accuracy of BO information. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider complementary measures as necessary to support the accuracy of beneficial ownership information, e.g. discrepancy reporting."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For legal persons, "up-to-date" BO information is as current as possible and updated within a reasonable period, e.g. within one month, after any change. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is updated within a reasonable period (e.g. within one month) following any change"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities, particularly law enforcement and FIUs, should have all powers needed for timely access to basic and BO information held by relevant parties, including rapid and efficient access to information held by a public authority or body or other competent authority, and/or on which FIs or DNFBPs hold it. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including rapid and efficient access to information held or obtained by a public authority or body"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure national public authorities and others as appropriate have timely access to basic and BO information on legal persons in the course of public procurement. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "timely access to basic and beneficial ownership information on legal persons in the course of public procurement"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should require their company registry to facilitate timely access by FIs, DNFBPs and foreign competent authorities to its public information, at minimum the paragraph 4(a) basic information. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require their company registry to facilitate timely access by financial institutions, DNFBPs"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider facilitating timely FI and DNFBP access to shareholder register information (4(b)) and BO information held under paragraph 7, and could consider facilitating public access to it. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and could consider facilitating public access to this information"</sub>

## D. OBSTACLES TO TRANSPARENCY

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should prevent and mitigate misuse of bearer shares and bearer share warrants (or similar untraceable instruments, per footnote 77) by prohibiting issuance of new ones. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by prohibiting the issuance of new bearer shares and bearer share warrants"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For existing bearer shares and warrants, countries should within a reasonable timeframe either convert them to registered form or immobilise them by requiring them to be held with a regulated FI or professional intermediary, with timely competent authority access. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "immobilising them by requiring them to be held with a regulated financial institution or"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Until conversion or immobilisation is complete, holders of bearer instruments should be required to notify the company, and the company to record their identity, before any associated rights can be exercised. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to notify the company, and the company to record their identity before any rights associated therewith can be exercised"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 78: the bearer share requirements do not apply to bearer shares or warrants of exchange-listed companies subject to disclosure requirements ensuring adequate BO transparency. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These requirements do not apply to newly issued and existing bearer shares or bearer share warrants of a company listed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should prevent and mitigate misuse of nominee shareholders and nominee directors by applying one or more of three mechanisms. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to prevent and mitigate the risk of the misuse of nominee shareholding and nominee directors"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Nominee mechanism (a): require nominees to disclose their status and nominator identity to the company and relevant registry, include it in the register and have it held by the public authority/body or alternative mechanism; nominee status should be public. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Nominee status should be included in public information"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Nominee mechanism (b): require nominees to be licensed, have their status and nominator identity held by the public authority/body or alternative mechanism, and maintain and provide to authorities on request information identifying their nominator and the natural person ultimately acted for. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring nominee shareholders and directors to be licensed79"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Nominee mechanism (c): enforce a prohibition on the use of nominee shareholders or nominee directors. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(c) enforcing a prohibition of the use of nominee shareholders or nominee directors."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 79: a country need not create a separate nominee licensing or registration system for persons already licensed or registered as FIs or DNFBPs that are permitted to perform nominee activities and already subject to full FATF obligations. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are permitted to perform nominee activities and which are already subject to the full range of applicable obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 80: where a nominee holds a controlling interest or exercises effective control, identifying the beneficial owner requires establishing the natural person on whose behalf the nominee ultimately, directly or indirectly, acts. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires establishing the identity of the natural person on whose behalf the nominee is ultimately, directly or indirectly, acting"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 81: intermediaries involved in nominee activities should refer to R.22 and R.28 for the relevant requirements. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For intermediaries involved in such nominee activities, reference should be made to R.22 and R.28"</sub>

## E. OTHER LEGAL PERSONS

- **expected / expectation**: For foundations, Anstalt, Waqf and limited liability partnerships, countries should apply measures and requirements similar to those for companies, accounting for their forms and structures; footnote 82 excludes countries where Waqf are legal arrangements under R.25. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In relation to foundations, Anstalt, Waqf 82 and limited liability partnerships"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For other legal persons, countries should consider their forms, structures and ML/TF risk levels to achieve appropriate transparency, and at minimum ensure similar basic information is recorded, kept accurate and current, and timely accessible to competent authorities. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At a minimum, countries should ensure that similar types of basic information should be recorded and kept accurate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should review ML/TF risks of other legal persons and, based on risk, determine measures ensuring timely competent authority access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date BO information on them. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should review the money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with such other legal persons"</sub>

## F. LIABILITY AND SANCTIONS

- **expected / expectation**: There should be a clearly stated responsibility to comply with INR.24, and liability and effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions as appropriate for any legal or natural person failing to comply properly. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There should be a clearly stated responsibility to comply with the requirements in this Interpretive Note"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should provide the widest possible international cooperation on basic and BO information under Recommendations 37 and 40, including facilitating foreign access to registry basic information, exchanging shareholder information, and using domestic powers to obtain BO information for foreign counterparts. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using their powers, in accordance with their domestic law, to obtain beneficial ownership information on behalf of foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should monitor the quality of assistance received from other countries in response to requests for basic and BO information or for locating beneficial owners abroad. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should monitor the quality of assistance they receive from other countries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should not place unduly restrictive conditions on BO information exchange, e.g. refusing requests because they involve fiscal or tax matters or bank secrecy. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should not place unduly restrictive conditions on the exchange of information or assistance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: BO identification information should be kept readily accessible to facilitate rapid international cooperation, and countries should designate and publicise the agency(ies) responsible for responding to international BO requests. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should designate and make publicly known the agency(ies) responsible for responding to all international requests for BO information."</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 25 (TRANSPARENCY AND BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP OF LEGAL ARRANGEMENTS)

- **expected / expectation**: Under INR.25, countries should require trustees of any express trust (and equivalents in similar arrangements) resident in, or administering trusts in, their country to obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date BO information on the trust. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require trustees of any express trust and persons holding an equivalent position in a similar legal arrangement"</sub>
- **expected / data or feature**: Trust BO information should cover the settlor(s), trustee(s), protectors (if any), each beneficiary or class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other natural person exercising ultimate effective control; for similar arrangements, persons in equivalent positions. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the class of beneficiaries 84 and objects of a power, and (v) any other natural person(s) exercising ultimate effective control"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where parties to a trust are legal persons or arrangements, trustees should also obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and BO information on those parties. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where the parties to the trusts or other similar legal arrangements are legal persons or arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Trustees should also hold basic information on other regulated agents and service providers to the trust, including investment advisors or managers, accountants and tax advisors. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including but not limited to investment advisors or managers, accountants, and tax advisors"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 83: BO information for legal arrangements is that referred to in INR.10 paragraph 5(b)(ii) and the Glossary. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Beneficial ownership information for legal arrangements is the information referred to in the interpretive note to Recommendation 10"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 84: where no beneficiaries are ascertainable when a trust is set up, the trustee should obtain and hold information on the class of beneficiaries and its characteristics, and objects of a power. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where there are no ascertainable beneficiaries at the time of setting up the trust"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 84: on a risk-based approach, countries may decide individual beneficiaries need not be identified for certain charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not necessary to identify the individual beneficiaries of certain charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries whose law governs express trusts or similar arrangements should have mechanisms identifying their types, forms and basic features, describing processes for setting them up and obtaining basic and BO information, and making this publicly available. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries with express trusts and other similar legal arrangements governed under their law should have mechanisms that:"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 85: basic information on a legal arrangement means its identifier (e.g. name, tax identification number or equivalent where it exists), the trust deed (or equivalent) and purposes if any, and the residence of the trustee/equivalent or place of administration. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In relation to a legal arrangement, basic information means the identifier of the legal arrangement"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should assess ML/TF risks of trusts and similar arrangements governed by their law, administered in their country or with a trustee resident there, and foreign arrangements with sufficient links, and take steps to manage and mitigate identified risks (via national and/or supranational measures per footnote 87). (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should assess the money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with different types of trusts"</sub>
- **permissive / definition**: Footnote 86: countries may define a "sufficient link" for foreign legal arrangements on a risk basis, e.g. significant ongoing business relations with FIs or DNFBPs, significant real estate or other local investment, or tax residence of the arrangement or trustee in the country. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has significant and ongoing business relations with financial institutions or DNFBPs, has significant real estate/other local investment"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure trustees and equivalents disclose their status to FIs and DNFBPs when forming a business relationship or conducting an above-threshold occasional transaction in that capacity. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "disclose their status to financial institutions and DNFBPs when, in their function, forming a business relationship"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Trustees should cooperate fully with competent authorities (domestic, or foreign via international cooperation requests, per footnote 88) and not be prevented by law from providing necessary trust information. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should cooperate to the fullest extent possible with, and not be prevented by law or enforceable means"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure trustees are not prevented by law from providing FIs and DNFBPs, on request, with information on the trust's beneficial ownership and the assets to be held or managed in the business relationship. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on the beneficial ownership and the assets of the trust or legal arrangement to be held or managed"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: So competent authorities can access trust information efficiently other than through trustees, countries should consider, based on risk, context and materiality, sources such as a public authority or body holding trust BO information, e.g. a central trust registry or asset registries for land, property, vehicles or shares; information need not be in a single body. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in a central registry of trusts; or in asset registries for land, property, vehicles, shares or other assets"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Other sources countries should consider for trust information include other competent authorities such as tax authorities collecting asset and income information on trusts, and agents or service providers such as TCSPs, investment advisors or managers, accountants, lawyers or financial institutions. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Other agents or service providers, including trust and company service providers,"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should have mechanisms ensuring information on trusts and similar arrangements, including that provided under paragraphs 4 and 5, is adequate, accurate and up-to-date. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have mechanisms that ensure that information on trusts and other similar legal arrangements"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For legal arrangements, adequate information suffices to identify the natural-person beneficial owners and their role (settlors, trustees, protectors, beneficiaries/class, objects of a power, other controllers; and the beneficial owner of any legal-person party, per footnote 91); accurate information is verified using reliable documents with risk-based extent; up-to-date information is updated within a reasonable period after change. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the beneficial owner of that legal person should be identified."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 90: for beneficiaries designated by characteristics or class, trustees are not expected to obtain fully adequate and accurate information until the person becomes entitled at payout or intends to exercise vested rights, per the risk-based approach. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "trustees/equivalent are not expected to obtain fully adequate and accurate information until the person becomes entitled"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure competent authorities, especially law enforcement and FIUs, have powers for timely access to information held by trustees and others, in particular FIs and DNFBPs, on the arrangement's basic and BO information, trustees' residence, and assets held or managed for trustees. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any assets held or managed by the financial institution or DNFBP"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Trustees and equivalents should be required to keep paragraph 1 information for at least five years after their involvement with the trust ends. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for at least five years after their involvement with the trust or similar legal arrangement ceases"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries are encouraged to require other authorities, persons and entities in paragraph 5 to keep trust information for at least five years. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries are encouraged to require the other authorities, persons and entities mentioned in paragraph 5 above"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should require paragraph 1 trust information to be kept accurate and up-to-date and updated within a reasonable period after any change. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require that any information held pursuant to paragraph 1 above should be kept accurate and up-to-date"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider measures facilitating access by FIs and DNFBPs performing R.10 and R.22 CDD to trust information held by the paragraph 5 authorities, persons and entities. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider measures to facilitate access to information that is held on trusts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Countries are not required to give legal recognition to trusts and need not legislate paragraphs 1, 4, 8, 9 and 13 if equivalent trustee obligations exist, e.g. through common law or case law. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries are not required to give legal recognition to trusts"</sub>

## International Cooperation

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should provide rapid, constructive and effective international cooperation on trust information, including BO information, under Recommendations 37 and 40: facilitating foreign access to registry/authority information, exchanging domestic information, and using powers to obtain BO information for foreign counterparts. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exchanging domestically available information on the trusts or other legal arrangement"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should not refuse trust information requests on grounds such as fiscal (including tax) matters or bank secrecy. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refuse a request on the grounds that it involves fiscal (including tax) matters, bank secrecy, etc."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where possible, countries should designate and publicise the agency(ies) responding to international BO requests on legal arrangements, and should consider keeping BO information readily accessible. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider keeping information held or obtained for the purpose of identifying beneficial ownership in a readily accessible manner"</sub>

## Liability and Sanctions

- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure clear responsibilities to comply with INR.25 and that trustees are either legally liable for failures to meet paragraphs 1, 4, 8 and 9 or subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal, civil or administrative sanctions. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are either legally liable for any failure to perform the duties relevant to meeting the obligations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions for failing to give competent authorities timely access to trust information under paragraphs 1 and 8. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for failing to grant to competent authorities timely access to information regarding the trust"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 92: INR.25 liability provisions do not affect requirements for sanctions for non-compliance elsewhere in the Recommendations. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This does not affect the requirements for effective, proportionate, and dissuasive sanctions for failure to comply"</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 26 (REGULATION AND SUPERVISION OF FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS)

- **descriptive / definition**: Under INR.26, a risk-based approach to supervision means both the general process by which a supervisor allocates AML/CFT resources according to its understanding of risk, and the specific process of supervising institutions that apply an AML/CFT risk-based approach. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allocates its resources to AML/CFT supervision; and (b) the specific process of supervising institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Risk-based supervision lets supervisors shift resources to areas perceived as higher risk and use resources more effectively. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allows supervisory authorities to shift resources to those areas that are perceived to present higher risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should have a clear understanding of ML/TF risks in the country, and on-site and off-site access to all relevant information on domestic and international risks of supervised institutions' customers, products and services, including the quality of the compliance function. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the quality of the compliance function of the financial institution or group"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The frequency and intensity of on-site and off-site AML/CFT supervision should be based on the institution's ML/TF risks and controls as assessed in its risk profile, and on country ML/TF risks. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The frequency and intensity of on-site and off-site AML/CFT supervision of financial institutions/groups should be based"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An institution's ML/TF risk profile assessment, including non-compliance risk, should be reviewed periodically and when major events or developments occur in its management and operations; it should not be static. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be reviewed both periodically and when there are major events or developments in the management and operations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervision of institutions applying an RBA should consider the discretion they are allowed and review the underlying risk assessments and the adequacy and implementation of their policies, internal controls and procedures. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should take into account the degree of discretion allowed under the RBA to the financial institution/group"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Risk-based supervision principles should apply to all financial institutions/groups, with supervisors considering their characteristics, particularly their diversity and number and the discretion allowed under the RBA. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in particular the diversity and number of financial institutions, and the degree of discretion allowed to them under the RBA"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure financial supervisors have adequate financial, human and technical resources and sufficient operational independence and autonomy to be free from undue influence or interference. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These supervisors should have sufficient operational independence and autonomy to ensure freedom from undue influence or interference."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should have processes ensuring supervisory staff maintain high professional standards, including on confidentiality, and are of high integrity and appropriately skilled. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "standards, including standards concerning confidentiality, and should be of high integrity and be appropriately skilled."</sub>
