## INTRODUCTION

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Recommendations set an international standard that countries should implement through measures adapted to their circumstances, since legal, administrative, operational and financial frameworks differ. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which countries should implement through measures adapted to their particular circumstances"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Recommendations set essential measures to identify risks and develop policy and domestic coordination; pursue ML, TF and PF; apply preventive measures; establish competent authorities' powers; enhance beneficial ownership transparency of legal persons and arrangements; and facilitate international cooperation. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enhance the transparency and availability of beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The original FATF Forty Recommendations were drawn up in 1990 against laundering of drug money; they were first revised in 1996 to broaden scope beyond drug-money laundering. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The original FATF Forty Recommendations were drawn up in 1990"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In October 2001 FATF expanded its mandate to terrorist financing and created the Eight (later Nine) Special Recommendations; the Recommendations were revised again in 2003 and, with the Special Recommendations, endorsed by over 180 countries. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "creating the Eight (later expanded to Nine) Special Recommendations on Terrorist Financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: After the third round of mutual evaluations, FATF updated the Recommendations with the FSRBs and observers (IMF, World Bank, UN) to address new threats and clarify and strengthen obligations while keeping stability. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Following the conclusion of the third round of mutual evaluations of its members"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FATF Standards strengthen requirements for higher risk situations; countries should first identify, assess and understand their ML/TF risks and then adopt measures to mitigate them. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should first identify, assess and understand the risks of money laundering and terrorist finance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The risk-based approach lets countries adopt more flexible measures within FATF requirements to target resources and apply preventive measures proportionate to risk. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply preventive measures that are proportionate to the nature of risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Most former TF-specific measures are integrated throughout the Recommendations, removing the need for Special Recommendations; TF-unique ones in Section C are R.5 (criminalising TF), R.6 (TF targeted financial sanctions) and R.8 (preventing NPO misuse). (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "therefore obviating the need for the Special Recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In 2008 FATF's mandate was expanded to proliferation financing, and Recommendation 7 aims at consistent, effective implementation of targeted financial sanctions called for by the UN Security Council. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in 2008 the FATF’s mandate was expanded to include dealing with the financing of proliferation"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF Standards comprise the Recommendations, their Interpretive Notes and the applicable Glossary definitions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Standards comprise the Recommendations themselves and their Interpretive Notes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: All FATF and FSRB members should implement the FATF Standards, with implementation assessed through Mutual Evaluations and IMF and World Bank assessments using FATF's common methodology. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their implementation is assessed rigorously through Mutual Evaluation processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Examples in Interpretive Notes and Glossary definitions are not mandatory, are guidance only, not comprehensive, and may not be relevant in all circumstances. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These examples are not mandatory elements of the FATF Standards, and are included for guidance only"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF Guidance and Best Practice Papers are not mandatory for assessing compliance but countries may find them valuable; a list is annexed. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These other documents are not mandatory for assessing compliance with the Standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF commits to constructive dialogue with the private sector and civil society, consulted extensively in the revision, will keep considering changes in light of emerging threats, and calls on all countries to bring national systems into compliance. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF calls upon all countries to implement effective measures"</sub>

## A. AML/CFT POLICIES AND COORDINATION

- **expected / requirement**: FATF Recommendation 1 requires countries to identify, assess and understand their ML/TF risks and act, including designating an authority or mechanism to coordinate risk assessment and apply resources to mitigate risks effectively. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should identify, assess, and understand the money laundering and terrorist financing risks for the country"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 1 countries should apply a risk-based approach (RBA) so ML/TF prevention and mitigation measures are proportionate to identified risks; the RBA is an essential foundation for resource allocation across the AML/CFT regime. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This approach should be an essential foundation to efficient allocation of resources"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 1, where countries identify higher risks their AML/CFT regime should adequately address them; where lower risks are identified they should allow and encourage simplified measures as appropriate. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where countries identify lower risks, they should allow and encourage simplified measures as appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 1 also requires countries to identify, assess and understand proliferation financing risks and take proportionate mitigating action, including designating a coordinating authority or mechanism. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also identify, assess, and understand the proliferation financing risks for the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For Recommendation 1, "proliferation financing risk" refers strictly and only to the potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of the targeted financial sanctions obligations in Recommendation 7. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers strictly and only to the potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of the targeted financial sanctions obligations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 1, where PF risks are lower, measures should be proportionate while still ensuring full implementation of Recommendation 7 targeted financial sanctions. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while still ensuring full implementation of the targeted financial sanctions as required in Recommendation 7"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 1 requires countries to require financial institutions and DNFBPs to identify, assess and take effective risk-based action to mitigate their ML, TF and PF risks. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs) to identify, assess"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 2 requires risk-informed national AML/CFT/CPF policies, regularly reviewed, with a designated authority or coordination mechanism responsible for them. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies, informed by the risks1 identified"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 2 requires policy-makers, the FIU, law enforcement, supervisors and other competent authorities to have effective mechanisms to cooperate and, where appropriate, coordinate and exchange information domestically at policy and operational levels. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have effective mechanisms in place which enable them to cooperate"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 2 cooperation should include ensuring AML/CFT/CPF requirements are compatible with Data Protection and Privacy rules and similar provisions such as data security/localisation. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "compatibility of AML/CFT/CPF requirements with Data Protection and Privacy rules"</sub>

## B. MONEY LAUNDERING AND CONFISCATION

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 3 requires countries to criminalise money laundering on the basis of the Vienna Convention and the Palermo Convention. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should criminalise money laundering on the basis of the Vienna Convention and the Palermo Convention"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 3 requires the ML offence to apply to all serious offences, aiming to include the widest range of predicate offences. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with a view to including the widest range of predicate offences"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4 requires policies and operational frameworks that prioritise asset recovery domestically and internationally. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "policies and operational frameworks that prioritise asset recovery"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4, considering the Vienna, Palermo, UN Convention against Corruption and Terrorist Financing Conventions, requires measures including legislation enabling authorities to identify, trace and evaluate criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify, trace and evaluate criminal property and property of corresponding value"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4 requires authorities to be able to suspend or withhold consent to a transaction and take appropriate investigative measures. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "suspend or withhold consent to a transaction"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4 requires authorities to expeditiously apply provisional measures such as freezing and seizing to prevent dealing, transfer or disposal of criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "expeditiously carry out provisional measures, such as freezing and seizing"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4 requires conviction-based confiscation of criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confiscate criminal property and property of corresponding value through conviction-"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4 requires the ability to confiscate criminal property through non-conviction based confiscation. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confiscate criminal property through non-conviction based confiscation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 4 requires authorities to be able to enforce confiscation orders and effectively manage frozen, seized or confiscated property. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ensure effective management of property that is frozen, seized or confiscated"</sub>
