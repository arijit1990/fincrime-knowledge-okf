## D. DE-LISTING, UNFREEZING AND PROVIDING ACCESS TO FROZEN FUNDS OR OTHER ASSETS

- **expected / expectation**: Under the FATF proliferation-financing targeted financial sanctions interpretive note (para 7), countries should develop and implement publicly known procedures for submitting de-listing requests to the UN Security Council for designated persons and entities that, in the country's view, do not or no longer meet the designation criteria. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should develop and implement publicly known procedures to submit de-listing requests to the Security Council"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Once the UN Security Council or the relevant Sanctions Committee de-lists a person or entity, the obligation to freeze that person's or entity's funds no longer exists. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has de-listed the person or entity, the obligation to freeze no longer exists"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For resolution 1718 (2006) and its successor resolutions (DPRK), de-listing procedures and criteria should accord with any applicable guidelines or procedures adopted by the Security Council under resolution 1730 (2006) and successor resolutions, including those of the Focal Point mechanism established under resolution 1730. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the case of resolution 1718 (2006) and its successor resolutions, such procedures and criteria should be"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should either enable listed persons and entities to petition for de-listing at the Focal Point for de-listing established under resolution 1730 (2006), or inform designated persons or entities that they may petition the Focal Point directly. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should enable listed persons and entities to petition a request for delisting at the Focal Point"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A footnote (28) states that Security Council resolutions apply to all natural and legal persons within the country. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Security Council resolutions apply to all natural and legal persons within the country."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For persons or entities with the same or similar name as a designated person who are inadvertently caught by a freezing mechanism (a false positive), countries should develop and implement publicly known procedures to unfreeze their funds or other assets in a timely manner once it is verified that they are not designated. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who are inadvertently affected by a freezing mechanism (i.e., a false positive)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where countries determine that the exemption conditions in resolution 1718 (2006), resolution 1737 (2006) or resolution 2231 (2015) are met, they should authorise access to funds or other assets in accordance with the procedures set out in those resolutions. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should authorise access to funds or other assets in accordance with the procedures set out therein"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should permit interest or other earnings due on accounts frozen under resolution 1718 (2006) or 1737 (2006), and payments due under contracts, agreements or obligations that arose before the accounts became subject to the freeze, to be added to those accounts, provided such additions remain subject to the provisions and are themselves frozen. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should permit the addition to the accounts frozen pursuant to resolution 1718 (2006) or resolution 1737 (2006)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Freezing action under resolution 1737 (2006) shall not prevent a designated person or entity from making a payment due under a contract entered into before its listing, subject to three conditions set out in para 11 (a)-(c). (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Freezing action taken pursuant to resolution 1737 (2006) shall not prevent a designated person or entity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Condition (a) for a pre-listing contract payment under resolution 1737 (2006): the relevant countries must have determined that the contract is unrelated to any prohibited items, materials, equipment, goods, technologies, assistance, training, financial assistance, investment, brokering or services referred to in the relevant Security Council resolution. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the relevant countries have determined that the contract is not related to any of the"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Condition (b) for a pre-listing contract payment under resolution 1737 (2006): the relevant countries must have determined that the payment is not directly or indirectly received by a person or entity subject to the 1737 (2006) measures. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the relevant countries have determined that the payment is not directly or indirectly"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Condition (c) for a pre-listing contract payment under resolution 1737 (2006): the relevant countries must have notified the 1737 Sanctions Committee of the intention to make or receive the payment or to authorise unfreezing of funds, other financial assets or economic resources for that purpose, ten working days before such authorisation. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ten working days prior to such authorisation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should have mechanisms to communicate de-listings and unfreezings to the financial sector and DNFBPs immediately upon taking such action, and to give adequate guidance to financial institutions and other persons or entities, including DNFBPs, that may hold targeted funds or assets on their obligations to respect a de-listing or unfreezing. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have mechanisms for communicating de-listings and unfreezings to the financial sector and the DNFBPs immediately"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Footnote 29: where the designated person or entity is a financial institution, jurisdictions should consider the FATF guidance annexed to 'The Implementation of Financial Provisions of United Nations Security Council Resolutions to Counter the Proliferation of Weapons of Mass Destruction', adopted in June 2013. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In cases where the designated person or entity is a financial institution, jurisdictions should consider the FATF guidance"</sub>

## A. INTRODUCTION

- **descriptive / definition**: Because NPO legal forms vary by country, the FATF uses a functional definition of a non-profit organisation (NPO) for Recommendation 8, based on the activities and characteristics that may expose an organisation to TF abuse rather than merely its non-profit status. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF has adopted a functional definition of an NPO"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For Recommendation 8, an NPO is a legal person, arrangement or organisation that primarily raises or disburses funds for charitable, religious, cultural, educational, social or fraternal purposes, or for other types of "good works". (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NPO refers to a legal person or arrangement or organisation that primarily engages in raising or disbursing funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Without prejudice to Recommendation 1, Recommendation 8 applies only to organisations within the FATF definition of an NPO, not to the whole universe of not-for-profit organisations in a country. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It does not apply to the entire universe of organisations working in the not-for-profit realm in a country."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF recognises the vital role of NPOs in the world economy and in national economies and social systems, complementing government and business in providing essential services, and acknowledges the difficulty of assisting people in high-risk areas and conflict zones. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NPOs play a vital role in the world economy and in many national economies and social systems."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF acknowledges NPOs' efforts to promote transparency and prevent terrorist financing abuse, including programmes to discourage radicalisation and violent extremism. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including through the development of programmes aimed at discouraging radicalisation and violent extremism"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Factors that may put some NPOs at risk of TF abuse include public trust giving access to considerable funds, being cash-intensive in some contexts, and a global presence providing a framework for national and international operations and transactions, possibly within or near areas most exposed to terrorist activity. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NPOs enjoy public trust, which gives some access to considerable sources of funds, and in some contexts are cash-intensive."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In rare cases terrorist organisations have infiltrated NPOs and misused their funds and operations to cover for or support terrorist activity; terrorists have also created sham NPOs or engaged in fraudulent fundraising. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there have been cases where terrorists create sham NPOs or engage in fraudulent fundraising for these purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Cases have been identified where terrorists exploit some NPOs to raise and move funds, provide logistical support, encourage terrorist recruitment or otherwise support terrorist organisations and operations. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exploit some NPOs in the sector to raise and move funds, provide logistical support, encourage terrorist recruitment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Misuse of NPOs for terrorism facilitates terrorist activity and also undermines donor and financial institution confidence and jeopardises NPO integrity. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "undermines the confidence of donors and financial institutions and jeopardises the very integrity of NPOs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Measures to protect NPOs from potential TF abuse should be focused and in line with the risk-based approach. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Measures to protect NPOs from potential terrorist financing abuse should be focused and in line with the risk-based approach."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Measures protecting NPOs should be implemented in a way that respects countries' obligations under the UN Charter and international law, particularly international human rights, refugee and humanitarian law (footnote 32 refers to UNSC resolution 2462(2019) paras 6 and 23 and UNSC resolution 2664(2022) para.1). (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in particular international human rights, international refugee law and international humanitarian law"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The objective of Recommendation 8 is to ensure NPOs are not abused by terrorists to (i) pose as legitimate entities, (ii) exploit legitimate entities as TF conduits, including to escape asset freezing, or (iii) conceal or obscure the clandestine diversion of funds meant for legitimate purposes to terrorist purposes. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The objective of Recommendation 8 is to ensure that NPOs are not abused by terrorists and terrorist organisations"</sub>

## B. OBJECTIVES AND GENERAL PRINCIPLES

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 8 principle (a): past and ongoing TF abuse of NPOs requires countries to have focused, proportionate and risk-based measures for identified risks; a risk-based approach is essential given sector diversity, differing risk degrees, the need for legitimate NPO activity to flourish, and limited resources. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "focused, proportionate and risk-based measures in dealing with identified risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 8 principle (b): flexibility in the national response to TF abuse of NPOs is essential so it can evolve with the changing TF threat. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "essential, in order to allow it to evolve over time as it faces and responds to the changing nature"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 8 principle (c): measures to protect NPOs should not unduly disrupt or discourage legitimate NPO activities; they should promote accountability and build confidence among NPOs, donors, financial institutions and the public that funds and services reach intended legitimate beneficiaries. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from terrorist financing abuse should not unduly disrupt or discourage legitimate NPO activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Systems promoting a high degree of accountability, integrity and public confidence in NPO management are described as integral to ensuring NPOs cannot be abused for terrorist financing. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Systems that promote achieving a high degree of accountability, integrity and public confidence in the management and functioning of NPOs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 8 principle (d): countries should identify and take effective, proportionate action against NPOs that are exploited by, or knowingly support, terrorists or terrorist organisations, and should aim to prevent and prosecute TF and other terrorist support as appropriate. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should identify and take effective and proportionate action against NPOs that"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where NPOs suspected of or implicated in TF or other terrorist support are identified, countries' first priority must be to investigate and halt that financing or support; actions must respect the rule of law and should minimise, as far as reasonably possible, the impact on innocent and legitimate beneficiaries, though this cannot excuse failing to act immediately and effectively. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the first priority of countries must be to investigate and halt such terrorist financing or support"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 8 principle (e): countries should understand the differing degrees of TF risk posed to NPOs and the proportionate measures to mitigate them under the risk-based approach. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should develop an understanding of the different degrees of TF risk posed to"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Many NPOs may face low TF risk, may have adequate self-regulatory and internal control measures, and/or may already be subject to adequate legal and regulatory requirements, so additional measures may be unnecessary. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Many NPOs may face low TF risk exposure, may have adequate self-regulatory measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should be mindful of the impact of measures on legitimate NPO activities and apply them only where necessary to mitigate assessed TF risks. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should be mindful of the potential impact of measures on legitimate NPO activities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Applying Recommendation 8 TF-protection measures to not-for-profit organisations outside the FATF functional definition of NPOs is not in line with Recommendation 8. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when they do not fall within the FATF’s functional definition of NPOs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Implementing measures that are disproportionate to assessed TF risks, and therefore overly burdensome or restrictive, is not in line with Recommendation 8. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to implement any measures that are not proportionate to the assessed TF risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: NPOs are not reporting entities and should not be required to conduct customer due diligence. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NPOs are not reporting entities and should not be required to conduct customer due diligence."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 8 principle (f): cooperative relationships among public and private sectors and with NPOs are critical to understanding NPO risks and mitigation, raising awareness, increasing effectiveness and building capability against TF abuse. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NPOs is critical to understanding NPOs’ risks and risk mitigation strategies, raising awareness"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should encourage academic research on, and information-sharing in, NPOs to address TF-related issues. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should encourage the development of academic research on, and information-sharing in, NPOs"</sub>
