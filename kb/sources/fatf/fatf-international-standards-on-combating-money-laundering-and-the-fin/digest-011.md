## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 20 (REPORTING OF SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS)

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 23 (DNFBPS – OTHER MEASURES)

- **descriptive / context**: Each country determines what falls under legal professional privilege or professional secrecy; it would normally cover information legal professionals receive from or through a client when ascertaining the client's legal position or defending/representing the client in or concerning judicial, administrative, arbitration or mediation proceedings. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is for each country to determine the matters that would fall under legal professional privilege or professional secrecy."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Countries may allow lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants to send STRs to their self-regulatory organisations, provided there is appropriate cooperation between those organisations and the FIU. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to send their STR to their appropriate self-regulatory organisations, provided that there are appropriate forms of cooperation"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Where legal professionals and accountants acting as independent legal professionals seek to dissuade a client from illegal activity, this does not amount to tipping-off. (p. 96)[^original]
