## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 2 (NATIONAL COOPERATION AND COORDINATION)

- **expected / requirement**: INR.2 requires countries to establish inter-agency frameworks for cooperation and coordination on ML, TF and PF, either a single framework or separate ones for each. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These may be a single framework or different frameworks for ML, TF and PF respectively"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.2 says such frameworks should be led by one or more designated authorities or another mechanism responsible for national policy and inter-agency coordination. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be led by one or more designated authorities, or another mechanism"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.2 lists authorities that could be included: central government departments (finance, trade and commerce, home, justice, foreign affairs), law enforcement, asset recovery and prosecution, the FIU, security and intelligence agencies, customs and border, supervisors and SRBs, tax, import/export control, company and beneficial ownership registries, and others. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company registries, and where they exist, beneficial ownership registries"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: INR.2 examples of such mechanisms: clarifying each authority's role, information needs and sources; standard formats and secure channels for timely information flow; and practical tools such as joint teams or shared data platforms. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "joint teams or shared data platforms"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.2 says countries should ensure mechanisms exist to permit effective operational co-operation and, where appropriate, co-ordination and timely domestic sharing of relevant information between competent authorities for operational purposes related to AML, CFT and CPF, both proactively and on request. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "co-ordination and timely sharing of relevant information domestically between different competent authorities"</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 3 (MONEY LAUNDERING OFFENCE)

- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires criminalising ML on the basis of the 1988 UN Vienna Convention on illicit drug traffic and the 2000 UN Palermo Convention on transnational organized crime. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime, 2000 (the Palermo Convention)"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: INR.3 allows predicate offences to be defined by reference to all offences, a threshold linked to serious offence categories or imprisonment penalty, a list, or a combination. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or to a list of predicate offences; or a combination of these approaches"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under a threshold approach in INR.3, predicates should at minimum include all serious offences under national law, or offences with a maximum penalty of more than one year's imprisonment, or, where a minimum threshold system exists, offences with a minimum penalty of more than six months imprisonment. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "punished by a minimum penalty of more than six months imprisonment"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires each country, whatever its approach, to include a range of offences within each designated category of offences. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "include a range of offences within each of the designated categories of offences"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires the ML offence to extend to any property of any value directly or indirectly representing crime proceeds. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should extend to any type of property, regardless of its value"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 states proving property is crime proceeds should not require a conviction for a predicate offence. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should not be necessary that a person be convicted of a predicate offence"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires predicates to extend to foreign conduct that is an offence there and would be a predicate domestically; countries may require only that it would have been a domestic predicate. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Predicate offences for money laundering should extend to conduct that occurred in another country"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: INR.3 lets countries exclude self-laundering (ML offence not applying to the predicate offender) where fundamental domestic law principles require. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not apply to persons who committed the predicate offence"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires that intent and knowledge for ML may be inferred from objective factual circumstances. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The intent and knowledge required to prove the offence of money laundering may be"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal sanctions for natural persons convicted of ML. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal sanctions should apply to natural"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 (and INR.5 for TF) require criminal liability for legal persons, or civil/administrative liability where criminal is impossible under domestic law, without precluding parallel proceedings or prejudicing natural persons' liability. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This should not preclude parallel criminal, civil or administrative proceedings with respect to legal persons"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.3 requires ancillary ML offences: participation, association or conspiracy, attempt, aiding and abetting, facilitating and counselling, unless barred by domestic law principles. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including participation in, association with or conspiracy to commit, attempt, aiding and abetting"</sub>

## A. Prioritisation and asset recovery frameworks

- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires countries to review their asset recovery regime for ongoing effectiveness and resource asset recovery sufficiently. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should review their asset recovery regime to ensure its ongoing effectiveness"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires domestic cooperation and coordination frameworks and agency structures, consistent with Recommendation 2, to use asset recovery measures effectively. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "domestic cooperation and coordination frameworks and agency structures"</sub>

## B. Criminal property and property of corresponding value

- **descriptive / definition**: INR.4 states criminal property and property of corresponding value extend to property owned or held by third parties, without prejudicing bona fide third parties' rights. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "extends to property owned or held by third parties, but without prejudicing the rights of bona fide third parties"</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 4 (CONFISCATION AND PROVISIONAL MEASURES)

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: INR.4 examples of non-bona fide third-party holdings: property under the defendant's effective control held by family, associates or legal persons and arrangements; and property gifted or transferred for significantly above or below market value. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "significantly above or below market value"</sub>

## C. Provisional measures

- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires enabling the FIU or another authority to act immediately to withhold consent to or suspend a transaction suspected of ML, predicate or TF links, with a specified maximum duration allowing analysis and initiation of freezing or seizure. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The maximum duration of this measure should be specified"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires that initial freeze or seize applications can be made ex parte or without prior notice, and that provisional measures avoid unreasonable conditions such as demonstrating dissipation risk. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as in relation to demonstrating the risk of dissipation"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: INR.4 footnote 7: ex parte proceedings may carry domestic safeguards such as triggering notice or an inter partes review after the measure. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the triggering of notice or an inter partes review after the implementation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires, where necessary for speed, authority to freeze and seize without a court order, subject to judicial review within a period; if inconsistent with domestic law, an alternative mechanism that systematically prevents dissipation quickly enough may be used. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "freeze and seize criminal property and property of corresponding value without a court order"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires measures to prevent or void actions prejudicing the ability to freeze, seize or confiscate. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prevent or void actions that prejudice the country’s ability to freeze or seize or confiscate"</sub>

## D. Confiscation

- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires a comprehensive range of confiscation measures, applied depending on case circumstances, respecting substantive and procedural rights and safeguards. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "respects the substantive and procedural rights and safeguards that may be implicated by confiscation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires measures enabling confiscation of criminal property and corresponding value following conviction. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "following the conviction of a person"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires, consistent with domestic law principles, extended confiscation of other property of persons convicted of ML, predicates or TF where the court is satisfied it derives from criminal conduct; it may be limited to serious offences. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable confiscation to be extended to other property of a person convicted"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: INR.4 footnote 9: indicators that property derives from criminal conduct include its value representing a criminal lifestyle or being disproportionate to the convicted person's lawful income. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is disproportionate to the lawful income of the convicted person"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires non-conviction based confiscation in ML, predicate or TF cases, consistent with domestic law, with flexibility in implementation; it may be limited to serious offences. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries have flexibility in how they implement non-conviction based confiscation"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: INR.4 says countries should consider requiring offenders to demonstrate the lawful origin of property alleged liable to confiscation. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require an offender to demonstrate the lawful origin of the property"</sub>

## E. Asset recovery and tax authorities

- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires competent and tax authorities to cooperate, coordinate and share information domestically to enhance asset recovery and identify criminal property, which may support recovery of tax liabilities. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "competent authorities and tax authorities to cooperate"</sub>

## F. Asset management, return and disposal

- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires effective mechanisms to manage, preserve and dispose of frozen, seized or confiscated property, including pre-confiscation sale where appropriate to preserve value. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should include the pre-confiscation sale of property, where appropriate"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: INR.4 says countries should consider an asset recovery fund receiving all or part of confiscated property for law enforcement, health, education or other appropriate purposes. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider establishing an asset recovery fund into which all, or a portion of, confiscated property"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.4 requires ability to enforce confiscation orders and realise property leading to permanent deprivation, and mechanisms to return property to prior legitimate owners or compensate crime victims. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "return confiscated property to its prior legitimate owners or to use it to compensate victims of crime"</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 5 (TERRORIST FINANCING OFFENCE)

- **descriptive / context**: INR.5 states Recommendation 5 aims to give countries legal capacity to prosecute and sanction terrorism financiers and to emphasise the link with ML by making TF offences ML predicates. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legal capacity to prosecute and apply criminal sanctions to persons that finance terrorism"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 requires TF offences to cover anyone wilfully providing or collecting funds or assets by any means, directly or indirectly, intending or knowing they will be used in whole or part for a terrorist act, by a terrorist organisation, or by an individual terrorist. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "wilfully provides or collects funds or other assets by any means, directly or indirectly"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: INR.5 states TF includes financing travel of individuals to a State other than their residence or nationality to perpetrate, plan, prepare or participate in terrorist acts or give or receive terrorist training. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financing the travel of individuals who travel to a State other than their States of residence or nationality"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 states criminalising TF solely via aiding and abetting, attempt or conspiracy is insufficient. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Criminalising terrorist financing solely on the basis of aiding and abetting, attempt, or conspiracy is not sufficient"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 requires TF offences to cover funds from legitimate or illegitimate sources and not require that funds were actually used for, or linked to, a specific terrorist act. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether from a legitimate or illegitimate source"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 requires that TF intent and knowledge may be inferred from objective facts, and that effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal sanctions apply to convicted natural persons. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal sanctions should apply to natural persons convicted of terrorist financing"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 requires attempted TF to be an offence, as well as participating as an accomplice and organising or directing others to commit TF. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It should also be an offence to attempt to commit the offence of terrorist financing"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 requires criminalising intentional contribution to TF by a group with common purpose, made either to further the group's TF activity or purpose or knowing the group intends to commit TF. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "be made in the knowledge of the intention of the group to commit a terrorist financing offence"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.5 requires TF offences to apply regardless of whether the offender is in the same or a different country from the terrorist, organisation or terrorist act. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regardless of whether the person alleged to have committed the offence(s) is in the same country"</sub>

## A. OBJECTIVE

- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires each country to implement targeted financial sanctions freezing without delay, and prohibiting availability of, funds or assets of persons (natural or legal) or entities designated by the UNSC under Chapter VII per resolution 1267 (1999) and successors, or designated by the country under resolution 1373 (2001). (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 6 requires each country to implement targeted financial sanctions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INR.6 states Recommendation 6 does not replace other measures for dealing with funds in criminal, civil or administrative proceedings under Recommendation 4; it may complement criminal proceedings but is not conditional on them, focusing on preventive measures to stop flows to and use of funds by terrorist groups. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but are not conditional upon the existence of such proceedings"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: INR.6 states that in setting and building support for a CFT regime countries must respect human rights and the rule of law and recognise the rights of innocent third parties. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries must also respect human rights, respect the rule of law, and recognise the rights of innocent third parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INR.6 footnote 13: freezing, seizure and confiscation obligations in the Vienna, Palermo and Terrorist Financing Conventions exist separately from Recommendation 6 and UNSC TF resolutions. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Those obligations exist separately and apart from the obligations set forth in Recommendation 6"</sub>

## B. IDENTIFYING AND DESIGNATING PERSONS AND ENTITIES FINANCING OR SUPPORTING TERRORIST ACTIVITIES

- **descriptive / context**: Under resolution 1267 and successors, Al-Qaida designations are made by the 1267 Committee and Taliban/Afghanistan-related designations by the 1988 Committee, both under Chapter VII; resolution 1373 designations are made nationally or supranationally on a country's own motion or at another country's request if supported by reasonable grounds or a reasonable basis. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designations pertaining to the Taliban and related threats to Afghanistan are made by the 1988 Committee"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires countries to have authority and effective procedures to identify and propose designations to the UNSC under resolution 1267 and successors, and to identify and make designations under S/RES/1373 (2001). (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify and initiate proposals for designations of persons and entities targeted by resolution 1267"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INR.6 footnote 14: UNSC resolutions do not require countries to actually identify and submit persons to UN Committees, but to have the authority and mechanisms to be able to do so. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The relevant Security Council resolutions do not require countries to identify persons or entities and submit these"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires a competent authority or court responsible for proposing to the 1267 Committee persons meeting resolution 1989 (2011) Al-Qaida criteria, proposing to the 1988 Committee persons meeting resolution 1988 (2011) Taliban criteria, and designating persons meeting resolution 1373 (2001) criteria. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proposing to the 1267 Committee, for designation as appropriate, persons or entities that meet the specific criteria"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires mechanisms to identify designation targets and to examine and give effect to other countries' resolution 1373 freezing actions, making a prompt determination on received requests. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they make a prompt determination whether they are satisfied"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires competent authorities to have legal powers and procedures to collect or solicit as much information as possible from all relevant sources to identify persons meeting designation criteria. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collect or solicit as much information as possible from all relevant sources"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires a "reasonable grounds" or "reasonable basis" evidentiary standard for designation decisions, applied per each country's legal system for resolution 1373, whether on own motion or another country's request, and not conditional on criminal proceedings. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such (proposals for) designations should not be conditional upon the existence of a criminal proceeding"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: When proposing names to the 1267 Committee for the Al-Qaida Sanctions List, countries should follow its procedures and standard forms, provide maximum identifying information including that needed for an Interpol Special Notice, provide a detailed statement of case releasable on request except confidential parts, and specify whether their designating-state status may be disclosed. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "specify whether their status as a designating state may be made known"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: When proposing names to the 1988 Committee for the Taliban Sanctions List, countries should follow its listing procedures, provide maximum identifying information including Interpol Special Notice data, and provide a detailed statement of case including connections to designated persons. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for inclusion on the Taliban Sanctions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: When asking another country to give effect to resolution 1373 freezing actions, the initiating country should provide detailed identifying information and specific information supporting that designation criteria are met. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the initiating country should provide as much detail as possible on: the proposed name"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires procedures to operate ex parte against a person or entity whose designation is being considered. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have procedures to be able to operate ex parte against a person or"</sub>

## C. FREEZING AND PROHIBITING DEALING IN FUNDS OR OTHER ASSETS OF DESIGNATED PERSONS AND ENTITIES

- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 obliges countries to implement TFS without delay against 1267 and 1988 Committee designations; for resolution 1373 the freezing and dealing prohibition obligation is triggered by a (supra-)national designation. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is an obligation for countries to implement targeted financial sanctions without delay"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 para 5bis requires countries implementing UNSC Chapter VII asset freezes to comply with resolutions 2664 (2022), 2761 (2024) and 2615 (2021), as applicable. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "resolution 2664 (2022), resolution 2761 (2024), and resolution 2615 (2021)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.6 requires necessary legal authority and designated domestic competent authorities for implementing and enforcing TFS, including requiring all natural and legal persons in the country to freeze. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify domestic competent authorities responsible for implementing and enforcing targeted financial sanctions"</sub>
