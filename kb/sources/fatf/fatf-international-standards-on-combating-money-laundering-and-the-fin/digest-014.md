## D. SANCTIONS

- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 32 safeguards should ensure proper use of information without in any way restricting trade payments between countries for goods and services or the freedom of capital movements. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "trade payments between countries for goods and services; or (ii) the freedom of capital movements, in any way"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Persons making a false declaration or disclosure of currency or BNIs should be subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive criminal, civil or administrative sanctions. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Persons who make a false declaration or disclosure should be subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Persons physically transporting currency or BNIs across borders in connection with TF, ML or predicate offences should face effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions and measures consistent with Recommendation 4 enabling confiscation of the currency or BNIs. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be subject to measures, consistent with Recommendation 4, which would enable the confiscation of such currency or BNIs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities implementing Recommendation 32 should have adequate financial, human and technical resources and staff with high professional standards (including confidentiality), integrity and skills. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities responsible for implementation of Recommendation 32 should have adequate financial, human and technical resources."</sub>

## E. GOLD, PRECIOUS METALS AND PRECIOUS STONES

- **descriptive / limitation**: Gold, precious metals and precious stones are excluded from Recommendation 32 despite their high liquidity and use as a means of exchange or value transmission; they may be covered by customs laws. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "gold, precious metals and precious stones are not included, despite their high liquidity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A country discovering an unusual cross-border movement of gold, precious metals or stones should consider notifying the customs or other competent authorities of origin and/or destination countries and cooperate to establish the source, destination and purpose and take appropriate action. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If a country discovers an unusual cross-border movement of gold, precious metals or precious stones"</sub>

## Glossary of specific terms used in this Recommendation

- **descriptive / definition**: R.32 glossary: a false declaration is a misrepresentation of the value of currency or BNIs transported or of other required data, including failing to make a required declaration. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This includes failing to make a declaration as required."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.32 glossary: a false disclosure is a misrepresentation of the value of currency or BNIs or other data asked for on request, including failing to make a required disclosure. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This includes failing to make a disclosure as required."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.32 glossary: physical cross-border transportation is any in-bound or out-bound transport of currency or BNIs between countries, including by a natural person or their accompanying luggage or vehicle, shipment through containerised cargo, or mailing by a natural or legal person. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shipment of currency or BNIs through containerised cargo or (3) the mailing of currency or BNIs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.32 glossary: currency or BNIs "related to terrorist financing or money laundering" are those that are proceeds of, used in, or intended for financing terrorism, terrorist acts or organisations, or that are laundered, proceeds of ML or predicate offences, or instrumentalities used or intended for these offences. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proceeds from money laundering or predicate offences, or instrumentalities used in or intended for use"</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 38 (MUTUAL LEGAL ASSISTANCE: FREEZING AND CONFISCATION)

- **expected / expectation**: Under INR.38, countries should be able to act expeditiously on cooperation requests in the widest possible range of circumstances, including requests based on conviction and non-conviction based confiscation proceedings and related provisional measures under Recommendation 4. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requests made on the basis of conviction and non-conviction based confiscation proceedings and related provisional measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 93: for non-conviction based confiscation requests, countries should at minimum be able to assist where the perpetrator is unavailable due to death, flight or absence, or is unknown, as far as consistent with fundamental principles of domestic law. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when a perpetrator is unavailable by reason of death, flight, absence, or the perpetrator is unknown"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When recognising and enforcing foreign freezing, seizing or confiscation orders, requested countries should be able to rely on the foreign order's findings of fact, and enforcement should not depend on a domestic investigation. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Enforcement should not be made conditional on conducting a domestic investigation."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Courts in the requested country may review a foreign order and issue orders needed to give it effect for property located there. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "courts in the requested country may review the foreign order and issue any orders necessary to give it effect"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where the requested country needs a court order, requesting countries should ensure their courts can issue freezing, seizing and confiscation orders for property abroad, or have mechanisms for domestic judicial review and validation of orders submitted for enforcement. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requesting countries should ensure that their courts have authority to issue freezing, seizing, and confiscation orders for property located abroad"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should be able to provide further related assistance on an initial request without requiring a supplemental request, in appropriate cases. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide further related assistance on an initial request, without requiring a supplemental request"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should have effective mechanisms for managing, preserving and, when necessary, disposing of frozen, seized or confiscated property, per Recommendation 4. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have effective mechanisms for managing, preserving, and, when necessary, disposing of"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should be able to share confiscated property with other countries, particularly when confiscation results from coordinated law enforcement action, and to arrange to deduct or share substantial or extraordinary enforcement costs. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should be able to share confiscated property with other countries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should enable informal communication with other countries in asset recovery cases, including assistance before a formal request and status updates on requests. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable informal communication with other countries in asset recovery cases"</sub>

## A. PRINCIPLES APPLICABLE TO ALL FORMS OF INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION

- **expected / expectation**: On request, requesting authorities should give the requested authority feedback on the use and usefulness of information obtained. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide feedback to the requested competent authority on the use and usefulness of the information obtained"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should not prohibit or place unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions on information exchange or assistance. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should not prohibit or place unreasonable or unduly restrictive conditions on the provision of exchange"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should not refuse assistance because the request also involves fiscal matters. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the request is also considered to involve fiscal matters; and/or"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should not refuse assistance because laws require FIs or DNFBPs to maintain secrecy or confidentiality, except where the information is held under legal privilege or legal professional secrecy. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that is sought is held in circumstances where legal privilege or legal professional secrecy applies) to maintain secrecy"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should not refuse assistance because an inquiry, investigation or proceeding is underway in the requested country, unless assistance would impede it. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unless the assistance would impede that inquiry, investigation or proceeding"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should not refuse assistance because the requesting authority's nature or status (civil, administrative, law enforcement, etc.) differs from its foreign counterpart's. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nature or status (civil, administrative, law enforcement, etc.) of the requesting"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Exchanged information should be used only for the purpose for which it was sought or provided; further dissemination or use for administrative, investigative, prosecutorial or judicial purposes beyond those approved requires prior authorisation from the requested authority. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Exchanged information should be used only for the purpose for which the information was sought or provided."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should keep cooperation requests and exchanged information appropriately confidential, consistent with privacy and data protection obligations, and protect it at least as they would similar domestic information; footnote 94 allows disclosure needed to carry out the request. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "protect exchanged information in the same manner as they would protect similar information received from domestic sources"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should establish controls and safeguards so exchanged information is used only as authorised, and exchanges should occur securely through reliable channels. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Exchange of information should take place in a secure way, and through reliable channels or mechanisms."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Requested authorities may refuse to provide information if the requesting authority cannot protect it effectively. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refuse to provide information if the requesting competent authority cannot protect the information effectively"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should be able to conduct inquiries for foreign counterparts and exchange all information they could obtain if the inquiries were domestic. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Competent authorities should be able to conduct inquiries on behalf of a foreign counterpart"</sub>

## B. PRINCIPLES APPLICABLE TO SPECIFIC FORMS OF INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION

- **descriptive / context**: The general cooperation principles apply to all information exchange between counterparts or non-counterparts, subject to the specific paragraphs that follow. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should apply to all forms of exchange of information between counterparts or non-counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIUs should exchange information with foreign FIUs regardless of their administrative, law enforcement, judicial or other status, and should have an adequate legal basis for cooperation on ML, predicate offences and TF. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should exchange information with foreign FIUs, regardless of their respective status"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On request and whenever possible, FIUs should give foreign counterparts feedback on use of the information and the outcome of analysis based on it. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as well as on the outcome of the analysis conducted, based on the information provided"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIUs should have power to exchange all information they must be able to access or obtain under the FATF Recommendations (especially R.29), and any other information they can obtain domestically, subject to reciprocity. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any other information which they have the power to obtain or access, directly or"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure the FIU or another competent authority can act immediately, directly or indirectly, to withhold consent to or suspend a transaction suspected of ML, predicate offences or TF at a foreign counterpart's request; if the empowered authorities are not counterparts, the FIU should be able to send or receive such requests. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to withhold consent to or suspend a transaction suspected of being related to money laundering"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Financial supervisors (those that are competent authorities, per footnote 95) should cooperate with foreign counterparts regardless of nature or status, with an adequate legal basis consistent with international supervisory standards, particularly for AML/CFT-relevant supervisory information. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial supervisors should cooperate with their foreign counterparts, regardless of their respective nature or status."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Financial supervisors should be able to exchange domestically available information, including information held by FIs, proportionately, especially with supervisors sharing responsibility for institutions in the same group. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in particular with other relevant supervisors that have a shared responsibility for financial institutions operating in the same group"</sub>
- **expected / data or feature**: Supervisory information types exchangeable for AML/CFT: regulatory information (domestic regulatory system, general sector information) and prudential information, particularly for Core Principle Supervisors (business activities, beneficial ownership, management, fit and properness). (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "on the financial institution’s business activities, beneficial ownership, management, and fit and properness."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When FIUs make a cooperation request, they should make best efforts to provide complete factual and, as appropriate, legal information, including a description of the case being analysed and its potential link to the requested country. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the description of the case being analysed and the potential link to the requested country"</sub>
- **expected / data or feature**: AML/CFT information exchangeable between supervisors includes institutions' internal AML/CFT policies and procedures, CDD information, customer files, samples of accounts and transaction information. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "institutions, customer due diligence information, customer files, samples of accounts and transaction information."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Financial supervisors should be able to conduct inquiries for foreign counterparts and, as appropriate, authorise or facilitate foreign counterparts' own inquiries in the country, to facilitate group supervision. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to authorise or facilitate the ability of foreign counterparts to conduct inquiries themselves in the country"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Dissemination or use of supervisory information exchanged should require the requested supervisor's prior authorisation, unless the requesting supervisor is legally obliged to disclose it, in which case it should promptly inform the requested authority; prior authorisation includes deemed authorisation under an MoU or a core principles standard-setter's Multi-lateral MoU. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unless the requesting financial supervisor is under a legal obligation to disclose or report the information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement authorities should be able to exchange domestically available information with foreign counterparts for intelligence or investigative purposes on ML, predicate offences or TF. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement authorities should be able to exchange domestically available information with foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement should be able to use their powers and domestic investigative techniques to make inquiries and obtain information for foreign counterparts; regimes such as agreements with Interpol, Europol or Eurojust should govern use restrictions. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Europol or Eurojust and individual countries, should govern any restrictions on use imposed by the requested law enforcement authority."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement should be able to form joint investigative teams, with countries establishing bilateral or multilateral arrangements where necessary. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement authorities should be able to form joint investigative teams to conduct cooperative investigations"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries are encouraged to join and support AML/CFT law enforcement networks and develop bilateral contacts, including posting liaison officers abroad. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including placing liaison officers abroad, in order to facilitate timely and effective cooperation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement should cooperate with foreign counterparts to identify and trace criminal property and property of corresponding value and support freezing, seizing and confiscation via formal mutual legal assistance. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to identify and trace criminal property and property of corresponding value, and in support of the freezing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement should be able, in appropriate cases, to open domestic investigations or proceedings based on information from foreign counterparts. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be able to commence domestic investigations or proceedings based on such information received from foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement should be able to spontaneously share information on criminal property with foreign counterparts without prior request, and to spontaneously identify and trace property suspected to relate to a foreign investigation in their jurisdiction, in appropriate cases. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "spontaneously share relevant information regarding criminal property and property of corresponding value with foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Law enforcement retains discretion over when and under what conditions to share such information, e.g. to avoid prejudicing domestic investigations. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement authorities have discretion on when and under what conditions to share such information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should participate in and actively support multilateral asset recovery networks and apply for membership of a relevant Asset Recovery Inter-agency Network (ARIN) or similar body. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should apply for membership in a relevant Asset Recovery Inter-agency Network (ARIN)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should permit competent authorities to exchange information indirectly with non-counterparts, applying the relevant principles. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should permit their competent authorities to exchange information indirectly with non-counterparts"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Indirect exchange of information means requested information passes from the requested authority through one or more domestic or foreign authorities before reaching the requester; it and its use may require authorisation by competent authorities of the requested country. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Indirect exchange of information refers to the requested information passing from the requested authority through one or more"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An authority requesting information indirectly should always make clear the purpose and on whose behalf the request is made. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should always make it clear for what purpose and on whose behalf the request is made"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries are encouraged to permit prompt and constructive direct information exchange with non-counterparts. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries are also encouraged to permit a prompt and constructive exchange of information directly with non-counterparts."</sub>
