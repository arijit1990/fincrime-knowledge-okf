## C. TERRORIST FINANCING AND FINANCING OF PROLIFERATION

- **expected / requirement**: FATF Recommendation 5 requires countries to criminalise terrorist financing on the basis of the Terrorist Financing Convention, covering financing of terrorist acts and also of terrorist organisations and individual terrorists even without a link to a specific terrorist act. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "even in the absence of a link to a specific terrorist act or acts"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 5 requires TF offences to be designated as money laundering predicate offences. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should ensure that such offences are designated as money laundering predicate offences"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 6 requires targeted financial sanctions regimes complying with UNSC terrorism and TF resolutions: freezing without delay funds or assets of, and making none available directly or indirectly to or for, persons or entities designated by the UNSC under Chapter VII (including resolution 1267 (1999) and successors) or designated by the country under resolution 1373 (2001). (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designated by that country pursuant to resolution 1373 (2001)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 7 requires targeted financial sanctions complying with UNSC resolutions on preventing, suppressing and disrupting WMD proliferation and its financing: freezing without delay and prohibiting provision of funds or assets to persons or entities designated by or under the UNSC under Chapter VII. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prevention, suppression and disruption of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and its financing"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 8 requires countries to identify organisations within the FATF definition of non-profit organisations (NPOs) and assess their TF risks. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should identify the organisations which fall within the FATF definition of non-profit organisations (NPOs)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 8 requires focused, proportionate, risk-based measures for NPOs that do not unduly disrupt or discourage legitimate NPO activity. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without unduly disrupting or discouraging legitimate NPO activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Recommendation 8 identifies TF abuse of NPOs by: terrorist organisations posing as legitimate entities; exploiting legitimate entities as TF conduits, including to escape asset freezes; and concealing clandestine diversion of legitimately intended funds to terrorist organisations. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by terrorist organisations posing as legitimate entities"</sub>

## D. PREVENTIVE MEASURES

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 9 requires countries to ensure financial institution secrecy laws do not inhibit implementation of the FATF Recommendations. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial institution secrecy laws do not inhibit implementation of the FATF Recommendations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 10 requires financial institutions to be prohibited from keeping anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prohibited from keeping anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 10 requires CDD when establishing business relations, and for occasional transactions above the designated threshold (USD/EUR 15,000) or that are payments/value transfers covered by the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 16. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "carrying out occasional transactions: (i) above the applicable designated threshold"</sub>

## CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE AND RECORD-KEEPING

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 10 also requires CDD where there is suspicion of ML or TF, or where the institution doubts the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained identification data. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 10 the principle that FIs conduct CDD should be set out in law; each country may impose specific CDD obligations through law or enforceable means. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The principle that financial institutions should conduct CDD should be set out in law"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: CDD measure (a) under Recommendation 10: identify the customer and verify identity using reliable, independent source documents, data or information. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "independent source documents, data or information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: CDD measure (b): identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify identity so the FI is satisfied it knows who the beneficial owner is; for legal persons and arrangements this includes understanding ownership and control structure. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understanding the ownership and control structure of the customer"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: CDD measure (c): understand and, as appropriate, obtain information on the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtaining information on the purpose and intended"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: CDD measure (d): conduct ongoing due diligence and scrutiny of transactions to ensure consistency with the institution's knowledge of the customer, business and risk profile, including source of funds where necessary. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transactions undertaken throughout the course of that relationship"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FIs must apply each CDD measure (a) to (d) but determine their extent using a risk-based approach per the Interpretive Notes to Recommendations 10 and 1. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should determine the extent of such measures using a risk-based approach (RBA)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 10 requires verification of customer and beneficial owner identity before or during establishment of a business relationship or occasional transactions. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "verify the identity of the customer and beneficial owner before or during the course of establishing a business relationship"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Countries may permit verification to be completed as soon as reasonably practicable after establishing the relationship where ML/TF risks are effectively managed and this is essential not to interrupt normal business. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as soon as reasonably practicable following the establishment of the relationship"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: If an FI cannot comply with CDD measures (a) to (d), it should not open the account, start relations or perform the transaction, or should terminate the relationship, and should consider filing a suspicious transaction report. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider making a suspicious transactions report in relation to the customer"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 10 applies to all new customers, and FIs should apply it to existing customers based on materiality and risk, conducting due diligence on existing relationships at appropriate times. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply this Recommendation to existing customers on the basis of materiality and risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 11 requires FIs to keep all necessary domestic and international transaction records for at least five years to comply swiftly with competent authority requests. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain, for at least five years, all necessary records on transactions, both domestic and international"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 11 transaction records must allow reconstruction of individual transactions, including amounts and currency types, to provide evidence for prosecution if needed. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sufficient to permit reconstruction of individual transactions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 11 requires keeping CDD records (e.g. copies of passports, identity cards, driving licences), account files, business correspondence and analysis results (e.g. inquiries into complex, unusual large transactions) for at least five years after the relationship ends or after the occasional transaction. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for at least five years after the business relationship is ended, or after the date of the occasional transaction"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 11 requires FIs to be required by law to maintain transaction and CDD records, which should be available to domestic competent authorities upon appropriate authority. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions should be required by law to maintain records on transactions"</sub>

## ADDITIONAL MEASURES FOR SPECIFIC CUSTOMERS AND ACTIVITIES

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 12 requires FIs, for foreign PEPs (customer or beneficial owner), in addition to normal CDD, to have risk-management systems to determine PEP status, obtain senior management approval to establish or continue the relationship, take reasonable measures to establish source of wealth and funds, and conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtain senior management approval for establishing (or continuing, for existing"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 12 requires reasonable measures to determine whether a customer or beneficial owner is a domestic PEP or entrusted with a prominent function by an international organisation; for higher risk relationships with them, measures (b), (c) and (d) apply. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "entrusted with a prominent function by an international organisation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 12 requirements for all types of PEP also apply to their family members or close associates. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also apply to family members or close associates of such PEPs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 13 requires FIs in cross-border correspondent relationships, beyond normal CDD, to gather sufficient information to understand the respondent's business and determine from public information its reputation and supervision quality, including any ML/TF investigation or regulatory action. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether it has been subject to a money laundering or terrorist financing investigation or regulatory action"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 13 requires assessing the respondent's AML/CFT controls, obtaining senior management approval before new correspondent relationships, and clearly understanding each institution's responsibilities. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assess the respondent institution’s AML/CFT controls"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For "payable-through accounts", Recommendation 13 requires being satisfied the respondent has conducted CDD on customers with direct access to the correspondent's accounts and can provide CDD information on request. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducted CDD on the customers having direct access to accounts of the correspondent bank"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 13 prohibits entering or continuing correspondent relationships with shell banks and requires FIs to satisfy themselves respondents do not let shell banks use their accounts. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prohibited from entering into, or continuing, a correspondent banking relationship with shell banks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 14 requires MVTS providers to be licensed or registered and subject to effective compliance monitoring, and countries to identify unlicensed or unregistered MVTS and apply appropriate sanctions. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify natural or legal persons that carry out MVTS without a license or registration"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 14 requires MVTS agents to be licensed or registered, or the provider to keep a current agent list accessible to competent authorities where provider and agents operate; providers must include agents in AML/CFT programmes and monitor their compliance. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the MVTS provider should maintain a current list of its agents accessible by competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 15 requires countries and FIs to identify and assess ML/TF risks from new products, business practices and delivery mechanisms, and from new or developing technologies for new and existing products, and to manage and mitigate them. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the use of new or developing technologies for both new and pre-existing products"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 15, FIs' risk assessment must take place before launching new products, practices or technologies. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such a risk assessment should take place prior to the launch of the new products"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 15 requires virtual asset service providers to be regulated for AML/CFT, licensed or registered, and subject to effective compliance monitoring. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "virtual asset service providers are regulated for AML/CFT purposes"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 16 requires FIs to include required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information on payments or value transfers and related messages, structured where possible and kept with the transfer throughout the payment chain. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should remain with such payment or value transfer or related message throughout the payment chain"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 16 requires FIs to monitor payments to detect those lacking required originator and/or beneficiary information and take appropriate measures. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "detecting those which lack required originator and/or beneficiary information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 16 requires FIs processing payments to take freezing action and not transact with designated persons and entities under UNSC resolutions such as 1267 (1999), 1373(2001) and proliferation-related resolutions. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial institutions take freezing action and should prohibit conducting transactions with designated persons and entities"</sub>

## RELIANCE, CONTROLS AND FINANCIAL GROUPS

- **permissive / requirement**: Recommendation 17 lets countries permit FIs to rely on third parties for CDD elements (a)-(c) of Recommendation 10 or to introduce business if criteria are met; ultimate CDD responsibility remains with the relying FI. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ultimate responsibility for CDD measures remains with the financial institution relying on the third party"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 17 the FI should be satisfied the third party is regulated, supervised or monitored and has CDD and record-keeping measures in line with Recommendations 10 and 11; countries should consider country risk when determining where third parties may be based. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should have regard to information available on the level of country risk"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: For intra-group reliance where the group applies CDD and record-keeping per Recommendations 10, 11 and 12 and programmes per Recommendation 18, supervised at group level, authorities may treat criteria (b) and (c) as met via the group programme and may waive (d) where group policies adequately mitigate higher country risk. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may decide that (d) is not a necessary precondition to reliance"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 18 requires FIs to implement AML/CFT programmes, and financial groups to implement group-wide programmes including policies and procedures for intra-group information sharing for AML/CFT. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including policies and procedures for sharing information within the group for AML/CFT purposes"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 18 requires FIs to ensure foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries apply AML/CFT measures consistent with home country requirements through group programmes. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "foreign branches and majority- owned subsidiaries apply AML/CFT measures consistent with the home country requirements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 19 requires FIs to apply effective, proportionate enhanced due diligence to relationships and transactions with persons and FIs from countries for which the FATF calls for it. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from countries for which this is called for by the FATF"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 17, a financial institution relying on a third party should take adequate steps to satisfy itself that copies of identification data and other relevant CDD documentation will be available from the third party on request without delay. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "relating to the CDD requirements will be made available from the third party upon request without delay"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under Recommendation 17, a financial institution relying on a third party should immediately obtain the necessary information on elements (a)-(c) of the CDD measures in Recommendation 10. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A financial institution relying upon a third party should immediately obtain the"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 19 requires countries to be able to apply effective, proportionate countermeasures when called on by the FATF and also independently of any FATF call. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply countermeasures independently of any call by the FATF to do so"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 20 requires an FI that suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect funds are criminal proceeds or TF-related to be required by law to report promptly to the FIU. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity"</sub>

## REPORTING OF SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 21 requires FIs and their directors, officers and employees to be legally protected from criminal and civil liability for breaching disclosure restrictions when reporting suspicions in good faith to the FIU, even without knowing the underlying crime and regardless of whether illegal activity occurred. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regardless of whether illegal activity actually occurred"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 21 requires FIs and their staff to be prohibited by law from "tipping-off" that an STR or related information is being filed with the FIU; this does not inhibit information sharing under Recommendation 18. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These provisions are not intended to inhibit information sharing under Recommendation 18"</sub>
