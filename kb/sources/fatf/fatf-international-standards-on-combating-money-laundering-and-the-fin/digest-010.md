## A. OBJECTIVE

- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 16 aims to prevent terrorists, money launderers, perpetrators of predicate offences (e.g. fraud) and other criminals from having unfettered access to payments or value transfers to move funds, and to detect such misuse when it occurs. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from having unfettered access to payments or value transfers for moving their funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 16 seeks to make basic originator and beneficiary information immediately available to law enforcement and/or prosecutorial authorities to help detect, investigate and prosecute terrorists or other criminals and trace their assets. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to appropriate law enforcement and/or prosecutorial authorities to assist them in detecting, investigating, and prosecuting"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 16 seeks to make originator and beneficiary information immediately available to FIUs for analysing and disseminating suspicious or unusual activity. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to financial intelligence units for analysing suspicious or unusual activity, and disseminating it as necessary"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 16 seeks to make originator and beneficiary information available to ordering, intermediary and beneficiary financial institutions so they can identify and report suspicious transactions and implement freezing and transaction prohibitions under UNSC resolutions such as 1267 (1999) and successors, 1373 (2001), and WMD proliferation-related resolutions. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to ordering, intermediary and beneficiary financial institutions to facilitate the identification and reporting of suspicious transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 50: the R.16 terms "originator" and "beneficiary" are interchangeable with "debtor" and "creditor" respectively, as used in messaging standards such as ISO 20022. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These terms are interchangeable with the terms "debtor" and "creditor" respectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 51: Recommendation 16 does not specify whether or how transmitted information should be screened against sanctions lists, since various mechanisms may ensure compliance with targeted financial sanctions. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 16 does not specify whether or how the information transmitted should be screened against sanction lists"</sub>

## B. SCOPE

- **expected / expectation**: To meet R.16 objectives, countries should be able to trace all payments or value transfers. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should have the ability to trace all payments or value transfers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Because small payments may pose a TF threat, countries should minimise thresholds, while considering the risk of driving transactions underground and the importance of financial inclusion. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Due to the potential terrorist financing threat posed by small payments or value transfers, countries should minimise thresholds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF does not intend to impose rigid standards or mandate a single operating process that would harm the payment system or financial inclusion. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is not the intention of the FATF to impose rigid standards or to mandate a single operating process"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Recommendation 16 applies to cross-border and domestic payments or value transfers, including serial and cover payments, with differentiated obligations by transaction type. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 16 applies to cross-border and domestic payments or value transfers, including serial payments and cover payments."</sub>

## C. INFORMATION REQUIREMENTS

- **expected / expectation**: Information accompanying cross-border and domestic payments should, as far as possible, be structured according to the established standards of the system used, such as ISO 20022, and be detailed enough to identify originator and beneficiary. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in accordance with the established standards of the system used such as ISO 20022"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For R.16, the payment chain starts at the financial institution receiving the originator's instructions and ends at the institution servicing the beneficiary's account or providing cash to the beneficiary. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the payment chain starts at the financial institution that receives the instructions from the originator"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Cross-border payment messages should allow the relevant institutions and authorities to identify which financial institutions service the originator's and beneficiary's accounts and in which countries they are located. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to identify which financial institution is servicing the account of the originator and beneficiary respectively"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Financial institutions should ensure account numbers are not used to disguise the country where the account-servicing institution resides. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "account numbers should not be used to disguise the identification of the country where the financial institution"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Countries may adopt a de minimis threshold of no higher than USD/EUR 1 000 for cross-border payments or value transfers, other than cash withdrawals. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may adopt a de minimis threshold (no higher than USD/EUR 1 000) for cross-border payments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Below the cross-border de minimis threshold, financial institutions should include the originator's and beneficiary's names and account numbers (where an account is used), or otherwise a unique transaction reference number permitting traceability. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the absence of an account, a unique transaction reference number should be included, which permits traceability"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Below-threshold information need not be verified for accuracy unless there is an ML/TF suspicion, in which case the institution should verify information about its customer. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such information need not be verified for accuracy, unless there is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnotes 52/53: where funds are drawn from a financial institution other than the ordering institution, the account number and name of the institution from which funds are drawn should be included. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the account number and the name of financial institution from where the funds are drawn"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Cross-border payments above the applicable threshold should always carry the originator's and beneficiary's names and account numbers (or a unique transaction reference number if no account). (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information accompanying all cross-border payments or value transfers above the applicable threshold should always contain:"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Above-threshold cross-border payments should include the originator's address and the beneficiary's country and town name (or nearest alternative); footnote 54 says country and town suffice for the originator where no standardised postal address exists. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the absence of standardised postal address information for the originator, the country and town name"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Above-threshold cross-border payments should include the originator's date of birth where the originator is a natural person; footnote 55 says only the year of birth is required when the full date is unavailable. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When full information of the date of birth is not available, only the year of birth is required."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Where the originator and/or beneficiary is a legal person, above-threshold cross-border payments should include, where it exists, the connected BIC, the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI), or the unique official identifier. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the connected business identifier code (BIC), or (ii) the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Countries may also adopt a de minimis threshold of no higher than USD/EUR 1 000 for domestic payments; below it, institutions should include the originator's name and either the originator's account number or a unique transaction reference number traceable to originator or beneficiary. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may also adopt a de minimis threshold (no higher than USD/EUR 1 000), for domestic payments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Domestic payments should carry the originator information required for cross-border payments under paragraph 9 (or paragraph 10 if below threshold), unless it can be made available to the beneficiary institution and authorities by other means. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unless this information can be made available to the beneficiary financial institution and appropriate authorities by other means"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where domestic originator information is available by other means, the ordering institution need include only the account number or a unique transaction reference number that allows tracing to originator or beneficiary. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ordering financial institution need only include the account number or a unique transaction reference number"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The ordering institution should make the information available within three business days of a request from the beneficiary or intermediary institution or from competent authorities. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The information should be made available by the ordering financial institution within three business days"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement authorities should be able to compel immediate production of payment information. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement authorities should be able to compel immediate production of such information."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: No information need accompany FI-to-FI transfers and settlements where both originator and beneficiary are financial institutions acting on their own behalf. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No information is required to accompany financial institution-to-financial institution transfers and settlements"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Where a net settlement arises from customer payments, underlying transaction information need not accompany the net settlement, but R.16 requirements still apply to the underlying transactions. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information about the underlying transactions is not required to accompany the net settlement"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Batched cross-border transfers from a single originator may be exempted from paragraphs 8 and 9 originator information requirements if they include the originator's account number or unique transaction reference number and the batch file contains required, accurate originator information and full beneficiary information fully traceable in the beneficiary country. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are bundled in a batch file for transmission to beneficiaries they may be exempted from the requirements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For transfers flowing from a credit, debit or prepaid card purchase of goods or services, the card number should accompany all resulting transfers, and the name and location of the card issuing and merchant acquiring institutions should be available on request. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the credit or debit or prepaid card number should accompany all transfers flowing from the transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 56: purchase of goods or services means purchases from individuals/entities onboarded by the relevant institution, after required CDD, to accept card payments. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to purchases from individuals/entities who are onboarded by the relevant financial institution to accept card payments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 57: card issuer and merchant acquirer information should allow institutions and authorities to identify which institutions hold full cardholder and merchant information and in which countries they are located. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to identify which financial institutions are in possession of the full cardholder and merchant information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Footnote 58: card-related information should be made available in a timely manner (e.g. with direct or indirect help of the card network) to other institutions in the payment chain and through them to competent authorities. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with the direct or indirect assistance of the relevant card network"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: When a card is used for other payment types such as person-to-person transfers, the transaction is subject to the domestic or cross-border payment requirements. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When a credit or debit or prepaid card is used to effect other types of payment or value transfer"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Only the account number or card number is required to accompany domestic cash withdrawals. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No information beyond the account number or card number is required to accompany domestic cash withdrawals."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For cross-border card cash withdrawals through a different institution, the card number should accompany the withdrawal and the cardholder's name should be sent to the acquiring institution on request within three business days. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "request, within three business days of receiving the request."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 59: cross-border cash withdrawal requirements do not apply to withdrawals at ATMs operated by the account-holding institution, provided the paragraph 19 information is available by other means under Recommendation 18. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These requirements do not apply to withdrawals from ATMs operated by the same institution where the account is held"</sub>

## D. RESPONSIBILITIES OF ORDERING, INTERMEDIARY AND BENEFICIARY FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS INCLUDING MVTS

- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 60: "ordering financial institution" and "beneficiary financial institution" are interchangeable with "debtor agent" and "creditor agent" used in messaging standards such as ISO 20022. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These terms are interchangeable with terms “debtor agent” and “creditor agent” respectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Footnote 61: the section D responsibilities do not apply to cash withdrawals and card payments for purchase of goods or services. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The responsibilities set out in this section do not apply to cash withdrawals and card payments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The ordering institution should ensure above-de-minimis cross-border transfers contain required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "contain required and accurate originator information, and required beneficiary information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The ordering institution should ensure below-de-minimis cross-border transfers contain originator and beneficiary names and an account number for each, or a unique transaction reference number. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "below the de minimis threshold contain the name of the originator and the beneficiary and an account number for each"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The ordering institution should maintain all collected originator and beneficiary information in accordance with Recommendation 11. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ordering financial institution should maintain all originator and beneficiary information collected"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The ordering institution should not be allowed to execute a payment or value transfer that does not comply with the R.16 requirements. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ordering financial institution should not be allowed to execute the payments or value transfer if it does not comply"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Intermediary institutions in cross-border payment chains should ensure all accompanying originator and beneficiary information is retained with the transfer. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should ensure that all originator and beneficiary information that accompanies a payment or value transfer is retained with it"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where technical limitations prevent required cross-border information remaining with a related domestic transfer, the receiving intermediary should keep a record of all information received for at least five years. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a record should be kept, for at least five years, by the receiving intermediary financial institution"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Intermediary institutions should take reasonable measures, consistent with straight-through processing, to identify cross-border transfers lacking required information. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such measures should be consistent with straight-through processing."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Intermediary institutions should have effective risk-based policies and procedures for deciding when to execute, reject or suspend transfers lacking required originator or beneficiary information, and the appropriate follow-up. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An intermediary financial institution should have effective risk-based policies and procedures for determining"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Beneficiary institutions should take reasonable measures to identify cross-border transfers lacking required originator or beneficiary information, which may include post-event or, where feasible, real-time monitoring. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such measures may include post-event monitoring or real-time monitoring where feasible."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For above-de-minimis cross-border transfers, the beneficiary institution should verify the beneficiary's identity if not previously verified and maintain it per Recommendation 11. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a beneficiary financial institution should verify the identity of the beneficiary, if the identity has not been previously verified"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For above-de-minimis cross-border transfers, intended-beneficiary information should inform the beneficiary institution's monitoring to detect misdirected payments (e.g. due to ML, fraud or error), and institutions should take measures to mitigate the risk of payment to an unintended beneficiary. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with the objective of detecting misdirected payments (e.g. due to possible money laundering, fraud, or error)"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Misdirection-mitigation option (a): for each transaction, the beneficiary institution checks how far the beneficiary name and account number in the payment message align with its own records. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which the name and account number of the beneficiary in the payment message aligns62 with the information held"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Misdirection-mitigation option (b): risk-based holistic ongoing monitoring to identify anomalous accounts, transactions and activity, including misaligned beneficiary information. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify anomalous accounts, transactions, and activity, including misaligned beneficiary information, following a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Misdirection-mitigation option (c): if both ordering and beneficiary institutions participate in a pre-validation mechanism such as confirmation/verification of payee checking name and account alignment per transaction, it may be used instead of (a) or (b). (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pre- validation mechanism such as confirmation/verification of payee to check, for each transaction"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Beneficiary institutions should implement at least one of the three misdirection-mitigation measures (a), (b) or (c). (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such measures should include at least one of the following (a), (b), or (c):"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 62: alignment of beneficiary information does not require an exact match; the expected degree of alignment may vary with risk and context. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Alignment does not imply that there must be an exact match."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Beneficiary institutions should have effective risk-based policies and procedures for deciding when to execute, reject or suspend transfers lacking required information or identified as potentially misdirected, and for appropriate follow-up. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or when they identify potentially misdirected payments in the implementation of the requirements under paragraph 30"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: MVTS providers should be required to comply with all relevant R.16 requirements in the countries where they operate, directly or through agents. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money or value transfer service (MVTS) providers should be required to comply with all of the relevant requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An MVTS provider controlling both ordering and beneficiary sides of a transfer should consider information from both sides when deciding whether to file an STR. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should take into account all the information from both the ordering and beneficiary sides"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such an MVTS provider should file an STR in any country affected by the suspicious transfers and make relevant transaction information available to the FIU. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should file an STR in any country affected by the suspicious payments or value transfers"</sub>

## Glossary of specific terms used in this Recommendation

- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: "accurate" describes information verified for accuracy; "address" means the physical location of a residence or business. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Accurate is used to describe information that has been verified for accuracy."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a batch transfer comprises several individual transfers sent to the same financial institutions, which may or may not be ultimately intended for different persons. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Batch transfer is a transfer comprised of a number of individual payments or value transfers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: the beneficiary is the natural or legal person or legal arrangement identified by the originator as receiver of the transfer. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who is identified by the originator as the receiver of the requested payments or value transfer"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: the beneficiary financial institution services the beneficiary's account or remits cash to the beneficiary and is the end point of the payment chain. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The beneficiary financial institution is the end point in a payment chain."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a BIC is a universal business identifier code based on ISO 9362 assigned to financial and non-financial institutions; connected BICs are those used by financial institutions, e.g. to access the SWIFT network. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "BIC refers to a universal business identifier code based on the ISO 9362 standard"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a cover payment combines a payment message sent directly from ordering to beneficiary institution with the funding instruction (the cover) routed through one or more intermediary institutions. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with the routing of the funding instruction (the cover) from the ordering financial institution"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a cross-border payment is one where ordering and beneficiary institutions are in different countries, including any chain in which at least one institution is in a different country. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in which at least one of the financial institutions involved is located in a different country"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a domestic payment is one where ordering and beneficiary institutions are in the same country, even if the messaging system is located elsewhere, and includes chains entirely within the European Economic Area (EEA). (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "even though the system used to transfer the payment message may be located in another country"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 63: an entity may petition the FATF to be designated a supra-national jurisdiction solely for assessing Recommendation 16 compliance. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An entity may petition the FATF to be designated as a supra-national jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: an intermediary financial institution receives and transmits a transfer on behalf of the ordering and beneficiary institutions or another intermediary. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to a financial institution in a payment chain that receives and transmits a payment or value transfer"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: the Legal Entity Identifier is a unique alphanumeric code based on ISO 17442 assigned by the Global LEI System. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Legal Entity Identifier refers to a unique alphanumeric reference code based on the ISO 17442 standard"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: an MVTS network is an MVTS and its agents, or two or more MVTS bound by agreements to carry out transfers, including net settlement. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "two or more MVTS bound by one or several agreements to proceed to payments or value transfers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: the ordering financial institution receives the originator's instructions and is the start point of the payment chain. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ordering financial institution is the start point of the payment chain."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: the originator is the account holder requesting the transfer, or where there is no account, the person placing the order with the ordering institution. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Originator refers to the account holder who requests the payment or value transfer from that account"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a serial payment is a direct sequential chain where the transfer and message travel together from ordering to beneficiary institution, directly or via intermediaries such as correspondent banks. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Serial Payment refers to a direct sequential chain of payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: straight-through processing means payments conducted electronically without manual intervention. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to payment transactions that are conducted electronically without the need for manual intervention."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a unique official identifier is a public-sector-issued scheme in which each identifier maps to a unique person, entity or arrangement and each has only one identifier. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an identification scheme that is issued by the public sector in the relevant jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a unique transaction reference number is a combination of letters, numbers or symbols set by the payment service provider under the protocols of the payment, settlement or messaging system used. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to a combination of letters, numbers or symbols, determined by the payment service provider"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: R.16 glossary: a payment or value transfer is any electronic transaction on behalf of an originator through an ordering institution to make funds available to a beneficiary at a beneficiary institution, whether or not originator and beneficiary are the same; it includes cash withdrawals. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "irrespective of whether the originator and the beneficiary are the same. This includes cash withdrawals."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The third-party reliance Recommendation (interpretive note para 1) does not apply to outsourcing or agency relationships. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Recommendation does not apply to outsourcing or agency relationships."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A relied-upon third party usually has its own existing relationship with the customer, independent of the relying institution, and applies its own CDD procedures; by contrast, an outsourced entity applies CDD on the delegating institution's behalf, under its procedures and control. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in which the outsourced entity applies the CDD measures on behalf of the delegating financial institution"</sub>
