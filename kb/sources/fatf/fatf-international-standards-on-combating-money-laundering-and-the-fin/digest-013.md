## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 28 (REGULATION AND SUPERVISION OF DNFBPS)

- **descriptive / definition**: Under INR.28, risk-based supervision of DNFBPs means both the general process by which a supervisor or SRB allocates AML/CFT supervisory resources according to its understanding of risk, and the specific process of supervising or monitoring DNFBPs that apply an AML/CFT risk-based approach. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the general process by which a supervisor or SRB, according to its understanding of risks, allocates its resources"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors or SRBs should set the frequency and intensity of DNFBP supervision or monitoring based on their understanding of ML/TF risks, considering DNFBP characteristics, particularly their diversity and number. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should determine the frequency and intensity of their supervisory or monitoring actions on DNFBPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: DNFBP supervisors need a clear understanding of ML/TF risks present in the country and those associated with the type of DNFBP and its customers, products and services. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "associated with the type of DNFBP and their customers, products and services"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When assessing DNFBPs' AML/CFT internal controls, policies and procedures, supervisors or SRBs should properly consider the DNFBPs' ML/TF risk profile and the discretion allowed to them under the RBA. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should properly take into account the money laundering and terrorist financing risk profile of those DNFBPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: DNFBP supervisors or SRBs should have adequate powers, including to monitor and sanction, and adequate financial, human and technical resources. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors or SRBs should have adequate powers to perform their functions (including powers to monitor and sanction)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should have processes ensuring DNFBP supervisory staff maintain high professional standards, including confidentiality, and are of high integrity and appropriately skilled. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have in place processes to ensure that the staff of those authorities maintain high professional standards"</sub>

## A. GENERAL

- **descriptive / context**: INR.29 explains the core mandate and functions of a financial intelligence unit (FIU); the FIU is part of and central to a country's AML/CFT operational network and supports other competent authorities. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIU is part of, and plays a central role in, a country’s AML/CFT operational network"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 29 does not prejudge a country's choice of FIU model and applies equally to all models. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 29 does not prejudge a country’s choice for a particular model, and applies equally to all of them"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIU is the central agency receiving disclosures from reporting entities; at minimum this should include STRs required by Recommendations 20 and 23, plus other information required by national law such as cash transaction reports, wire transfer reports and other threshold-based declarations. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as cash transaction reports, wire transfers reports and other threshold-based declarations/disclosures"</sub>

## B. FUNCTIONS

- **expected / expectation**: FIU analysis should add value to the information received and held; while all information should be considered, analysis may focus on each disclosure or on selected information depending on disclosure type and volume and expected use after dissemination. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU analysis should add value to the information received and held by the FIU."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FIUs should be encouraged to use analytical software to process information more efficiently and help establish relevant links. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should be encouraged to use analytical software to process information more efficiently"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Analytical tools cannot fully replace the human judgement element of FIU analysis. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such tools cannot fully replace the human judgement element of analysis"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: FIUs should conduct operational analysis: using available and obtainable information to identify specific targets (e.g. persons, assets, criminal networks and associations), follow the trail of activities or transactions, and link targets to possible proceeds of crime, ML, predicate offences or TF. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "specific targets (e.g. persons, assets, criminal networks and associations), to follow the trail of particular activities"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: FIUs should conduct strategic analysis: using available and obtainable information, including data from other competent authorities, to identify ML/TF trends and patterns, which the FIU or other state entities use to determine ML/TF threats and vulnerabilities. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that may be provided by other competent authorities, to identify money laundering and terrorist financing related trends and patterns"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Strategic analysis may also help set policies and goals for the FIU or more broadly for other entities in the AML/CFT regime. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Strategic analysis may also help establish policies and goals for the FIU"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should be able to disseminate information and analysis results to relevant competent authorities both spontaneously and on request, using dedicated, secure and protected channels. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Dedicated, secure and protected channels should be used for the dissemination."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should be able to disseminate spontaneously when there are grounds to suspect ML, predicate offences or TF; dissemination should be selective, based on analysis, so recipients can focus on relevant cases. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the dissemination of information should be selective and allow the recipient authorities to focus on relevant cases/information"</sub>

## C. ACCESS TO INFORMATION

- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should be able to respond to information requests from competent authorities under Recommendation 31, but the decision whether to analyse and/or disseminate to the requesting authority should remain with the FIU. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the decision on conducting analysis and/or dissemination of information to the requesting authority should remain with the FIU"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Beyond reported information, the FIU should be able to obtain and use additional information from reporting entities as needed for analysis, potentially including information they must keep under Recommendations 10, 11 and 22. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtain and use additional information from reporting entities as needed to perform its analysis properly"</sub>

## D. INFORMATION SECURITY AND CONFIDENTIALITY

- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should have access to the widest possible range of financial, administrative and law enforcement information, including open/public sources, information held by or for other authorities, and where appropriate commercially held data. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FIU should have access to the widest possible range of financial, administrative and law enforcement information"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Information received, processed, held or disseminated by the FIU must be securely protected and exchanged and used only per agreed procedures, policies and applicable law. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information received, processed, held or disseminated by the FIU must be securely protected"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: An FIU must have rules governing security and confidentiality of information, including procedures for handling, storage, dissemination, protection of and access to it. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An FIU must, therefore, have rules in place governing the security and confidentiality of such information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should ensure staff have necessary security clearance and understand their responsibilities for sensitive information, and should limit access to its facilities and information, including IT systems. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIU should ensure that there is limited access to its facilities and information, including information technology systems."</sub>

## E. OPERATIONAL INDEPENDENCE

- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should be operationally independent and autonomous, with authority and capacity to carry out its functions freely, including autonomously deciding to analyse, request and/or disseminate information, and the independent right to forward information to competent authorities. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FIU has the independent right to forward or disseminate information to competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: An FIU may sit within an existing authority, but its core functions should then be distinct from that authority's. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FIU’s core functions should be distinct from those of the other authority"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should receive adequate financial, human and technical resources in a way that secures its autonomy and independence; its staff should maintain high professional standards, including confidentiality, and be of high integrity and appropriately skilled. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in a manner that secures its autonomy and independence and allows it to conduct its mandate effectively"</sub>

## F. UNDUE INFLUENCE OR INTERFERENCE

- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should be able to independently make arrangements or engage with domestic competent authorities or foreign counterparts on information exchange. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIU should also be able to make arrangements or engage independently with other domestic competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIU should be able to obtain and deploy resources for its functions free from undue political, government or industry influence or interference that could compromise operational independence. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "free from any undue political, government or industry influence or interference"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure the FIU has regard to the Egmont Group Statement of Purpose and its Principles for Information Exchange Between FIUs, and the FIU should apply for Egmont Group membership. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIU should apply for membership in the Egmont Group."</sub>

## H. LARGE CASH TRANSACTION REPORTING

- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider the feasibility and utility of a system in which FIs and DNFBPs report all domestic and international currency transactions above a fixed amount. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "report all domestic and international currency transactions above a fixed amount"</sub>

## INTERPRETIVE NOTE TO RECOMMENDATION 30 (RESPONSIBILITIES OF LAW ENFORCEMENT AND INVESTIGATIVE AUTHORITIES)

- **expected / expectation**: Under INR.30, there should be designated law enforcement authorities responsible for ensuring ML, predicate offences and TF are properly investigated through financial investigation. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are properly investigated through the conduct of a financial investigation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should designate one or more competent authorities to identify, trace and initiate freezing and seizing of criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designate one or more competent authorities to identify, trace, and initiate freezing and seizing of criminal property"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A 'financial investigation' is an enquiry into financial affairs related to criminal activity aimed at identifying the extent of criminal networks or scale of criminality, identifying and tracing criminal property and property of corresponding value, and developing evidence for criminal and/or confiscation proceedings. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "means an enquiry into the financial affairs related to criminal activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A 'parallel financial investigation' is a financial investigation conducted alongside or within a traditional criminal investigation into ML, TF and/or predicate offences. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to conducting a financial investigation alongside, or in the context of, a (traditional) criminal investigation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Predicate offence investigators should either be authorised to pursue related ML/TF offences during a parallel investigation or be able to refer the case to another agency to follow up. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or be able to refer the case to another agency to follow up with such investigations"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider measures, including legislation, allowing ML/TF investigators to postpone or waive arrest of suspects and/or seizure of money to identify participants or gather evidence; without such measures, controlled deliveries and undercover operations are precluded. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to postpone or waive the arrest of suspected persons and/or the seizure of the money"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 30 also applies to competent authorities that are not law enforcement per se but are responsible for financial investigations of predicate offences, to the extent they exercise R.30 functions. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which are not law enforcement authorities, per se, but which have the responsibility for pursuing financial investigations"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Anti-corruption enforcement authorities with enforcement powers may be designated to investigate ML/TF arising from or related to corruption, and should have sufficient powers to identify, trace and initiate freezing and seizing of criminal property. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Anti-corruption enforcement authorities with enforcement powers may be designated to investigate money laundering"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The range of law enforcement and other competent authorities should be taken into account when countries use multi-disciplinary groups in financial investigations. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when countries make use of multi-disciplinary groups in financial investigations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement and prosecutorial authorities, including asset recovery authorities, should have adequate financial, human and technical resources, and staff with high professional standards, integrity and skills. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including those authorities responsible for asset recovery, should have adequate financial, human and technical resources"</sub>

## A. OBJECTIVES

- **descriptive / context**: Recommendation 32 aims to ensure terrorists and other criminals cannot finance activities or launder proceeds through physical cross-border transportation of currency and bearer negotiable instruments (BNIs). (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "through the physical cross-border transportation of currency and bearer negotiable instruments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 32 aims for countries to have measures to detect cross-border transport of currency and BNIs; stop or restrain those suspected of TF/ML or falsely declared or disclosed; sanction false declarations or disclosures; and enable confiscation of currency or BNIs related to TF or ML. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "stop or restrain currency or bearer negotiable instruments that are falsely declared or disclosed"</sub>

## B. THE TYPES OF SYSTEMS THAT MAY BE IMPLEMENTED TO ADDRESS THE ISSUE OF CASH COURIERS

- **permissive / context**: Countries may meet R.32 obligations with one of the system types described, and need not use the same type for incoming and outgoing cross-border transportation. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries do not have to use the same type of system for incoming and outgoing cross-border transportation"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under a declaration system, all persons physically transporting currency or BNIs across borders with a value exceeding a pre-set maximum threshold of USD/EUR 15,000 are required to submit a truthful declaration to designated competent authorities. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which are of a value exceeding a pre-set, maximum threshold of USD/EUR 15,000, are required to submit a truthful declaration"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Countries may choose among three declaration systems: written declaration for all travellers, written declaration for travellers above a threshold, or oral declaration; mixed systems are not uncommon. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not uncommon for countries to opt for a mixed system"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In a written declaration system for all travellers, every traveller completes a written declaration before entering the country, declaring whether or not they carry currency or BNIs (e.g. ticking a yes or no box). (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "travellers have to make a declaration whether or not they are carrying currency or BNIs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In a threshold-based written declaration system, only travellers carrying currency or BNIs above a pre-set threshold must complete a written declaration. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not required to fill out any forms if they are not carrying currency or BNIs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In an oral declaration system, travellers orally declare amounts above a threshold, usually by choosing a customs "red channel" (goods to declare) or "green channel" (nothing to declare), the channel choice being the declaration. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The choice of channel that the traveller makes is considered to be the oral declaration."</sub>
- **permissive / definition**: Under a disclosure system, countries may require travellers to give authorities appropriate information on request, without an upfront declaration, but travellers must answer truthfully when asked. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "travellers need to be required to give a truthful answer to competent authorities upon request"</sub>

## C. ADDITIONAL ELEMENTS APPLICABLE TO BOTH SYSTEMS

- **expected / expectation**: Whichever R.32 system is used, it should apply to both incoming and outgoing transportation of currency and BNIs. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The declaration/disclosure system should apply to both incoming and outgoing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: On discovering a false declaration/disclosure or failure to declare, designated authorities should be able to request and obtain further information from the carrier on the origin and intended use of the currency or BNIs. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have the authority to request and obtain further information from the carrier with regard to the origin"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Declaration/disclosure information should be available to the FIU, either through notification of suspicious cross-border incidents or by making the information directly available in another way. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FIU, either through a system whereby the FIU is notified about suspicious cross- border transportation incidents"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should ensure adequate domestic coordination among customs, immigration and related authorities on R.32 implementation. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "among customs, immigration and other related authorities on issues related to the implementation of Recommendation 32"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should be able to stop or restrain cash or BNIs for a reasonable time to ascertain whether ML/TF evidence may be found, where there is ML/TF suspicion or a false declaration or disclosure. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in order to ascertain whether evidence of money laundering or terrorist financing may be found"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The declaration/disclosure system should allow the greatest possible international cooperation and assistance in line with Recommendations 36 to 40. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "international cooperation and assistance in accordance with Recommendations 36 to 40"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Information shall be retained for competent authorities where a declaration/disclosure exceeds USD/EUR 15,000, where there is a false declaration/disclosure, or where ML/TF is suspected; at minimum it covers the amount declared, disclosed or detected and the bearer(s)' identification data. (p. 116)[^original]
