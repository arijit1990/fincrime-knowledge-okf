## LEGAL BASIS OF REQUIREMENTS ON FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS AND DNFBPS AND VASPS

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 1 of the legal-basis note says all requirements for financial institutions, DNFBPs or VASPs should be introduced in law (specifically for Recommendations 10, 11 and 20), or, for all other cases, in law or enforceable means, with the country having discretion. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be introduced either (a) in law (see the specific requirements in Recommendations 10, 11 and 20"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In Recommendations 10, 11 and 20, "law" means legislation issued or approved through a Parliamentary process or equivalent means under the constitutional framework, imposing mandatory requirements with sanctions for non-compliance that should be effective, proportionate and dissuasive (see Recommendation 35). It also covers judicial decisions imposing relevant requirements that are binding and authoritative in all parts of the country. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "imposes mandatory requirements with sanctions for non- compliance"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: "Enforceable means" covers regulations, guidelines, instructions and other documents or mechanisms that lay down enforceable AML/CFT requirements in obligatory wording, backed by penalties for non-compliance, and issued or approved by a competent authority. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to regulations, guidelines, instructions or other documents or mechanisms that set out enforceable AML/CFT requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Sanctions for non-compliance under enforceable means should be effective, proportionate and dissuasive (see Recommendation 35). (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The sanctions for non-compliance should be effective, proportionate and dissuasive (see Recommendation 35)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For a document or mechanism to amount to enforceable means, there must be a document or mechanism that sets out or underpins requirements on the issues covered by the FATF Recommendations, with clearly stated requirements that are understood as such. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There must be a document or mechanism that sets out or underpins requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When assessing enforceable means, if particular measures use the word shall or must, this should be considered mandatory. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "if particular measures use the word shall or must, this should be considered mandatory"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Measures phrased with "should" could be mandatory if both the regulator and the regulated institutions demonstrate that the actions are directly or indirectly required and are being implemented. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this could be mandatory if both the regulator and the regulated institutions demonstrate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Wording such as measures being encouraged, recommended, or institutions "should consider" is less likely to be regarded as mandatory; where weaker language is used, there is a presumption that it is not mandatory unless the country can demonstrate otherwise. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In any case where weaker language is used, there is a presumption that the language is not mandatory"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For enforceable means, the document or mechanism must be issued or approved by a competent authority. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The document/mechanism must be issued or approved by a competent authority."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For enforceable means, there must be sanctions for non-compliance, which should be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. They need not appear in the same document as the requirement and can be in another document, provided there are clear links between the requirement and the available sanctions. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There must be sanctions for non-compliance (sanctions need not be in the same"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In assessing sanctions under enforceable means, there should be an adequate range of effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions available if persons fail to comply with their obligations. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there should be an adequate range of effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions available"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Sanctions should be directly or indirectly applicable for failure to comply with an AML/CFT requirement. If no sanction is directly attached, sanctions for breach of broader requirements (such as lacking proper systems and controls or not operating in a safe and sound manner) are satisfactory provided that, at a minimum, failure to meet AML/CFT requirements could be (and has been as appropriate) adequately sanctioned without needing to prove additional prudential failures unrelated to AML/CFT. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without a need to prove additional prudential failures unrelated to AML/CFT"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One issue to consider in assessing sanctions is whether there is satisfactory evidence that effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions have actually been applied in practice. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether there is satisfactory evidence that effective, proportionate and"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In all cases it should be evident that financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs know that breaches would be penalised and what the penalties could be. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understand that sanctions would be applied for non-compliance and what those sanctions could be"</sub>

## GENERAL GLOSSARY

- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: references to "accounts" are to be read as including other similar business relationships between financial institutions and their customers. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "other similar business relationships between financial institutions and their customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: for Recommendations 14 and 16, an "agent" is any natural or legal person providing MVTS on behalf of an MVTS provider, whether by contract with or under the direction of the provider. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any natural or legal person providing MVTS on behalf of an MVTS provider"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "asset recovery" is the process of identifying, tracing, evaluating, freezing, seizing, confiscating and enforcing an order for, managing, and disposing of (including returning or sharing) criminal property and property of corresponding value. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identifying, tracing, evaluating, freezing, seizing, confiscating and enforcing a resulting order for"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: bearer negotiable instruments (BNIs) include monetary instruments in bearer form such as traveller's cheques; negotiable instruments (cheques, promissory notes, money orders) that are in bearer form, endorsed without restriction, made out to a fictitious payee, or otherwise passing title on delivery; and incomplete instruments signed but with the payee's name omitted. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "made out to a fictitious payee, or otherwise in such form that title thereto passes upon delivery"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "bearer shares" are negotiable instruments according ownership of a legal person to whoever possesses the physical certificate, and similar instruments without traceability. The term does not cover dematerialised or registered share forms whose owner can be identified. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accord ownership in a legal person to the person who possesses the physical bearer share certificate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "bearer share warrants" are negotiable instruments according entitlement to ownership of a legal person to the holder of the physical warrant certificate, and similar untraceable instruments. They exclude dematerialised or registered forms whose owner can be identified, and instruments conferring only a right to subscribe for ownership on specified conditions until exercised. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only confers a right to subscribe for ownership in a legal person at specified conditions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: for legal persons, "beneficial owner" means the natural person(s) who ultimately own or control a customer and/or the natural person on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, and also those natural persons who exercise ultimate effective control over a legal person. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to the natural person(s) who ultimately96 owns or controls a customer97"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: an ultimate beneficial owner can only be a natural person, and a given legal person can have more than one natural person as its ultimate beneficial owner. (p. 126)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only a natural person can be an ultimate beneficial owner, and more than one natural person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary footnotes 96 and 97: references to "ultimately owns or controls" and "ultimate effective control" cover ownership or control exercised through a chain of ownership or by means other than direct control; the beneficial owner definition also applies to the beneficial owner of a beneficiary under a life or other investment linked insurance policy. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ownership/control is exercised through a chain of ownership or by means of control other than direct control"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Glossary footnote 98: R.10 recognises that in CDD it may not be possible to verify the identity of ultimate beneficial owners through reasonable measures. Where there is doubt whether a person with a controlling ownership interest is the ultimate beneficial owner, or no natural person exerts control through ownership interests, the identity of the natural persons (if any) exercising control through other means should be determined. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where no natural person exerts control through ownership interests, the identity should be determined of the natural persons"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Glossary footnote 98: where no natural person is identified as exercising control, the natural person who holds the position of senior managing official should be identified and recorded as holding that position. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the natural person who holds the position of senior managing official should be identified and recorded"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Glossary footnote 98: the R.10 provision does not change the definition of who the beneficial owner is; it only sets out how CDD should be conducted where the beneficial owner cannot be identified. (p. 127)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This provision of R.10 does not amend or supersede the definition of who the beneficial owner is"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: for legal arrangements, the beneficial owner includes the settlor(s), trustee(s), protector(s) if any, each beneficiary or where applicable the class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other natural person(s) exercising ultimate effective control. For arrangements similar to an express trust, it means natural persons holding equivalent positions; where the trustee or another party is a legal person, that legal person's beneficial owner should be identified. (p. 128)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the settlor(s); (ii) the trustee(s); (iii) the protector(s) (if any)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: in trust law a "beneficiary" is the person(s) who are or may become entitled to benefit under a trust, and may be a natural person, legal person or legal arrangement. All trusts other than charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts must have ascertainable beneficiaries, though at set-up they may have only a class of beneficiaries and objects of a power until someone becomes entitled after a defined period (the accumulation period) or on exercise of trustee discretion. (p. 128)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All trusts (other than charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts) are required to have ascertainable beneficiaries"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: in life insurance or other investment linked insurance, a "beneficiary" is the natural or legal person, legal arrangement or category of persons who will be paid the policy proceeds when or if an insured event covered by the policy occurs. (p. 128)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who will be paid the policy proceeds when/if an insured event occurs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "competent authorities" means all public authorities with designated AML/CFT responsibilities, including the FIU, authorities investigating or prosecuting ML, predicate offences and TF and seizing, freezing and confiscating criminal assets, authorities receiving cross-border currency and BNI reports, and AML/CFT supervisors or monitors. SRBs are not to be regarded as competent authorities. Footnote 100: this includes financial supervisors established as independent non-governmental authorities with statutory powers. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SRBs are not to be regarded as a competent authorities."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "confiscation" (including forfeiture where applicable) is permanent deprivation of property by order of a competent authority or court, through a judicial or administrative procedure that generally transfers ownership to the State, with the former interest holder losing all rights in principle. It also includes deprivation by order for corresponding value, where sale receipts rather than title are transferred to the State. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the permanent deprivation of property by order of a competent authority or a court"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "Core Principles" are the Basel Committee's Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision, IOSCO's Objectives and Principles for Securities Regulation, and the IAIS Insurance Supervisory Principles. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision issued by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "correspondent banking" is the provision of banking services by one bank (the correspondent) to another (the respondent). Large international banks typically act as correspondents for thousands of banks, and services can include cash management, international wire transfers, cheque clearing, payable-through accounts and foreign exchange services. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Correspondent banking is the provision of banking services by one bank"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: all references to "country" or "countries" in the FATF Recommendations apply equally to territories or jurisdictions. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply equally to territories or jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "criminal activity" means (a) all criminal acts that would constitute a predicate offence for ML in the country, or (b) at a minimum those offences that would constitute a predicate offence as required by Recommendation 3. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "at a minimum to those offences that would constitute a predicate offence as required by Recommendation 3"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "criminal property" covers proceeds of ML or predicate offences (including income or benefits from them); instrumentalities used or intended for use in ML or predicate offences; property laundered; property used, intended or allocated for use in financing terrorism, terrorist acts or terrorist organisations; and proceeds of such terrorist financing. (p. 129)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "instrumentalities used in or intended for use in, money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "currency" means banknotes and coins in circulation as a medium of exchange. (p. 130)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banknotes and coins that are in circulation as a medium of exchange"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: the designated categories of offences are participation in an organised criminal group and racketeering; terrorism including terrorist financing; trafficking in human beings and migrant smuggling; sexual exploitation including of children; illicit trafficking in narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances; illicit arms trafficking; illicit trafficking in stolen and other goods; corruption and bribery; and fraud. (p. 130)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "participation in an organised criminal group and racketeering;"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: the designated categories of offences also include counterfeiting currency; counterfeiting and piracy of products; environmental crime (for example criminal harvesting, extraction or trafficking of protected species, precious metals and stones, other natural resources, or waste); murder and grievous bodily injury; kidnapping, illegal restraint and hostage-taking; robbery or theft; smuggling (including customs and excise duties and taxes); tax crimes (direct and indirect taxes); extortion; forgery; piracy; and insider trading and market manipulation. (p. 130)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "tax crimes (related to direct taxes and indirect taxes);"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: When deciding the range of offences covered as predicate offences within each designated category, each country may decide, in accordance with its domestic law, how to define those offences and the nature of any elements that make them serious offences. (p. 130)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each country may decide, in accordance with its domestic law, how it will define those offences"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: designated non-financial businesses and professions are casinos (footnote 101: including internet- and ship-based casinos), real estate agents, dealers in precious metals, dealers in precious stones, lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants, and trust and company service providers. (p. 130)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Casinos101 b) Real estate agents. c) Dealers in precious metals."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: the lawyer/notary/legal professional/accountant category refers to sole practitioners, partners or employed professionals within professional firms. It is not meant to cover internal professionals employed by other types of businesses, nor professionals working for government agencies who may already be subject to AML/CFT measures. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "professionals that are employees of other types of businesses, nor to professionals working for government agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: trust and company service providers are all persons or businesses not covered elsewhere in the Recommendations that, as a business, provide third parties with any of: acting as formation agent of legal persons; acting as (or arranging for another to act as) director, secretary, partner or similar; providing a registered office, business, correspondence or administrative address; acting as (or arranging for another to act as) trustee of an express trust or equivalent; or acting as (or arranging for another to act as) nominee shareholder. (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "acting as a formation agent of legal persons;"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "designated person or entity" is one designated by the UN 1267 Committee (Al-Qaida associated) or 1988 Committee (Taliban associated), by jurisdictions or a supra-national jurisdiction under resolution 1373 (2001), for targeted financial sanctions under resolution 1718 (2006) and successors (including the 1718 Sanctions Committee), or under resolution 1737 (2006) and successors (including the 1737 Sanctions Committee). (p. 131)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any natural or legal person or entity designated by jurisdictions or a"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "designation" is the identification of a person (natural or legal) or entity subject to targeted financial sanctions under UNSCR 1267 (1999) and successors; 1373 (2001), including the determination that sanctions will apply and public communication of it; 1718 (2006) and future successors; 1737 (2006) and future successors; and any future resolutions imposing targeted financial sanctions on proliferation financing of weapons of mass destruction. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "determination that the relevant sanctions will be applied to the person or entity and the public communication"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "ex parte" means proceeding without prior notification and participation of the affected party. An "express trust" is a trust clearly created by the settlor, usually in a document such as a written deed of trust, in contrast to trusts arising by operation of law without the settlor's clear intent (for example constructive trusts). (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proceeding without prior notification and participation of the affected party"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "financial group" is a group consisting of a parent company or other legal person exercising control and coordinating functions over the rest of the group, together with branches and/or subsidiaries subject to AML/CFT policies and procedures at group level. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exercising control and coordinating functions over the rest of the group"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "financial institutions" means any natural or legal person conducting as a business, for or on behalf of a customer, activities including: acceptance of deposits and other repayable funds from the public (footnote 103: also captures private banking); lending (footnote 104: includes consumer credit, mortgage credit, factoring with or without recourse, and finance of commercial transactions including forfeiting); financial leasing (footnote 105: not extending to consumer product leasing); and money or value transfer services. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Acceptance of deposits and other repayable funds from the public.103"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: financial institution activities also include issuing and managing means of payment (for example credit and debit cards, cheques, traveller's cheques, money orders, bankers' drafts, electronic money); financial guarantees and commitments; trading in money market instruments, foreign exchange, exchange, interest rate and index instruments, transferable securities and commodity futures; and participation in securities issues and related financial services. Footnote 106: the MVTS limb does not apply to those providing financial institutions solely with message or other support systems for transmitting funds. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Issuing and managing means of payment (e.g. credit and debit cards"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: financial institution activities further include individual and collective portfolio management; safekeeping and administration of cash or liquid securities for others; otherwise investing, administering or managing funds or money for others; underwriting and placement of life insurance and other investment related insurance (footnote 107: applies to insurance undertakings and intermediaries, agents and brokers); and money and currency changing. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Safekeeping and administration of cash or liquid securities on behalf of other persons."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "foreign counterparts" are foreign competent authorities exercising similar responsibilities and functions in relation to the cooperation sought, even where of a different nature or status (for example AML/CFT supervision may be done by a supervisor with prudential responsibilities or by a supervisory unit of the FIU). (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "foreign competent authorities that exercise similar responsibilities and functions in relation to the cooperation which is sought"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: in the context of confiscation and provisional measures (for example Recommendations 4, 32 and 38), to "freeze" means to prohibit transfer, conversion, disposition or movement of property on the basis of, and for the duration of, an action by a competent authority or court under a freezing mechanism, or until a forfeiture or confiscation determination is made. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prohibit the transfer, conversion, disposition or movement of any property on the basis of"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: for Recommendations 6 and 7 on targeted financial sanctions, to "freeze" means to prohibit transfer, conversion, disposition or movement of funds or other assets owned or controlled by designated persons or entities, based on and for the duration of an action by the UN Security Council or, per applicable resolutions, a competent authority or court. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "owned or controlled by designated persons or entities on the basis of"</sub>

In all cases frozen property remains the property of those who held an interest at the time of freezing and may continue to be administered by third parties or through arrangements they established before the freezing action, or under other national provisions. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may decide to take control of the property, equipment, instrumentalities, or funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "fundamental principles of domestic law" are the basic legal principles on which national legal systems are based, normally contained in a constitution or similar document or in decisions of the highest court with power to make binding interpretations. Examples, which vary by country, include rights of due process, the presumption of innocence, and a person's right to effective protection by the courts. (p. 135)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "rights of due process, the presumption of innocence, and a person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "funds or other assets" means any assets including financial assets, economic resources (including oil and other natural resources), property of every kind, and legal documents evidencing title or interest, including bank credits, traveller's cheques, bank cheques, money orders, shares, securities, bonds, drafts or letters of credit, any interest, dividends or other income or value accruing from them, and any other assets potentially usable to obtain funds, goods or services. (p. 135)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any other assets which potentially may be used to obtain funds, goods or services"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "identification data" refers to reliable, independent source documents, data or information. (p. 135)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reliable, independent source documents, data or information"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "funds" means assets of every kind, whether corporeal or incorporeal, tangible or intangible, movable or immovable, however acquired, together with legal documents or instruments in any form, including electronic or digital, evidencing title to or interest in such assets. (p. 135)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to assets of every kind, whether corporeal or incorporeal, tangible or intangible, movable or immovable"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "international organisations" are entities established by formal political agreements between member States with the status of international treaties, recognised by law in member countries and not treated as resident institutional units of the countries where located. Examples given include the United Nations and affiliates such as the International Maritime Organisation, the Council of Europe, EU institutions, the OSCE, the Organization of American States, NATO, the WTO and ASEAN. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are not treated as resident institutional units of the countries in which they are located"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "legal arrangements" refers to express trusts and other similar legal arrangements; examples (for AML/CFT purposes) may include fiducie, certain types of Treuhand, fideicomiso and Waqf.   <sub>anchor: "fiducie, certain types of Treuhand, fideicomiso and Waqf"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "legal persons" are any entities other than natural persons that can establish a permanent customer relationship with a financial institution or otherwise own property, and can include companies, bodies corporate, foundations, anstalt, partnerships, associations and other relevantly similar entities. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can establish a permanent customer relationship with a financial institution or otherwise own property"</sub>
