## GENERAL GLOSSARY

- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: foreign PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country, for example Heads of State or government, senior politicians, senior government, judicial or military officials, senior executives of state owned corporations, and important political party officials. (p. 137)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Foreign PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: domestic PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted domestically with prominent public functions, with the same examples as for foreign PEPs (Heads of State or government, senior politicians, senior government, judicial or military officials, senior executives of state owned corporations, important political party officials). (p. 137)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Domestic PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted domestically with prominent public functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: persons entrusted with a prominent function by an international organisation are members of senior management, i.e. directors, deputy directors and board members or equivalent functions. The PEP definition is not intended to cover middle ranking or more junior individuals in these categories. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The definition of PEPs is not intended to cover middle ranking or more junior individuals"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "proceeds" means any property derived from or obtained, directly or indirectly, through the commission of an offence. "Property" means assets of every kind, corporeal or incorporeal, moveable or immoveable, tangible or intangible, and legal documents or instruments evidencing title to or interest in such assets. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any property derived from or obtained, directly or indirectly, through the commission of an offence"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "reasonable measures" means appropriate measures that are proportionate to the money laundering or terrorist financing risks. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate measures which are proportionate to the money laundering or terrorist financing risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: all references to "risk" refer to the risk of money laundering and/or terrorist financing, and the term should be read together with the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 1. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All references to risk refer to the risk of money laundering and/or terrorist financing"</sub>
- **mandatory / definition**: Glossary: where a financial institution is referred to as being "satisfied" as to a matter, that institution must be able to justify its assessment to competent authorities. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that institution must be able to justify its assessment to competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: to "seize" means to prohibit transfer, conversion, disposition or movement of property based on an action by a competent authority or court. Unlike freezing, a seizure uses a mechanism allowing the authority or court to take control of the specified property; the property remains that of the person holding an interest at the time of seizure, although the authority or court will often take over possession, administration or management. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unlike a freezing action, a seizure is effected by a mechanism that allows the competent authority"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a self-regulatory body (SRB) represents a profession (for example lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals or accountants), is made up of members of the profession, has a role in regulating who may enter and practise in it, and performs certain supervisory or monitoring functions. Such bodies should enforce rules to ensure high ethical and moral standards are maintained by practitioners. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such bodies should enforce rules to ensure that high ethical and moral standards are maintained"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "settlors" are natural or legal persons who transfer ownership of their assets to trustees by means of a trust deed or similar arrangement. (p. 138)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transfer ownership of their assets to trustees by means of a trust deed or similar arrangement"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "shell bank" is a bank with no physical presence in the country where it is incorporated and licensed and that is unaffiliated with a regulated financial group subject to effective consolidated supervision. (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a bank that has no physical presence in the country in which it is incorporated and licensed"</sub>
- **mandatory / definition**: Glossary: for the purposes of assessing compliance with the FATF Recommendations, the word "should" has the same meaning as "must". (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the word should has the same meaning as must"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "supervisors" are the designated competent authorities or non-public bodies responsible for ensuring FI ("financial supervisors", footnote 110: including Core Principles supervisors performing functions related to FATF Recommendations) and/or DNFBP compliance with AML/CFT requirements. Non-public bodies, which could include certain types of SRBs, should have power to supervise and sanction FIs or DNFBPs for AML/CFT requirements, should be empowered by law to exercise those functions, and should be supervised by a competent authority in relation to them. (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have the power to supervise and sanction financial institutions or DNFBPs in relation to the AML/CFT requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "terrorist" is any natural person who commits or attempts to commit terrorist acts by any means, directly or indirectly, unlawfully and wilfully; participates as an accomplice; organises or directs others to commit terrorist acts; or contributes to terrorist acts by a group acting with a common purpose where the contribution is intentional and aims to further the act or is made with knowledge of the group's intention to commit a terrorist act. (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "organises or directs others to commit terrorist acts"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "physical presence" means meaningful mind and management located within a country; having merely a local agent or low-level staff is not enough to amount to physical presence. (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Physical presence means meaningful mind and management located within a country."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: the term "targeted financial sanctions" covers both asset freezing and prohibitions on making funds or other assets available, directly or indirectly, for the benefit of designated persons and entities. (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "means both asset freezing and prohibitions to prevent funds or other assets from being made available"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "terrorist act" includes (a) an act constituting an offence within the scope of and as defined in one of ten listed treaties, being conventions and protocols on unlawful seizure of aircraft (1970), civil aviation safety (1971), internationally protected persons (1973), hostage-taking (1979), physical protection of nuclear material (1980), airport violence (1988), maritime navigation (2005), fixed platforms on the continental shelf (2005), terrorist bombings (1997) and financing of terrorism (1999). (p. 140)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism (1999)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "terrorist act" also includes (b) any other act intended to cause death or serious bodily injury to a civilian or other person not taking an active part in hostilities in armed conflict, when the purpose by its nature or context is to intimidate a population or compel a Government or international organisation to do or abstain from doing any act. (p. 140)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when the purpose of such act, by its nature or context, is to intimidate a population"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "terrorist financing" is the financing of terrorist acts, and of terrorists and terrorist organisations. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Terrorist financing is the financing of terrorist acts, and of terrorists and terrorist organisations."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: except in Recommendation 4, references to a "terrorist financing offence" cover not only the primary offence(s) but also ancillary offences. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "References (except in Recommendation 4) to a terrorist financing offence refer not only to the primary offence"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "terrorist organisation" is any group of terrorists that commits or attempts terrorist acts, participates as accomplice, organises or directs others to commit terrorist acts, or contributes to terrorist acts by a group acting with a common purpose, with the same intent or knowledge conditions as in the definition of terrorist. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to any group of terrorists that: (i) commits, or attempts to commit, terrorist acts"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: for the purposes of Recommendations 6 and 7, "third parties" includes, but is not limited to, financial institutions and DNFBPs. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the term third parties includes, but is not limited to, financial institutions and DNFBPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 111 reproduces Hague Convention Article 2: a "trust" is a legal relationship created inter vivos or on death by a settlor when assets are placed under the control of a trustee for a beneficiary's benefit or a specified purpose. Characteristics are that the assets form a separate fund not part of the trustee's estate, title stands in the name of the trustee or another on the trustee's behalf, and the trustee has power and duty, for which he is accountable, to manage, employ or dispose of the assets per the trust terms and legal duties. A settlor's reservation of certain rights and powers, and the trustee also holding rights as beneficiary, are not necessarily inconsistent with a trust. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the assets constitute a separate fund and are not a part of the trustee's own estate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a trustee may be professional (for example, depending on the jurisdiction, a lawyer or trust company) when paid to act as trustee in the course of business, or non-professional, meaning not in the business of being a trustee, such as a person acting on behalf of family. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a non-professional who is not in the business of being a trustee"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "virtual asset" is a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded or transferred and used for payment or investment purposes. It does not include digital representations of fiat currencies, securities and other financial assets already covered elsewhere in the FATF Recommendations. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded, or transferred"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: a "virtual asset service provider" is any natural or legal person not covered elsewhere under the Recommendations who as a business conducts for or on behalf of another person: (i) exchange between virtual assets and fiat currencies; (ii) exchange between forms of virtual assets; (iii) transfer of virtual assets; (iv) safekeeping and/or administration of virtual assets or instruments enabling control over them; and (v) participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer and/or sale of a virtual asset. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exchange between virtual assets and fiat currencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary footnote 112: in the virtual asset context, "transfer" means conducting a transaction on behalf of another natural or legal person that moves a virtual asset from one virtual asset address or account to another. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "moves a virtual asset from one virtual asset address or account to another"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: "without delay" means, ideally, within a matter of hours of a designation by the UN Security Council or its relevant Sanctions Committee (for example the 1267 Committee, the 1988 Committee, the 1718 Sanctions Committee). (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "means, ideally, within a matter of hours of a designation by the United Nations Security Council"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In both cases, "without delay" should be interpreted in the context of the need to prevent the flight or dissipation of funds or other assets linked to terrorists, terrorist organisations, those who finance terrorism and proliferation financing of weapons of mass destruction, and the need for global, concerted action to interdict and disrupt their flow swiftly. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the need to prevent the flight or dissipation of funds or other assets which are linked to terrorists"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary: for the purposes of S/RES/1373(2001), "without delay" means once there are reasonable grounds, or a reasonable basis, to suspect or believe that a person or entity is a terrorist, a terrorist financier or a terrorist organisation. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "upon having reasonable grounds, or a reasonable basis, to suspect or believe that a person or entity is a terrorist"</sub>

## ANNEX I: FATF GUIDANCE DOCUMENTS

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF has published a large body of Guidance and Best Practices papers, available on its website in the Guidance and Best Practices section; the annex lists amendments made to the FATF Recommendations since the text was adopted in February 2012. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF has published a large body of Guidance and Best Practices papers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: February 2013 amendment to R.37(d): aligned the Standards between R.37 and R.40 by inserting that DNFBP secrecy or confidentiality laws should not affect provision of mutual legal assistance, except where the information sought is held in circumstances where legal professional privilege or legal professional secrecy applies. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not affect the provision of mutual legal assistance, except where the relevant information that is sought"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: October 2015 revision of the Interpretive Note to R.5 inserted B.3 to incorporate UNSCR 2178 on foreign terrorist fighters, clarifying that Recommendation 5 requires countries to criminalise financing the travel of individuals to a State other than their State of residence or nationality for the perpetration, planning or preparation of, or participation in, terrorist acts, or providing or receiving terrorist training. Existing B.3-11 became B.4-12. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 5 requires countries to criminalise financing the travel of individuals"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: October 2016 revision of INR.5 replaced "funds" with "funds or other assets" throughout, for the same scope as R.6, and revised the Glossary definition of "funds or other assets" by adding references to oil and other natural resources and to other assets that may potentially be used to obtain funds. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in order to have the same scope as R.6"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: June 2017 revision of INR.7 and the Glossary definitions of "Designated person or entity", "Designation" and "Without delay" brought the text in line with recent UN Security Council Resolutions and clarified implementation of targeted financial sanctions relating to proliferation financing. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to clarify the implementation of targeted financial sanctions relating to proliferation financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The June 2016 revision of R.8 and its Interpretive Note clarified which subset of NPOs should be subject to supervision and monitoring. It aligned INR.8 with the June 2014 FATF Typologies Report on Risk of Terrorist Abuse of NPOs and the June 2015 FATF Best Practices on Combatting the Abuse of NPOs, which clarify that not all NPOs are high risk, and better aligned R.8/INR.8 implementation with the risk-based approach. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not all NPOs are high risk and intended to be addressed by R.8"</sub>
