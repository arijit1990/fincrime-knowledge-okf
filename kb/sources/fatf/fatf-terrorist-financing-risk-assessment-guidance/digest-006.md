## Annex C. TERRORIST FINANCING RISK EVENTS: PRACTICAL TOOL

- **descriptive / definition**: Annex C paragraph 1: understanding TF risks involves considering known or suspected TF threats and vulnerabilities in the jurisdiction and how they interact. One approach to articulating this interaction is the use of risk events, which are hypothetical scenarios derived from identified TF threats, vulnerabilities and consequences. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These are hypothetical scenarios derived from identified TF threats, vulnerabilities and consequences."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Annex C paragraph 2: identified risk events can be taken into account in the analysis stage of the TF risk assessment, which includes assessing the nature, sources, likelihood and consequences of the possible risk events. The threats and vulnerabilities listed in the Annex are examples only and not intended to be exhaustive. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessing the nature, sources, likelihood and consequences of the possible risk events"</sub>

## Identification of threats and vulnerabilities

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 3: the first step is to identify jurisdiction-specific TF threats and vulnerabilities, best conducted with an open mind to brainstorm so that the widest range of possible risk events is identified initially. These can then be refined based on the primary methods and payment mechanisms used, the key sectors exploited, and the primary reasons why those carrying out TF have not been apprehended or deprived of their assets. Chapter 2 gives further information. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is best conducted with an open mind to brainstorm potential threats and vulnerabilities"</sub>

## Combining threats and vulnerabilities into risk events

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 4: the second step is to combine identified threats and vulnerabilities into risk events. They can be combined in different ways because different threats may seek to exploit more than one vulnerability in the jurisdiction. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Threats and vulnerabilities can be combined in a number of different ways"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Example risk event: domestic terrorist group X's activities combined with the presence of individuals, groups or organisations supporting or promoting violent extremism yields the risk event that group X raises funds via cash donations obtained within the jurisdiction. (p. 62)[^original]
