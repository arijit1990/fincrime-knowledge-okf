## Adequate beneficial ownership information

- **expected / expectation**: FATF guidance (para 83) says countries should have mechanisms to ensure beneficial ownership information on trusts and similar legal arrangements is adequate. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should have mechanisms in place that ensure beneficial ownership information on trusts and other similar legal arrangements is adequate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Adequate beneficial ownership information for legal arrangements is data sufficient to identify the natural persons who are the beneficial owners and their role in the arrangement. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information is data that is sufficient to identify the natural persons who are the beneficial owner(s), and their role"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where the trustee or any other party to a trust is a legal person or legal arrangement, its beneficial owner should be identified using the methods specified for R.24 and R.25. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be identified in accordance with the methods specified in relation to R.24 and R.25"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Per INR.25 (para 84), the requirement to obtain adequate information applies to trustees of express trusts and equivalents in similar arrangements who reside in the country or who administer express trusts or similar arrangements in the country. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The requirement to obtain adequate information specifically concerns “trustees of any express trust"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF guidance (para 85) says the trust instrument (or equivalent document for other legal arrangements) should be the primary source of information on trust parties, though some information may sit in accompanying documents. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be the primary source for information on trust parties"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF guidance (para 87) states that information on a beneficial owner's role must make clear whether the person is the settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiary, or a natural person exercising ultimate effective control over the trust. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must make clear whether this beneficial owner is the settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where trust parties are legal persons or arrangements, information on them should include their beneficial owners, the means and mechanisms through which beneficial ownership is exercised, and the scope of the beneficial interest. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the means and mechanisms through which the beneficial owner exercises beneficial ownership and the scope of their beneficial interest"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF notes that information on legal intermediaries or legal entities involved in the ownership chain may also be useful. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on legal intermediaries or legal entities involved in the chain"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Per INR.25 and the risk-based approach (para 89), trustees are not expected to obtain fully adequate and accurate information on beneficiaries designated by characteristics or class until the person becomes entitled at payout or intends to exercise vested rights. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not expected to obtain fully adequate and accurate information on the beneficiary(ies) of trusts/similar legal arrangement which are designated by characteristics"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For beneficiaries designated by class or characteristics, the trustee is expected to hold information clearly identifying the class or characteristics, and once a beneficiary becomes entitled the trustee should obtain and hold information on their identity. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when the beneficiary(ies) becomes entitled, the trustee should obtain and hold information on their identity"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Countries may decide that identifying individual beneficiaries of certain charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts is unnecessary, but this decision should be based on, and clearly align with, a risk assessment of that type of trust. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not necessary to identify the individual beneficiaries of certain charitable or statutory permitted non-charitable trusts"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: FATF guidance (para 90) says trustees may take a risk-based approach to decide what details to hold about objects of a power; for a very broad class, holding class characteristics that let the trustee determine whether a person is a member may suffice. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trustees may take a risk-based approach to determine the details of information that they should hold about objects of a power"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where individuals are identified as objects of a power, the trustee should at minimum obtain and hold enough detail to identify and verify the individual if a discretion or power is exercised in their favour, making them a beneficiary. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the trustee should obtain and hold, as a minimum, sufficient details to be able to identify and verify the identity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF guidance (para 92) states it is not mandatory to obtain official identity documents for an object of a power, since that person may not yet know the trust exists. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is not mandatory to obtain official identity documentation as the object of a power might not be yet aware"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Risk-sensitive verification options for an object of a power include: a settlor declaration confirming identification details (for trustees); relying on details in a trust deed, letter of wishes or similar; or checking publicly available sources or accessible registries. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtaining a declaration confirming the identification details of the object of a power from the settlor"</sub>

## Accurate beneficial ownership information

- **permissive / expectation**: FATF guidance (para 93, citing INR.25 paragraph 6) says countries may adopt different verification mechanisms at various stages to ensure beneficial ownership information is accurate. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may adopt different verification mechanisms at the various stages (as outlined below)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Verifying beneficial ownership information could involve reviewing submitted documents such as the trust instrument and letters of wishes. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Verifying beneficial ownership information could involve a review of documents submitted"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Depending on risk and who verifies, verification could also use government-issued ID and cross-checks against databases such as bank account registries, population or national identity registers, taxpayer identification registers, and vehicle and land registries. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "bank account registries, population or national identity registers, taxpayers’ identification register, vehicles, and land registries"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 96) says verification should also cover status: appropriate steps should verify the basis on which a person was identified as a beneficial owner. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Appropriate steps should be taken to verify the basis of identification of a person as a beneficial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 97) states the aim of verification is to reduce the risk of inaccurate information and allow enforcement of transparency rules, but it does not imply a zero-failure approach; it is about increasing confidence and systematically catching obvious errors, falsehoods or inconsistencies. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "However, this does not imply a zero-failure approach"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may consider automated checks where possible to reduce verification burden and improve processing timeliness. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may consider automated checks where possible to minimise the burden of verification"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: FATF paragraph 95 says the chosen approach to verifying beneficial owner identities could include verification by trustees and equivalent persons in any case; by the authority or body recording trust beneficial ownership where a registry exists; by other authorities holding or obtaining information on the arrangements and trustees; and by agents and service providers (including TCSPs, investment advisors, lawyers, accountants and FIs) creating, operating or managing a legal arrangement, as well as FIs in CDD under R. 10 and DNFBPs in CDD under R. 22. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "By trustees and persons holding an equivalent position in a similar legal arrangement, in any cases"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 98) permits a risk-based approach to verification, and in higher-risk cases the extent and/or frequency of verification measures should be enhanced. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the extent and/or frequency of verification measures should be enhanced"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Higher-risk examples warranting enhanced verification: arrangements with complex structures across multiple countries, arrangements flagged high-risk in a risk assessment, those with a history of inaccurate beneficial ownership reporting, or where sufficient documentation cannot be obtained. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legal arrangements with complex structures across multiple countries, legal arrangements identified as high-risk in a risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 99) lists points at which information might be verified: when a trust is first established, when registered, when a change happens (e.g., new trustee), and when an entity's identity becomes apparent (e.g., a class member becomes entitled or an ultimate controller is identified). (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when the identity of an entity becomes apparent"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Information might also be verified when a trust enters an arrangement with an FI or government body (e.g., opening a bank account, buying real property, lodging a tax return), and when automated exchange with reliable national systems occurs, at a risk-based frequency. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when a trust enters an arrangement with a financial institution or government body"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: In countries requiring a professional intermediary to set up trusts, regulated professional intermediaries could be required to perform enhanced checks. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regulated professional intermediaries could be required to perform such enhanced checks"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: FATF paragraph 99 says that, subject to the approach in paragraph 95, beneficial ownership information might be verified at points such as when a trust is established or registered, when a change occurs, when an entity's identity becomes apparent, when the trust enters an arrangement with a financial institution or government body, or on automated exchange of information with reliable national systems. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Subject to the approach set out in paragraph 95 above, information might be verified"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: In an ongoing relationship, FATF paragraph 100 says it may be appropriate to check that beneficial ownership information continues to be accurate, with the frequency of checks depending on a risk assessment. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the case of an ongoing relationship, it may be appropriate to check that information continues to be accurate"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Factors in the risk assessment that sets how often ongoing accuracy checks occur (paragraph 100) include: frequency and value of transactions or dealings; significance of the information for addressing ML/TF risks; likelihood of change; other relevant legal obligations and checks; ease of verification; source and value of assets settled in trust; country of origin and residence of the settlor, protector and other parties including the object of a power; status of the object of a power or other party (e.g., PEP or other high-risk customer); complexity of the structure; and purpose of settling property in trust. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the frequency and value of transactions or dealings"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: FATF paragraph 101 says enhanced verification mechanisms can also be used to detect inaccuracies and deliberate concealment, such as undisclosed nominee relationships, and that such investigative checks may be conducted by law enforcement authorities; where a professional intermediary is required to set up trusts, regulated intermediaries could be required to perform them. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "deliberate concealment, such as undisclosed nominee relationships"</sub>

## Box 4.1. Mechanisms to identify and mitigate inaccuracies

- **recommended / recommendation**: Where reliant on a registry, countries may consider a system to review and adjudicate discrepancy reports fairly and efficiently, emphasising due process and a risk-based approach. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "implementing a system to review and adjudicate discrepancy reports fairly and efficiently"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: FATF (para 102) says countries may require a declaration that information disclosed at submission is truthful and complete; this puts the primary burden on the submitting party (which may include legal persons, representatives, TCSPs or other service providers) but should not replace verification by the recipient. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also require a declaration that the information disclosed at the time of submission is truthful and complete"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF Box 4.1 says countries may consider mechanisms that allow reporting entities access to beneficial ownership information, for example to complement their CDD obligations. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Access by reporting entities to information so that it may be used, for example, to complement CDD obligations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Within the discrepancy-report system described in FATF Box 4.1, trustees and persons in equivalent positions should be informed at the appropriate time of reported discrepancies, with reasons, so that data can be rectified in a timely manner, whilst avoiding tipping off where applicable. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be informed at the appropriate time of the reported discrepancies (with reasons)"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF Box 4.1 says that, upon resolving a discrepancy, countries should consider notifying the reporting entity so that all information is aligned. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Upon resolving a difference, countries should consider notifying the reporting entity to align all information"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF Box 4.1 says countries should consider properly recording information so that potential users are aware that the data might not be adequate, accurate or up to date. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "potential users of this information are aware that this data might not be adequate, accurate, or up to date"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The legal framework should provide dissuasive sanctions where a false or inaccurate declaration is established. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dissuasive sanctions to be applied where it is established that a false or inaccurate declaration has been provided"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 103) suggests countries may oblige trust parties and beneficial owners to give the trustee relevant information, on request or spontaneously, to keep information accurate and up to date. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establishing an obligation on the parties and beneficial owners of the trusts to provide the trustee"</sub>

## Verification of identity of the beneficial owner

- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 104) says identity verification should prove that an identified beneficial owner exists and is who they claim, e.g., via government-issued ID or other reliable documents or information. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate steps should be taken to prove that a natural person, who has been identified as a beneficial owner, exists"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF describes an identity as a combination of attributes belonging to a person, such as name, date of birth and nationality. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An identity is a combination of “attributes” that belong to a person"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: For registries, identity verification could be done by automated data exchange with reliable national systems (residence, tax, passport, electoral, bank account, population, vehicle or land registries) if it gives the same level of assurance. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could be done by an automated data exchange with a reliable national system such as a residence register"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 105) states the robustness of evidence must be considered when verifying identity, relating to the amount and reliability of source data, with a risk-based approach applied. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When verifying a person’s identity, the robustness of the evidence must be considered"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 106) treats identity information as accurate where it is verified using reliable documents, data or information, with the type and extent of verification set by the risk of the relationship or occasional transaction. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identity information is considered accurate where it is verified using reliable documents, data, or information"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Risk elements for identity verification include source and value of settled assets, countries of origin/residence of settlor, protector and other parties (including objects of a power), PEP or high-risk status, structure complexity, and purpose of settling property in trust if known. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the purpose of the settling of property in trust, if known"</sub>

## Verification of status of the beneficial owner

- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 107) lists status-verification checks including whether the person meets the definition of beneficial owner (settlor/trustee/protector/beneficiary/object of a power). (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Does the person identified as the beneficial owner meet the definition of a beneficial owner of the trust"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Status verification also asks whether any other person can exercise ultimate effective control and whether the settlor or any beneficiary is acting as a nominee. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Is the settlor or any of the beneficiaries acting as a nominee?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Status verification also asks whether legal persons/arrangements are among the trust parties and if so whether their natural-person beneficial owners have been identified. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Are there any legal persons/arrangements among the parties of the trust/similar legal arrangements?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Status verification also asks whether nominee arrangements involve trust parties and if so whether the nominator (or its beneficial owners if a legal person/arrangement) has been identified. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Are there any nominee arrangements involving parties to the trust/similar legal arrangements?"</sub>

## Verification of identity of the beneficial owner

- **permissive / method**: FATF paragraph 104 says that, in the case of registries, identity verification could be done by automated data exchange with a reliable national system (e.g., residence register, tax register, passport database, electoral information, bank account, population, vehicle or land registries) if the exchange provides the same level of assurance. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such verification could be done by an automated data exchange with a reliable national system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For verifying the identity of a beneficial owner located abroad, FATF paragraph 105 says the receiving end of the beneficial ownership information should take steps to verify the authenticity of legal documentation provided from abroad. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should take steps to verify the authenticity of legal documentation provided from abroad"</sub>

## Verification of status of the beneficial owner

- **permissive / recommendation**: Because beneficial owner status can change, FATF (para 108) suggests countries may require verification whenever information is updated rather than relying only on checks at formation or initial registry filing; historical data may be useful to authorities, FIs or DNFBPs. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider requiring that the information be verified where it is updated"</sub>

## Up-to-date beneficial ownership information22

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 109) says countries should require trustees and equivalents to obtain and hold up-to-date beneficial ownership information available to competent authorities, and should consider measures to facilitate access by FIs and DNFBPs performing R.10 and R.22 requirements. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should require trustees and persons holding an equivalent position in similar legal arrangements to obtain and hold up-to-date"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: As best practice, countries may consider requiring risk-based periodic validation of beneficial ownership information, for trustees and other access mechanisms, to uncover changes that a trustee inadvertently fails to report. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider requiring periodical validation of the beneficial ownership information on a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 22 refers to the Guidance on R.24, stating its requirements on up-to-date information are equally applicable. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Refer to Guidance on R.24 as requirements are equally applicable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When basic or beneficial ownership information changes (e.g., new beneficiaries, appointment of co-trustees or protectors, changed identity details), trustees are expected to obtain and verify the information within a reasonable time of the change. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "trustees are expected to obtain and verify this information within a reasonable time from when the change occurs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should have mechanisms ensuring information is updated within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the mechanism used for competent-authority access. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should have mechanisms in place to ensure that the information is updated within a reasonable timeframe"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: FATF (para 113) suggests the reasonable update time may be shorter where the trustee causes the change (e.g., appointing a beneficiary) or becomes immediately aware of it (e.g., amendments require trustee approval). (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a reasonable time to update information may be shorter in cases where such changes are occasioned by the trustee himself"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF paragraph 110 says that, as a best practice for keeping information up to date, countries may consider requiring periodical validation of beneficial ownership information on a risk-based approach, for example by reviewing or verifying it. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider requiring periodical validation of the beneficial ownership information on a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: FATF paragraph 111 says specific events which may warrant updating information within the 'reasonable timeframe' include beneficiaries previously identified by class or characteristics becoming ascertainable, for example through the expiration of an accumulation period or a discretionary trustee exercising discretionary powers to determine the beneficiaries. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the expiration of an accumulation period or the trustee of a discretionary trust exercising their discretionary powers"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF notes that while trustees are normally immediately aware of changes, this may not hold where changes are not reported, are identified later, or are not reported to trustees in a timely way. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when such changes are not reported but identified later or not reported in a timely manner to trustees"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 114) expects trustees to conduct periodic reviews to detect updates, with periodicity based on ML/TF risk. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "trustees are expected to conduct periodic reviews to detect any updates. The periodicity is to be determined based on ML/TF risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Trustees are also expected to review on trigger events such as payout of significant amounts, instructions from a newly appointed protector, or settlement of additional assets in trust. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "payout of significant amounts, receiving instructions from a newly appointed protector, settlement of additional assets in trust"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 115) says countries should ensure trustees retain beneficial ownership records for at least five years after their involvement with the trust ceases; this is a minimum and longer retention may be kept to meet other legal requirements (e.g., trust law) or manage liability. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "trustees retain beneficial ownership records for at least five years after their involvement with the trust or legal arrangement ceases"</sub>

## 5. Mechanisms and sources for obtaining beneficial ownership information

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 116) says countries should ensure adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and beneficial ownership information on trusts, trustees and trust assets is efficiently and timely accessible to competent authorities. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is accessible efficiently and in a timely manner by competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Besides trustees, countries should consider (per INR.25.5, based on risk, context and materiality) using a public authority or body holding trust beneficial ownership, such as a central trust registry or asset registries for land, property, vehicles or shares; information need not be held by a single body. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information need not be held by a single body only"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Another source countries should consider is other competent authorities holding trust information, e.g., tax authorities, provided the information is accessible according to internationally agreed standards. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided that this information is accessible according to internationally agreed standards"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF paragraph 116 says that, beyond trustees, countries should consider, on the basis of risk, context and materiality, using as necessary sources including other agents or service providers such as TCSPs, investment advisors or managers, accountants, lawyers or financial institutions. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Other agents or service providers, including trust and company service providers, investment advisors or managers"</sub>

## Box 5.1. Mechanisms to ensure the availability of beneficial ownership information

- **descriptive / requirement**: Box 5.1 example: trustee obligations are supervised and enforced by a competent authority, with dissuasive and proportionate sanctions for failing to hold the information or to give authorities timely access. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "trustees are subject to dissuasive and proportionate sanctions for failure to hold the required information"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Box 5.1: trustees of express trusts are required to disclose their status to financial institutions, with sanctions such as administrative penalties for providing false information. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trustees of express trusts are required to disclose their status to financial institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Box 5.1: TCSPs, lawyers and accountants carry out CDD, understand beneficial ownership CDD obligations, and are subject to AML/CFT supervision in line with R.10. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TCSPs, lawyers and accountants carry out CDD and understand their CDD obligations"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Box 5.1: a country could set up a central trust registry holding the settlor(s), trustee(s), protectors (if any), each beneficiary or class of beneficiaries and objects of a power, and any other natural person exercising ultimate effective control. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A country could establish a central registry of trusts which includes information on (i) the settlor(s)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF Box 5.1 lists, among example mechanisms (paragraph 117 notes some are mandatory in line with the standards), that trustees are required to obtain and hold information on the trustee, settlor, protector (if any), beneficiaries or class of beneficiaries, object of a power, and any other person exercising control over the trust. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trustees are required to obtain and hold information on the trustee, the settlor, the protector (if any)"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF Box 5.1 says trustees could hold the information in electronic form so that they can provide it to competent authorities upon request in a timely manner. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trustees could hold the information in electronic form"</sub>

## Trustees

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 118) says countries should require resident trustees of express trusts (and equivalents), or those administering such arrangements in the country, to obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to obtain and hold adequate, accurate, and up-to-date beneficial ownership information regarding the trust or similar legal arrangement"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Determining the beneficial owner of a complex trust structure may require good knowledge of trust principles and trust law, including nuances of laws governing legal arrangements in different countries. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A good understanding and knowledge of trust principles and trusts law may be necessary"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Understanding the flexibility and types of trusts may be instrumental in determining whether any other person exercises effective control over a trust. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understanding the flexibility and different types of trusts/legal arrangements might be instrumental"</sub>

## The registry approach

- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 120) says the R.24 registry approach may also be considered for trusts under R.25, depending on risk, context and materiality, as an additional source to the default rule that trustees collect and maintain beneficial ownership information. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also be considered in relation to trusts and other legal arrangements under R.25"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some countries may hold one beneficial ownership register for both legal persons and trusts (e.g., countries not recognising trusts), while others may set up separate registers for trusts and other arrangements. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some countries may opt to hold one register of beneficial ownership for both legal persons as well as trusts"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: FATF paragraph 120 says the 'registry approach' used for legal persons under R.24 may also be considered for trusts and other legal arrangements under R.25, as an additional source beyond the default rule that trustees collect and maintain beneficial ownership information, taking account of risk, context and materiality. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also be considered in relation to trusts and other legal arrangements under R.25"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: FATF paragraph 122 says countries that maintain registers may cover all express trusts and similar arrangements administered in the country or whose trustee resides there, and the registers may also consider covering foreign trusts with sufficient links to the country if the risk assessment has shown major risks requiring this. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also consider covering foreign trusts and similar legal arrangements with sufficient links with the country"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF paragraph 123 says countries using a public authority or body to hold beneficial ownership information should consider the resources and expertise needed to maintain the register, keep its information adequate, accurate and up to date, and allow timely and efficient access. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider the resources and expertise that will be required to maintain the register"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Among the features that a registry mechanism could include (paragraph 124), trustees or equivalents may be required by countries, following a risk-based approach, to make a declaration (e.g., a sworn statement) on the beneficial owner and ownership structure, which could include copies of identity verification documents. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to make a declaration (e.g., sworn statement) regarding the beneficial owner and the ownership structure"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Among the features that a registry mechanism could include (paragraph 124, 'some or all' of the listed features), the registry body is required, on a risk-based approach, to establish different mechanisms to verify the identity of beneficial owners and that they satisfy the beneficial owner criteria. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establish different mechanisms to verify the identity of the beneficial owners"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries using a public authority to hold beneficial ownership information should consider the resources and expertise needed to maintain the register and keep it adequate, accurate, up to date and accessible. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider the resources and expertise that will be required to maintain the register"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 124) features of a trust BO register mechanism: trustees must provide beneficial ownership information to the register body and update it regularly and within a reasonable period after any change. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trustees or equivalent are required to update beneficial ownership information regularly and within a reasonable period following any change"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Register feature: the register body is empowered to impose, and effectively applies, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions on trustees who fail to provide beneficial ownership information. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is empowered to impose, and effectively applies, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions on trustees"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Register feature: providing incorrect information is subject to proportionate and dissuasive administrative and/or criminal sanctions, and a trust company director or other manager could be held personally liable. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A director of a trust company or any other person entrusted with the management and administration thereof could also be held personally liable"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Register feature: the register body regularly applies sanctions when obligations are breached or reports breaches, including filing suspicious reports to the appropriate authority if necessary. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the filing of any suspicious reports to the appropriate authority if this is deemed necessary"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Register feature: the register body takes a proactive role, checking information against other sources (professional trustees' regulatory submissions, asset, tax or national ID registries) using risk-based verification and technology to identify anomalies and reduce document fraud or improper disclosure. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to identify anomalies or inconsistencies and reduce the risk of fraud on supporting documents or improper disclosure"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Register feature: FIs, DNFBPs and, if appropriate, competent authorities identify discrepancies between register data and information available to them, and the register body or other authority investigates and corrects them within a reasonable timeframe. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "takes appropriate actions to investigate these inaccuracies and to correct the information within a reasonable timeframe"</sub>
