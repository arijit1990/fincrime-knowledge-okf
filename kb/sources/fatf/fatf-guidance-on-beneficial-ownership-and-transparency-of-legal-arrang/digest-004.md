## Access by competent authorities

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 130) says competent authorities, particularly LEAs and FIUs, should have powers to access in a timely manner information held by trustees, equivalents and other parties including FIs and DNFBPs, on the basic and beneficial ownership of the legal arrangement. - **expected / requirement**: Competent authority access powers should also cover any assets held or managed by an FI or DNFBP for trusts with which they have a business relationship or occasional transaction. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any assets held or managed by the financial institution or DNFBP, in relation to any trusts or similar legal arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 132) says countries should ensure competent authorities such as LEAs and FIUs have sufficient powers to obtain accurate and up-to-date information directly from the party, an independent third party or a government body. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has sufficient powers to obtain accurate and up-to-date information, either directly from the party"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 133) says countries should ensure trustees are not legally prevented from providing competent authorities with relevant trust information. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should ensure that trustees are not prevented by law from providing competent authorities with relevant information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where countries use sources other than trustees (e.g., registries), competent authorities should know which body or person holds adequate, accurate and up-to-date trust information and how to access it, and countries should ensure authorities are aware of and can access these sources.   <sub>anchor: "competent authorities should have sufficient knowledge of which public authority or body or other person/entity holds adequate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 135) says parties holding relevant information should understand their disclosure obligations, fully co-operate with competent authorities, and provide information as quickly as possible within a timeframe enabling authorities to do their work.   <sub>anchor: "should understand their disclosure obligations, fully co-operate with competent authorities, and provide the information as quickly as possible"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF paragraph 131 (referring to paragraph 5 of INR.25) says countries should consider using different sources of information as necessary, on the basis of risk, context and materiality, so that competent authorities can efficiently and promptly access adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on trusts, trustees and trust assets.   <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider using different sources of information as necessary to ensure that adequate, accurate and up-to-date information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF paragraph 132 says countries should ensure that a competent authority, such as an LEA or FIU, has sufficient powers to obtain accurate and up-to-date information, either directly from the party, from an independent third party or from a government body. ## 6. Sanctions

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 139) says countries should ensure R.25 and INR.25 requirements are clear and unambiguous, with trustees either legally liable for failures relating to paragraphs 1, 4, 8 and 9 of INR.25 or subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legally liable for any failure to perform the duties relevant to meeting the obligations in paragraphs 1, 4, 8 and 9 of INR.25"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Sanctions for failing to grant competent authorities timely access to trust information should be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any sanctions imposed for failing to grant timely access to information regarding the trust to competent authorities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 140) says trustee obligations should be set out in written, publicly available rules for legal certainty, and those rules should directly provide sanctions for breaches of obligations corresponding to paragraphs 1, 4, 8 and 9 of R.25. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be clearly laid out in written and publicly available rules for legal certainty"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: It may also be appropriate to sanction other entities a country relies on for adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and beneficial ownership information. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It may also be appropriate to impose sanctions on other entities that the country relies on"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities (e.g., LEAs, FIUs) empowered to access information held by trustees and others such as FIs and DNFBPs should have sanctioning power for failure to provide it. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have sanctioning power for failure to provide such information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 142) says where countries rely on other BO sources, they should ensure effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions for failing to give competent authorities timely access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date trust information. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where countries choose to rely on other sources of beneficial ownership information, they should ensure that there are effective"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 143) says sanctions should apply to both natural and legal persons failing to comply with R.25. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sanctions should apply to both natural and legal persons that fail to comply with the requirements of R.25"</sub>

(p. 40)[^original]

## Issues relating to the legal profession

- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 151) says where the legal profession helps establish and administer legal arrangements, the expectations set for TCSPs apply to it. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the above expectations for TCSPs would apply to the legal profession"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Because lawyers often act as trustees, nominees or both, practical issues with legal professional privilege arise when they have AML/CFT obligations; a client's right to candid legal advice without fear of prejudicial disclosure is an important feature of the profession. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "practical issues relating to legal professional privilege can arise when lawyers have AML/CFT obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 31: this right is an aspect of access to justice in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and the FATF Recommendations exclude privileged or professionally secret information from STR filing obligations, leaving each country to define those terms. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exclude information covered by legal professional privilege or professional secrecy from the obligation to file a suspicious transaction report"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 152) notes the scope of legal professional privilege and secrecy often derives from constitutional or common law and treaty-based fundamental rights, may differ by state or province in federal systems, and may vary by type of legal professional and service. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also vary across different types of legal professionals within a country and the types of services being offered"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: FATF (para 153, citing World Bank/UNODC StAR report 2011, p.94) identifies use of legal professional privilege and professional secrecy to refuse to divulge ownership and control information as a frequent obstacle to accessing trust information. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a frequent obstacle to accessing information about some trusts and similar legal arrangements is the use of legal professional privilege"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Privilege claims are appropriate when correctly made under the law and tied specifically to legal advice, but occasionally extremely wide claims exceed the generally understood protections in the country. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "occasionally extremely wide claims of privilege are made that exceed the generally understood provisions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities and professional bodies should work to ensure a clear, shared understanding of the scope of legal professional privilege and secrecy in their country. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "competent authorities and professional bodies should work to ensure that there is a clear and shared understanding"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 34 cites the RBA Guidance for Legal Professionals (2019) confirming privilege or professional secrecy does not protect a legal professional from knowingly facilitating a client's illegal conduct. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not protect a legal professional from knowingly facilitating a client’s illegal conduct"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 155) says countries should seek clear understanding of what privilege covers so that investigations involving trusts are not inappropriately impeded. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "investigations involving suspected trusts or other similar legal arrangements are not inappropriately impeded"</sub>
- **expected / typology or red flag**: Where a legal professional, or an entity they control, acts as settlor, beneficiary, controlling person or protector, a nominee arrangement will often exist, so more in-depth scrutiny should be applied to identify the actual beneficial owner.   <sub>anchor: "a nominee arrangement will often exist. Therefore, more in-depth scrutiny should be applied"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Non-professional trustees are commonly appointed in trust for family arrangements, succession, and estate planning"</sub>

## Aligned interpretation of R.10/22 and R.25

- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 159) notes R.10 and R.22 CDD obligations are close to those in R.25; e.g., professional trustees are required under R.22 to identify and verify the beneficial owners of trusts they administer, obtaining adequate, accurate and up-to-date information. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "professional trustees (or equivalent function holders) are required to identify and verify the beneficial owners of trusts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Per para 5 of INR.25, trust information collected by FIs and DNFBPs through CDD can be one source of information. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collected by FIs and DNFBPs, as part of CDD can be one source of information"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under R.10 and R.22, FIs and DNFBPs are required to identify and verify customer identity and understand the nature of business and ownership and control structure, via CDD per the interpretive note to R.10, para. 5(b)(ii). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as required under the interpretive note to R.10, para. 5(b)(ii)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The obligation to understand the customer and its risk profile should be ongoing, and countries should ensure CDD information is adequate, accurate and up to date. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The obligation to understand their customer and their risk profile should be ongoing"</sub>

## Consistent application of the beneficial ownership definition for trusts and similar legal arrangements

- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 162) notes the revised FATF Recommendations define beneficial ownership for trusts and similar arrangements to cover holders of an object of a power. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "covering holders of object of a power in that trust or similar arrangement"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: All FIs and DNFBPs identifying and verifying beneficial owners of trusts shall identify and verify all parties listed in the glossary definition of beneficial owner, in accordance with INR.25.1. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shall identify and verify all parties as listed under the glossary definition of beneficial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Examples of trust parties that are legal persons: a commercial trust-services trustee, an enterprise settlor setting up an employee benefit trust fund, or a legal entity acting as nominee for an individual settlor or on instructions of an individual who provided funds. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the settlor may be an enterprise setting up a trust fund for the benefit of its employees"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In such cases the trustee and other FIs or DNFBPs serving the trust should identify and verify the legal person or arrangement and its beneficial owners; though explicit only in INR.25 for trustees, this also applies under R.10 and R.22 to other FIs and DNFBPs. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is likewise applicable in the context of R.10 and R.22 when all other FIs and DNFBPs are identifying the beneficial owners"</sub>

## Application of identification and verification measures where parties to the trust and similar legal arrangement are legal persons or arrangements.

- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 164) says trustees, other FIs and DNFBPs should identify and verify trust parties that are legal persons or arrangements by collecting and verifying their basic and beneficial ownership information in line with R.10. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by collecting and verifying their basic and beneficial ownership information in line with R.10"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 165) says an FI or DNFBP other than the trustee serving an express trust should determine each beneficial owner's role (settlor, trustee, beneficiary or object of a power). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should determine the role/status that such a beneficial owner has within the legal arrangement"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: To comply with R.10, an FI or DNFBP may rely on the trustee to declare whether there is any object of a power and who it is, since only the trustee can determine this; per INR.10 the information should satisfy the FI/DNFBP it can establish the beneficiary's identity at payout or when vested rights are exercised. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may rely on the trustee (who is obliged to disclose their status to FI or DNFBPs) to indicate or declare"</sub>

## Applying identification and verification requirements in respect of objects of a power

- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 166) states it is not mandatory to obtain official documentation (ID or passport) for an object of a power, who may not yet know the trust exists. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not mandatory to obtain official documentation (such as identity documentation or passports)"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Risk-sensitive verification options for objects of a power: a declaration from the settlor (for trustees) or the trustee (for FIs/DNFBPs); details in a trust deed or letter of wishes; public sources or accessible registries; or obtaining official documentation or delaying this until payout. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtaining official documentation or delaying this until the time of pay out"</sub>

## Holding basic information on regulated agents and service providers to the trust or similar legal arrangement

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 167) says countries should require resident or administering trustees to identify the trust's agents and service providers (e.g., investment advisors, accountants, lawyers, tax advisors); INR.25 stipulates trustees should collect 'basic information' on them. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to identify agents and service providers of the trust or similar legal arrangements"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: This obligation arises only under R.25 and applies only to trustees, not to other FIs and DNFBPs servicing the trust (e.g., a bank providing an account) unless acting as service providers, since they cannot be expected to know about other service providers. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this obligation is envisaged only by R.25 and hence applicable only to trustees"</sub>

## Identifying persons that are acting on behalf of a trust or similar legal arrangements

- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 169) states R.25 requires trustees and equivalents to disclose their status when entering a business relationship or occasional transaction with an FI or DNFBP. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "R.25 requires trustees and persons holding equivalent functions in other legal arrangements to disclose their status"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: FIs and DNFBPs may rely on trustees' status disclosure but should be alert to indicators that the person is actually acting for an undisclosed natural person, legal person or arrangement. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is acting on behalf of another natural person, legal person or arrangement without disclosing this"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Red flag of an undisclosed principal: the declared trustee cannot give immediate directions and asks for time to do so. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the declared trustee is unable to give immediate directions and asks to be allowed time to do so"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Red flag: funding or assets held by the FI/DNFBP come from a source other than the declared trustee. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the funding or the assets held by the FI/DNFBP originate from a source other than the declared trustee"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Red flag: doubts about the disclosed purpose of the business relationship or the rationale of certain transactions. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "doubts exist about the disclosed purpose of the business relationship or rationale of certain transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Red flag: transactions do not align with the disclosed purpose of the relationship, e.g., the destination of funds is inconsistent with it. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "destination of funds is inconsistent with the disclosed purpose"</sub>

## Wire transfers and beneficial ownership as part of CDD

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 171) says FIs should be required to apply R.10 CDD for occasional transactions in circumstances covered by R.16 (wire transfers), including identifying and taking reasonable measures to verify the beneficial owner of an originator or beneficiary that is a legal arrangement. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify and take reasonable measures to verify the identity of the beneficial owner of the originator or beneficiary"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.16 also requires FIs to collect certain originator information and ensure it accompanies the wire transfer (INR.16, paragraphs 11-18). (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collecting certain originator information and ensuring that this information accompanies a wire transfer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 172) describes the risk-based approach as central to effective implementation: supervisors, FIs and DNFBPs should identify, assess and understand their ML/TF risks and apply the most appropriate mitigation. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should identify, assess, and understand the ML/TF risks to which they are exposed"</sub>

## FATF RBA Guidance

- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 173) notes its RBA Guidance gives sector-specific guidance and highlights the importance of supervising beneficial ownership requirements and nominee arrangements, including how supervision can confirm adequate, accurate and up-to-date BO information is maintained. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "highlights the importance of supervising beneficial ownership requirements and nominee arrangements"</sub>
