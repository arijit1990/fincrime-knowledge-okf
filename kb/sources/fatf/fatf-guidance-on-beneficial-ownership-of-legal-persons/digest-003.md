## Verification of Identity of the Beneficial Owner

- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 63) says identity verification should prove that an identified beneficial owner actually exists and is who they claim, e.g., by reviewing government-issued identity documents; an identity is a combination of attributes such as name, date of birth and nationality. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate steps should be taken to prove that a natural person, who has been identified as a beneficial owner, actually exists"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where identity evidence is suspected to be falsified or stolen, or other related risks exist, steps should check that the claimed identity belongs to the person presenting it, e.g., by requiring a government-issued passport or driver's licence. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "steps should be taken to check whether the claimed identity belongs to the person presenting the evidence"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Identity verification could also use automated data exchange with reliable national systems (residence register, tax register, passport database, electoral information), and the verifier may rely on it if it gives the same level of assurance. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The verifying party may rely on such an exchange, if it provides the same level of assurance"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 64) states the robustness of identity evidence must be considered, relating to the amount and reliability of independent source data, under a risk-based approach. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the robustness of the evidence must be considered"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For a beneficial owner located abroad, the recipient of BO information should take steps to verify the authenticity of legal documentation from abroad. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information should take steps to verify the authenticity of legal documentation provided from abroad"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 40 states that the party submitting the information could include legal persons, their representatives, TCSPs or other service providers. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which could include legal persons, their representatives, TCSPs or other service providers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 65) notes identity information may need updating when identity documents expire or nationality changes; the person has the onus to update it and the update should be verified. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the person has the onus to update the information which should be subject to verification"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: There should be sufficient safeguards and data security (e.g., a secured portal) so a verified identity cannot be stolen or impersonated. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sufficient safeguards and data security to ensure that the verified identity could not be stolen or impersonated"</sub>

## Verification of Status of the Beneficial Owner

- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 66) status-verification checks include whether the person has ownership, voting or control rights such that they meet the beneficial owner definition. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have ownership, voting rights or control rights in the legal person, such that they meet the definition of a beneficial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Status verification also asks whether the person actually exercises the associated rights in practice in their own name, or under instruction from or agreement with an undisclosed third party. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "under instruction from, or by agreement with, an undisclosed third party41"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Status verification also asks whether the identified beneficial owners are consistent with the structure and risk profile of the legal person. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Whether the identified beneficial owners are consistent with the structure and risk profile of the legal person?"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF footnote 41: a nominee is never the beneficial owner of a legal person. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A nominee is never the beneficial owner of a legal person"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 67) says because status (even the nature of control) can change, information should continue to be verified rather than relying on incorporation-time checks; historical information is useful to authorities, FIs and DNFBPs. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the information should continue to be verified (instead of simply relying on the checks carried out at the point of incorporation)"</sub>

## Discrepancy Reporting Mechanisms

- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 68, citing INR.24 paragraph 11) says countries may consider discrepancy reporting as a complementary risk-based measure, most likely for a register or alternative mechanism, which should complement and not replace verification. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should serve to complement the verification measures to various mechanisms outlined above; it should not replace them"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 69) describes discrepancy reporting as allowing authorities and entities with access to BO data to report to the BO-holding body where their information differs from the body's. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Discrepancy reporting generally allows authorities and entities with access to beneficial ownership data to report"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Discrepancy reporting can improve BO accuracy and completeness but imposes resource costs on reporters; countries can develop procedures to minimise burden where both FIs/DNFBPs and the customer could report. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any of these obligations imposed on the reporting end will give rise to resource implications"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: FATF (para 70a): reporting entities (FIs/DNFBPs or other obliged entities) need access to registry/alternative-mechanism BO data to report differences in accuracy and completeness against their CDD information. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would need access to the beneficial ownership information held in a registry/alternative mechanism"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Because discrepancy volumes may be very high, countries should clearly define a materiality threshold for reportable discrepancies, typically focusing on factual errors rather than typing or spelling mistakes. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should clearly define the material threshold for discrepancies which should be reported"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Countries should consider recording discrepancy reports (e.g., in register excerpts) so users know data may be unreliable, which can help competent authorities, and may monitor the number of reports and reasons. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider monitoring the number of discrepancy reports and the reasons for them"</sub>

## 8. UP-TO-DATE BASIC AND BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 71) states the INR.24 requires mechanisms so basic and BO information is as current as possible and updated within a reasonable period (e.g., within one month) after any change or identification of outdated information. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is updated within a reasonable period (e.g., within one month)43"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 43: the one-month update period is intended as an indicative timeline. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is intended to serve as an indicative timeline"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should establish a clear, practical framework specifying what 'a reasonable period' means and how it will be enforced for each approach used to keep BO information up to date. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The framework should specify the meaning of “a reasonable period” and how the framework will be enforced"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 72) says the Standards give countries flexibility to set the reasonable period according to risk, context, institutional setup and domestic circumstances. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Standards provide a degree of flexibility to enable countries to determine the appropriate “reasonable period”"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF (para 73) notes practical hurdles may warrant more time, e.g., force majeure, records unavailable due to IT failures, or the BO not responding to reminders or no longer reachable at the known address. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where the BO fails to respond to reminders or can no longer be reached at the known address"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF footnote 45 defines force majeure as acts, events or circumstances beyond the parties' control, e.g., natural disasters, war or death of a beneficial owner. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Force majeure is defined as acts, events or circumstances beyond the control of the parties"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 74) says countries should consider identifying such hurdles for each mechanism and, where relevant, specify a period as short as practicable with justifiable mitigating measures (e.g., legislative reform, technology). (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "specifying a period which is as short as practicable"</sub>

## Discrepancy Reporting Mechanisms

- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider implementing a system to review and adjudicate discrepancy reports in a fair and efficient manner, with emphasis on due process and a risk-based approach. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider implementing a system to review and adjudicate discrepancy reports in a fair and efficient manner"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Companies and other legal entities should be informed at the appropriate time of the discrepancies reported about them, with reasons, so that the data can be rectified in a timely manner. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Companies and other legal entities should be informed at the appropriate time of the reported discrepancies (with reasons)"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Upon resolving a difference, countries should consider notifying the reporting entity so that all information is aligned. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should consider notifying the reporting entity so that all information would be aligned"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider properly recording discrepancy reports made, so that potential users of the information are aware the data might not be adequate, accurate or up-to-date. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider properly recording discrepancy reports made"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: From a broader management perspective, countries may consider monitoring the number of discrepancy reports and the reasons for them. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider monitoring the number of discrepancy reports and the reasons for them"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When implementing discrepancy reporting mechanisms, countries should take into account data privacy laws, client confidentiality and other relevant concerns, and safeguards should be made to prevent data leakage. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should take into account data privacy laws, client confidentiality, and other relevant concerns"</sub>

## 8. UP-TO-DATE BASIC AND BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF (para 74) says countries should consider identifying practical hurdles and specific situations that may hinder beneficial ownership information from being kept up-to-date for each mechanism they use. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider identifying these practical hurdles and/or specific situations"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF (para 74) says that, where relevant, countries should also consider specifying a period which is as short as practicable, with appropriate and justifiable measures that adequately mitigate or overcome these hurdles. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should also consider specifying a period which is as short as practicable"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 75) describes as a best practice that countries may consider requiring companies, and the other mechanisms used to give competent authorities timely access to information, to periodically validate their beneficial ownership information on a risk-based approach, for example by reviewing or verifying what they hold. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to periodically validate their beneficial ownership information on a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 75) says regular validation could help uncover changes in the beneficial ownership of corporate structures and would be useful if a company inadvertently fails to identify and report such changes. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such regular validation could contribute to uncovering changes in the beneficial ownership of corporate structures"</sub>

## 9. OBLIGATIONS OF COMPANIES UNDER THE COMPANY APPROACH

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 76, citing INR.24 paragraph 7(a)) says under the company approach countries should require companies to obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on their own beneficial ownership. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To obtain and hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on the company’s own beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Companies should be required to co-operate fully with competent authorities in determining the beneficial owner, including making information available in a timely manner. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To co-operate with competent authorities to the fullest extent possible in determining the beneficial owner"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Companies should be required to co-operate with FIs/DNFBPs by providing adequate, accurate and up-to-date BO information. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To co-operate with financial institutions/DNFBPs to provide adequate, accurate and up-to-date information"</sub>
- **expected / limitation**: FATF (para 77, citing INR.24 paragraph 4) says companies should keep a shareholder/member list accessible to authorities, but this alone is insufficient since it shows legal, not necessarily beneficial, ownership. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shareholder registers contain information on legal ownership but not necessarily on beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Company approach considerations: procedures ensuring company-held BO information is adequate, accurate, up to date and timely accessible, and whether companies have powers to require updated information, including BO information, from shareholders at any time. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Do companies have powers to require updated information from their shareholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further considerations: whether shareholders must disclose nominators; whether shareholders and BOs must notify the company of changes within a set period (e.g., within one month); whether companies must periodically validate BO information; and whether BOs face penalties for not providing information. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Are shareholders required to disclose the names of person(s) on whose behalf shares are held (i.e., nominators)?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further consideration: whether effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions apply to the company and its representatives for failing to collect and maintain BO information. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sanctions against the company and its representatives for failing to carry out their obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further consideration: whether authorities can compel company co-operation with sanctions for non-co-operation, whether BO information is accessible in the country of incorporation, and how companies without physical presence are handled (e.g., a local-resident natural person director with access to BO information). (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How are companies that have no physical presence in the country of incorporation dealt with?52"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further consideration: whether authorities have given FIs/DNFBPs clear guidance on what to do if companies do not co-operate, e.g., reporting non-co-operation or not engaging or continuing the business relationship. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reporting the non-cooperation to the relevant authorities, not engage with or continue the business relationship"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further consideration: how companies learn their obligations, whether adequate public guidance exists, and whether there are avenues to engage and educate companies. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Is there adequate guidance explaining their obligations, and is this guidance publicly available?"</sub>

## 10. THE REGISTRY APPROACH

- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 78, citing INR.24 paragraph 7(b)(i)) notes one way to ensure access to BO information is for a public authority or body to hold it, e.g., a tax authority, FIU, companies registry or BO registry. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for example a tax authority, FIU, companies registry, or beneficial ownership registry"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INR.24 footnote 11 (cited in FATF footnote 53): a body may record BO information alongside other data, the source may be multiple registries (by province, sector or legal person type such as NPOs), or a private body entrusted by a public authority. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or of a private body entrusted with this task by the public authority"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FATF (para 79) says a BO register can be effective as it gives competent authorities rapid, efficient (often real-time) access from a direct source, generally conditional on the registrar having sufficient resources and ability to request more information when in doubt. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allows competent authorities to access such information from a direct source in a rapid and efficient manner (often in real time)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 80) says countries have flexibility in register design; decentralised or federal countries may have several interconnected registers, which require consistent approaches and interoperability for seamless competent-authority access. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important to ensure consistency of approaches and interoperability of the systems"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure registers cover all relevant legal persons, including relevant foreign legal persons, rather than a particular group; the onus is on countries to ensure efficient access to reliable information. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that their registers have a scope sufficiently wide to cover all relevant legal persons"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FATF (para 81) says most register implementation challenges are institutional: whether the registry is set up and empowered to collect adequate, accurate, up-to-date information, whether sanctions are applied, and whether it has sufficient resources. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most of the challenges in implementing beneficial ownership registers originate from the institutional level"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 82) notes some countries integrate BO into existing company registers, while others create BO registries populated by legal persons, FIs/DNFBPs, competent authorities or automated reports from interconnected registries, which authorities (and sometimes obliged parties) can cross-check. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "populated by legal persons, FIs/DNFBPs, competent authorities or to some extent by automated reports via interconnected registries"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 83) notes in some countries the notary profession keeps a centralised BO database from incorporations and other acts, creating a repository that may validate company registry information. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the notary profession keeps a centralised database on the beneficial ownership of legal persons"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 84) notes tax authorities in some countries hold BO information on legal persons with income, ownership, real estate transactions or employees, and some require all legal persons filing with tax authorities to have a bank account subject to bank CDD; such databases should be adequate, accurate and up to date. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require that all legal persons making disclosures to the tax authorities have a bank account"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 85) says countries using a public authority should consider the resources and expertise needed for maintenance, verification and rapid access; without sufficient resources effectiveness will be compromised. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Without sufficient resources, the effectiveness of the public authority or body holding beneficial ownership information will also be compromised"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: FATF (para 87) says R.24 allows countries to consider public access to BO information, balancing public interest in preventing ML/TF against beneficial owners' fundamental rights such as data protection, possibly via tiered disclosure. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 24 allows countries to consider facilitating public access to beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries with public access may clearly and exhaustively define public fields, of a more general nature (e.g., name and reason for BO status, company name and registered address), to minimise prejudice to beneficial owners. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as name and reason why the person is identified as beneficial owner, company name and registered address"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Registry consideration (para 88a): jurisdictional or constitutional impediments, e.g., where state/provincial authorities register legal persons and the constitution limits national authority to impose BO requirements. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there are constitutional impediments that limit the national authorities’ jurisdiction to impose beneficial ownership requirements"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF (para 85) says countries using a public authority or body to hold beneficial ownership information should consider the resources and expertise needed to maintain the register and keep its information adequate, accurate and up-to-date and rapidly accessible. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider the resources and expertise that will be required in order to maintain the register"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry considerations: whether statutory powers cover collecting, verifying and maintaining BO information, and whether the body has enough human and capital resources, including corporate-law expertise and understanding of economic context to detect control through other means. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Are the registry’s statutory powers and objectives sufficiently broad to cover the role of collecting, verifying and maintaining"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Some registries run thematic/horizontal analyses of the legal persons they hold to detect outliers or incorrect information, and countries may consider whether the register's technical design enables such analysis. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "run thematic/horizontal analyses of the legal persons for which it holds beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry consideration: whether applicants must submit accurate BO information at creation (e.g., incorporation prohibited without it), whether there is risk-based post-incorporation monitoring, and whether the registry verifies using reliable independent sources. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by prohibiting incorporation if such information is not provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry consideration: whether authorities, FIs and DNFBPs can report discrepancies and whether reasonable steps remedy them in an appropriate timeframe. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Is there a process that allows authorities and FIs and DNFBPs to report any discrepancies to the registry?54"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: FATF footnote 54: consistent data standards and BO definitions between registers and reporting entities help common understanding, inconsistency creates burden and reporting unrelated to ML/TF risk, and managing tipping-off risk and feedback are important. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consistent data standards and definitions of BO between central registers and reporting entities may help develop common understanding"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry consideration: how BO changes are monitored and recorded over time, whether changes and corrections must be filed within a reasonable timeframe, and whether there are periodic confirmation requirements. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How are changes in beneficial ownership information monitored and recorded over time?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry consideration: whether a competent authority enforces reporting and whether sanctions cover all relevant breaches (non-disclosure, inaccurate or incomplete information) and the appropriate persons (INR.24 paragraph 16). (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Is there a competent authority with responsibility for enforcing the requirement to report to the register?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry consideration: whether competent authorities get rapid, efficient access, whether the register is publicised, and whether search serves their needs (by legal person and BO name, disambiguation of same names, aggregation tools). (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it allows searches by the name of the legal person and the name of the beneficial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Registry consideration: whether access is direct (e.g., remote login) or by request, whether request-based access remains rapid and efficient, and whether disclosure rules protect against improper disclosure. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Does the register provide direct access through remote login or similar mechanisms"</sub>

## Example features – Public authority or body holding beneficial ownership information

- **descriptive / method**: Example register features (para 89): companies provide basic and BO information on registration, and regularly and within a reasonable period (e.g., within one month) after any change. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Companies are required to provide basic and beneficial ownership information to the company register upon registration"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: companies make a declaration (e.g., sworn statement) on the beneficial owner and ownership structure, possibly with copies of identity documents. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Companies are required to make a declaration (e.g., sworn statement) regarding the beneficial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: the register body must, on a risk basis, verify BO identity and that they meet the BO criteria. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to verify the identity of the beneficial owners and that they indeed satisfy the criteria"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: companies failing to provide BO information face proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, such as restrictions on incorporation, which are applied. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as restrictions on incorporation, and such sanctions are applied"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: incorrect information attracts proportionate and dissuasive administrative and/or criminal sanctions for the company, and its representative could be personally liable; the register body regularly applies sanctions or reports breaches. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The company’s representative could also be held personally liable"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: the register body proactively checks information against other sources (shareholder, population or national ID registries) using risk-based verification and technology to find anomalies and reduce document fraud or improper disclosure. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "checking information against other sources (such as shareholder, population or national identity registries)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example features: BO information is recorded digitally and searchable by multiple fields, and competent authorities have rapid, efficient online access with full search capability. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is recorded digitally and is searchable. The search function supports searches by multiple fields"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: the register body can identify indicators of misuse or unusual activity (red flags) within the database. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has the capability to identify indicators of misuse or unusual activity (red flags) in the database"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: basic company information is public, and some or all BO information could be public or at minimum available to FIs and DNFBPs. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Basic information on the company is publicly available"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: FIs, DNFBPs and, if appropriate, competent authorities report discrepancies, and the register body or other authority corrects the information within a reasonable timeframe. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "takes appropriate actions to correct the information within a reasonable timeframe"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example features: the register body may also hold shareholder information and collects information on the board, senior management and anyone authorised to act for the company. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collects information on the board of directors, senior management and any other natural person authorised to act"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: the register mechanism is complemented by other approaches so authorities can determine BO in a timely manner (INR.24 paragraph 7(a) and 7(c)). (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The mechanism is complemented by other approaches to ensure that the beneficial ownership information of a company can be determined"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Example feature: data protection and privacy safeguards, including restricting what different register users can see, to prevent improper disclosure. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data protection and privacy safeguards are in place, including restrictions on the information available to the different users"</sub>
