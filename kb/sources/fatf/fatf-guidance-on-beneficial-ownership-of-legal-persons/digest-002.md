## Companies

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 29) says companies should be required to obtain and record basic information: name, proof of incorporation, legal form and status, registered office address, basic regulating powers, list of directors and a unique identifier such as a TIN where it exists. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Companies should be required to obtain and record basic information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Companies should also keep a register of shareholders or members with names, number of shares held by each, and share categories including the nature of associated voting rights. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a register of their shareholders or members, containing the names of the shareholders and members and number of shares held"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 30) says this information can be recorded by the company or a third person under its responsibility, and should be kept in the country at the registered office or another location notified to the registry. (p. 15)[^original]

## Company registries

## 4. BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **mandatory / requirement**: Recommendation 24 requires countries to ensure adequate, accurate and up-to-date information is available on the beneficial ownership and control of legal persons (INR.24 paragraph 1). (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 24 requires countries to ensure that there is adequate, accurate and up-to-date information available on the beneficial ownership"</sub>

## Box 2. Definition of “beneficial owner”

- **descriptive / definition**: FATF Glossary: a beneficial owner is the natural person(s) who ultimately owns or controls a customer and/or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, including those exercising ultimate effective control over a legal person or arrangement. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Beneficial owner refers to the natural person(s) who ultimately70 owns or controls a customer71"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary note 70: 'ultimately owns or controls' and 'ultimate effective control' refer to ownership or control exercised through a chain of ownership or by means other than direct control. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refer to situations in which ownership/control is exercised through a chain of ownership or by means of control other than direct control"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Glossary note 71: the beneficial owner definition also applies to the beneficial owner of a beneficiary under a life or other investment-linked insurance policy. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "beneficial owner of a beneficiary under a life or other investment linked insurance policy"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Glossary note 72 / R.10 cascade: where there is doubt whether a controlling-interest holder is the ultimate beneficial owner, or no natural person controls through ownership, identify natural persons controlling through other means; if none, identify and record the senior managing official. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the natural person who holds the position of senior managing official should be identified and recorded as holding this position"</sub>

## 4. BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 32) defines beneficial ownership information of legal persons as information on the identities and extent of control of natural persons who ultimately have a controlling ownership interest (if any) and natural persons exercising ultimate effective control through other means (if any). (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The natural person(s) exercising ultimate effective control over the legal person through other means than ownership interests"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 27: ownership interests can be so diversified that no natural person, alone or together, controls the legal person through legal ownership. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ownership interests can be so diversified that there are no natural persons, whether acting alone or together"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 33) says where no beneficial owner can be identified, reporting entities identify and record the senior managing official per R.10; this does not change the beneficial owner definition but governs CDD, and should apply across all collection approaches in the multi-pronged approach. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not amend or supersede the definition of who the beneficial owner is but sets out how CDD should be conducted"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 34, citing INR.10 para 5(b)(i) and INR.24 para 9) says reasonable measures should verify both identity and beneficial owner status, with reasonableness determined on a risk-based approach. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reasonable measures should be taken to verify the identity(ies) of such persons, and their status as a beneficial owner"</sub>

## Box 2. Definition of beneficial owner

- **descriptive / definition**: Under the definition of beneficial owner, only a natural person can qualify as an ultimate beneficial owner, and a given legal person or arrangement can have more than one natural person as its ultimate beneficial owner. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only a natural person can be an ultimate beneficial owner"</sub>

## Distinction between legal ownership and beneficial ownership

- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 35) states legal ownership and beneficial ownership are separate concepts; legal title or controlling shareholding may be held by an individual or legal person other than the beneficial owner who ultimately controls the entity. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Legal ownership and beneficial ownership over a legal person are two separate concepts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Individuals exercising ultimate control over a legal person should be identified as beneficial owners regardless of whether their shareholding exceeds any minimum ownership threshold. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regardless of whether they own shares above any specified minimum ownership threshold"</sub>

## Beneficial ownership through ownership interests

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 37) says countries may use an ownership threshold (direct or indirect percentage of shares) to determine beneficial ownership, but it should not exceed a maximum of 25%; combined interests of shareholders acting together may be considered. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such a threshold should not exceed a maximum of 25%"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 38) lists considerations for setting the threshold: ML/TF risk of the legal person type, sector of operation and complexity of ownership/control structure; a case-by-case risk assessment for each threshold application is not needed. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A risk assessment would not be needed for each application of a threshold on a case-by-case basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Higher ML/TF risk could signal a need for a lower ownership threshold; low thresholds help avoid loopholes by capturing more information on owners and controllers, while increasing identification effort. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A higher ML/TF risk could signal the need for a lower ownership threshold"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 39, citing INR.24 para 2(d-e)) says threshold risk factors should come from a risk assessment of legal persons; they vary with the legal framework, AML/CFT enforcement quality, supervision quality of FIs and DNFBPs (especially TCSPs), and sectoral and geographic risks. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the quality of supervision of FIs and DNFBPs (especially, TCSPs), and sectoral and geographical risks"</sub>

## Distinction between legal ownership and beneficial ownership

- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF (para 36) says countries should consider the various types of ownership interest and ways to exercise control over a legal person in their jurisdiction under commercial and administrative law, including voting rights, economic rights, and convertible stock or debt convertible into voting equity; they should also consider how ownership interests and ways of exercising control have evolved in practice. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including voting rights, economic rights, convertible stock or outstanding debt that is convertible into voting equity"</sub>

## Beneficial ownership through ownership interests

- **permissive / context**: Risk assessment results may lead countries to apply different thresholds across economic sectors or legal person types. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may lead countries to apply different thresholds e.g., across different sectors of the economy"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Threshold consideration (para 40a): known trends of opaque structures using nominee directors and/or shareholders, including foreign corporate shareholders, especially within longer chains of opaque entities. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Known trends of opaque ownership/control structures: e.g., through use of nominee directors and/or nominee shareholders30"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Threshold consideration: complex multi-jurisdictional structures (corporate shareholders, assets and nominees split across jurisdictions) can be high risk as they 'slip through the cracks' of national regulation and enforcement, sometimes deliberately to evade rules and obscure the beneficial owner. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can be high risk because they can ‘slip through the cracks’ of national-level regulation, supervision, and law enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: FATF footnote 31 example of a multi-jurisdictional structure: a company domiciled in one jurisdiction, formed by a TCSP in a second, with a nominee director from a third and a corporate legal owner created in a fourth. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "formed by a TCSP in a second jurisdiction, has a nominee director from a third jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Threshold consideration: sectoral risk concentrated where predicate offences are prevalent, e.g., extractives (oil and gas), public procurement, real estate and wildlife trade. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "extractive activities (such as oil and gas), public procurement, real estate, and wildlife trade"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Threshold consideration: known misuse of pass-through entities, operational or non-operational (domiciliary) companies, and shell companies lacking independent operations, significant assets, ongoing activity or employees (citing FATF-Egmont 2018, Concealment of Beneficial Ownership). (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "shell companies with no independent operations, significant assets, ongoing business activities, or employees"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF footnote 32 defines pass-through entities as entities passing profits of legal persons to owners or beneficiaries, acting as fund-flow enablers with no other real purpose (their business income is treated as owners' personal income). (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "acting as fund-flow enablers but not having any other real purpose"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Further threshold considerations: entities owned or controlled by domestic or foreign PEPs, entities opened and closed within short periods, and entities used to access public tenders or obtain interests in public assets. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Known risks of entities subject to opening and closure within short periods of time"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: FATF (para 41) notes ownership may be direct or indirect (via chains of corporate vehicles or nominee shareholders) and several people can be beneficial owners; e.g., with a 20% threshold, five individuals each owning 20% would all be beneficial owners. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "five individuals each owning 20% of shares would all be considered beneficial owners"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF (para 42) notes that with a 20% threshold a company with as few as six shareholders may have no one above the threshold, so no shareholder would be reportable based on the ownership threshold alone. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a company with as few as six shareholders can have no shareholder owning a share above the minimum threshold"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 44) says countries should prioritise clarity and practicality in applying the ownership-interest concept, with rules workable and enforceable for companies and other legal persons. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should prioritise clarity and practicality in the implementation of the concept of ownership interest"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 45) states an ownership threshold is not the only way to determine beneficial ownership, as the FATF definition covers both ownership and control. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The application of an ownership threshold is not the only way that beneficial ownership should be determined"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Differential voting rights through share classes can give a minority shareholder well below the threshold control; recent reforms in the UK, Singapore and Hong Kong, China have made dual class shares more common. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recent reforms in some jurisdictions (UK; Singapore; Hong Kong, China) have made such dual class share arrangements more common"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Control may arise from the power to appoint the majority of senior management, directly or indirectly (e.g., via a wholly owned company), but a minority shareholder's or stakeholder's right to appoint one representative does not by itself confer control. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the right of minority shareholders or certain stakeholders to appoint one representative to senior management does not by itself confer control"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Control can be exercised through debt instruments or financing, e.g., a lender controlling via loan terms such as debt convertible into voting equity, or a third party influencing a shareholder through a financial relationship; however a bank financing a legal person rarely controls it by that act alone. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a bank providing financing to a legal person will rarely be considered as exercising control over the legal person by the act per se"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Natural persons with substantial control who are responsible for strategic decisions fundamentally affecting the legal person's business practices or direction may in some circumstances be beneficial owners; directors may or may not actively control depending on the entity and law. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "directors may or may not take an active role in exercising control over the affairs of the entity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: FATF (para 43) notes shareholders may control a company on the basis of their ownership alone or together with other shareholders, including through any contract, understanding, relationship, intermediary or tiered entity; shareholders may also collaborate to increase their control through formal or informal agreements or nominee shareholders, and control structures may be identified from relevant company documents. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Shareholders may collaborate to increase the level of control through formal or informal agreements"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Control may also be informal, e.g., through close personal connections to relatives or associates; an individual using, enjoying or benefiting from the legal person's assets could warrant further investigation if able to exercise control. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "control over a legal person may be exercised through informal means, such as through close personal connections to relatives or associates"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Informal-control cases are harder to detect and in practice less relevant to routine collection of beneficial ownership information by a registry or agency. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these cases are harder to detect and will in practice be less relevant with routine collection"</sub>

## Beneficial ownership in respect of different types of legal persons

- **descriptive / limitation**: Companies without shares (e.g., US non-stock companies, British companies limited by guarantee) are not captured by share-based thresholds; they are common among not-for-profits but also used for profit, and companies may switch between stock and non-stock. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Beneficial owners of companies that do not issue shares will not be captured by any ownership threshold expressed in share ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: TCSPs have in some cases marketed companies without shares as a way to defeat beneficial ownership disclosure and Controlled Foreign Company tax rules; such entities need a control-based approach to determine beneficial ownership. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "companies without shares have been marketed by TCSPs as a means to defeat beneficial ownership disclosure and tax rules"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Limited and unlimited liability partnerships have legal personality distinct from partners but in some countries have no shares; in some (e.g., British Virgin Islands) founders can choose whether the partnership has legal personality. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those forming a partnership can choose whether or not the partnership will have legal personality"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Absent a share threshold, a person controls a partnership if they have the right to exercise, or actually exercise, significant influence over its activities, e.g., appointing or removing partners, directing or vetoing investment decisions, profit share or capital returns, amending the partnership agreement, or dissolving or converting it. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the right to appoint or remove any partner, to direct or veto the investment decisions, profit share or capital returns"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Foundations and other non-company legal persons without shares are not captured by share thresholds and need a control-based approach; foundations have no owners and are controlled by a board. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "foundations have no owners and are controlled by a board"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Where foundations resemble trusts, founders, beneficiaries and management members may be considered to control them; anyone with the right to or actual exercise of significant influence (e.g., appointing/removing board members, directing or vetoing distributions or investments, winding up or converting) can also have control. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to direct or veto the distribution of the foundation funds or assets or its investment decisions, to wind up or convert the foundation"</sub>

## 5. A MULTI-PRONGED APPROACH TO BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **descriptive / finding or result**: FATF (para 46) reports mutual evaluation experience suggests a multi-pronged approach using several information sources is more effective than a single approach, as sources supplement each other, improve quality and help mitigate individual-source accuracy problems (possibly via technology). (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a multipronged approach using several sources of information is more effective than using a single approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 47) says the core of the multi-pronged approach combines company-held information with information held by or for public authorities in a registry (or an alternative mechanism ensuring rapid and efficient access), plus additional measures as necessary. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The core of a multi-pronged approach combines information held and/or supplied by companies themselves"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The three pillars of adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information are equally important and none should be prioritised at the expense of another; responsibilities of the various parties should be clear. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The three pillars of ensuring that beneficial ownership information is adequate, accurate and up-to-date are equally important"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 48) states that since the March 2022 revision of R.24 the standards require at minimum (1) a company approach and (2) a registry or alternative mechanism, plus (3) supplementary sources as necessary commensurate with risk. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including as a minimum (1) a company approach and (2) a registry or alternative mechanism"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: CDD information held by FIs/DNFBPs under R.10 and R.22 may serve as an alternative mechanism or supplementary measure, and supplementary information could include data held by regulators or stock exchanges; countries may decide precise measures based on context, materiality and risk. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supplementary information could also include basic and beneficial ownership information held by regulators or stock exchanges"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 50, citing Recommendation 31) says countries should ensure adequate powers to compel financial records and obtain evidence in investigations, so authorities can determine in a timely manner whether a company has or controls accounts with an FI in the country. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether a company has or controls accounts with a financial institution within the country"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 49) says countries could consider enabling competent authorities that collect beneficial ownership information to access and exchange it. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries could consider enabling competent authorities involved in collecting beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF (para 49) says countries are encouraged to extend this access and exchange of information to other sources of beneficial ownership information, to further strengthen cross-checking and verification. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries are also encouraged to extend this access and exchange of information to other sources of beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 49) says countries may consider extending these responsibilities beyond identifying errors in and improving the quality of basic and beneficial ownership information, so that the information also helps inform the national understanding of current and emerging risks. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may consider extending these responsibilities further beyond simply identifying errors in and improving the quality"</sub>

## 6. ADEQUATE BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 51) states the INR.24 requires countries to have mechanisms ensuring basic and beneficial ownership information is adequate, including information given to the registry and information referred to in paragraph 7 of INR.24. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires countries to have mechanisms in place that ensure that basic information and beneficial ownership information is adequate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF Box 3: adequate information is information sufficient to identify the natural person(s) who are beneficial owners and the means and mechanisms through ownership, control or other means. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Adequate information is information that is sufficient to identify the natural person(s) who are the beneficial owner(s)"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: FATF (para 52) distinguishes data necessary to identify a beneficial owner (first and last name, nationality(ies), date of birth) from data useful to confirm identity (unique national ID number such as tax, identity or social security number; passport number and document type; place of birth; residential address; TIN or equivalent in country of residence). (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some information may be considered as necessary in order to identify a person (e.g., first and last name, nationality(ies) and date of birth)"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: FATF (para 53) says data necessary to identify the means and mechanisms of beneficial ownership include type of participation, voting rights or other control, and the scope (e.g., percentage of shares or voting rights). (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "type of participation, voting rights or control through other means) and the scope"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Useful confirming data include multiple forms of beneficial ownership, legal intermediaries or entities in the chain, involvement in any nominee relationship, and whether the interest is held directly or indirectly. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on whether the beneficial owner’s interest in the legal person is held directly or indirectly"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 54) says for foundations and comparable legal persons countries may consider recording the natural person's position or role (founder, beneficiary, management member). (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider recording the relevant position/role (e.g., founder, beneficiary and member of the management)"</sub>

## 7. ACCURATE INFORMATION – MEANS OF VERIFICATION OF BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION

- **expected / definition**: FATF (para 55, citing INR.24 para 9) says identified beneficial ownership information should be verified; verification is a combination of checks and processes at various stages to ensure data accuracy, and applies to all prongs of the multi-pronged approach. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Verification is a combination of checks and other processes that a country should adopt at the various stages"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Verification may require professional expertise; the aim is that a country's overall mechanisms ensure accuracy and a degree of consistency across sources. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide a degree in consistency of information across different sources"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 56) says verification may involve reviewing share certificates, shareholder registers, board resolutions and powers of attorney, and risk-dependent manual or automated cross-checks with population/national ID, taxpayer, vehicle and land registries. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "share certificates, shareholder register, board meeting resolutions, and power of attorney documents"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 57) says each approach chosen within the multi-pronged approach should include verification: by companies (company approach), by the register authority (registry approach), by entities under the alternative mechanism, and by entities in supplementary measures (regulators, stock exchanges, FIs/DNFBPs under R.10/22). (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each approach that countries choose as part of the multi-pronged approach should include verification"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 58) says criteria for identifying beneficial owners across prongs should be consistent with applicable requirements, particularly R.10/R.22 CDD, and verification may comprise identity verification and status verification. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the criteria for identifying a beneficial owner applied to the different prongs are consistent with relevant applicable requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 59) says verification aims to reduce inaccuracy risk and enable enforcement of reporting rules, but does not imply zero failure; it should systematically catch obvious errors, falsehoods or inconsistencies, and automated checks may reduce burden and improve timeliness. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obvious errors, falsehoods or inconsistencies are spotted and corrected in a systematic manner"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 60) says countries should adopt a risk-based approach to verification, enhancing extent and/or frequency in higher-risk cases: complex multi-jurisdiction structures, nominee directors or shareholders, entities assessed high-risk, a history of inaccurate reporting, or insufficient documentation. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should adopt a risk-based approach to verification"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: For lower risk, e.g., a micro company whose legal owner, director and beneficial owner are the same person, countries may adjust verification (e.g., only verify identity). (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as a micro company whose legal owner, director and beneficial owner are all the same person"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: FATF (para 61) says enhanced verification can detect inaccuracies or deliberate concealment such as undisclosed nominee relationships, may be done by law enforcement, and where intermediaries are mandatory for formation, regulated intermediaries may be required to perform enhanced checks. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regulated professional intermediaries may be required to perform such enhanced checks"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: FATF (para 62) says countries may require a declaration that submitted information is truthful and complete; this places the primary burden on the submitter but should not replace verification by the recipient. (p. 26)[^original]
