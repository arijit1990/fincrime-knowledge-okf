## Issues relating to the legal profession

- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 161) notes the legal profession often acts as trustees, nominees or both, so legal professional privilege issues arise when lawyers have AML/CFT obligations; a client's right to candid legal advice without fear of prejudicial disclosure is an important feature of the profession. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "practical issues relating to legal professional privilege can arise when lawyers have AML/CFT obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 95: this right is part of access to justice under the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and FATF Recommendations exclude privileged or professionally secret information from STR filing obligations, leaving each country to define those terms. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exclude information covered by legal professional privilege or professional secrecy from the obligation to file a suspicious transaction report"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (paras 162-163) notes privilege and secrecy scope often stems from constitutional or common law and treaty-based fundamental rights, depends on each country's (and in federal systems each state's or province's) framework, and may vary by type of legal professional and service. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also vary across different types of legal professionals within a country and the types of services being offered"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: FATF (para 164, citing World Bank/UNODC StAR 2011, p.94) reports investigators find claims of legal professional privilege and secrecy are a frequent obstacle to accessing ownership and control information on corporate vehicles. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "investigators have found that a frequent obstacle to accessing information about corporate vehicles is the use of legal professional privilege"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FATF (para 165) says such claims are appropriate when made correctly under law, but case studies show occasional extremely wide privilege claims exceeding the generally understood protections in the country. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some of the case studies show that occasionally extremely wide claims of privilege are made"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities and professional bodies should work toward a clear, shared understanding of the scope of legal professional privilege and secrecy in their country. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "competent authorities and professional bodies should work to ensure that there is a clear and shared understanding"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 166) says countries should ensure clarity on what privilege does and does not cover so investigations of suspected corporate vehicles are not inappropriately impeded. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "investigations involving suspected corporate vehicles are not inappropriately impeded"</sub>

## Issues relating to fit and proper tests for FIs/DNFBPs/VASPs

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 167) says licensing authorities and/or supervisors should prevent criminals or associates from holding or beneficially owning significant or controlling interests, or holding management functions, in FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "criminals or their associates are prevented from holding or being the beneficial owner of a significant or controlling interest"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Fit and proper tests should be applied at licensing/registration and on an ongoing basis, including when ownership or control structure changes. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which should be carried out both at the licensing/registration stage and on an ongoing basis thereafter"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs should provide adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on their beneficial owners to relevant authorities, including at licensing or registration. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs should provide adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on their beneficial owners to relevant authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Supervisory measures to enforce fit and proper tests may include suspending voting rights, requiring sale of shares, and other actions against shareholders who are not fit and proper or refuse to provide information on request. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "suspension of voting rights, requesting the selling of shares, and other actions against shareholders that are not fit and proper"</sub>

## 19. INTERNATIONAL CO-OPERATION

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: FATF (para 168) notes corporate networks set up illicitly to hide the origin of criminal proceeds are often multi-jurisdictional. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Corporate networks set up illicitly to hide the origin of proceeds of crime are often multi-jurisdictional"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Effective international co-operation under FATF Recommendations 37 and 40 requires access, through full co-operation of jurisdictional authorities, to accurate beneficial owner information in international ML/TF investigations. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective international cooperation, as set out in FATF Recommendations 37 and 40, requires access"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries where legal persons are established should be able to obtain basic and BO information, even on beneficial owners residing abroad, and countries where beneficial owners reside need to respond to requests to identify beneficial ownership. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be able to obtain basic and beneficial ownership information (even on those beneficial owners residing abroad)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.24 requires facilitating foreign competent authority access to basic company registry information, e.g., online or via an efficient request mechanism. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitating access by foreign competent authorities to basic information held by company registries"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.24 requires exchanging shareholder information (including that held by the company or stock exchange) so foreign authorities can quickly move along a chain of legal ownership. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exchanging information on shareholders (including when it is held by the company or stock exchange)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.24 requires using competent authorities' powers to obtain BO information on behalf of foreign counterparts, not only in their own investigations. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using their competent authorities’ powers to obtain beneficial ownership information on behalf of foreign counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 169) says exchange with foreign counterparts should avoid unduly restrictive conditions, subject to international standards, which may include refusing requests on fiscal (including tax) or bank secrecy grounds. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should avoid unduly restrictive conditions for accessing information, subject to internationally agreed standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF footnote 99: tax-related requests should follow international tax information agreements, and tax authorities declining assistance where those standards-aligned conditions are not met, or where declining is allowed, should not be judged as imposing unduly restrictive conditions. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "tax-related requests should be made in accordance with international tax information agreements between jurisdictions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 170) says points of contact, agency or registry information and access/request procedures should be made publicly available, e.g., online or through posted guidance. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be made publicly available (e.g., online) or through specific posted guidance on procedures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 171) says countries should have mechanisms to identify and describe the types, forms and basic features of their legal persons; basic and/or BO information in registries or alternative mechanisms may be publicly available online. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "basic and/or beneficial ownership information held by various registries or alternative mechanism may be publicly available"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 172) says LEAs and other authorities should expect most basic information to be public, and countries should publicise how to make formal requests for more (e.g., mutual legal assistance). (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement and other competent authorities should expect to find most of the basic information publicly available"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 173) says countries should publish contact information for receiving and responding to requests, designate agencies (e.g., ministries or registry agencies) to process foreign BO requests, and give clear guidance on process, requirements and restrictions. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should make contact information for receiving and responding to requests publicly available"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries need an adequate internal inter-agency procedure for processing foreign requests, with a defined, reasonable response time transparently reflected in it. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries need to have in place an adequate internal procedure for interagency cooperation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 174) says countries should monitor the quality of assistance they receive from other countries. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should monitor the quality of the assistance which they receive from other countries"</sub>

## BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP OF LEGAL PERSONS

- **descriptive / context**: The March 2023 guidance aims to help countries implement R.24, including ensuring competent authority access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on true company owners and assessing and mitigating ML/TF risks of foreign companies; it followed several months of consultation with external stakeholders and the private sector. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also assess and mitigate the money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with foreign companies"</sub>
