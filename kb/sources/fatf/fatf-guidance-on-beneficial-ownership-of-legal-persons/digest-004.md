## 11. MECHANISMS AND SOURCES FOR OBTAINING BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP INFORMATION OF LEGAL PERSONS: CHARACTERISTICS OF ALTERNATIVE MECHANISMS

- **permissive / requirement**: FATF (para 90, citing INR.24 paragraph 7(b)(ii)) says countries may use an alternative mechanism instead of a BO registry within the multi-pronged approach, provided it gives competent authorities efficient access to adequate, accurate and up-to-date BO information. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by using an alternative mechanism instead of a beneficial ownership registry if the mechanism also provides competent authorities with efficient access"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: 'Efficient' access means rapid and reliable access through a trusted source without undue delay, enabling LEAs and the FIU to conduct investigations and analyses quickly. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Efficient in this context refers to access that is rapid and reliable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 91) says countries may build alternative mechanisms on existing sources in line with their risks and context, but the onus is on them to ensure a specific, clear and comprehensive process or system giving competent authorities access to reliable BO information. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The onus is on the countries to ensure that there is a specific mechanism(s) that provides efficient access to reliable information"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Alternative mechanism design consideration (legal/operational framework): a clear legal basis for authority access and source disclosure, protection of sources from liability for authorised disclosure, a requirement for timely disclosure, or else sufficient existing powers. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Is there a legal framework to protect the source of information from liability for authorised disclosures?"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Design consideration (scope): coverage of all relevant legal persons including relevant foreign ones, and domestic legal persons posing ML/TF risk that may not be clients of specific FIs or DNFBPs. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Does it cover domestic legal persons that may pose ML/TF risks but may not be clients of specific FIs or DNFBPs?"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Design considerations (resources and awareness): sufficient human and capital resources, clear guidelines so LEAs and FIUs know how to use the mechanism, and sources understanding their disclosure obligations. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Are there clear guidelines to make the competent authorities (particularly law enforcement authorities and FIUs) aware of the alternative mechanism"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Design consideration (responsiveness): rapid, efficient access via a specific process, e.g., a single point of contact where BO information is held by different entities, to avoid undue delays. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with the single point of contact or with the parties that hold beneficial ownership information to avoid undue delays"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Design consideration (quality): complete, reliable information; underlying sources performing quality control and verification; corporate-law expertise; consistency across multiple sources; and a process to resolve data differences. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If competent authorities rely on multiple sources to identify the beneficial owners, is the data consistent across sources?"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Design consideration (oversight and data protection): oversight, supervision or sanctions ensuring efficient access, and access protocols and safeguards for data protection at both holders and requesting authorities. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Do the parties that hold beneficial ownership information have appropriate access protocols and safeguards in place"</sub>
- **expected / method**: FATF (para 93) gives the example of a secure electronic portal letting competent authorities query multiple sources at once and get rapid responses; countries should implement verification and supervision so underlying data are adequate, accurate and up to date. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a secure communication electronic portal/website that enables competent authorities to reach out to multiple sources at once"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: High-quality source information plus a mechanism for efficient access may together constitute an alternative mechanism. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These two elements (high-quality source information plus mechanism for efficient access) may constitute an alternative mechanism"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: FATF (para 94) example sources for alternative mechanisms: a bank account register identifying legal persons with bank, payment and other financial accounts (e.g., custodial or investment). (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a bank account register that identifies legal persons holding bank accounts, payment accounts and other financial services"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Other example sources: a public authority holding which FIs/DNFBPs a legal person has continuous business relationships with, and a credit bureau system with updated information on legal persons borrowing from FIs. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a system with credit bureau information which collects and maintains updated information of legal persons having borrowing relationships"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF (para 95) states BO information held by FIs/DNFBPs under R.10/22 alone is not sufficient to qualify as an alternative mechanism, though it may be used to build one; emerging digital solutions may offer further possibilities. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "alone is not sufficient to qualify as an alternative mechanism"</sub>

## 12. ADDITIONAL SUPPLEMENTARY MEASURES

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 96, citing INR.24 paragraph 7(c)) says countries should use additional supplementary information as necessary, e.g., held by other regulators/supervisors, stock exchanges, FIs under R.10 and DNFBPs under R.22. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should use additional supplementary information as necessary59"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: FATF (para 97) says countries may use existing information-sharing arrangements with financial regulators and supervisors to review, challenge and check BO information and locate inaccurate or outdated data. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may rely on existing information sharing arrangements with financial regulators and/or other supervisory bodies"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: FATF (para 98) says open sources such as stock exchanges help obtain and verify information; where a listed company is subject to adequate BO transparency requirements, countries may allow that information to validate BO data. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may consider allowing the use of that information to validate the beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 99) says under R.10 countries should require FIs to identify and take reasonable measures to verify the beneficial owner so the FI is satisfied it knows who it is, and to understand the customer's ownership and control structure and business and risk profile on an ongoing basis. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such that the FI is satisfied that it knows who the beneficial owner is"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF (para 100) notes where a country relies heavily on an effectively regulated DNFBP sector involved in company formation (TCSPs, lawyers, notaries, accountants), their R.22 information can be a supplementary measure. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "involved in company formation (notably TCSPs, lawyers, notaries, and accountants)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 101) says using FI/DNFBP information as a supplementary measure requires effective monitoring and supervision of their CDD compliance, with requirements extended to professions creating and managing corporate vehicles. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is also essential to have effective monitoring and supervision of FIs and DNFBPs to ensure that they are complying with CDD requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A regulated FI/DNFBP regime would include an effectively enforced sanctioning regime for failing to update information timely, failing to supply information, and submitting inaccurate information to authorities. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "failing to update information in a timely manner, failing to supply information and submitting inaccurate information to authorities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 102) states availability of BO information from other approaches does not exempt FIs and DNFBPs from R.10 and R.22, and they should not rely exclusively on such information for CDD. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They should, in any case, not rely exclusively on such information when conducting CDD"</sub>

## Access by competent authorities

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 103) says competent authorities, particularly LEAs and FIUs, should have all powers necessary for timely access to basic and BO information held by relevant parties, though access characteristics may vary. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have all the powers necessary for obtaining timely access to basic and beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 104) says access to information held by a public body or alternative mechanism should be rapid and efficient (quick, reliable, without undue delay), and authorities should know which body holds the information and how to access it. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "access should be rapid and efficient, which means that it should be quick and reliable, without undue delay or impediment"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For company-held information, LEAs (and other authorities where allowed) should have timely access with full company co-operation, most commonly in investigations and generally via a production order or equivalent. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "generally subject to the issuance of a production order or equivalent"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Competent authorities should also know which additional supplementary measures are available for accessing basic and BO information. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "competent authorities should have sufficient knowledge of which additional supplementary measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 105) says holders should understand disclosure obligations, co-operate fully and provide information quickly, and countries should ensure a clear legal framework authorising access and protecting sources from liability for authorised disclosure. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fully cooperate with competent authorities, and provide the information as quickly as possible"</sub>

## Access in the course of public procurement

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 106) says countries should ensure national public authorities (and others as appropriate) have powers for timely access to basic and BO information on legal persons in public procurement, covering contract bidders and contract recipients. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtain timely access to basic and beneficial ownership information on legal persons in the course of public procurement"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Implementation option 1: make providing basic and BO information (directly or e.g. via registry extract) a condition of participating in public procurement, with authorities also able to use registry, alternative mechanism or supplementary sources for verification. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Make it a requirement for participation in public procurement that contract bidders and recipients provide basic and beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Implementation option 2: give procurement authorities access to bidder and recipient BO information held by a public body or registry, alternative mechanism or supplementary measures. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Provide relevant public procurement authorities with access to basic and beneficial ownership information in respect of contract bidders"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Implementation option 3: allow reliance on other publicly available BO information on bidders and recipients (e.g., a procurement contractor database), which must be adequate, accurate and up to date. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "e.g., information held in a database on procurement contractors"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 61: BO information given to procurement authorities would generally resemble what bidders give their FIs or DNFBPs, enabling timely provision and minimising burden. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would not be dissimilar to that contract bidders and recipients would provide to their FIs or DNFBPs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Whatever procurement measure is used, the legal framework should protect information sources from liability for authorised disclosures where necessary. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that the legal framework protects, where necessary, the source(s) of information from liability for authorized disclosures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Operationally, procurement authorities should know available BO sources and how to access them; scoping questions such as a contract value threshold should be decided on risk, context and materiality using existing procurement frameworks. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether the requirement should only apply to contracts over a certain value threshold"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may consider publishing BO information of contract recipients, especially if awarded contracts are already public. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may consider making beneficial ownership information of contract recipients publicly available"</sub>

## Access by FIs, DNFBPs, other countries’ competent authorities, and general public62

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 107, citing INR.24 paragraph 13) says company registries should provide or facilitate timely access by FIs, DNFBPs and foreign competent authorities to public information and at least basic information: name, proof of incorporation, legal form and status, registered office, basic regulating powers, directors list and unique identifier such as a TIN. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provides or facilitates timely access by FIs, DNFBPs and other countries’ competent authorities to the public information they hold"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF footnote 63: INR.24 paragraph 15(a) also requires nominee status to be included in public information. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "also requires nominee status to be included in public information"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: FATF (para 108) says countries should consider facilitating FI/DNFBP access to shareholder/member registers (names, shares held, share categories and voting rights) and to BO information in registries, alternative mechanisms or supplementary measures, for CDD and discrepancy reporting, subject to data protection safeguards. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitating timely access by FIs and DNFBPs to the following additional information on legal persons to facilitate compliance with CDD obligations"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: FATF (para 109) says countries may facilitate public access to basic and BO information, letting civil society and others cross-check it to improve accuracy and identify misuse such as tax evasion, fraud or corruption. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public access to this information can enable civil society, other organisations and individuals to cross check the information"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Public access alone is not a sufficient mechanism to ensure accuracy; countries should weigh data protection, privacy, security and confidentiality, possibly limiting public fields or using tiered disclosure based on legitimate interest. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "public access alone is not a sufficient mechanism to ensure accuracy of information"</sub>

## Cost of access

- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 110) says any fee structure should not create unnecessary delays or obstacles to competent authorities' efficient and rapid access. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would not create unnecessary delays or obstacles to the efficient and rapid access"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Absent a compelling case, good practice is free access for competent authorities and national public procurement authorities; for others, fees should be proportionate to or not exceed administrative costs including register maintenance and development. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the absence of a compelling case, it would be good practice to ensure that competent authorities"</sub>

## Access by FIs, DNFBPs, other countries' competent authorities, and general public

- **recommended / recommendation**: FATF (para 108) says countries should also consider facilitating timely access by FIs and DNFBPs to additional information on legal persons, namely the shareholder or member register and beneficial ownership information held in a registry or alternative mechanism, to help CDD compliance and supplementary verification such as discrepancy reporting, subject to adequate data protection and privacy safeguards. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should also consider, facilitating timely access by FIs and DNFBPs to the following additional information on legal persons"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 64 states that the shareholder-register information on shares held applies to the nominal owner of all registered shares. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is applicable to the nominal owner of all registered shares"</sub>

## 14. MECHANISMS FOR PREVENTING AND MITIGATING RISK OF THE MISUSE OF BEARER SHARES AND BEARER SHARE WARRANTS

- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 111) states R.24 and its Interpretive Note say countries should not permit legal persons to issue new bearer shares or bearer share warrants and should prevent misuse of existing ones. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should not permit legal persons to issue new bearer shares or bearer share warrants"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Existing bearer shares or bearer share warrants should be converted into registered form or immobilised. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any existing bearer shares or bearer share warrant should be converted into a registered form or for them to be immobilised"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF Glossary: bearer shares are negotiable instruments giving ownership in a legal person to whoever possesses the physical bearer share certificate, plus similar untraceable instruments, excluding dematerialised and/or registered shares whose owner can be identified. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accord ownership in a legal person to the person who possesses the physical bearer share certificate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF Glossary: bearer share warrants are negotiable instruments giving entitlement to ownership to whoever holds the physical warrant certificate, plus similar untraceable instruments, excluding identifiable dematerialised/registered forms and instruments only conferring a subscription right until exercised. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only confers a right to subscribe for ownership in a legal person at specified conditions"</sub>

## Definitions

- **descriptive / definition**: FATF (para 113) identifies the key features of bearer shares and warrants as a physical certificate and untraceable ownership. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Physical bearer share certificate or physical bearer share warrant certificate; and"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: FATF (para 114) states bearer shares and warrants present increased ML/TF risk because ownership of the certificates is concealed. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Bearer shares and bearer warrants present increased risk of money laundering and terrorist financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The bearer share restrictions do not cover new or existing bearer shares or warrants of exchange-listed companies subject to disclosure requirements (stock exchange rules, law or enforceable means) ensuring adequate BO transparency (Footnote 61 to R.24). (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not cover newly issued and existing bearer shares or bearer share warrants of a company listed on a stock exchange"</sub>

## Variations of bearer shares and bearer share warrants

- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF (para 115) states a dematerialised and/or registered bearer instrument is outside R.24 where the owner can be identified, e.g., in France all securities are dematerialised by law (since Law n°83-1179 of 29 December 1983) and bearer securities are book entries in the owner's name held by a custodian. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "bearer securities are, in principle, evidenced by a book entry in the name of their owner67"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 66 notes that since French Order n° 2017-1674, registered securities may also be recorded on distributed ledger technology. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "registered securities may also be registered on distributed ledger technology"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: A physical bearer certificate falls within R.24 only where no legal mechanism ensures traceability; it may be outside scope if the owner must be registered with an intermediary that can reveal the holder to the issuer. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may fall within the scope of Recommendation 24 only when no mechanism exists under applicable law to ensure the traceability"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Such traceable cases are very limited, e.g., listed shares or shares immobilised in a clearing and deposit bank or accredited central securities depository, letting issuers trace holdings to the individual shareholder; CSDs may also hold materialised certificates for depositary receipts meant to be held exclusively abroad. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in particular an accredited central securities depository or a recognised third-country central securities depository"</sub>

## Range of conversion/immobilisation measures

- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 116) states that, for existing bearer shares, the standard requires countries to take measures to prevent and mitigate the risks through a range of conversion/immobilisation measures within a reasonable timeframe. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the standard requires countries to take measures to prevent and mitigate the risks by a range of conversion/immobilisation measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: For bearer shares not held by an intermediary, conversion options: oblige the holder to intermediate shares with a supervised intermediary that must identify the beneficial owner, or oblige the issuer to amend bylaws to allow only registered shares. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an obligation for the issuer of the bearer shares to change its bylaws to allow only registered shares"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: For bearer shares held by an intermediary: the intermediary must identify the beneficial owner, or is responsible for converting the shares into dematerialised or registered shares. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the intermediary would be under the duty to identify the beneficial owner of the bearer shares"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 118) says immobilisation can require bearer certificates and warrants to be held with a regulated FI or supervised professional intermediary, with timely competent authority access. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring them to be held with a regulated financial institution or professional intermediary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 119) says regulated FIs and professional intermediaries becoming custodians of bearer certificates should fully identify the bearer and record information, with procedures ensuring timely authority access. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "undertake full identification of the bearer to be able to record the relevant information for competent authorities"</sub>

## Timeframes for immobilisation/conversion of bearer share certificates and bearer share warrants

- **descriptive / requirement**: FATF (para 120) says the reasonable timeframe for bearer share measures could be about two years, allowing for legislative/administrative lead time. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this period could be about two years68"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF footnote 68: countries should start legislative changes after revised R.24 is adopted, but will be assessed against the new standards from the next (fifth) round of mutual evaluations. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries will be assessed against the new standards from the next (fifth) round of mutual evaluations"</sub>

## Box 5. FRANCE

- **descriptive / case example**: France's law of 30 December 1982 dematerialising bearer securities did not mandate conversion but used indirect sanctions: holders had 18 months to register physical bearer shares or lose voting rights, issuers had to sell remaining physical bearer shares after five years, and non-enforcing executives were fiscally penalised. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Holders of said physical bearer shares had 18 months as of the entry into force of the law to have their shares registered"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FATF (para 121) notes another option is converting bearer shares into registered shares after a set period (which could be two years). (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have the bearer shares converted into registered shares after a certain period of time (which could be two years)"</sub>

## Box 6. SWITZERLAND

- **descriptive / case example**: Switzerland's federal act implementing the Global Forum recommendations limited bearer shares to listed companies or intermediated shares, and automatically converted all other bearer shares into registered shares 18 months after entry into force. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "organised an automatic conversion of bearer shares into registered shares 18 months after its entry into force"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FATF (para 122) says before conversion or immobilisation is complete, countries should require bearer instrument holders to notify the company and the company to record their identity before any associated rights can be exercised. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should require holders of bearer instruments to notify the company, and the company to record their identity"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries may set an interim limited period after which existing bearer shares and warrants are converted, immobilised or eventually cancelled. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would be either converted or immobilised or, eventually cancelled"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF (para 123) says the timeframe's reasonableness should reflect the ML/TF risks of the bearer instruments or holders, and holders failing to comply by the deadline would lose all or part of their shareholder rights. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the holder of bearer shares would lose all or part of its rights as a shareholder"</sub>

## Box 7. FRANCE AND SWITZERLAND

- **descriptive / case example**: Both the French and Swiss Acts gave an 18-month grace period to implement bearer share conversion. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both French and Swiss Acts gave an 18-month grace period to implement the conversion measure"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF (para 124) states R.24 and its Interpretive Note require countries to prevent and mitigate misuse of nominee shareholding and nominee directors. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require countries to take measures to prevent and mitigate the risk of the misuse of nominee shareholding and nominee directors69"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: FATF Glossary (para 125): a nominee is a natural or legal person holding a role in a company as an agent acting on instructions of a nominator with a more substantive claim to control and/or ownership; in many cases the nominator is the beneficial owner. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "holding a role in a company as an agent acting upon instructions of a nominator"</sub>
