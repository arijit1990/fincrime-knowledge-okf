## Annex B. Additional Regtech Cases Studies on New Technologies for Private-to-Private AML/CFT Data Sharing and Analysis.

- **descriptive / case example**: In the Financial Crime Index case, Bank A used a Regtech's Financial Crime Index, which combines a bank's own data with publicly available sources and the Regtech's proprietary dataset to generate a monthly overall financial crime risk score, plus scores and reports across nine financial crime risk themes. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to generate a monthly overall financial crime risk score, in addition to scores and reports"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the secure end-to-end encryption platform case, a Regtech built a platform for banks and FIs to exchange AML-relevant information via one-to-one messaging (requests for information) or one-to-many "data pooling", to resolve basic inquiries (e.g., sanction alerts needing counterpart information), conduct complex joint investigations (e.g., second-level transaction monitoring alerts involving multiple institutions) and enrich high-risk customer due diligence data (e.g., PEPs, RCAs, UBOs, source of funds). (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one-to-one messaging (requests for information / RFI’s) or one-to-many “data pooling”"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The platform uses end-to-end encryption, with exchanged data protected by encryption keys and password protection; one-sided encryption and hashing are used for data pooling so FIs can share with multiple parties, hashing verifies authenticity of exchanged data or files, the platform host has no access to unencrypted data, and all critical activities are logged for full auditability. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The platform host does not have access to any unencrypted data"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the blind matching case, a software company worked with a public authority on a pilot using homomorphic encryption to link data from private and public sources, including multiple FIs, for statistical purposes to inform public policy; data contributors submitted encrypted data to the recipient. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pilot that enabled blind matching of records using homomorphic encryption"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The blind matching pilot's controls ensured that only encrypted data left contributor environments; data could not be linked before reaching the recipient, which required a third-party intermediary to convert encrypted contributor data into a linkable tokenised dataset; and the recipient could not reverse the processing to obtain original raw data but could link the datasets. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The recipient required a third-party (an intermediary) to convert the encrypted contributor data to a linkable, tokenised dataset"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The pilot demonstrated linking of data on a common attribute without that attribute being visible to any party after leaving the recipient environment, protecting individuals' privacy while allowing population analysis for policy; the technology is now in production in the UK, used by National Health Service Digital. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without that attribute being visible to any party after leaving the recipient environment"</sub>

## Annex C. Suggested Actions to Support the Use of Technology in AML/CFT

- **mandatory / expectation**: Annex C states, advancing the San Jose Principle endorsed by the FATF in 2017, that new technologies for AML/CFT must be developed and implemented in a way that reflects threats as well as opportunities, ensuring their use is compatible with international standards of data protection and privacy, and cybersecurity. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "San Jose Principle to pursue positive and responsible innovation endorsed by FATF in 2017"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 1 of Annex C is for government and the private sector to create an enabling environment for responsible innovation to enhance AML/CFT effectiveness, including innovative solutions that facilitate AML/CFT measures (risk assessments, CDD, other requirements) and strengthen supervision and examination, and good practices for updating or replacing internal legacy systems. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Create an enabling environment by both government and the private sector for responsible innovation"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 2 of Annex C is to ensure privacy and data protection when implementing new technologies: ensure a valid legal basis for processing personal data, protect personal information in line with national and international legal frameworks, process data for explicit, specified and legitimate purposes, and support responsible development and adoption of innovative privacy-preserving technologies to enable robust AML/CFT information sharing and analysis while preserving privacy. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ensure there is a valid legal basis for the processing of personal data when deploying new technologies"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 3 of Annex C is to promote AML/CFT innovation that supports financial inclusion by design: mitigate obstacles to financial inclusion through innovative solutions and ensure responsible innovation consistent with the FATF objective to promote financial inclusion. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Promote AML/CFT innovation which supports financial inclusion by design"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 4 of Annex C is to develop and communicate policies and regulatory approaches to innovation that are flexible, technology-neutral, outcomes-based and in line with the risk-based approach. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "flexible, technology-neutral, outcomes-based and in line with the risk-based approach"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Annex C says that using new technologies responsibly, such as digital identity and advanced transaction monitoring and analysis solutions (including collaborative analytics), can support effective, risk-based implementation of the FATF Standards by the public and private sectors and can also promote financial inclusion. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can assist effective, risk-based implementation of the FATF Standards by the public and private sectors"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 1 (create an enabling environment for responsible innovation) lists, among the appropriate safeguards and features for new AML/CFT solutions: explainable and transparent processes and outcomes, human oversight, respect for privacy and data protection, strong cybersecurity, and alignment with global, national and technical standards and best practices. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "explainability and transparency of processes and outcomes; oversight by humans; respect for privacy and data protection"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Under Suggested Action 4, authorities are to consider the impact of new technologies holistically, in the context of accompanying structural and organisational changes, possible unintended consequences and overall impact on AML/CFT effectiveness and financial inclusion; issue and/or update clear policy statements, guidance, use cases, best practices or regulations as necessary; and consult with counterparts and regulated entities to inform policy and decision-making. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consider the impact of new technologies holistically, in the context of the structural and organisational changes"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 5 of Annex C is to exercise informed oversight: build expertise in new technologies to enable informed regulation and supervision, identify explicit, well-defined uses of new technologies for AML/CFT supervision and examination, understand the risks and benefits and appropriate risk-mitigation measures that preserve benefits, and use technology to enhance AML/CFT supervision. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Build expertise in new technologies, to enable informed regulation and supervision of their use"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Suggested Action 6 of Annex C is to promote and facilitate cooperation: co-operate and co-ordinate with all relevant authorities, including data protection and privacy authorities, for a comprehensive coordinated approach to risks and benefits of new technologies; consider developing collaborative environments for cross-government and/or public private research and development; and participate in international efforts to develop global principles governing use of new technologies for AML/CFT aligned with human rights, cybersecurity, data privacy and protection measures and relevant technical standards and trust frameworks. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Consider developing collaborative environments to facilitate cross-government and/or public private research and development"</sub>
