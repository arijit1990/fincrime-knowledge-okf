## 8. Concluding Remarks

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF concludes that considerations on private-to-private AML/CFT data sharing are not new to it, but available and emerging technologies may offer new ways to share and analyse data to more efficiently and effectively detect potential suspicious activity or comply with other AML/CFT obligations, and may also offer solutions to better protect personal data. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may offer new ways to share and analyse data in order to more efficiently and effectively detect potential suspicious activity"</sub>
- **mandatory / expectation**: The report states that the compliance of the considered developments with a jurisdiction's data protection requirements needs to be duly assessed before implementation so as not to impede the protection of fundamental rights, and that in some jurisdictions such initiatives may not presently be legally permissible. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "needs to be duly assessed before implementation in order not to impede the protection of fundamental rights"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF states it will build on the Stocktake by continuing dialogue between AML/CFT supervisors, technology developers, FIs, DPP authorities and other experts so that new technologies that can contribute to enhanced AML/CFT effectiveness may be fully utilised, consistent with national and international DPP frameworks. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF will build upon this Stocktake paper by continuing this dialogue"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The stocktake describes it as important that, before they are carried out, data pooling and collaboration initiatives give balanced weight to, and reduce, the risks of de-risking and of legitimate customers being denied access to the financial system. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important that data pooling and collaboration initiatives equally weigh and mitigate any risks"</sub>

## Annex A. Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines advanced analytics as autonomous or semi-autonomous examination of data or content using sophisticated techniques and digital tools, typically beyond traditional business intelligence, to discover deeper insights, make predictions or generate recommendations; techniques include data/text mining, machine learning, pattern matching, forecasting, visualisation, semantic analysis, sentiment analysis, network and cluster analysis, multivariate statistics, graph analysis, simulation, complex event processing and neural networks, and typically rely on big data. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Advanced analytics refers to the autonomous or semi- autonomous examination of data or content"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines an AI system (citing OECD 2020) as a machine-based system that can, for a given set of human-defined objectives, make predictions, recommendations or decisions influencing real or virtual environments, operating with varying levels of autonomy; it notes there is no consensus on what constitutes "thinking", "intelligence" or "fully autonomous", and that machine learning is at present the most familiar and developed form of AI. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "make predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing real or virtual environments"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines an application as software designed to help a user perform specific tasks, an API as a set of definitions and protocols for building and integrating application software that lets digital products or services communicate with others, and an algorithm as a set of step-by-step instructions to perform a specific task. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An API is a set of definitions and protocols for building and integrating application software"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines black box as AI/machine learning and other technologies that are opaque, non-intuitive and do not provide adequate information about their decision-making and results, i.e., technology lacking explainability. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "i.e., black box technology lacks explainability"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary reports the Financial Stability Board definition of big data as the very large volume of data produced by growing use of digital tools and information systems, with examples being financial transaction data, social media data and machine data (such as Internet of Things, computer and mobile phone data). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the massive volume of data that is generated by the increasing use of digital tools and information systems"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines benchmarking as determining the actual and relative capabilities of a technology-based process, product or service and identifying performance gaps by testing it against the best performance achieved for the function, using hard performance data measured by specified criteria; it may be used to compare new technology with legacy systems, or one new technology with alternatives. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "performance of new technology vs legacy systems, or one new technology against alternative new technologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines collaborative analytics as analysis in which data is not moved to a central location but the analytical tools come to the data, making it easier to keep data secure and control who accesses what data for what purposes; it defines data pooling as combining digital data from different sources into a fuller, more useful data set for analysis, with pools organised in a centralised manner. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data pooling refers to a process where digital data from different sources are combined"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines cybersecurity as the comprehensive process of protecting data and the systems for moving, storing and authenticating that data (a broader term than data security), and data security as protecting data from unauthorised access and corruption throughout its lifecycle, including encryption, hashing, tokenisation and key management practices (narrower than cybersecurity). (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cybersecurity, a broader term than data security"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines data standardisation as converting data to a uniform format so users can process and analyse it, essential to big data processing and advanced analytics; for example financial data varies within and across entities and standardisation converts it to a common form enabling large-scale analytics. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data standardisation is the process of converting data to a uniform format"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines digital ID systems/solutions as identity systems or products and services that identify/verify a natural or legal person's identity, bind the proofed identity to a digital credential, and use the credential and potentially other authentication factors to confirm that a person claiming the identity is the identity-proofed person. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "binding the proofed identity to a digital credential"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines distributed ledger technology (DLT, a.k.a. blockchain) as a technology protocol enabling simultaneous access, validation and updating of an immutable ledger distributed across multiple computers, typically across multiple entities or locations, and deep learning as an advanced type of machine learning in which artificial neural networks with numerous (deep) layers learn from large amounts of data in highly autonomous ways. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DL is an advanced type of machine learning in which artificial neural networks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary distinguishes digitalisation (using digital technologies and digitised data to change a business model, how work is done and how customers and companies interact) from digitisation (conversion of analogue information into digital form, i.e., binary code), and defines dynamic data as a continuous real-time stream of data points in constant flux, as distinct from static data that is fixed after collection. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Digitisation is the conversion of data, information, text, pictures, sound or other representations in analogue form"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines explainability as technology-based processes, solutions or systems being capable of being explained, understood and accounted for, providing adequate understanding of how solutions work and produce results; it calls explainability a basic condition for trust and responsible use, and says explainable AI provides transparency into the data, variables and decision points used to reach a result. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Explainability is a basic condition for trust and responsible use"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines FinTech broadly as the use of new and emerging digital technologies in the financial sector; initially it referred to technology-based customer-facing products and services (e.g., mobile payments, online marketplace lending, algorithmic savings and investment tools, virtual currency payments, crowd funding, remote check capture, mobile banking) and now also covers automated mid- and back-office functions such as algorithms, big data, AI, machine learning and link analytics, and regulatory compliance activities. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FinTech refers broadly to the use of new and emerging digital technologies in the financial sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines fuzzy logic as a subset of AI that processes imprecise input and multiple values to produce output including a range of intermediate possibilities between YES and NO (e.g., certainly yes, possibly yes, cannot say, possibly no, certainly no), producing definite output from incomplete, ambiguous, distorted or inaccurate input. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "produces output that includes a range of intermediate possibilities between YES and NO"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines the Internet of Things as the global network of Internet-enabled devices and machines that collect, send, share and act on data using embedded sensors, processors and communication hardware without human interaction, and interoperability as the ability of different IT systems and software applications to communicate, exchange data and use the information seamlessly in real time. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Interoperability: refers to the ability of different information technology systems and software applications to communicate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines machine learning as a subset of AI that trains computer systems to learn from data, identify patterns and make decisions with minimal human intervention, a method of data analysis that automates analytical model building. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is a method of data analysis that automates analytical model building"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines machine readable regulation as replacing rules written in natural legal language with computer code to enable the use of AI for regulatory reporting, and natural language processing (NLP) as a branch of AI enabling computers to understand, interpret and manipulate human language. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "replaces rules written in natural legal language with computer code"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary says the term privacy enhancing technologies encompasses an array of encryption-using technologies that would be useful primarily in allowing the protection of privacy as data is used. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would be useful primarily in allowing the protection of privacy as data is used"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines real-time analytics as a machine learning process in which a system processes and analyses instantaneously loaded data and almost immediately generates meaningful output, and real-time data (RTD) as information delivered immediately after collection, enabling real-time analytics and which can be dynamic or static. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Real-time analytics is a machine learning process in which a system processes and analyses data"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines RegTech as a sub-set of FinTech that uses new technologies to comply with regulatory requirements more efficiently and effectively than existing capabilities, and says innovation is responsible when it is fit for purpose and complies with applicable regulatory requirements, including AML/CFT, consumer protection, cybersecurity and privacy protections. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Innovation is responsible when it is fit for purpose and complies with applicable regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines smart machines as computer hardware and software systems that use AI algorithms and are designed to make decisions, often using real-time data; unlike passive machines, they use sensors, digital data and remote inputs, combine information from different sources, analyse it instantly and act on the insights, mimicking human intelligence by advanced computational processes. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Smart machines are designed to make decisions, often using real- time data"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines supervised learning as a machine learning process that teaches algorithms predictive models by feeding input data with known outcomes (labelled data), with the algorithm adjusting the model on the training data set to minimise error; it uses a validation data set to measure learning progress and a test data set to evaluate performance on never-before-seen data to determine whether the model learned its training data effectively and can generalise. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "uses a validation data set to measure the algorithm’s progress in learning the model"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary defines unsupervised learning as a machine learning process that analyses and clusters unlabelled datasets to discover hidden patterns, data groupings or anomalies without human intervention, determining correlations without an answer key, with modelling becoming more accurate and refined as data exposure grows; it defines SupTech as the use of innovative technology by supervisory authorities to support supervision and examination. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "analyse and cluster unlabelled datasets to discover hidden patterns, data groupings"</sub>

## Annex B. Additional Regtech Cases Studies on New Technologies for Private-to-Private AML/CFT Data Sharing and Analysis.

- **descriptive / case example**: In the Annex B homomorphic encryption pilot, a Regtech partnered with an FI to implement a privacy-enhanced inter-bank information sharing system using encrypted queries for financial crime and compliance; the partners are adding banks, defining new use cases (including intra-bank cross-border use cases around customer risk ratings and model thresholds) and moving toward production. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A Regtech partnered with an FI to implement a privacy enhanced inter-bank information sharing system"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The homomorphic encryption pilot is said to enable distribution of search queries without disclosing search terms to one another, which negates risks of disclosure, tipping off and regulatory breaches; its main obstacle is a lack of regulatory clarity on what type of data can be shared, under what circumstances and how. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The main obstacle for this project is a lack of regulatory clarity on what type of data can be shared"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the German Collaborative Analytical Platform case, a FinTech is partnering with a consortium of top-tier banks, software vendors and academics to develop a platform with a data pool and analytical toolset for transactional and financial data shared between institutions, intended to let FIs across Germany and Europe construct a view of customers' overall activity network and expose hidden relationships and patterns of transactional behaviour. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "develop a new collaborative analytical platform for fighting financial crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the Nordic KYC Platform case, a Regtech founded in 2019 by six leading Nordic banks developed a common data standard for KYC information, made available through its KYC services and digital platform with an end-customer portal; participating FIs can use the KYC information as a foundation for their own risk assessments, control of the customer relationship stays with the institution, and privacy is ensured via a secure hybrid cloud architecture based on privacy and secure-by-design principles. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The six founding banks developed a common data standard for KYC- information"</sub>
