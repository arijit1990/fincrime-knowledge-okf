## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF stocktake (July 2021) states that recent technological advances help financial institutions analyse large amounts of structured and unstructured data more efficiently and identify patterns and trends more effectively. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "analyse large amounts of structured and unstructured data more efficiently and identify patterns and trends more effectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: According to the FATF stocktake, pooling data and using collaborative analytics can help financial institutions better understand, assess and mitigate money laundering and terrorist financing risks, giving a more dynamic, effective and efficient identification of these activities and helping the private sector comply with AML/CFT requirements in a timelier and less burdensome manner. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial institutions can better understand, assess, and mitigate money laundering and terrorist financing risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The stocktake says data pooling and collaborative analytics can help prevent criminals from exploiting information gaps when they use multiple domestic and international financial institutions to launder illicit funds, each of which has only a limited and partial view of transactions. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each having a limited and partial view of transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report acknowledges that AML/CFT and data privacy and protection are both significant public interests with important objectives that are neither in opposition nor inherently mutually exclusive. It states it is essential that legal regimes facilitate both objectives, so as to prevent money laundering, terrorist financing, proliferation financing and other financial crimes in a way that respects individuals' fundamental rights to privacy and data protection. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These objectives are neither in opposition nor inherently mutually exclusive"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Privacy-enhancing technologies are described as relying on a range of cryptographic tools that enable multiple parties to interact meaningfully to achieve an application goal without revealing underlying private information to one another or to third parties; they are said to offer promising ways to protect information in specific use cases in line with data protection and privacy frameworks. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without revealing underlying private information to one another or to third parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The stocktake states that there are not yet any technical standards for privacy-enhancing technologies and that much work remains to develop standards and open source references, which would clarify the specific use-cases where these technologies can protect data privacy. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there are not yet any technical standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The stocktake examines commercially available and emerging technologies that facilitate advanced AML/CFT analytics within individual regulated entities and collaborative analytics between them, analyses the intended objectives and drivers for using them, and identifies policy considerations and potential solutions. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "examines commercially available and emerging technologies that facilitate advanced AML/CFT analytics"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF states it will continue its dialogue between AML/CFT supervisors, technology developers, financial institutions, data privacy and protection authorities and other relevant experts, so that new technologies that can improve AML/CFT effectiveness are fully utilised consistent with national and international data privacy and protection frameworks. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF will continue its dialogue between AML/CFT supervisors, technology developers, financial institutions"</sub>

## 1. Introduction

- **descriptive / definition**: The report describes data pooling and collaborative analytics as a process where (digital) data from different sources are analysed (including by multiple parties); pools may be organised in a centralised way (data pooling) or a distributed way (collaborative analytics). (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "data from different sources are analysed (including by multiple parties)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The paper addresses data pooling and collaborative analytics between financial institutions (FIs), including within and outside international financial groups. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "between financial institutions (FIs), including within and outside international financial groups"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report says data pooling and collaborative analytics may enable analytical tools that strengthen the shared understanding, assessment and mitigation of ML and TF risks, can reduce the number of false positives, and can help prevent exploitation of information gaps that enable regulatory arbitrage by criminals engaging with multiple domestic and international FIs. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It can reduce the number of false positives"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report notes that using big data and advanced analytics such as artificial intelligence has the potential to enhance AML/CFT compliance in the financial sector, but carries risks to fundamental and individual rights when personal data is shared, or when processes lack adequate explainability and may produce biased or otherwise erroneous results. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lack adequate explainability and may produce biased or otherwise erroneous results"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report states that FIs could leverage advanced analytics to more accurately identify suspicious activities, screen their customers and manage risks. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs could leverage advanced analytics to more accurately identify suspicious activities, screen their customers, and manage risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report says the accuracy of advanced analytics largely corresponds to the size, quality and relevancy of the data set, so the efficacy and efficiency of these tools may depend on the ability of FIs (within and outside financial groups) to share information. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the accuracy of advanced analytics largely corresponds to the size, quality and relevancy of the data set"</sub>
- **mandatory / expectation**: The report states that technologies that exchange, pool or analyse data must protect personal information in line with national and international legal frameworks, so the need for data sharing requires careful analysis of both AML/CFT and data protection and privacy implications. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must protect personal information in line with national and international legal frameworks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The report says FIs should only collect and process personal data that is necessary (data minimisation) to fulfil a specific and defined purpose (purpose limitation), and should not further process it in a manner incompatible with those purposes. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIs should only collect and process personal data that is necessary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The report says information should be shared to achieve an aim that cannot be achieved through less invasive measures requiring less access to personal identifiable information; collected data should not be retained for longer than necessary and should not be transferred to an entity that does not have compatible data protection rules. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information should also be shared to achieve a certain aim that cannot be achieved through less invasive measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The report notes that where privacy-enhancing technologies are meant to use data to identify a specific natural or legal person, such as in customer on-boarding, their data privacy protections may be weakened. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when the aim of such technologies is to use data to identify a specific natural or legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Because of this, the report says data sharing initiatives in some jurisdictions may for now be confined to non-personal data, for example corporate data without customer-related data, which falls outside the scope of the relevant DPP legal requirements. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "data sharing initiatives in some jurisdictions may currently be limited to sharing non-personal data"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 1 explains that in collaborative analytics, data is not moved to a central location to be analysed with other data assets; instead the analytical tools come to the data, which makes it easier to keep the data secure and control who accesses what data for what purposes. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the analytical tools come to the data, not the other way around"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In June 2020, in line with the German FATF Presidency priorities on AML/CFT digital transformation, the FATF agreed to conduct a stocktake on Data Pooling, Collaborative Analytics and Data Protection, to examine commercially available or emerging technologies for advanced AML/CFT analytics within regulated entities or collaborative analytics between FIs, and to identify challenges and potential solutions. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF agreed to conduct a stocktake on Data Pooling, Collaborative Analytics and Data Protection"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report states that the solutions proposed by respondents for wider deployment of new technologies are not presently endorsed by the FATF. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(which are not presently endorsed by the FATF)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The scope of the paper is private-to-private data pooling and collaborative analytics (including efforts supported or initiated by public authorities); public-private information sharing, in particular between reporting entities and financial intelligence units or law enforcement agencies, is examined in a separate paper on Digital Transformation of AML/CFT for Operational Agencies. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this paper examines private-to-private data pooling and collaborative analytics"</sub>

## 2. Methodology

- **descriptive / method**: The paper summarises the Questionnaire results together with desk-based research and interviews with public and private sector stakeholders, including representatives from FIs, technology developers, and AML/CFT and data protection and privacy authorities. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "desk-based research and interviews with public and private sector stakeholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The questionnaire gathered views on the intended results of using new technologies for collaborative analytics, how they are used to secure, collaborate and analyse data, the challenges and policy considerations of implementation, and engagement with AML/CFT and data protection supervisors; it also sought case studies illustrating good practices. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "also sought case-studies to illustrate good practices by"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Among questionnaire respondents, the majority of public sector respondents were supervisors and the majority of private sector input came from FIs and technology developers; institutions classified as large banks were the main contributors. By region, respondents were based in Europe (53%), Americas (20%), Asia/Oceania (18%) and Africa (9%). (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The majority of respondents are based in Europe (53%), followed by Americas (20%), Asia/Oceania (18%), and Africa (9%)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FATF sent an online Questionnaire on Digital Transformation in November 2020 to AML/CFT national authorities and private sector stakeholders, including academia, FIs and technology developers, to identify new technologies available for collaborative analytics. It received 188 completed responses. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In total, 188 completed responses were received"</sub>
