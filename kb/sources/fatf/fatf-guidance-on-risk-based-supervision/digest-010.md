## 9.2.1. Kingdom of Saudi Arabia

- **descriptive / case example**: Saudi Arabia conducted an ML/TF risk assessment on VAs and VASPs to identify, assess and understand the sector's risks. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has conducted a ML/TF risk assessment on VAs and VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Saudi VA risk assessment questionnaires captured STRs, cases, investigations, prosecutions and convictions, international co-operation requests involving VAs/VASPs, internet traffic on VA activity, website services and host countries, private sector business relationships with VASPs, and VA transactions. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the internet traffic on VA or VASP activities, the nature and type of services provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Additional Saudi risk questions covered how many foreign VASP legal persons/arrangements operate in KSA, how many ICOs were organised through Saudi or KSA-registered foreign entities, VASP activity types, products and services, places of operation and registration, customer types and origins, and geographic service regions. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "How many ICOs were organized through a Saudi legal entity or a foreign entity registered in KSA?"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The Saudi assessment classified VASP activities as: VA-fiat exchange; exchange between VA forms; VA transfer; safekeeping and/or administration of VAs or control instruments; and financial services related to an issuer's VA offer and/or sale. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "safekeeping and/or administration of virtual assets or instruments enabling control over virtual assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The Saudi assessment also asked whether VAs had been frozen under TF/PF-related targeted financial sanctions. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Have VAs been frozen under TF/PF-related targeted financial sanctions?"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Saudi workshops with public and private stakeholders discussed questionnaire data and provided expert judgement; the assessment examined extent of VASP operations in KSA, population VA use, criminal misuse, and authorities' ability to detect, deter and repress VA crime, with outcomes used to set policy responses. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ability of the authorities to detect, deter and repress criminal activity involving VAs/VASPs"</sub>

## 9.2.2. Japan

- **descriptive / method**: The JFSA annually collects AML/CFT statistical and qualitative data from obliged entities, approximately 60 sector-tailored Key Performance Indicators, to rate individual entity risk and build an annual off-site monitoring plan. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approximately 60 Key Performance Indicator data, which are tailored to each sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: JFSA's VASP data (non-exclusive, revised annually) include use of blockchain analysis tools for monitoring or risk analysis, VA types offered, number of customers detected using mixers and/or tumblers, and percentage of hardware or paper wallet allocation. ## 9.4. Use of technology in VASP supervision

- **descriptive / method**: FATF notes most VA transactions are recorded on a ledger with some data public, and blockchain analytics can help supervisors focus firm assessments, categorise highest risk firms by activity, and assess strategic and global risks for regulation and national risk assessments. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "helping to categorise the highest risk firms based on their activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: FATF notes blockchain analytics require financial resources and recruitment and training of capable staff, and not all VAs are covered by all vendors. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not all VAs are covered by all vendors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF says because VASPs and some FIs use blockchain analytics to monitor exposure (e.g. mixer or tumbler transactions, funds from known illicit websites), supervisors should understand how these tools work to assess VASPs' risk-based frameworks and controls. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors should understand how they function in order to adequately assess a VASP’s implementation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FATF says supervisors using blockchain analytics should consider how use of the derived data meets their jurisdiction's data protection requirements. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider how the use of the data derived from these solutions meets the data protection requirements"</sub>

## 9.5.1. United Kingdom (Financial Conduct Authority)

## 10. Supervision in the COVID-19 context

- **descriptive / case example**: During COVID-19 some FATF members continued onsite, hybrid or virtual inspections prioritising high-risk sectors or entities; some gave risk-based flexibility on annual reports and data returns and delayed new licences, especially for sectors closed by lockdowns. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF members have continued onsite inspections or hybrid or virtual on-sites, prioritising high-risk sectors or entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF notes communication, guidance and outreach were important in balancing access and controls during COVID-19, with examples in Annex B of its COVID-19 Risks and Policy Responses report. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Communication, guidance and outreach has played an important role in balancing access and controls"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: US banking supervisors reminded banks not to view the charitable sector as a whole as uniformly or unacceptably high ML/TF risk; banks should evaluate NPOs by their particular characteristics, and supervisors gave non-binding guidance on factors for NPO risk profiling. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "avoid viewing the charitable sector as a whole as presenting uniform or unacceptably high ML/TF risks"</sub>

## Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: FATF defines AML/CFT systems or controls as an entity's measures to mitigate ML/TF risks, including the FATF Recommendations' preventive measures. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AML/CFT systems or controls are the measures in place within an entity to mitigate ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Core Principles means the BCBS Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision, IOSCO Objectives and Principles for Securities Regulation, and IAIS Insurance Supervisory Principles. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core Principles refers to the Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: DNFBPs are casinos (including ship and online), real estate agents, dealers in precious metals, dealers in precious stones, lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants, and TCSPs, the latter two groups when performing activities in the FATF Glossary definition. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Casinos (include ship and online casinos)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Emerging risks means recently identified but not fully explored ML/TF threats, vulnerabilities or phenomena, including previously identified risks appearing in new or unfamiliar conditions. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recently identified but not fully explored ML/TF threats or vulnerabilities or other phenomena"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Financial group means a parent or other controlling legal person exercising control and coordination for group supervision under the Core Principles, with branches and/or subsidiaries subject to group AML/CFT policies. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial group means a group that consists of a parent company"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Financial institutions means any person conducting as a business one or more FATF Glossary-listed activities for or on behalf of a customer. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions means any natural or legal person who conducts as a business"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Inherent risk is the ML/FT risk in an entity or sector before mitigation, often assessed from customer base, products, delivery channels, services and jurisdictions of business. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inherent risk refers to the ML/FT risks present in an entity or sector before mitigating measures are applied"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Inspection/examination are used interchangeably for intrusive reviews of AML/CFT systems in practice, covering policies plus implementation through interviews, systems testing and review of risk assessment and customer files; commonly on-site, though technology may allow off-site inspections. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These terms are used interchangeably to refer to intrusive/vigorous reviews"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Internal controls under R. 18 and INR.18 mean AML/CFT programmes including internal policies, procedures and controls with compliance management and employee screening, ongoing employee training, and an independent audit function. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as defined in the FATF Standards under R. 18 and INR.18"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Management Information (MI) means systems and processes giving boards, management and dedicated officers timely, appropriate information on the entity's risk management and internal control framework. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Management Information (MI): refers to systems and processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Monitoring broadly means processes controlling effective application of AML/CFT requirements and mitigation, from examining real documents, files and transactions to identifying "root causes" of weaknesses for remediation; monitoring tools let supervisors observe risk profile changes or atypical behaviour. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Monitoring tools enable supervisors to observe changes in risk profiles or detect atypical behaviour"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: MVTS means accepting cash, cheques, monetary instruments or stores of value and paying a corresponding sum to a beneficiary via communication, message, transfer or clearing network; may involve intermediaries and new payment methods, and includes regional forms such as hawala, hundi and fei-chen. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "described using a variety of specific terms, including hawala, hundi, and fei-chen"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: On-site supervision means supervisors independently verifying adequate policies, procedures and controls, confirming reliability of reported information, obtaining further information on the entity and related companies, and monitoring follow-up on concerns. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors independently verify that adequate policies, procedures and controls exist at regulated entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Off-site supervision (including monitoring and risk surveillance) means desk-level review and analysis of entities' condition, follow-up, identification of developing risks and setting priorities and scope for further work. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Off-site supervision (including monitoring and risk surveillance) refers to off-site, or desk-level, supervisory work"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulated entities means FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs; residual risks are ML/TF risks remaining after AML/CFT systems and controls address inherent risks. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regulated entities refers to FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Risk tolerance is the accepted level of unmitigated or unmitigatable risk, recognising residual risk is never zero; for an entity it is a factor of risk appetite, for a supervisor the level of residual risk it will accept given potential impact. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "‘Risk tolerance’ refers to the accepted level of unmitigated or unmitigatable risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Supervisors' risk tolerance is generally lower for entities with higher ML/TF risk and weaker controls or where control failures could materially affect the financial system, and may be higher where entities have shown ability to monitor and mitigate rising residual risk. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors’ risk tolerance is generally lower for entities with higher ML/TF risks yet weaker controls"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Risk indicators are metrics or statistics giving insight into risk exposure and monitoring its main drivers; in AML/CFT they commonly assess inherent risk but can also monitor control quality. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "risk indicators can also be established to monitor the quality of AML/CFT control measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Robotic Process Automation (RPA) is business process automation using software robots (bots) or AI/digital workers. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Robotic Process Automation (RPA) is a form of business process automation technology"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: A Self-Regulatory Body (SRB) represents a profession, is made up of its members, regulates entry and practice, performs supervisory or monitoring functions, and should enforce rules to maintain high ethical and moral standards. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A SRB is a body that represents a profession"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: Supervisors are designated competent authorities or non-public bodies (including Core Principles supervisors) responsible for ensuring AML/CFT compliance; non-public bodies should have power to supervise and sanction, be empowered by law, and be supervised by a competent authority. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Non-public bodies (which could include certain types of SRBs) should have the power to supervise and sanction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Supervisory risk assessments (SRA) help supervisors develop, document and update their ML/TF risk understanding. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisory risk assessments (SRA) help supervisors develop, document and update their ML/TF risk understanding"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A supervisory strategy, informed by the supervisory risk assessment, plans activity risk-sensitively, sets AML/CFT objectives and explains how identified and emerging risks will be addressed. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a supervisory strategy helps supervisors plan their activities in a risk-sensitive manner"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Systems for monitoring means ongoing, generally less intrusive observation of regulated entities to identify compliance weaknesses or breaches (see R.14, 15, 26 and 28). (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in a manner that is generally less intrusive than traditional supervision regime"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Three lines of defence in AML/CFT: business units (first line) identify, assess and control risks; the AML/CFT chief officer, compliance, HR and technology form the second; internal audit is the third. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The third line of defence is ensured by the internal audit function"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Targeted financial sanctions means both asset freezing and prohibitions on making funds or assets available, directly or indirectly, to designated persons and entities (R.6 and R.7). (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "means both asset freezing and prohibitions to prevent funds or other assets from being made available"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In October 2018 FATF extended AML/CFT requirements to VASPs under Recommendation 15; a VASP is a person not covered elsewhere in the Recommendations that as a business conducts FATF Glossary-listed VA activities for or on behalf of another person. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In October 2018, FATF extended AML/CFT requirements to VASPs under Recommendation 15"</sub>

## RISK-BASED SUPERVISION

- **descriptive / context**: FATF states supervisors ensure banks, other FIs, VASPs, accountants, real estate agents, DPMS and other DNFBPs understand and mitigate their risks, comply with AML/CFT obligations, and face appropriate action if they fail. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "take appropriate action if they fail to do so"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF encourages moving beyond a tick-box approach; the March 2021 guidance aims to help supervisors address the full risk spectrum and focus resources on highest risks, noting a risk-based approach is less burdensome on lower risk sectors, which is critical for financial inclusion. (p. 104)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF encourages countries to move beyond a tick-box approach"</sub>
