## RISK-BASED SUPERVISION

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Guidance on Risk-Based Supervision was published in March 2021 by FATF, an inter-governmental body whose Recommendations are recognised as the global AML/CFT standard. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Recommendations are recognised as the global anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CFT) standard."</sub>

## Acknowledgements

- **descriptive / context**: The guidance helps supervisors assess risks in the sectors they oversee, adapt resources accordingly, and address common challenges; it drew on input from the FATF Global Network of more than 200 jurisdictions and informal consultation with private sector and financial inclusion stakeholders. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "together making up more than 200 jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF describes effective, risk-based supervision as an essential part of a strong anti-money laundering system. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective, risk-based supervision is an essential part of a strong anti-money laundering system."</sub>

## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / context**: FATF states that preventing ML/TF protects communities from harm more effectively than prosecuting the activity after it happens. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is more effective in protecting communities from harm than pursuing prosecution"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: AML/CFT supervisors protect the financial system and other sectors by raising regulated entities' awareness of ML/TF risks, setting obligations and encouraging good practice; enforcing and monitoring compliance; and taking appropriate measures where deficiencies are found. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enforcing and monitoring compliance with AML/CFT obligations, and (3) taking appropriate measures"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: To perform their function effectively and efficiently, supervisors must implement a risk-based approach. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors must implement a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A risk-based approach to supervision means tailoring the supervisory response to fit assessed risks, allowing finite resources to be allocated to mitigate identified ML/TF risks aligned with national priorities. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A risk-based approach involves tailoring the supervisory response to fit the assessed risks."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Risk-tailored supervision reduces opportunities for laundering and terrorist financing, improves information available to law enforcement, and avoids unwarranted burden on lower risk sectors, entities and activities, which is critical for financial inclusion that can reduce ML/TF risk through greater transparency. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not place an unwarranted burden on lower risk sectors, entities, and activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A robust risk-based approach covers the full spectrum from higher to lower risk sectors and entities and, properly implemented, is more responsive, less burdensome and delegates decisions to those best placed to make them. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is more responsive, less burdensome, and delegates more decisions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Mutual evaluations show that transitioning to risk-based supervision is challenging; success requires good risk understanding, a strong legal basis (mandate and powers), political and organisational support, and adequate capacity and resources. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mutual evaluations reveal that making the transition to risk-based supervision is a challenging task."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Moving from rule-based to risk-based supervision takes time and requires a change in supervisory culture, staff capacity building and training, and a comprehensive supervisory toolkit. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The transition from a rule-based to a risk-based approach takes time."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Guidance has three parts: high-level guidance (Part One), practical advice on common implementation challenges (Part Two), and country examples including DNFBP and VASP supervision (Part Three); it should be read alongside forthcoming PF guidance on requirements introduced in October 2020. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "practical advice to address common implementation challenges in Part Two and country examples in Part Three"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In this Guidance, 'supervisors' means designated competent authorities or non-public bodies responsible for ensuring regulated entities comply with AML/CFT requirements, including designated SRBs. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the term ‘supervisors’ refers to the designated competent authorities or non-public bodies"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulated entities under the FATF Standards are FIs, VASPs and DNFBPs (casinos; real estate agents; dealers in precious metals and stones; lawyers, notaries, other legal professionals and accountants; and TCSPs), plus any other businesses a country adds based on risk. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casinos; real estate agents; dealers in precious metals and stones; lawyers, notaries and other legal professionals and accountants"</sub>

## 1.1. Objectives and scope

- **descriptive / context**: The Guidance is non-binding; it explains how supervisors should apply a risk-based approach in line with FATF Standards and identifies innovative practices to improve AML/CFT supervision effectiveness. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The objective of this non-binding Guidance is to clarify and explain how supervisors should apply a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Guidance focuses on how supervisors allocate resources and adopt risk-appropriate tools; it does not identify or address sectoral risks and complements FATF's sector-specific RBA guidance. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it does not seek to identify or address sectoral risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Sector-specific FATF RBA guidance exists for legal professionals (2019), accountants (2019), TCSPs (2019), securities (2018), life insurance (2018), MVTS (2016), virtual currencies (2015), banking (2014), prepaid cards/mobile/internet payments (2013), casinos (2008), dealers in precious metals and stones (2008) and real estate agents (2008). (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Legal professionals (2019), Accountants (2019), Trust and Company Service Providers (2019), Securities (2018)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Guidance does not advocate a specific institutional framework; arrangements should fit each jurisdiction's context, including existing institutional and prudential frameworks, sector size and complexity, and ML/TF risk exposure. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Guidance does not advocate any specific institutional framework for supervision."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: References to particular jurisdictions' practices are examples only and do not signify FATF approval or endorsement of those systems' effectiveness. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is not to be considered FATF-approval or endorsement of the effectiveness of that system"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF assesses the effectiveness of AML/CFT supervision under Immediate Outcome 3 of the FATF Methodology. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF assesses the effectiveness of AML/CFT supervision under Immediate Outcome 3 of the FATF Methodology"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.1 and INR.1 require jurisdictions to identify, assess and understand ML/TF risks and apply a risk-based approach to mitigate them, including in supervisory activities; R.2 highlights national co-ordination including among AML/CFT supervisors. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "R.1 and INR.1 require jurisdictions to identify, assess and understand the ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: INR.1 requires supervisors to review and consider the risk profiles and assessments developed by financial institutions and DNFBPs when applying the RBA. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR.1 requires supervisors to review and consider risk profiles and assessments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The R.1 RBA applies to which entities are covered by the AML/CFT regime and to what extent, how they comply, and how they are supervised, including scope, frequency and intensity of supervision. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the scope, frequency and intensity of the supervisory activities"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Since FATF amended R.1 and INR.1 in October 2020 to require PF risk assessment, supervisors must consider supervised entities' PF risk exposure and ensure effective TFS implementation, and may use existing AML/CFT and TFS frameworks rather than create new ones. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors are now required to consider how the entities they supervise or monitor are exposed to PF risks"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.26 requires risk-based supervision of financial institutions, R.28 requires risk-based supervision or monitoring of DNFBPs, and R.15 requires risk-based supervision or monitoring of VASPs. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recommendation 26 (R.26) requires risk-based supervision of financial institutions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: INR 15, 26 and 28 provide that supervisors should allocate resources based on risk, which requires understanding ML/TF risk in their jurisdiction, sector and entities and having on-site and off-site access to all relevant information. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INR 15, 26 and 28 recommend that supervisors should allocate their supervisory resources based on risk."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.15, 27 and 28 require supervisors to have powers to impose a range of effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, in line with R.35, for AML/CFT failures. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require supervisors to have powers to impose a range of effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: R.28 allows DNFBPs other than casinos to be overseen by a supervisor or an appropriate SRB, if the SRB can ensure members comply with AML/CFT obligations. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allows for DNFBPs other than casinos to be regulated by a supervisor or an appropriate self-regulating body"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Entities providing services across several designated activities need not be captured under two supervisory regimes, but covered activities must meet relevant requirements, e.g. a casino exchanging funds in virtual assets is subject to R.15 and INR.15. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when a casino exchanges funds in virtual assets (partially or exclusively)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FIs subject to the Core Principles should be licensed and supervised under the Core Principles and R.26; all other FIs (including MVTS and money or currency changers) and VASPs must be licensed or registered and supervised or monitored according to ML/TF risk under R.14, R.15 and R.26. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All other financial institutions (including MVTS or money or currency changing providers) and VASPS must be licenced or registered"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Casinos should be licensed, regulated and supervised under R.28; other DNFBPs should be subject to effective risk-sensitive systems for monitoring and ensuring AML/CFT compliance. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Casinos should be licenced, regulated and supervised in line with R.28."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: 'Supervision' and 'systems for monitoring' span a spectrum of risk-based activities and tools; a system for monitoring implies a different approach and focus rather than fundamentally different tools. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "implies a difference in the approach and focus of supervisors rather a fundamental difference in tools"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under a system for monitoring, oversight is generally less intrusive, e.g. without regular inspection cycles, but supervisors should still be able to use intrusive interventions where risks are identified and not be limited to off-site work. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be able to use a range of interventions, including intrusive measures, where risks are identified"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Monitoring is more reactive to materialised risks but requires proactive measures such as periodic data returns, periodic risk assessment updates, ongoing monitoring of STR filings and significant risk events, and active interventions. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "periodic data returns, periodic updates of risk assessments to identify changing risk profiles"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: A monitoring system helps detect entities consistently failing to perform CDD or report STRs or potentially facilitating illicit flows, which triggers more intrusive supervisory intervention. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "detect entities that are consistently failing to undertake CDD or report STRs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Supervisors deciding whether monitoring suffices must consider sector ML/TF risk; while the Standards allow most DNFBPs to be monitored, many countries apply 'supervision'-level oversight to higher-risk DNFBP sectors. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in many countries higher-risk DNFBP sectors are subject to a level of oversight"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under a system for monitoring, supervisors must still provide adequate guidance and feedback, ensure compliance, and be able to apply effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions per R.35. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervisors are also required to provide adequate guidance and feedback"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: There is no clear line between 'supervision' and 'monitoring'; the Guidance uses 'monitoring' in the sense of the FATF Recommendations, and does not apply to monitoring of NPOs under R.8. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this guidance does not apply in the context of monitoring of relevant non-profit organisations (NPOs) under R.8"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.3 Core Issue 3.2 asks how well supervisors identify and maintain an understanding of ML/TF risks across sectors, between sector and institution types, and in individual institutions. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core Issue 3.2: How well do the supervisors identify and maintain an understanding of the ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: IO.3 Core Issue 3.3 asks how well supervisors, on a risk-sensitive basis, supervise or monitor FI, DNFBP and VASP compliance with AML/CFT requirements to mitigate risks. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core Issue 3.3: With a view to mitigating the risks, how well do supervisors, on a risk-sensitive basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: R.34 and Core Issue 3.6 stress guidance and feedback; because inspections reach only a percentage of regulated entities, clear guidance, education and innovative outreach are important parts of a supervisory programme. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisory inspections will only ever reach a percentage of the regulated entity population."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: R.15, 26 and 28 and Core Issue 3.1 market-entry requirements should also be applied risk-based, adjusting measures to potential risks such as different ownership types. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "highlight market-entry requirements which should also apply in risk-based manner"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Core Issue 3.4 (sanctions) is covered briefly in section 3.7 and in FATF's Guidance on Effective Supervision and Enforcement; Core Issue 3.5 (supervisors' effect on compliance) is covered in section 3.8. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core Issue 3.5 on demonstrating supervisors’ effect on compliance by entities is briefly addressed in section 3.8."</sub>

## 1.3. Common supervisory frameworks

- **descriptive / context**: FATF focuses on outcomes rather than process and does not prescribe a supervisory framework, provided outcomes effectively address ML/TF risks. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF focuses on outcomes rather than process"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Effective communication and co-ordination among AML/CFT supervisors and prudential supervisors, SRBs, central banks, finance ministries, FIUs and LEAs is critical to an effective overall risk-based approach. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is critical to ensuring that the jurisdiction is applying an effective risk-based approach overall"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Common framework: a single AML/CFT supervisor for all regulated entities, usually the same authority acting as FIU or prudential supervisor. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A single AML/CFT supervisor responsible for AML/CFT supervision of all regulated entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Common framework: partial integration, e.g. integrated AML/CFT and prudential supervision of the financial sector, with the FIU, tax or another authority supervising some or all non-financial sectors. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "integrated AML/CFT and prudential supervision of the financial sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Common framework: a decentralised model with multiple agencies and/or SRBs supervising across and within sectors, possibly with the FIU or another authority overseeing or coordinating DNFBP supervision. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A decentralised model for AML/CFT supervision with multiple agencies and/or SRBs"</sub>
