- **descriptive / finding or result**: Compared with the 2023 survey results, jurisdictions, including some with materially important VASP activity, have made progress in putting AML/CFT regulation in place or are in the process of doing so. ## Key Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2024 survey indicates only marginal improvements on many elements of R.15 implementation, although there is greater progress since 2023 in specific areas, such as the number of jurisdictions registering or licensing VASPs in practice. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The findings from the 2024 survey indicate only marginal improvements on many elements of R.15 implementation across jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As of April 2024, 130 FATF mutual evaluation reports and follow-up reports (MERs/FURs) had been completed and published since the 2019 adoption of the VA/VASP standards, with 32 additional MERs and FURs assessing R.15 published since April 2023. Three quarters of jurisdictions (75%; 97 of 130) are only partially compliant or not compliant with R.15, the same proportion as in April 2023 (75%; 73 of 98). (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Three quarters of jurisdictions (75%; 97 of 130) are only partially or not compliant with R.15"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Of the 147 respondents to the 2024 survey on R.15 implementation (151 responded in 2023), 29% (42 of 147) have not conducted a virtual asset risk assessment at all, and MER/FUR results show that 75% (97 of 130) of jurisdictions have not conducted adequate risk assessments. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "29% (42 of 147) have not conducted a virtual asset risk assessment at all"</sub>

## Executive summary

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF extended its AML/CFT standards to virtual assets (VAs) and virtual asset service providers (VASPs) in 2019. In February 2023 it adopted a Roadmap aimed at strengthening implementation of Recommendation 15 (R.15), and under the Roadmap it published a table showing R.15 implementation status for FATF members and other jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To strengthen implementation of Recommendation 15 (R.15), the FATF adopted a Roadmap in February 2023"</sub>

## Key Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: More than a quarter of survey respondents (27%; 39 of 147) have yet to decide if and how to regulate the VASP sector. Of those that had determined an approach, 60% (88 of 147) decided to permit VAs and VASPs and 14% (20 of 147) report opting to prohibit VASPs partially or explicitly. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "More than a quarter of survey respondents (27%; 39 of 147) have yet to decide"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As in the 2023 Targeted Update, MER and FUR results indicate that prohibiting VASPs effectively is difficult: only two of twenty jurisdictions with a partial or explicit prohibition approach were assessed as largely compliant with FATF requirements, and more than half were assessed as partially or not compliant. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only two of twenty jurisdictions taking a partial or explicit5 prohibition approach have been assessed as largely compliant"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: It is unclear to what extent decisions to prohibit VASPs followed a thorough risk assessment: 20% of jurisdictions with prohibitions reported that they have not conducted a risk assessment. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "20% of jurisdictions with prohibitions reported that they have not conducted a risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In this report, an "explicit" prohibition approach means a comprehensive approach that fully bans the use of VAs and VASPs regardless of purposes. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to the comprehensive prohibition approach that fully bans the use of VAs and VASPs regardless of purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nearly one third of survey respondents (30%; 29 of 94), excluding those that explicitly prohibit VASPs (that is, those that permit VASPs and those that prohibit them partially), have not passed legislation implementing the Travel Rule. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have not passed legislation implementing the Travel Rule"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One third (32%; 11 of 34) of jurisdictions that assessed VAs/VASPs as high risk and do not take an explicit prohibition approach have not yet passed legislation implementing the Travel Rule. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One third (32%; 11 of 34) of the jurisdictions who assessed VAs/VASPs as high risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: VAs are increasingly used by terrorist groups, in particular by ISIL in Asia and groups in Syria; terrorist groups using VAs often use stablecoins and experiment with anonymity-enhancing cryptocurrencies. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "terrorist groups that are using virtual assets often use stablecoins and experiment with anonymity-enhancing cryptocurrencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: During VACG engagements, private sector stakeholders reported increasing use of stablecoins for ML, TF and PF purposes and continued hacks of decentralised finance (DeFi) arrangements, and noted some progress in risk mitigation measures leveraging smart contracts. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the increasing use of stablecoins for ML, TF and PF purposes and continued hacks of decentralised finance (DeFi) arrangements"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Supervision and enforcement of the Travel Rule stays at a low level even in jurisdictions that have legislated for it: under a third of them (26%; 17 of 65) have issued findings or directives, or taken enforcement or other supervisory action, against VASPs on Travel Rule compliance. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "less than one third (26%; 17 of 65) have issued findings or directives"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Progress reported by several jurisdictions includes new AML/CFT/CPF requirements for stablecoin service providers (Travel Rule among them), regulatory and enforcement steps against DeFi arrangements, and risk assessments covering DeFi and unhosted wallets, P2P transactions included. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "taking regulatory and enforcement actions against DeFi arrangements, and conducting risk assessments on DeFi and unhosted wallets"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The report states that it is vital that all jurisdictions, particularly those with materially important VASP activity, act rapidly to fully implement the FATF requirements on VAs and VASPs; its recommendations identify actions all jurisdictions should urgently take. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is vital that all jurisdictions act rapidly to fully implement the FATF"</sub>

## Risk assessment and policy approach to VASPs

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 2: jurisdictions should develop and implement jurisdictional approaches to VASPs, i.e. permitting the use of VAs and VASPs or partially or explicitly prohibiting their use. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should develop and implement jurisdictional approaches to VASPs"</sub>

## Licensing/registering and supervising VASPs

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 3 (continued): if jurisdictions are not taking a ban or prohibition approach, the action should include requiring VASPs to be licensed or registered, conducting supervisory inspections of VASPs, and taking enforcement or supervisory actions against VASPs as appropriate (the report cites paragraphs 31-43 of the 2021 FATF Updated Guidance). (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring VASPs to be licensed or registered, conducting supervisory inspections of VASPs"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 4: in developing a licensing or registration framework, jurisdictions are encouraged to consider risks associated with offshore VASPs (VASPs not incorporated or physically based in their jurisdiction) and incorporate appropriate risk mitigation measures. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions are encouraged to consider risks associated with offshore VASPs"</sub>

## Implementation of the Travel Rule

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 5: jurisdictions that have not yet introduced legislation or regulation to implement the Travel Rule should urgently do so. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions that have not yet introduced legislation/regulation to implement the Travel Rule should urgently do so."</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 6: jurisdictions that have introduced the Travel Rule should rapidly operationalise it, including through effective supervision and enforcement in case of non-compliance. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions that have introduced the Travel Rule should rapidly operationalise it"</sub>

## Risk assessment and policy approach to VASPs

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 1: jurisdictions that have not done so yet should identify and assess the ML and TF risks linked to VAs and VASPs, and introduce risk mitigation measures that also address regulatory and supervisory challenges identified. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should identify and assess the ML and TF risks associated with VAs and VASPs"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 2: jurisdictions should develop and apply a jurisdictional approach to VASPs, either permitting VAs and VASPs or prohibiting them partially or explicitly. Whichever route is chosen, they should monitor or supervise the VASPs present and enforce against non-compliance, sanctioning VASPs that do not meet their obligations. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should monitor or supervise their VASP population and enforce against non-compliance"</sub>

## Licensing/registering and supervising VASPs

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 3: jurisdictions should act immediately to reduce the ML, TF and PF risks tied to VAs and VASPs, including by fully implementing R.15. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should take immediate action to mitigate ML, TF, and PF risks related to VAs and VASPs"</sub>

## Implementation of the Travel Rule

- **recommended / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 7: jurisdictions are strongly encouraged to keep and publicise details of the VASPs licensed or registered in their jurisdiction, so as to support counterparty due diligence under R.16 and R.13. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions are strongly encouraged to maintain and publicise information on VASPs that are registered or licensed in their jurisdiction"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 8: jurisdictions should enhance their knowledge base of Travel Rule compliance tools, including by engaging with their VASP sector to identify commonly used tools and ensure these tools meet all the FATF requirements. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should enhance their knowledge base of the Travel Rule compliance tools"</sub>

## Addressing emerging and increasing risks such as Stablecoin, DeFi, unhosted wallets including P2P transactions, and Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs)

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 9: given increasing adoption of stablecoins, their use by illicit actors and the ability to transfer stablecoins P2P like other VAs, jurisdictions should monitor market developments, assess the illicit finance risks, and take appropriate risk mitigation measures. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions should monitor market developments, assess the illicit finance risks, and take appropriate risk mitigation measures"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 10: jurisdictions should assess and monitor illicit finance risks of DeFi arrangements, identify entities that could fall within the definition of VASPs, develop a regulatory framework to capture the responsible entity(ies), take supervisory and enforcement action as appropriate, and share good practices and remaining challenges with VACG members. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should assess and monitor illicit finance risks associated with DeFi arrangements"</sub>

## 12. VASPs and Travel Rule compliance tool providers should:

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 12: VASPs and Travel Rule compliance tool providers should review their Travel Rule compliance tools to ensure they enable providers to fully comply with the FATF requirements. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "review their Travel Rule compliance tools to ensure they enable providers to fully comply with the FATF requirements"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 12: VASPs and Travel Rule compliance tool providers should improve compatibility between Travel Rule compliance tools (e.g. technological advancements, and mechanisms permitting transactions through a chain of mutually compatible tools) to facilitate effective Travel Rule implementation and to support VASPs' sanction screening and transaction monitoring to detect and prevent suspicious transactions not compliant with FATF requirements. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "improve the compatibility between Travel Rule compliance tools"</sub>

## Addressing emerging and increasing risks such as Stablecoin, DeFi, unhosted wallets including P2P transactions, and Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs)

- **expected / recommendation**: Public sector recommendation 11: jurisdictions should keep track of market developments and assess ML/TF/PF risks associated with unhosted wallets (P2P transactions included) and NFTs, and should share their experience, for instance on data collection and risk mitigation. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should monitor market developments and assess ML/TF/PF risks related to unhosted wallets"</sub>

## 12. VASPs and Travel Rule compliance tool providers should:

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 13: given persistent and significant ML, TF and PF threats, the private sector, particularly VASPs, should ensure they have appropriate risk identification and mitigation measures in line with R.15 and should adopt other risk-based measures as appropriate. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the private sector, particularly VASPs, should ensure they have appropriate risk identification and mitigation measures in line with R.15"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 13 (continued): the private sector's measures should include consideration and mitigation of risks associated with stablecoins, DeFi, NFTs and unhosted wallets including P2P transactions, as well as engagement with public sector stakeholders to develop a common risk understanding. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consideration and mitigation of risks associated with stablecoins, DeFi, NFTs, and unhosted wallets including P2P transactions"</sub>

## Next steps

- **descriptive / context**: In February 2023 the FATF adopted a Roadmap through June 2024 to strengthen R.15 implementation. Its published table sets out R.15 implementation status (e.g. undertaking a risk assessment, enacting legislation to regulate VASPs, conducting a supervisory inspection) for FATF members and other jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity, to enable the FATF network to support them and to encourage timely full implementation. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In February 2023, the FATF adopted a Roadmap through June 2024 to strengthen implementation of R.15"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF and VACG will continue to conduct outreach and provide assistance, particularly to jurisdictions with lower capacity and materially important VASP activity, in collaboration with FSRB Secretariats and relevant international organisations; they will keep sharing findings, experiences and challenges on R.15 implementation, including on DeFi and unhosted wallets including P2P transactions, and monitor market trends for developments that may need further FATF work. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly those with lower capacity and materially important VASP activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In February 2024 the FATF Plenary decided that the table on R.15 implementation status for FATF members and jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity would be refreshed and published in 2025. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will be updated and published in 2025, as decided by the FATF Plenary in February 2024"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: In October 2018 the FATF updated Recommendation 15 to extend AML/CFT requirements to VAs and VASPs, and in June 2019 it adopted an Interpretive Note to R.15 clarifying how the requirements apply. Since then it has worked to identify and address implementation gaps, provide guidance and monitor emerging VA risks. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In October 2018, the FATF updated Recommendation 15 (R.15) to extend AML/CFT requirements to virtual assets (VAs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF work on VAs/VASPs by year: 2018 R.15 amended; 2019 Interpretive Note to R.15, creation of the VACG, and initial guidance for regulators (updated 2021); 2020 first 12-month review, report to the G20 on so-called stablecoins, and list of red flag indicators of ML/TF through VAs; 2021 updated guidance and second 12-month review; 2022 targeted update report on R.15 compliance and Travel Rule; 2023 report on ransomware financing and targeted update on R.15 implementation; 2024 table of R.15 implementation status. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Overview of FATF work on VAs and VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The 2021 FATF guidance update focused on six areas: clarifying definitions of virtual assets and VASPs; how the Standards apply to so-called stablecoins; risks and tools for countries to address ML/TF risks of peer-to-peer transactions; licensing and registration of VASPs; Travel Rule implementation guidance for public and private sectors; and principles of information-sharing and co-operation among VASP supervisors. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "guidance on how the FATF Standards apply to socalled stablecoins"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: This is the FATF's fifth report on global implementation of its Standards on VAs and VASPs; it overviews global R.15 implementation including the Travel Rule, outlines implementation challenges, identifies progress and good practices, and overviews emerging VA risks and responses. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This report is the FATF’s fifth report on the global implementation of the FATF’s Standards on VAs and VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report draws on a March 2024 survey on R.15 implementation, including the Travel Rule and responses to emerging risks, which collected responses from 147 jurisdictions (35 FATF members and 112 FSRB members). Responses were self-reported and not verified. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Responses were self-reported and not verified."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The survey used conditional branching/skip logic, so respondents were directed to questions based on earlier answers (for example, those that had not decided whether to prohibit or regulate VASPs were not asked Travel Rule questions) and the number of respondents varied by question group: risk assessment and policy approach 147; licensing/registering and supervising VASPs 94; Travel Rule implementation 94; DeFi, NFTs, unhosted wallets and P2P 133; final comments 147. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "meaning respondents would be directed to certain questions based on their answer to a previous question"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report infers that the 58 jurisdictions that did not respond to the survey have not made progress on R.15, including Travel Rule implementation. The FATF and Global Network consist of 205 jurisdictions in total, of which 147 responded. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report infers that jurisdictions (58) that did not respond to the survey have not made progress on R.15"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report is also based on VACG meetings in late 2023 and early 2024, including consultations with VA private sector representatives in December 2023 and April 2024, and on published FATF MERs and FURs assessing R.15 as of April 2024. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consultations with representatives from the VA private sector in December 2023 and April 2024"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report has four sections: Section 1 on R.15 implementation and challenges in assessing ML/TF risks, licensing or registering VASPs and regulating offshore VASPs; Section 2 on Travel Rule implementation and public and private sector challenges and solutions; Section 3 on market developments and emerging risks (stablecoins, DeFi, P2P transactions and unhosted wallets, NFTs meeting the FATF definition of VAs, and use of VAs for terrorist financing and proliferation financing); Section 4 on next steps for the FATF and VACG. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Section 3 considers market developments and emerging risks, in particular stablecoins, decentralised finance (DeFi)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The report notes that unhosted wallets are also referred to as non-custodial, self-custodial or self-hosted wallets. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Also referred to as non-custodial, self-custodial or self-hosted wallets."</sub>

## Overall Status of R.15 Implementation in Mutual Evaluation and Follow-up Reports

- **descriptive / finding or result**: As of April 2024 the FATF and its Global Network had assessed 130 jurisdictions' compliance with revised R.15 (32 additional MERs/FURs published since April 2023). Most (75%; 97 of 130) are partially compliant (PC) or not compliant (NC); 32 jurisdictions (25%) are largely compliant (LC) and one is fully compliant (C). The 2023 Targeted Update had 25% (24 of 98) largely compliant. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most jurisdictions (75%; 97 of 130 jurisdictions) are only partially (PC) or not compliant (NC) with the FATF’s requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The share of PC or NC jurisdictions as of April 2024 is identical to April 2023 (75% PC or NC; 25% LC). Figure 1.1 shows 2024: 1 compliant, 32 (25%) largely compliant, 65 (50%) partially compliant, 32 (25%) not compliant; 2023: 1 compliant, 24 (25%), 50 (51%) and 23 (24%) respectively. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The proportion of PC or NC jurisdictions as of April 2024 is identical to that of April 2023"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The report cautions that the MER/FUR ratings may not reflect jurisdictions' current progress towards implementing the VA/VASP Standards, elements of which are shown in the analysis of the 2024 survey results. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may not reflect the current progress made by jurisdictions towards implementing the FATF Standards on VA and VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As in 2023, assessment results indicate that jurisdictions continue to struggle with several fundamental R.15 requirements, including conducting a risk assessment, developing a regime for VASPs (registering/licensing or prohibiting VASPs) and implementing the Travel Rule. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions continue to struggle with several fundamental requirements, including conducting a risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF assessment methodology for VA/VASP requirements comprises criteria R15.3 risk assessment and application of a risk-based approach; R15.4 licensing/registration of VASPs; R15.5 identification of natural persons or legal entities conducting VASP activities; R15.6 supervision/regulation of VASPs for AML/CFT compliance; R15.7 guidelines to assist VASPs in AML/CFT compliance; R15.8 sanctions compliance; R15.9 preventative AML/CFT measures including the Travel Rule; R15.10 targeted financial sanctions compliance; R15.11 international cooperation. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Establishment ofguidelines which assist VASPs in AML/CFT compliance"</sub>
