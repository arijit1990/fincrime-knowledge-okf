## 1. Background of R.16 revision

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF revised Recommendation 16 (R.16) to adapt the Standards to decades of change in payments, covering new products and services, market participants, business models, technologies and messaging standards, and the evolving risks arising from them. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adapt the FATF Standards to changes in payment domain in the past decades"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF aims to keep its Standards technology-neutral and applies the principle of 'same activity, same risk, same rules' in revising R.16. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "follow the principle of ‘same activity, same risk, same rules’"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The R.16 update forms part of the G20 Priority Action Plan to make cross-border payments faster, cheaper, more transparent and more inclusive while keeping them safe and secure. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "part of the G20 Priority Action Plan on making cross-border payments faster"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Fragmentation of payment chains through new players and technologies has made it harder for financial institutions to hold enough information to identify suspicious activity and comply with sanctions, and harder for law enforcement to access relevant information. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "payment chains have grown more fragmented with the arrival of new players"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF notes that illicit finance threats have evolved, with fraud now a major predicate crime for money laundering. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fraud now constituting a major predicate crime for money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The updated R.16 clarifies the roles and responsibilities of the different payment chain participants and improves the content and quality of basic originator and beneficiary information in payment messages, aiming for greater transparency and more effective AML/CFT controls. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clarifying the roles and responsibilities of different players involved in the payment chain"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In revising R.16 the FATF weighed implications for financial inclusion, data protection and privacy, and payment speed and cost, seeking a balance that maximises effectiveness and proportionality of the new obligations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as financial inclusion, data protection and privacy, and speed/cost of payments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Examples of balancing in revised R.16 include allowing year of birth as a fallback where full date of birth is absent, and limiting the beneficiary address requirement to country and town name only. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use of year of birth as a fallback option in absence of a full date of birth"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Revised R.16 includes flexible alignment check obligations, with clearer responsibilities for beneficiary FIs and the option to check alignment through pre-validation checks, post-validation checks or holistic monitoring. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "possibility of checking alignment via pre-validation checks, post-validation checks, or holistic monitoring"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Revised R.16 introduces a targeted regime for cross-border cash withdrawals focused on cardholder transparency while minimising disruption and data protection and privacy risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a targeted regime for cross-border cash withdrawals, that focuses on increasing transparency on cardholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: To promote implementation of revised R.16, the FATF will set up a public-private Payment Advisory Group, coordinate with other standard-setters and global organisations, and develop a guidance paper on payment transparency, expected for publication in late 2026. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "setting up a public-private advisory group (the Payment Advisory Group)"</sub>

## 2. Second public consultation

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF held a second public consultation on proposed R.16 revisions in February - April 2025, after a first public consultation in February-May 2024, and received a significant number of responses. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "second public consultation on proposed revisions to R.16 in February - April 2025"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: After the consultation, the FATF worked through the main issues at a dedicated Policy Development Group meeting in May 2025, producing an updated R.16 revision. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dedicated Policy Development Group meeting in May 2025"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The explanatory note on revised R.16 and INR.16 is not a binding part of the FATF Standards and is not formal guidance on their application. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This note does not constitute a binding part of the FATF Standards"</sub>

## Structural changes

- **descriptive / context**: INR.16 was restructured to differentiate responsibilities and obligations by type of payment or value transfer and to simplify the interaction of rules, exceptions and alternative measures (INR.16 Section C. Information requirements). (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "differentiates the responsibilities and obligations for different types of payments or value transfers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Revised R.16/INR.16 sets requirements according to types of activities rather than types of entities; most second-consultation respondents welcomed the new structure. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "differentiates the requirements based on the types of activities rather than types of entities"</sub>

## Updates to objectives section

- **descriptive / context**: The objective section of INR.16 now directly references UNSCRs on preventing, suppressing and disrupting proliferation financing and refers to combatting fraud, in addition to the historical emphasis on terrorism financing. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "direct reference to United Nations Security Council Resolutions (UNSCRs) relating to the prevention"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 2 of INR.16 explicitly states that the FATF does not intend R.16 to negatively affect financial inclusion. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not the FATF’s intention of requiring R.16 to negatively affect financial inclusion"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Footnote 49 of paragraph 1(c) of INR.16 clarifies that R.16 does not itself require real-time sanctions screening; this does not change the scope of existing targeted financial sanctions (TFS) requirements. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clarifies that R.16 does not itself require real-time sanctions screening"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under the FATF Standards and UNSCRs, the core TFS responsibilities of private entities are to freeze without delay the funds or other assets of designated persons or entities and to ensure no funds or other assets are made available to them. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "freeze without delay the funds or other assets of, and to ensure that no funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The modality of TFS screening by private sector entities is not set out in the FATF Standards but in national regulation or industry best practices. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The modality of TFS screening to be conducted by private sector entities is not set out"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Respondents noted the real-time screening footnote's impact could be limited by national TFS regimes that in practice require real-time screening of TFS-relevant information (e.g., name) in payment messages; FATF will consider this in upcoming guidance. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could be limited by national TFS regimes that require in practice the real-time screening"</sub>

## Information requirements for Cross-border payments or value transfers (above the de minimis threshold)

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF considers payment transparency has been eroded by the pace of change in payments and seeks closer alignment of originator and beneficiary information requirements to foster symmetry, more efficient compliance and timely FIU/LEA access to information. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Closer alignment in the information requirements for both originator and beneficiary seek to foster symmetry"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 9 of INR.16 sets the core required originator and beneficiary information that should accompany a cross-border payment or value transfer above applicable de minimis thresholds. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Paragraph 9 of INR.16 prescribes the core information requirements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Ordering financial institutions must send originator information under paragraph 9 of INR.16 that has been verified for accuracy. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ordering financial institutions need to send originator information that has been verified for accuracy"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Ordering financial institutions are not required to verify beneficiary information accuracy; they need only include information provided by the originator or otherwise conveyed to them (e.g., pull payments, verification of payee schemes). (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not required to verify the accuracy of the beneficiary information"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Under paragraph 29 of INR.16, beneficiary financial institutions should verify the beneficiary's identity, if not previously verified, for transfers above the de minimis threshold. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "beneficiary financial institutions should verify the beneficiary’s identity in cases where the transfers are above"</sub>

## Address

- **expected / requirement**: For the beneficiary, revised R.16 requires only country and town name rather than full address, because requiring unverified full beneficiary addresses would cause friction and data protection and privacy concerns without sufficient material benefit. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "decided to require only the country and town name for beneficiary, not the full address"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For the originator, full address is the default requirement, but country and town name suffice where standardised postal address information is absent; guidance will address inclusion concerns such as rural/remote areas. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "full address information is the default requirement while country and town name will suffice"</sub>

## Date of birth

- **expected / requirement**: Date of Birth (DoB) information is required only for an originator who is an individual, not for legal entities; where a full DoB is unavailable, year of birth alone suffices. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Date of Birth (DoB) information is required only with regard to an originator which is an individual"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Upcoming FATF guidance will address cases where no DoB is available or cannot be verified (e.g., unregistered births) and how to handle it, for example not making DoB available to the final beneficiary. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cases where no DoB information is available or cannot be verified (e.g., unregistered births)"</sub>

## Connected BIC/LEI/unique official identifier

- **descriptive / requirement**: INR.16 retains the reference to 'connected BICs' rather than 'published BICs' because connected BICs have a more robust validation and due diligence framework. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ultimately deciding to maintain ‘connected BICs’ given the former’s more robust framework"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: For domestic transfers (paragraphs 10-12 of INR.16, unchanged), only the originator's account number or a transaction reference number need accompany the payment message if originator information can be made available by other means within three business days of a request from the beneficiary FI or appropriate authority. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "within three business days upon request from the beneficiary FI or the appropriate authority"</sub>

## Virtual account numbers

- **expected / requirement**: The virtual account number principle was aligned with the ISO standard on IBAN by emphasising the country where the financial institution servicing the account resides. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further aligning the language with the ISO standard on IBAN"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Payment messages should enable all relevant institutions and authorities to identify the respective servicing financial institutions and their countries of location; this principle now sits in new paragraph 7 of INR.16. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "payment messages should enable all relevant institutions and authorities to identify the respective servicing financial institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Using virtual account numbers can hide where a customer's account is really located, which stops financial institutions and authorities from seeing the real nature of a transaction, for example that it is cross-border. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can obscure the true location of a customer’s account preventing financial institutions and authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The FATF does not intend to require country information in all types of account numbers (e.g., those without a country code) or to ban legitimate use of virtual account numbers such as virtual IBANs. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "nor to ban the legitimate use of virtual account numbers (such as virtual IBANs)"</sub>

## Cross-border Cash Withdrawals

- **descriptive / context**: For cross-border cash withdrawals, lack of systematic access to cardholder information challenges FIs and jurisdictions; international cooperation usually starts only once an investigation is underway, so suspicious activity may go undetected without information available under R.16. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lack of systematic and efficient access to cardholder information poses challenges"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Without cardholder information, authorities must make multiple requests to foreign counterparts to identify parties and flows before reaching the threshold for a formal investigation, making investigations and prosecutions slower or even impossible. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities would need to make multiple requests to foreign counterpart authorities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Revised R.16 introduces a targeted framework for cross-border cash withdrawals requiring the cardholder's name to be sent to the acquiring financial institution upon request, within three business days of receiving the request. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "send cardholder’s name to the acquiring financial institution upon request, within three business days"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The cardholder-name framework also supports financial intelligence, investigation or prosecution in the jurisdiction of a suspected cross-border cash withdrawal, as FIUs/LEAs need more than transaction records and card numbers. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU/LEAs need to be able to request additional information, such as cardholder name"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Typology: Person X, resident in country A, opens accounts in countries B and C, deposits funds and gets payment cards, returns to country A and makes frequent or large ATM cash withdrawals there using those foreign-issued cards. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "make frequent or large cash withdrawals at ATMs in their home country A"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In the cross-border cash withdrawal typology, under current R.16 FIs in country A have no cardholder identity information, only the issuers in B and C do, so activity is fragmented and Person X can circumvent country A's AML/CFT controls. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "no information on the identity of the cardholder is available to financial institutions in country A"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Per footnote 59, the responsibilities in INR.16 Section D (ordering, intermediary and beneficiary FIs) do not apply to cash withdrawal transactions. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not applicable to the transactions of cash withdrawals"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Cardholder name can support detection of structuring or other suspicious activity through ongoing monitoring; an acquiring FI that detects suspicious transactions can request the cardholder name to enrich suspicious transaction reporting. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the institution can request the cardholder name for enriched suspicious transaction reporting"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF dropped the more extensive requirements previously proposed under former 19 (c) after many respondents warned of significant unintended consequences for cash access and data protection and privacy. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "more extensive requirements previously proposed under the former 19 (c)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For cash withdrawals, the 'beneficiary FI' is the FI servicing or sponsoring the ATM where cash is withdrawn; an ATM operator that is not an FI is not required to receive cardholder information. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FI servicing or sponsoring the ATM where cash is withdrawn"</sub>

## Exemption for card payments for purchase of goods or services

- **descriptive / context**: In February 2024 the FATF proposed that, as a condition of the card exemption, the name and location of issuing and acquiring FIs accompany the transfer, and that the exemption cover only purchases 'from merchants', not person-to-person card transfers. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the name and location of the issuing and acquiring financial institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The FATF was concerned that the card exemption had gone beyond its intended scope and was enabling uncontrolled cross-border transfers that circumvented travel rule requirements. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enabling uncontrolled cross-border transfers, circumventing the travel rule requirements"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Instead of including issuer and acquirer name and location in card payment messages, the FATF will ensure access to that information through existing or future card network directory systems, so FIs, and through them FIUs and LEAs on request, can obtain it. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leveraging existing information directory systems developed or to be developed by card networks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Not requiring issuer/acquirer data in card payment messages reflects proportionality, given the very high cost of changing the standard or building parallel mechanisms. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "very high cost the inclusion of this information in card payment messages would have"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 54 for paragraph 16 of INR.16 explains what 'purchase of goods or services' means, delineating the card exemption scope in lieu of a definition; the exemption covers credit, debit or prepaid card purchases, both domestic and cross-border. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "footnote 54 for paragraph 16 of INR.16"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Where cards are used to fund other types of payment or value transfer, such as a person-to-person transfer, the relevant payment or value transfer information requirements apply rather than the card exemption. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In cases where cards are used to fund other types of payment or value transfer"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Second-consultation respondents welcomed removal of the term 'merchant' and use of directory services; most stressed that card network cooperation will be crucial to implementation. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "future cooperation of card networks will be crucial for the implementation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The term 'merchant' was dropped because no definition could be comprehensive enough and one risked unintended consequences for acquiring FIs' existing merchant due-diligence practices. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "no definition could be sufficiently comprehensive"</sub>

## Application of R.16 to instant payments

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF decided not to extend the goods-or-services exemption to instant payments because instant payment mechanisms are still evolving and vary in risk factors and controls (e.g., use cases, amounts, reversibility). (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "decided not to proceed with such an extension"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Instant payments for goods and services can already benefit from the more lenient regimes for domestic payments and for cross-border payments below a de minimis threshold, and a large majority of instant payment transactions do so. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A large majority of transactions on instant payment systems benefit from lighter requirements"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF stresses responsible innovation, including instant payment systems having sufficient transparency and robust AML/CFT/CPF controls, for example using modern analytics and richer ISO 20022 data flows. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leveraging modern analytics and richer data flows supported by ISO 20022"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF will, as a priority, work on risks and controls of different payment mechanisms to more closely align R.16 requirements for purchases of goods or services across mechanisms, including instant payments. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF will, as a priority, conduct further work on the risks and controls"</sub>

## De minimis threshold

- **descriptive / context**: FATF members agreed to better delineate the scope of the de minimis threshold in paragraph 8 (cross-border transfers) and paragraph 10 (domestic transfers) of INR.16, clarifying that it applies to both cross-border and domestic transfers but not to the targeted requirement for cross-border cash withdrawals. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "better delineate the scope of the de minimis threshold in paragraph 8 (cross-border transfers) and 10 (domestic transfers) of INR.16"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Countries will continue to be able to impose lower, risk-based requirements for domestic payments, and for cross-border payments no higher than USD/EUR 1,000. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "impose lower, risk-based requirements for domestic payments and cross-border payments no higher than USD/EUR 1,000"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Respondents sought clarity on how de minimis thresholds relate to other thresholds, such as occasional transaction thresholds, and on how to minimise financial exclusion; FATF will address this in guidance. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "how the de minimis thresholds relate to other thresholds, such as those on occasional transactions"</sub>

## Verification of alignment of information

- **expected / requirement**: Revised R.16 introduces alignment check obligations with options for FIs: post-validation checks, holistic ongoing monitoring, and pre-validation checks such as Confirmation of Payee systems. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "post-validation checks, holistic ongoing monitoring, and pre-validation checks"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Pre- or post-validation alignment checks are intended to occur for each transaction, while holistic monitoring is ongoing; check outcomes should inform any follow-up action by the beneficiary FI. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pre or post-validation checks are intended to take place for each transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Holistic monitoring was added because private sector feedback highlighted its effectiveness in detecting anomalous accounts, transactions and activity using many data points including misaligned beneficiary information. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "effectiveness of holistic monitoring frameworks seeking to detect anomalous accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: 'Alignment' in INR.16 is a flexible, graduated, risk- and context-sensitive concept and is not limited to exact name-matching. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is a flexible and graduated concept, sensitive to risk and context"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The alignment obligations sit within the beneficiary FI obligations section, and detecting misdirected payments was added to the chapeau in paragraph 30 of INR.16. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "detecting misdirected payments also added to the chapeau in paragraph 30 of INR.16"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Many respondents misread the proposal as mandating CoP/VoP for all ordering FIs; FATF's intent was a flexible requirement met by pre- or post-validation checks. Respondents also noted CoP has limited availability for cross-border transactions. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "limited availability yet for cross-border transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The targeted scope of alignment checks is name and account number, and the ability to rely on pre-validation solutions where available was retained. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a targeted scope for alignment (name and account number)"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Pre-validation checks may be used where the ordering and beneficiary FIs both participate in a pre-validation mechanism allowing per-transaction checks of the beneficiary name and account number in the payment message. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both participate in a pre-validation mechanism that enables them to check for each transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FATF states fraud cases have grown significantly in number and value and fraud is now the dominant type of proceeds-generating crime globally, citing Illicit Financial Flows from Cyber-Enabled Fraud (2023). (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fraud is now the dominant type of proceeds-"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The flexible alignment requirement aims to help reduce errors and misdirected payments, and to ensure FIs and jurisdictions unable to implement one of the options do not lose access to payment systems. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not suffer any loss of access to payment systems as a result"</sub>

## Definition of the payment chain

- **descriptive / context**: The February 2024 proposal offered two options for the payment chain start: the instruction route (FI receiving the customer instruction) or the funding route (FI from which customer funds are provided); feedback said the funding route was more complicated and ill-suited to hybrid chains. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the funding route would make implementation significantly more complicated"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Under paragraph 6 of INR.16, the payment chain begins with the FI that receives an instruction from the customer and ends with the FI that services the beneficiary's account or provides cash to the beneficiary. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the payment chain begins with the financial institution which receives an instruction from the customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.16 requires that the required information be included in a payment instruction and flow from the start to the end of the payment or value transfer. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must flow from the start to the end of the payment or value transfer"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Payment chain start and end points may be payment accounts and electronic money wallets rather than traditional bank accounts, as MVTS providers increasingly collect and disburse funds electronically. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could also include such as payment accounts and electronic money wallets"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Cross-border payment chains are often effectively broken into two or more domestic transfers, so participating FIs lack full information on the ultimate originator and/or beneficiary. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "broken into two or more domestic transfers"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: MVTS or other providers commonly send transfers without naming the ultimate originator, and customers send to them without naming the ultimate beneficiary, which can obscure the jurisdiction of origin or destination and impede screening, monitoring, supervision and law enforcement. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "send transfers to other financial institutions without specifying the name of the ultimate originator"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: R.16 requires cross-border payment instructions to include the beneficiary's account number or, absent an account, a unique transaction reference number. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in the absence of an account, a unique transaction reference number should be included"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FI that services the beneficiary's account means the account which settles the payment or value transfer with the beneficiary. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the account which settles the payment or value transfer with the beneficiary"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Following adoption of the instruction route, a footnote requires information on the origin of a transaction's funds to be included in payment messages; guidance will cover complex or hybrid chains including virtual assets and fiat currency. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on the origin of a transaction’s funds be included in payment messages"</sub>
