## Foundational elements of proliferation financing risk mitigation

- **expected / requirement**: In line with Recommendation 2 and its Interpretive Note, countries should have an inter-agency framework for mitigating PF risks, with effective co-operation among relevant bodies such as supervisors, import/export control and licensing authorities, customs, border controls and intelligence agencies, where possible. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should have an inter-agency framework in place to mitigate proliferation financing risks more effectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Close co-operation among competent authorities facilitates information exchange that can help initiate and pursue investigations into potential TFS violations. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This could help initiate and pursue investigations into potential violations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs should be supervised or monitored for full compliance with TFS obligations, and failures to comply should attract appropriate civil, administrative or criminal sanctions where required. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to comply should result in appropriate civil, administrative or criminal sanctions where required."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should consider PF risks faced by FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs in supervision, and periodically review the frequency, depth and intensity of supervision and the level of sanctions for compliance failures. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be reviewed periodically to ensure that risks are adequately addressed and mitigated"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The guidance states that regular, in-depth training by public and private sectors on TFS obligations and risks, for supervisors, customs and export control, financial intelligence and regulatory authorities, and FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs, should help build capacity and lead to better overall compliance with the TFS regime. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should help build capacity and lead to better overall compliance with the TFS regime"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Understanding public/private training needs and identifying priority areas for expanded training may advance the effective implementation of controls to mitigate PF risks. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Understanding public/private training needs and identifying priority areas for expanded training"</sub>

## Mitigating specific sanctions evasion risks at national level

- **descriptive / context**: Operational and strategic co-ordination among CPF authorities helps them respond to assistance requests and identify networks, funding channels and evasion avenues of designated persons; exchange between export control authorities and other competent authorities can uncover TFS evasion cases. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "effective exchange of actionable information between export controls authorities and relevant competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Authorities' own databases, such as export licence denials due to suspected links with designated persons, past evasion cases and suspected violations, can when shared in a timely way build a comprehensive picture of evasion trends and methods. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cases where export licences were denied due to suspected linkages with designated persons and entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Public-private information sharing partnerships let governments share typologies, evasion indicators and best practices so firms can analyse their own customer and transaction records for current and historical illicit activity including sanctions evasion, while the public side can issue targeted guidance. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which can then analyse their own customer and transaction records to identify current and historical potentially illicit activity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Suspected PF identified by firms through such analysis can be shared with the public sector within the domestic framework; such exchanges should be subject to legal requirements including data protection and privacy, and proper evaluation and verification. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such exchanges of information should be subject to legal requirements (including data protection and privacy considerations)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should conduct outreach to FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs explaining key elements of their TFS programmes, including the action required when a match against designated persons is found. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the action required if financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs find a match"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs should be able to obtain timely guidance from competent authorities, including supervisors, on potential matches, helping avoid inadvertent breaches and building public-private trust. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be able to access timely guidance from relevant competent authorities (including supervisors) on potential matches"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A key TFS implementation challenge is preventing evasion by ensuring FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs implement CDD adequately to establish ultimate beneficial ownership of customers. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are able to ascertain the ultimate beneficial owner of a customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Designated persons and their agents use offshore accounts, joint ventures with accessory or unaware third-party companies, shell and front companies, dummy accounts and strawmen to hide true beneficial owners and access the regulated financial system (citing UNSCR 1718 PoE May 2020 Report Section IV). (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dummy accounts and strawmen to access the regulated financial system"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: All countries should fully comply with the FATF Recommendations on transparency of beneficial ownership of legal persons and legal arrangements. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All countries should comply fully with the FATF Recommendations relevant in ensuring the transparency of beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Where TFS evasion risk cannot be mitigated by the private sector, countries could take regulatory actions such as limiting business relationships or transactions posing unacceptably high evasion risk. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "limiting business relationships or financial transactions) if they pose an unacceptably high risk of sanctions evasion"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Countries could issue regulatory or supervisory directives requiring specific measures such as EDD, transaction monitoring and screening, complemented by guidance and best practice papers. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such directives should be complemented by relevant guidance and best practice papers"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Supervisory actions could include additional or thematic inspections on at-risk business units, restricting activities of negligent firms and enhanced monitoring of firms. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "additional/thematic inspections focused on at-risk business units"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs, as front lines against PF, identify circumstances where customers and transactions may present PF risks and that their sanctions policies, controls and procedures address them, in accordance with national legislation. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs are at the front lines of combating proliferation financing."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Countries should provide relevant information such as sanitised case examples, typologies and national risk assessment results to help firms understand PF risks. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sanitised case examples, typologies, results of national risk assessments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs should understand the context and sources of their PF risk exposure and mitigate accordingly; measures could include improved onboarding (including beneficial owners), enhanced CDD, effective customer master data maintenance, regular checks of sanctions screening effectiveness, and leveraging existing compliance programmes. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regular controls to ensure effectiveness of procedures for sanctions screening"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Low risk FIs, DNFBPs and VASPs, such as small firms serving mainly local lower-risk customers, are not expected to devote significant resources to mitigation and may reasonably rely on public records and customer-supplied information when screening against designation lists. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not expected to devote a significant amount of time and resources to risk mitigation"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Most low risk institutions can reasonably be expected to maintain sanctions screening and CDD measures without deploying enhanced measures. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without the need to deploy enhanced measures despite the existence of low risk"</sub>

## Risk mitigation in case of low risk

- **permissive / requirement**: FATF Standards allow countries to exempt a type of FI, DNFBP or VASP from requirements to identify, assess, monitor, manage and mitigate PF risks, provided there is a proven low PF risk for it. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided there is a proven low proliferation financing risk"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Despite any exemption, full application of targeted financial sanctions under Recommendation 7 is mandatory in all cases. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "full application of the targeted financial sanctions as required by Recommendation 7 is mandatory in all cases"</sub>

## Mitigating the risks of a potential breach or non-implementation of sanctions

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Sanctions breaches and non-implementation typically result from inadequate internal controls: weak CDD and record keeping, screening delays, inadequate transaction monitoring and screening, out-of-date sanctions lists and inaccurate name matching. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use of out-of-date sanctions lists and lack of accuracy in matching names"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Mitigating breach/non-implementation risk essentially requires building sound processes and internal controls and ensuring they are followed. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mitigating these risks essentially requires building sound processes and internal controls"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF Standards require TFS implementation without delay; where domestic frameworks allow, firms could incorporate UN designation changes into monitoring systems without waiting for national transposition or communication. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without waiting for national transposition or communication"</sub>

## Risk mitigation in case of low risk

- **recommended / recommendation**: Countries should consider using the flexibility in the FATF Standards to exempt institutions with proven low PF risk, in a timely and responsive manner; the national risk assessment should provide background to identify low-risk situations and help in understanding financial inclusion products and the risks of financial exclusion. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should consider using the flexibility provided in the FATF Standards in a timely and responsive manner"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Because risk profiles can change over time, countries should monitor exemptions granted from PF risk requirements. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As risk profiles can change over time, countries should monitor such exemptions."</sub>

## Mitigating the risks of a potential breach or non-implementation of sanctions

- **expected / expectation**: Training is fundamental, especially for staff in onboarding, relationship management, transaction monitoring and screening and risk assessment; staff should know PF risks, TFS breach/evasion typologies and required mitigation, and training can be folded into existing sanctions or AML/CFT modules. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These training programmes can be rolled into the existing sanctions training or wider AML/CFT training modules."</sub>

## Mitigating the risks of evasion of sanctions

- **descriptive / expectation**: Mitigating sanctions evasion risk does not imply a "zero-failure" approach; it aims to reduce risk as far as reasonable and practicable through a proportionate approach. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mitigating sanctions evasion risks does not imply a “zero-failure” approach."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Even with good risk understanding, robust compliance and sound due diligence, firms may fail to detect all TFS evasion, which still creates financial, legal and reputational risk; risk rises when firms do not understand evasion schemes or how to apply tailored risk-based measures. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they might not be able to detect all potential evasion of targeted financial sanctions"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Higher-risk firms may proactively incorporate government guidance, risk indicators, typologies and UN Panel of Experts reports into risk management; these practices should be tailored to their risk profile and periodically reviewed. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "periodically reviewed to ensure they remain relevant and up-to-date with current trends"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Investment in machine learning and AI analytics may strengthen compliance at large, complex, higher PF-risk firms, enabling identification of linkages, building PF scenarios and recognising patterns such as transaction times, value, purpose, counterparties and geolocation. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "capable of machine learning and artificial intelligence to conduct analysis"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Stripping is the deliberate changing or removal of information in a payment or instruction to obscure the originator/beneficiary identity or their connection to designated persons. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Stripping is the deliberate act of changing or removing information from a payment or instruction"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As designated persons increasingly use advanced deception including wire/payment stripping to hide identities and beneficial owners, firms should be vigilant and deploy appropriate tools. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be vigilant to such risks and deploy appropriate tools to address such risks"</sub>

## Enhanced customer due diligence

- **descriptive / limitation**: Because designated persons create complex networks of opaquely owned corporate entities, screening against designation lists may be insufficient to manage PF-related TFS breach and evasion risk. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "screening against list of designated entities is insufficient to properly manage the risk"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Firms should consider adding PF-TFS-specific risk indicators to customer onboarding and ongoing monitoring criteria; some have already adapted CDD and transaction monitoring to detect TFS violations and evasion. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider using additional Proliferation financing – TFS specific risk indicators"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The scope of internal controls including CDD should reflect the nature of the business; e.g. small low-risk firms with regular customers making repeat micro-transactions tied to pay cycles may lack a board or sophisticated compliance function. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a pattern of repeat micro-transactions often linked to a pay or salary cycle"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should use a PF risk assessment to guide their compliance regime and employee awareness of risks and exposed customers. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use a proliferation financing risk assessment to guide institutional compliance regimes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where necessary firms should apply enhanced measures: more customer and relationship information, more frequent ID updates of customer and beneficial owner, source of funds and wealth, reasons for transactions, senior management approval, enhanced monitoring, and counterparty FI information requests where allowed. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtaining the approval of senior management to commence or continue business relationship"</sub>

## Correspondent banking relationships

- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 37: FATF PF requirements are limited to Recommendations 1, 2, 7 and 15; correspondent banking issues here are not assessed under Recommendation 13. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The requirements of the FATF Standards relating to proliferation financing are limited to Recommendations 1, 2, 7 and 15."</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Not all correspondent relationships present uniform or unacceptably high PF risk, and banks should not avoid respondents simply because their jurisdictions are perceived high risk. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it does not mean that all correspondent banking relationships present a uniform or unacceptably high risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Correspondent relationship risk should be assessed case by case, always considering the respondent's internal controls and mitigation, and correspondents should conduct ongoing due diligence including periodic CDD reviews per the 2016 FATF Guidance on Correspondent Banking Services (paragraph 29). (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk assessment of correspondent relationships should be done on a case-by-case basis"</sub>

## Shell and front companies

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Shell companies are quick and simple to set up, allow anonymous business, and are often used briefly for a transaction or series; designated persons have used extensive shell networks. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Often, these companies are abused for a brief period of time"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Failing to conduct thorough due diligence required under R.10, such as understanding the business and identifying beneficial owners, may let designated persons' involvement go undetected and cause significant compliance failures. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to conduct thorough due diligence, as required under R.10"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where appropriate, firms should supplement list-based screening with additional due diligence such as enhanced CDD, understand customers' business, and identify and verify authorised signatories and beneficial owners, vigilantly at onboarding and throughout the relationship. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should supplement the reliance on list-based screening by additional due diligence measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Company service providers, lawyers and accountants forming or managing legal persons/arrangements face particular risk, as structures may obscure ownership, lack economic purpose or exist to evade sanctions, and designated persons seek such professionals for respectability. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Designated entities and individuals seek the involvement of these professionals to provide respectability"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: These service providers should have internal policies to obtain beneficial ownership information and understand customers' true business and ownership and control structures, per national legislation. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these service providers should have internal policies and procedures to obtain information on the beneficial owners"</sub>

## SECTION THREE: SUPERVISION OF PROLIFERATION FINANCING RISK ASSESSMENT AND MITIGATION

- **descriptive / context**: Section Three gives general guidance on supervision and monitoring of PF risk assessment and mitigation by supervisors and SRBs, noting that mitigating sanctions evasion risk is not a "zero-failure" approach. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be supervised or monitored by supervisors and SRBs"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: Supervisors can assess firms' PF risk assessments within existing sanctions or financial crime compliance programmes, without obliging a separate assessment or dedicated PF compliance staff. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It need not oblige financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs to do a separate risk assessment"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Supervisors should consider relevant aspects of FATF's AML/CFT risk-based supervision guidance (2021 FATF Risk-based Supervision Guidance) when designing PF supervision; footnote 39 notes these PF supervision issues are not assessed under Recommendations 26, 27, 28 and 35. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not to be assessed under Recommendations 26, 27, 28 and 35"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should maintain an up-to-date understanding of the PF risk landscape and periodically identify and assess risk across sectors and individual entities, considering exposure and internal control efficacy. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtain and maintain an upto-date understanding of the proliferation financing risks landscape"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms' PF risk classification should feed into supervisory intensity and frequency, with lower-risk institutions receiving less frequent or intense scrutiny. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lower-risk institutions should attract less supervisory attention"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should keep risk assessment dynamic using internal and external information, such as NRAs, off-site and on-site data from firms, examination results, and FIU information including typologies and STR feedback. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should keep the risk assessment process dynamic"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should focus on effectiveness of internal controls, TFS screening, onboarding, and transaction monitoring and screening, and review whether CDD adequately identifies and verifies customers and beneficial owners, builds risk profiles and keeps information updated. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should focus on the effectiveness of internal controls, targeted financial sanctions screening processes"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: PF risks may be distributed differently from ML/TF risks within and between institutions, so PF supervision may need to focus on different business units and products than AML/CFT supervision. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PF risks may be distributed differently from ML/TF risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should use outreach, guidance and information sharing so institutions understand PF risks, and should consider institutions' and sectors' CPF capacity, experience and TFS understanding when building supervisory programmes. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consider the capacity and the counter proliferation financing experience of the supervised institutions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Based on supervisory risk assessment, supervisors should determine methodology and tools (e.g. questionnaires, off-site reporting, interviews, sample testing, on-site visits) and set intensity, type and frequency according to risk. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "questionnaires, off-site reporting, interviews, sample testing, on-site visits"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should assess board and senior management oversight of PF matters and adequacy of escalation of PF issues to them. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adequacy of escalation of proliferation financing-related issues to board and senior management"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Supervisors should focus on how institutions identify and manage legitimate matches and false positives in screening, and their ability to identify designated persons. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identification and management of legitimate matches and false positives during screening"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For DNFBP sectors, supervisors and SRBs should note vulnerabilities in company formation services provided by company service providers, lawyers and accountants. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the vulnerabilities associated with company formation services"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where weaknesses in risk assessment or mitigation are found, supervisors should follow up and assess the robustness of remediation and prevention of recurrence. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assess the robustness of remedial actions taken to rectify the deficiencies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: For compliance failures, supervisors should have a broad range of measures including administrative sanctions and licence withdrawal; proper enforcement encourages a compliance culture. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "administrative sanctions, withdrawal of licenses to operate"</sub>

## Annex A. FATF Recommendations on Counter Proliferation Financing

- **expected / requirement**: Recommendation 1 (PF extract): countries should identify, assess and understand their PF risks and take commensurate mitigating action, including designating an authority or mechanism to coordinate risk assessment and allocating resources efficiently. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including designating an authority or mechanism to coordinate actions to assess risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: R.1 states countries should require FIs and DNFBPs to identify, assess and take effective action to mitigate their ML, TF and PF risks. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identify, assess and take effective action to mitigate their money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INR.1: Recommendation 7 obligations impose strict, non-risk-based requirements on all natural and legal persons; risk-based PF measures by FIs and DNFBPs reinforce and complement full R.7 implementation by detecting and preventing TFS breach or evasion. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "place strict requirements on all natural and legal persons, which are not risk-based"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.1: FIs and DNFBPs should have processes to identify, assess, monitor, manage and mitigate PF risks, which may sit within existing TFS or compliance programmes; countries should ensure full R.7 implementation in any risk scenario. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have in place processes to identify, assess, monitor, manage and mitigate proliferation financing risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 1, where countries identify higher PF risks, they should ensure that they adequately address such risks. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where countries identify higher risks, they should ensure that they adequately address such risks."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 1, where countries identify lower PF risks, they should ensure the measures applied are commensurate with the level of risk, while still ensuring full implementation of the targeted financial sanctions required by Recommendation 7. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while still ensuring full implementation of the targeted financial sanctions as required in Recommendation 7"</sub>

## A. Obligations and decisions for countries

- **expected / requirement**: INR.1: countries should identify and assess PF risks on an ongoing basis to inform CPF regime changes, assist resource allocation and prioritisation, and inform FI and DNFBP risk assessments; they should keep assessments current and share results with competent authorities, SRBs, FIs and DNFBPs. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inform potential changes to the country’s CPF regime, including changes to laws, regulations and other measures"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: INR.1: countries should manage and mitigate identified PF risks, develop a shareable understanding of means of TFS breach, evasion and non-implementation, and ensure FIs and DNFBPs identify higher-risk circumstances and address them in their CPF regime. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should develop an understanding of the means of potential breaches, evasion and non-implementation"</sub>
