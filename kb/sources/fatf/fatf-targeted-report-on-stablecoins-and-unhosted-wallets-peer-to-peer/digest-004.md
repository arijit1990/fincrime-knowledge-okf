## For Jurisdictions

- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should apply Recommendation 15 to entities involved in stablecoin arrangements, so that stablecoin issuers, intermediary VASPs and other relevant participants are subject to clear, enforceable AML/CFT obligations. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should apply R.15 to entities involved in stablecoin arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should define the roles and responsibilities of all participants across the stablecoin ecosystem and impose appropriate AML/CFT obligations using a risk-based approach. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should define the roles and responsibilities of all participants throughout the stablecoin ecosystem"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should also consider requiring stablecoin issuers to implement allow-lists and deny-lists as appropriate, using a risk-based approach. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring stablecoin issuers to implement allow-lists and deny-lists as appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should institutionalise rigorous pre-launch and pre-licensing supervision and compliance reviews, to mitigate stablecoin ML/TF risks before launch. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "institutionalise rigorous pre-launch and pre-licensing supervision and compliance reviews"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should prioritise developing robust technical capabilities in competent authorities, including supervisors and LEAs: expertise on evolving risks, typologies, business models, smart contract functionalities and cross-chain transaction mechanics, and stronger capability to use blockchain analysis tools effectively. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should prioritise the development of robust technical capabilities by competent authorities"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should consider, when necessary, establishing multinational supervisory colleges to supervise stablecoin issuers and other entities involved in cross-border stablecoin arrangements. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establishing multinational supervisory colleges to supervise stablecoin issuers"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should give competent authorities, especially supervisors and LEAs, the tools to cooperate swiftly with domestic and international counterparts, including through established channels, MOUs and legal provisions enabling prompt exchange of information on stablecoin arrangements. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "necessary tools to cooperate swiftly with domestic and international counterparts"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should actively monitor existing macro vulnerabilities and loopholes in the stablecoin ecosystem, including the volume and risk of P2P transactions via unhosted wallets and the use of informal or unlicensed/unregistered redemption channels. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "actively monitor existing macro vulnerabilities and loopholes in the stablecoin ecosystem"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says supervisors should understand and assess the ML/TF/PF risks of business models that allow stablecoins to be used for payment and investment purposes, and apply proportionate mitigating measures. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should understand and assess the ML/TF/PF risks presented by business models"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should consider establishing structured public-private partnerships between competent authorities and stablecoin ecosystem stakeholders on typologies, risk indicators and emerging threats. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establishing structured public-private partnerships to strengthen cooperation between competent authorities"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should also consider creating tactical partnerships, particularly when investigations involve off-chain transactions or the freezing or burning of stablecoins. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "creating tactical partnerships, particularly when investigations involve off-chain transactions"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should consider AML/CFT measures tailored to stablecoin issuers and other obliged entities. Examples are obliging issuers to keep, and be able to carry out, the technical ability to burn, freeze and withdraw tokens in the secondary market; to apply CDD when tokens are redeemed; or to restrict issuance on high-risk chains. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring issuers to maintain and execute the technical capability to burn, freeze, and withdraw in the secondary market"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should weigh up, depending on risk and context, whether stablecoin issuers ought to be obliged to monitor proactively where their stablecoins are and how they are used in the secondary market, supported by blockchain analytics tools, and whether proportionate extra reporting duties should apply to them. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proactively monitor the location and use of their stablecoins in the secondary market"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says jurisdictions should consider, where appropriate, establishing sandboxes with industry participants to develop standards and solutions that enable innovation while safeguarding against misuse, for example through smart contract monitoring, blockchain forensics or automated sanctions screening. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establishing sandboxes with industry participants, where appropriate, to develop standards and solutions"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says VASPs and FIs should understand the different types of stablecoin arrangements and the ML/TF/PF risks at the issuance, circulation and redemption stages. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understand the different types of stablecoin arrangements and the ML/TF/PF risks at the issuance, circulation and redemption stages"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says VASPs and FIs should apply adequate and robust AML/CFT/CPF measures to all entities involved in stablecoin arrangements and transactions. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "apply adequate and robust AML/CFT/CPF measures to all entities involved in stablecoin arrangements and transactions"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: The FATF report says stablecoin issuers should be required to assess and mitigate the ML/TF/PF risks associated with their customers. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Stablecoin issuers should be required to assess and mitigate ML/TF/PF risks associated with their customers"</sub>

## For the Private Sector

- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says stablecoin issuers are encouraged to implement technical measures enabling them to block, freeze and withdraw stablecoins at any time (for example by burning and re-issuing), and to program smart contracts to include AML/CFT/CPF control measures such as CDD requirements. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "implement technical measures that enable them to block, freeze and withdraw"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The FATF report says stablecoin issuers should consider making public a centralised law enforcement contact that is monitored 24/7 and able to take measures to expeditiously freeze stablecoins. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "making public a centralised contact for law enforcement that is monitored 24/7"</sub>

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unusual transaction indicator 13: multiple transactions converting stablecoins into one or more VAs with simultaneous transfer of those assets, even in small unit amounts, until the balance reaches zero, especially if the funds used to buy the stablecoins came from legal tender transfers from different accounts. - **descriptive / typology or red flag**: TF/PF indicator 1: numerous donations (often USD 100-15,000 equivalents) to frequently changing wallets that nonetheless share artifacts (QR codes, domains or change addresses), with funds then routed through DEX routers and bridges before reconverging for cash out.
