## 4. Reducing barriers to innovation

- **descriptive / context**: Promising innovations that could address data-framework frictions include technologies for data sharing in protected environments, full payment traceability, improved customer verification and pre-payment validation. Their development and scaling depends on significant private sector effort, including willingness to invest financial and human resources and to bear legal and regulatory risk from unproven technologies. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "technologies that allow for data sharing in protected environments, full payment traceability"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Additional costs of navigating competing legal and regulatory requirements affect investment decisions on launching innovative products, and investing in these technologies carries opportunity cost from foregoing other product development. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "additional cost involved in navigating competing legal and regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Given these costs and risks, participants may not pursue even the most promising technologies absent a supportive legal, regulatory and policy environment. The report says it will be important to create space for innovation while maintaining security and consumer protection, through actions ranging from positive 'signalling' by regulators to regulatory exemptions and safe harbours. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may not pursue even the most promising new technologies absent a supportive legal, regulatory and policy environment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Support structures vary widely: many jurisdictions have regulatory sandboxes (typically letting firms experiment in a controlled setting, usually with activity limits, under regulatory supervision) or innovation offices (a channel for engaging regulators on new technology or novel business models), authorities have hosted 'tech-sprints', and the BIS Innovation Hub has projects targeting the frictions described. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "typically allow firms to experiment with cutting-edge technology in a controlled setting"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Industry feedback says sandboxes, innovation offices and tech-sprints often lack cross-border regulatory coordination for products operating in multiple jurisdictions, and there is a lack of mechanisms supporting the transition from sandboxes or tech sprints to operating live in the market. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they often lack cross-border regulatory coordination to facilitate the development of products"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Many countries have introduced fintech laws, regulations and guidance for a level playing field and clear regulatory pathways, such as new licences for non-bank PSPs (for example third-party providers that initiate payments) and regulation promoting open banking or open finance. The appropriate approach depends on national circumstances, including the domestic framework, the development and complexity of the financial sector, and frictions experienced. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The appropriate approach, or combination of approaches, will depend on national circumstances"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FSB stocktake identified open banking and open finance, which change how payments-related data are accessed and shared, as particularly promising for payment frictions; for example, many open banking initiatives are working on confirmation of payee solutions that can increase transparency and reduce erroneous or fraudulent payments. The CPMI will explore this area further. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confirmation of payee solutions that can increase transparency and reduce erroneous or fraudulent payments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Improving understanding of new technologies among public and private stakeholders is critical to supporting innovation; supervisory authorities may not be comfortable assessing technologies they are not yet expert in. The Forum could provide a channel for information sharing about technologies and best practices for regulators supporting innovation. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisory authorities, for example, may not be comfortable assessing new technologies"</sub>

## Box 2: Privacy Enhancing Technologies

- **descriptive / definition**: Privacy enhancing technologies (PETs) are a collection of digital technologies and approaches that permit collection, processing, analysis and sharing of information while protecting the confidentiality of personal data. The report calls them among the most promising innovations for data-related frictions in cross-border payments. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "permit collection, processing, analysis and sharing of information while protecting the confidentiality of personal data"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The OECD groups PETs into four categories, all possibly applicable to cross-border payments: (1) data obfuscation tools, including anonymisation or zero-knowledge proofs; (2) encrypted data processing tools, which run computations over data never visible or disclosed, the underlying data remaining unmodified but hidden by encryption; (3) federated and distributed analytics, which execute analytical tasks on data not visible to those running them so sensitive data stays with its source while third parties analyse it; (4) data accountability tools, giving new controls over how data can be gathered or used or providing transparency and immutability into transactions. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the OECD groups them into four categories, all of which may have applications for cross-border payments"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: One example of PETs addressing data frictions is the winning project in a challenge sponsored by the UK and US governments, which focused on enhancing cross-border data access to combat financial crime, including fraud. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "challenge sponsored by the UK and US governments"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Barriers to PET adoption in cross-border payments include a general lack of understanding among participants of both the technologies and their costs and benefits, and evolving PET regulation that makes many participants hesitate. Firms worry that using a new technology without a legal or regulatory safe harbour could raise legal and regulatory risk or heighten supervisors' expectations, and the report says lack of adoption is likely to hinder growth of the technology itself. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "absent a legal or regulatory safe harbour, could expose them to increased legal and regulatory risk"</sub>
