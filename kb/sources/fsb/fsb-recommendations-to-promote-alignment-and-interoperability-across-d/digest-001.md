## Executive summary

- **descriptive / context**: Enhancing the interaction between data frameworks and cross-border payments is a priority action of the G20 Roadmap for Enhancing Cross-Border Payments, which G20 Leaders endorsed in November 2020 to address the challenges of high costs, low speed, limited access and insufficient transparency relative to domestic payments. Greater alignment and interoperability of data frameworks would improve the efficiency of transferring payments data across borders and help reach the Roadmap targets while improving payment safety. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "high costs, low speed, limited access, and insufficient transparency"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FSB stocktake of national and regional data frameworks found frictions that pose significant challenges to the cost, speed, transparency and accessibility of cross-border payments. The report says some friction may be unavoidable and acceptable where regulation preserves transaction security, meets AML/CFT and sanctions objectives, and protects individual privacy, but the stocktake considered the extent of fragmentation across jurisdictions to be a major barrier. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be an unavoidable and acceptable consequence of regulations seeking to preserve the security of transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The recommendations were developed after engagement with the FATF, the Global Privacy Assembly (GPA), the OECD, the BIS Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures (CPMI), sanctions experts and financial authorities beyond FSB membership, with attention to authorities outside the financial services domain. The report was revised after a public consultation issued in July 2024. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Careful attention has been given to the involvement of authorities beyond the financial services domain"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The recommendations address four groups of frictions: (i) uncertainty about how to balance regulatory and supervisory obligations; (ii) promoting alignment and interoperability of regulatory and data requirements and their consistent and widespread implementation; (iii) mitigating restrictions on cross-border data flows; and (iv) reducing barriers to innovation. They aim to maintain safety and security of cross-border payments, and should materially enhance data flows when implemented together. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and (iv) reducing barriers to innovation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The actions and best practices include clear legal pathways to transfer payments-related data across borders and more effective collaboration among payments, data protection and privacy stakeholders. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clear legal pathways to transfer payments-related data across borders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB and its partners will establish a Forum on Cross-Border Payments Data. It will include public sector stakeholders in payments, AML/CFT, sanctions, and data privacy and protection, will coordinate and report on implementation of the recommendations and identify emerging issues, and will establish a private sector Advisory Group. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will establish a Forum on Cross-Border Payments Data"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Work has already begun on some of the recommended actions, including the FATF's revision of Recommendation 16 and the adoption of the CPMI's harmonised ISO 20022 data requirements. The report describes the recommendations as the start of a process for jurisdictions to identify actions and best practices for more efficient cross-border transfer of payments-related data while respecting public policy objectives on data use, storage and security. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including FATF’s revision of Recommendation 16 and the adoption of the CPMI’s harmonised ISO 20022 data requirements"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: The FSB published the Roadmap for Enhancing Cross-border Payments in October 2020, developed with the CPMI and other bodies to address high costs, low speed, limited access and insufficient transparency, and G20 Leaders endorsed it. Enhancing the interaction between data frameworks was later identified as a priority theme, supported by an FSB stocktake that found frictions arising from these frameworks. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identified priority themes to take the Roadmap forward"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The recommendations aim to mitigate unnecessary frictions from data frameworks without compromising the objectives of AML/CFT, sanctions and data protection and privacy rules, while accepting that a certain degree of friction may be unavoidable. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without compromising on the objectives underlying anti-money laundering/countering the financing of terrorism"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The proposed recommendations were published for comment in July 2024 and drew 34 public comments, six of them confidential, from banks, card networks, non-bank PSPs, trade associations, corporate registration service providers, public sector entities and data privacy advocacy groups located in the UK, US, Asia, Australia, EU and Africa. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "received 34 public comments, including six confidential comments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Category 1 of the recommendations (balancing obligations): market participants find it difficult to balance obligations under different data frameworks, for example AML/CFT and data protection and privacy; the recommendations aim to address conflicting requirements and help participants navigate different rules. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "difficulties in balancing their various obligations under different data frameworks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Category 2 (alignment and interoperability): implementation of rules and standards on the data accompanying a cross-border payment is not uniform across jurisdictions. Fragmentation has been seen in inconsistent implementation of FATF standards, messages and clearing requirements, and in data requirements for sanctions regimes. Recommendations here also cover interoperability of data protection and privacy regimes and appropriate cross-border transfer mechanisms for payments-related data. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inconsistent implementation of FATF standards, messages and clearing requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Category 3 (restrictions on data flow): requirements to store or process data in-country (data localisation) or to maintain data in the local jurisdiction (data mirroring) can undermine Roadmap objectives. They may serve legitimate public policy objectives but can make it harder to identify fraud, comply with AML/CFT and sanctions obligations, and manage risk enterprise-wide. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Measures that require data to be stored or processed in-country (data localisation)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Category 4 (barriers to innovation): technological innovations that might resolve data frictions appear difficult to implement; the recommendation aims to encourage progress on promising innovations and frameworks that support public and private sector work. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appear to be difficult to implement"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 7 says financial institutions include banks, financial market infrastructures (FMIs) and other entities providing financial services in payments. PSPs are banks and non-banks permitted to provide payment services, including remittance service providers, money services businesses and other MVTS providers, providers of prepaid transfers, pay later transfers such as credit card transfers, individuals providing payment services, and services for cash placement or withdrawal, issuing of payment instruments, acquiring of payment transactions, payment initiation and account information services. Third parties include, but are not limited to, entities involved in storage, transmission, analysis or other activity related to data relevant for cross-border payments. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PSPs are defined as banks and non-banks that are permitted to provide payment services"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Under Recommendation 1, the Forum would also include relevant stakeholders from international organisations (IOs), standard-setting bodies (SSBs), data protection and privacy authorities (DPAs) and regulatory agencies. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would also include relevant stakeholders from international organisations (IOs), SSBs, data protection and privacy authorities (DPAs)"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Under Recommendation 1, the Forum should develop channels for regular engagement with industry stakeholders involved in cross-border payments. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Forum should develop channels for regular engagement with industry stakeholders involved in cross border payments."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Forum's objective is to identify and discuss practical ways of mitigating frictions from different approaches to implementing laws, rules and requirements for collecting, storing and managing payment-related data, without compromising the overarching objectives of those frameworks. It will support implementation of the data framework recommendations and act as a hub for information exchange and policy research on the intersection of data governance and payment services, for example by fostering collaboration between domestic authorities, Forum stakeholders and academic experts and commissioning studies. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "act as a hub to promote and support the exchange of information and policy research"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Forum will identify areas of inconsistency in data frameworks and facilitate authorities' discussion on mitigating frictions while preserving transaction security, meeting AML/CFT and sanctions objectives, preventing fraud and protecting privacy. The report calls greater alignment of approaches essential to support innovation and says the Forum will also consider a process to identify and address newly emerging divergences (see Recommendation 2). (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Greater alignment of approaches to data frameworks related to cross-border payments is also essential to support innovation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Forum will comprise public sector experts in payments, AML/CFT, data protection and privacy, and sanctions, plus supervisory and regulatory authorities from FSB and non-FSB members. Each member organisation works under its own governance arrangements on possible policy actions, with coordination ensured among organisations leading work on a recommendation. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would work under its own governance arrangements with respect to possible policy actions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A group of private sector stakeholders will be identified to advise the Forum and, if relevant, specific working groups. The Forum will make efforts to ensure the private sector advisory body represents a range of market participants, diverse in geography (extending beyond G20 jurisdictions), size and business model. Footnote 8 notes the Advisory Group could include existing groups such as the FSB's Taskforce on Legal, Regulatory and Supervisory matters (LRS Taskforce) and other private stakeholders who are not LRS members. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "diversity in terms of geography (extending beyond G20 jurisdictions), size and business model"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The recommendation to establish the Forum responds to strong private and public sector interest in addressing unnecessary fragmentation in, and frictions among, data frameworks related to cross-border payments. In light of broad support in the public consultation, the FSB has started working with key stakeholders to develop an appropriate and agile organisational structure and plan for the Forum's initial work. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "develop an appropriate and agile organisational structure and plan for the initial work of the Forum"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report states that coordination within and beyond the Forum will be critical to its effectiveness; each member organisation would work under its own governance arrangements for possible policy actions, while coordination will be ensured among the organisations leading the work on a given recommendation. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Coordination _within_ and _beyond_ the Forum will be critical to its effectiveness"</sub>
