## 1. Introduction

- **descriptive / definition**: The FSB defines crypto-assets as a type of private sector digital asset that depend primarily on cryptography and distributed ledger or similar technology. Its February 2022 report concluded that crypto-asset markets are fast evolving and could reach a point where they threaten global financial stability. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are a type of private sector digital asset that depend primarily on cryptography and distributed ledger or similar technology"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The February 2022 G20 Finance Ministers and Central Bank Governors Communiqué encouraged the FSB, in close coordination with other standard-setting bodies, to accelerate and deepen work to monitor and share information on regulatory and supervisory approaches to unbacked crypto-assets, stablecoins, decentralized finance and other crypto-assets, and to address gaps and arbitrage, including by recommending coordinated and timely policy actions. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accelerate and deepen its work to monitor and share information on regulatory and supervisory approaches to unbacked crypto-assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On 11 July 2022 the FSB issued a public communication highlighting potential risks from crypto-assets, stressing that crypto-asset activities do not operate in a regulation-free space, expressing concern about lack of conformance with existing standards and rules, and stating that crypto-asset providers must not commence operations in any jurisdiction unless they meet all applicable regulatory requirements. It also reaffirmed the FSB's role in facilitating cooperation among jurisdictional authorities and standard-setting bodies (SSBs) so that activities are subject to effective regulation commensurate with risks while supporting responsible innovation and allowing flexibility for domestic approaches. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "crypto-assets providers must not commence operations in any jurisdiction unless any such service provider meets all applicable regulatory requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In October 2022 the FSB published a consultative report including high-level recommendations. The final report takes account of consultation feedback and stakeholder outreach and of events in crypto-asset markets in 2022 and early 2023, and reflects enhancements in key areas. It contains solely the high-level recommendations and the glossary; the analysis in Sections 2 and 3 of the consultative report remains valid and is not repeated. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This final report contains solely the high-level recommendations and the glossary, which are core components of the FSB framework."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB review of its High-level Recommendations on Global Stablecoin arrangements, published alongside this report, focuses on stablecoins as a subset of crypto-assets, whereas this report focuses on crypto-asset activities and markets more broadly. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "focus is on the crypto-asset activities and markets more broadly"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Because the FSB's mandate centres on regulatory, supervisory and oversight issues that help foster safe innovation, the report does not comprehensively address every specific risk category linked to crypto-asset activities. Categories it names as not comprehensively covered are AML/CFT, data privacy, cyber security, consumer and investor protection, market integrity, competition policy, taxation, monetary policy, monetary sovereignty and other macroeconomic concerns. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report therefore does not comprehensively address all specific risk categories related to crypto-asset activities, such as:"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB has worked closely with the IMF, World Bank, OECD, BCBS, CPMI, IOSCO and FATF so that work on monitoring and regulating crypto-asset activities and markets is coordinated and mutually supportive. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to ensure that the work underway regarding the monitoring and regulation of crypto-asset activities"</sub>

## 2.1. Objectives and scope

- **descriptive / context**: The recommendations seek to promote comprehensiveness and greater international consistency of regulatory and supervisory approaches to crypto-asset activities and markets, including issuers and service providers. They are intended to be flexible, to be incorporated into varied regulatory frameworks, and do not displace existing applicable frameworks. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not displace existing applicable regulatory, supervisory and oversight frameworks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The recommendations should be applied to crypto-asset issuers and service providers in a way that is proportionate to their risk, size, complexity and systemic importance. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issuers and service providers in a way that is proportionate to their risk, size, complexity and systemic importance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Crypto-asset activities that meet the FSB definition of a Global Stablecoin (GSC) arrangement should also be subject to regulatory and supervisory approaches implementing the FSB's GSC recommendations. Authorities may choose to apply the relevant GSC recommendations to stablecoin arrangements more widely, taking into account the risk, size and complexity of those stablecoins. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also be subject to regulatory and supervisory approaches that implement the"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The recommendations cover any type of crypto-asset in any jurisdiction, but central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) fall outside them. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) are not subject to these recommendations."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The recommendations should inform how any type of crypto-asset activity is regulated, including activity carried out through so-called decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols, where it poses or may pose financial stability risks individually or collectively. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should inform the regulation of any type of crypto-asset activities, including those conducted through so-called decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report notes that crypto-asset markets are developing quickly and may reach a point of threatening global financial stability, because of their scale, structural vulnerabilities and growing interconnectedness with the traditional financial system. Their rapid evolution and cross-border character also create potential for regulatory gaps, fragmentation and arbitrage, and financial stability risks could escalate quickly even though use of crypto-assets differs somewhat between jurisdictions. This is said to underline the need for timely and preemptive evaluation of possible policy responses, as well as regulatory action where existing requirements apply. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Crypto-asset markets are fast evolving and could reach a point where they represent a threat to global financial stability"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities need to be ready to regulate, supervise and oversee crypto-asset activities, and the related issuers and service providers, that have the potential to pose risks to financial stability. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities need to be ready to regulate, supervise, and oversee these activities and the associated issuers and service providers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 4 states that the terms crypto-asset issuers and service providers are defined in the Annex to the document. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Crypto-asset issuers and service providers are defined in the Annex of this document."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The recommendations are addressed to financial regulatory, supervisory and oversight authorities at a jurisdictional level. They set out key objectives of an effective framework but are high-level and flexible. They establish a global regulatory baseline, and some jurisdictions may decide to take more restrictive measures (for example, prohibiting certain or all crypto-asset activities). They aim to promote a framework that is technology-neutral and focuses on underlying activities and risks. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The recommendations establish a global regulatory baseline, and some jurisdictions may also decide to take more restrictive regulatory measures."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should seek to apply the recommendations consistent with their respective mandates. Effective application by authorities in jurisdictions where crypto-asset activities, issuers and service providers are active will help ensure comprehensive regulatory coverage and reduce scope for regulatory arbitrage or evasion. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should seek to apply the recommendations consistent with their respective mandates."</sub>

- **descriptive / context**: The FSB and SSBs will continue to encourage consistency and a common understanding of the key elements of comprehensive frameworks for crypto-asset activities and markets, and will support authorities in implementing the recommendations as activities and markets evolve. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will continue to encourage consistency and a common understanding of the key elements of comprehensive regulatory, supervisory and oversight frameworks"</sub>

## 2.1. Objectives and scope

- **descriptive / limitation**: The recommendations concentrate on financial stability risks and do not comprehensively cover other specific risk categories tied to crypto-asset activities: AML/CFT, data privacy, cyber security, consumer and investor protection, market integrity, competition policy, taxation, monetary policy, monetary sovereignty and other macroeconomic concerns. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not comprehensively cover all specific risk categories related to crypto-asset activities, such as: AML/CFT; data privacy"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB's view is that a full supervisory and regulatory framework which effectively handles those other policy objectives would make the crypto-asset market more stable and so lower the risk of negative spillovers to the wider financial system. The FSB says it supports efforts by standard-setting bodies and authorities towards such a framework, and gives as an example that rules on investor protection and market integrity can also lower financial stability risk by raising regulatory and public transparency. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will improve the stability of the crypto-asset market and thereby reduce the risks of negative spillovers to the wider financial system"</sub>

## 2.2. Follow-up and review

- **descriptive / context**: The FSB will, in close cooperation with relevant SSBs, (i) continue to coordinate international regulatory, supervisory and oversight approaches for crypto-asset activities so they are comprehensive, consistent and complementary, including by considering findings of vulnerability analysis work on DeFi and on crypto-asset service providers that combine multiple functions and whether additional policy work is warranted; and (ii) review implementation of the recommendations by end-2025, which may help determine whether a further review or further action to promote implementation may be necessary. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conduct a review of the implementation of the recommendations by end-2025"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Table 1 (follow-up work): the FSB will continue coordinating international regulatory and supervisory approaches to crypto-asset activities, and depending on its analysis of financial stability risks from DeFi and multi-function crypto-asset service providers will consider regulatory implications and whether additional policy work is warranted, with timeline by end-2024. The FSB will, in consultation with relevant SSBs and international organisations, review implementation of the recommendations in FSB jurisdictions and assess the need to update them, with timeline by end-2025. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Review the implementation of the recommendations"</sub>

## Recommendation 1: Regulatory powers and tools

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 1: authorities should have and utilise the appropriate powers and tools, and adequate resources, to regulate, supervise and oversee crypto-asset activities and markets and to enforce relevant laws and regulations effectively, as appropriate. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should have and utilise the appropriate powers and tools, and adequate resources to regulate, supervise,"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities within a jurisdiction, either independently or collectively, should have and utilise the appropriate powers, tools and adequate resources to regulate, supervise and oversee crypto-asset activities and markets as appropriate. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities within a jurisdiction, either independently or collectively, should have and utilise the appropriate powers and tools"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require that crypto-asset issuers and service providers meet all applicable regulatory, supervisory and oversight requirements of a jurisdiction before commencing any operations there, and adapt to new regulatory requirements as necessary and appropriate. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "before commencing any operations in that jurisdiction and adapt to new regulatory requirements as necessary and appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should have the powers and capabilities to enforce applicable requirements, including authorisation and licensing requirements, and the ability to undertake inspections or examinations. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including authorisation and licensing requirements, and the ability to undertake inspections or examinations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When issuers or service providers do not comply with applicable laws or regulations, authorities should have the powers and capabilities to require corrective actions and take enforcement actions as appropriate. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "authorities should have the powers and capabilities to require corrective actions and"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 1, authorities should ensure they have the powers and capabilities to deal with the risk that crypto-asset issuers and service providers try to escape regulation and oversight by operating from foreign jurisdictions. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "evade regulation and oversight by operating in foreign jurisdictions"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Authorities' response to evasion through foreign operation may include considering limits on domestic users' access to foreign crypto-asset service providers and markets, or on foreign issuers and service providers supplying services, where these do not comply with applicable domestic rules and are not regulated to international standards. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "restrictions on the access by domestic users to foreign crypto-asset service providers and markets"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 1 says authorities should require crypto-asset service providers to rest each material aspect of their activities, in every relevant jurisdiction, on a sound, clear, transparent and enforceable legal footing. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "well-founded, clear, transparent and enforceable legal basis for each material aspect of their activities in all relevant jurisdictions"</sub>

## Recommendation 2: General regulatory framework

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 2: authorities should apply comprehensive and effective regulation, supervision and oversight to crypto-asset activities and markets, including issuers and service providers, on a functional basis, proportionate to the financial stability risk they pose or potentially pose, consistent with authorities' mandates and in line with the principle "same activity, same risk, same regulation". (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proportionate to the financial stability risk they pose, or potentially pose, and"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should have comprehensive regulatory rules and policies for crypto-asset activities, issuers and service providers, proportionate to risk, size, complexity and systemic importance and consistent with the economic functions performed, in line with "same activity, same risk, same regulation" and relevant international standards while taking into account specific risks of crypto-asset activities. Given the fast-evolving nature of the market and potential for rapid escalation of risks, authorities should be ready to regulate and supervise activities that could pose financial stability risks. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Given the fast-evolving nature of crypto-asset activities and markets and the potential for financial stability risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should assess whether existing regulatory, supervisory and oversight requirements adequately address the financial stability risks of crypto-asset activities, including emerging or new risks, and if needed clarify or supplement them. Where crypto-asset activities outside the scope of financial regulation may pose financial stability risks, authorities should, as needed, seek to expand or adjust their regulatory perimeter, as appropriate. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seek to expand or adjust their regulatory perimeter, as appropriate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 2, when assessing potential financial stability risks authorities should take account of how interconnected the crypto-asset market is with the wider financial system, the overall size and nature of the activities (including financial intermediation, leverage, credit, liquidity and maturity transformation) and the risk of spillover into other jurisdictions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should take into account the interconnectedness between the crypto-asset market and the wider financial system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should aim for regulatory outcomes in the crypto-asset market that match those in traditional financial markets, so that traditional activities are not pushed into crypto-asset markets to circumvent regulation. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should target regulatory outcomes in the crypto-asset market equivalent to those in the traditional financial market"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: To achieve equivalent outcomes, authorities should consider relevant sectoral standards and policies. Footnote 7 gives as instances the IOSCO Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation, the CPMI-IOSCO Principles for financial market infrastructures, the Basel Framework, and FATF standards, in particular FATF Recommendations 15 and 16. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To this end, authorities should consider relevant sectoral standards and policies"</sub>

## Recommendation 3: Cross-border cooperation, coordination and information sharing

- **expected / expectation**: Recommendation 3 says authorities should cooperate and coordinate with each other at home and internationally, to promote efficient and effective communication, information sharing and consultation, to support one another as appropriate in carrying out their respective mandates, and to encourage consistent regulatory and supervisory outcomes. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should cooperate and coordinate with each other, both domestically and internationally"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should use existing cooperation and information-sharing arrangements, for example memoranda of understanding or ad-hoc arrangements, to the extent practicable, or consider setting up new ones that could cover further subject areas or authorities and take account of the cross-sectoral nature of some activities. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as memoranda of understanding, or ad-hoc arrangements, to the extent practicable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Cross-border cooperation and information sharing between authorities should aim to build a shared understanding of the risks and activities of crypto-assets, issuers and service providers across jurisdictions, both in normal times and in times of stress. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should aim to facilitate a shared understanding of the risks and activities of crypto-assets"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should have adequate information sharing in place about crypto-asset service providers and issuers that belong to a group operating in several jurisdictions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adequate information sharing about crypto-asset service providers and issuers which are part of a group operating in multiple jurisdictions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should share information and cooperate in a timely and effective way about crypto-asset issuers and service providers in financial or operational distress whose problems may affect more jurisdictions than those in which they mainly operate. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in financial or operational distress and which may affect a wider range of jurisdictions than where they primarily operate"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where authorities host crypto-asset issuers and service providers with a global reach, they should take additional steps to collaborate with authorities in the relevant jurisdictions, bearing in mind the risk of spillovers into other jurisdictions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when they host crypto-asset issuers and service providers with a global reach"</sub>

## Recommendation 2: General regulatory framework

- **expected / expectation**: Regardless of whether crypto-asset activities are conducted in purportedly decentralised structures or other ways that frustrate identification of a responsible entity or issuer, authorities should adopt or have in place a regulatory approach aiming at adequate protection for all relevant parties, including consumers and investors, and at achieving the same regulatory outcome. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regardless of whether crypto-asset activities are conducted in purportedly decentralised structures"</sub>

## Recommendation 3: Cross-border cooperation, coordination and information sharing

- **descriptive / context**: The FSB and SSBs will continue to promote consistency and a common understanding of key elements of frameworks for crypto-asset activities. The FSB will continue to monitor issues related to the cross-border nature of crypto-assets and consider mechanisms to facilitate exchange of information about the level of compliance by service providers operating across borders and out of jurisdictions that have not implemented a framework consistent with the FSB high-level recommendations and international standards. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "mechanisms that facilitate exchange of information about the level of compliance by service providers that are operating across borders"</sub>

## Recommendation 4: Governance

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 4: authorities, as appropriate, should require crypto-asset issuers and service providers to have in place and disclose a comprehensive governance framework with clear and direct lines of responsibility and accountability for all functions and activities, proportionate to their risk, size, complexity and systemic importance and to the financial stability risk of the activity or market they participate in. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have in place and disclose a comprehensive governance framework with clear and direct lines of responsibility and accountability"</sub>

## Recommendation 5: Risk management

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 5: authorities, as appropriate, should require crypto-asset service providers to have an effective risk management framework that comprehensively addresses all material risks of their activities, proportionate to risk, size, complexity, systemic importance and financial stability risk. To the extent necessary to achieve outcomes comparable to traditional finance, authorities should require crypto-asset issuers to address the financial stability risk posed by the activity or market they participate in. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have an effective risk management framework in place that comprehensively addresses all material risks associated with their activities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should understand the different risk profiles of crypto-asset issuers and service providers and require them, as appropriate, to establish a risk management framework proportionate to their risk, size, complexity and systemic importance and to the financial stability risk posed. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should understand the different risk profiles of crypto-asset issuers and service providers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should expect issuers and service providers to be directed by a management that is qualified and of good repute (for example by applying "fit and proper" standards, where applicable); to allocate adequate resources to risk management and other control functions (compliance and internal audit); and to ensure these control functions are carried out independently of business activities. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "directed by a management which is qualified and of good repute"</sub>

## Recommendation 4: Governance

- **expected / expectation**: Under Recommendation 4, authorities should require issuers and service providers to have a robust governance framework, proportionate to their risk, size, complexity and systemic importance and to the financial stability risk of their activity or market. It should set out clear and direct lines of responsibility and accountability, clearly define the roles and responsibilities of the management body and the decision-making process, and include procedures to identify, address and manage conflicts of interest. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including procedures for identifying, addressing and managing conflicts of interest"</sub>

(p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such conduct of activities must not undermine robust governance and accountability arrangements"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should require compliance with rules and regulations for effective governance irrespective of the structures"</sub>

## Recommendation 5: Risk management

- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should pay particular attention to the compliance functions of each service provider or issuer, which should be expected to monitor all of its activities, identify and address non-compliance issues and deficiencies, and ensure adherence to applicable laws and regulations. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should pay particular attention to the compliance functions of each service provider or issuer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should expect issuers and service providers to act honestly and fairly and require them to communicate with users and stakeholders in a clear and not misleading manner, and to identify, disclose, manage and, to the extent possible, prevent any conflict of interests. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "act honestly and fairly and require them to communicate with users and relevant stakeholders in a clear"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities, as appropriate, should require issuers and service providers, proportionately, to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control all material risks. Authorities should require service providers to effectively identify and manage risks arising from leverage and credit, liquidity, operational, compliance, and maturity transformation, and to have rules and procedures to effectively monitor the transactions they conduct. Authorities should also have rules, policies and enforcement tools that comprehensively address these risks in normal times and in times of stress. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to have rules and procedures to effectively monitor the transactions they conduct"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Authorities should, as appropriate, consider applying both prudential and market conduct regulatory tools. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should, as appropriate, consider applying both prudential and market conduct regulatory tools."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should pay particular attention to technological risks associated with crypto-asset activities, and should require service providers that are part of an internationally active group to properly identify, monitor and manage risks arising from exposures to other parts of the group. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities should pay particular attention to technological risks associated with crypto-asset activities."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities, as appropriate, should require issuers and service providers, proportionately, to establish effective contingency arrangements (including robust and credible recovery plans where warranted) and business continuity planning. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to establish effective contingency arrangements (including robust and credible recovery plans where warranted) and business continuity planning"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should ensure that crypto-asset issuers and service providers put appropriate AML/CFT measures in place consistent with FATF Standards, including requirements to comply with the FATF "travel rule". (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "put appropriate AML/CFT measures in place consistent with FATF Standards"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Taking account of jurisdictional differences in legal frameworks on ownership rights to crypto-assets held by a service provider, authorities should require every service provider that holds or safeguards crypto-assets to have adequate controls, to tell customers what ownership rights they have, and to protect those rights, including through segregation and record-keeping requirements. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including through segregation and record-keeping requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The safeguarding arrangements should minimise the risk of loss, misuse of or delayed access to assets, including if the service provider becomes insolvent. The same requirements should apply when holding or safeguarding activities are outsourced to a third party. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The same requirements should apply when the activities involved in holding or safeguarding crypto-assets are outsourced"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require crypto-asset service providers that facilitate trading to make sure their operations are resilient and transparent, and to put in place and maintain clear, transparent operating rules for the trading platform. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitating trading to ensure that their operations are resilient and transparent"</sub>

## Recommendation 6: Data collection, recording and reporting

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 6: authorities, as appropriate, should require issuers and service providers to have robust frameworks, including systems and processes, for collecting, storing, safeguarding and timely and accurate reporting of data, with relevant policies, procedures and infrastructure, proportionate to risk, size, complexity and systemic importance. Authorities should have access to the data as necessary and appropriate to fulfil their mandates. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for collecting, storing, safeguarding, and the timely and accurate reporting of data"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require, proportionately, that issuers and service providers have data management systems that record and safeguard relevant data collected and produced in operations, with adequate controls to safeguard integrity and security of data and conform to applicable regulation including on data retention, security and privacy. Appropriate infrastructures should be maintained to ensure data quality and reliability, with well-defined procedures to monitor data quality and rectify poor data, and service providers should have measures to ensure completeness, accuracy and reliability of data. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have in place well-defined procedures to monitor data quality and rectify poor data"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should have full, timely, complete and ongoing access to relevant data and information, wherever the data is located, to regulate, supervise and oversee crypto-asset activities and markets, considering the level and nature of risks posed, and should seek to address any impediments to data access or limitations of the data. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "wherever the data is located, to enable them to regulate, supervise and oversee"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Authorities may build on existing efforts to encourage consistent, comparable data collection and reporting organised by activity types and economic functions, or may consider developing new reporting frameworks or policies, as appropriate, to support data collection and sharing among the relevant authorities and jurisdictions. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities may leverage existing efforts to promote consistent and comparable data collection and reporting"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should seek to promote public understanding of crypto-asset markets. For service providers that facilitate a wide range of trading services and large trading volume, authorities should assess their ability to access data on, but not limited to, the instruments most frequently traded, principal amounts traded, and largest counterparties and intermediaries, and the extent to which these data should be made more widely available or publicly disseminated. ## Recommendation 7: Disclosures

- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 7: authorities should require issuers and service providers to disclose to users and relevant stakeholders comprehensive, clear and transparent information on their governance framework, operations, risk profiles and financial conditions, and the products they provide and activities they conduct. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "comprehensive, clear and transparent information regarding their governance framework, operations, risk profiles and financial conditions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require issuers and service providers to make available to users and stakeholders (customers, investors, shareholders) all necessary information on how they operate and transact, the risk features of their products and how they manage and mitigate risks, in a manner understandable to the intended audiences, including as appropriate the governance structure and procedures for main activities offered and important conflicts of interest arising from crypto-asset activities. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all necessary information regarding how they operate, how they transact, the risk features of their products,"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require issuers and service providers to adequately disclose information on product structure and operation of their activities; this may include, for example, a prospectus or equivalent document from an issuer. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This may include, for example, a prospectus or an equivalent document from a crypto-asset issuer."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require a service provider to give any custody client full and accurate disclosure of the terms and conditions of the custodial relationship and the risks if the custodian entered bankruptcy, including the ownership rights retained by the client, safeguards for client assets, any outsourcing arrangements, and circumstances where timely redemption or withdrawal might not be provided. Where ownership rights are intended to transfer to the service provider, authorities should require clear disclosure to the customer and the customer's explicit prior consent to the transfer. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clearly disclose the arrangement to the customer and to obtain the customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Authorities should require issuers and service providers to disclose any material risks associated with underlying technologies, such as cyber security risk, as well as environmental and climate risks and impacts, as appropriate and in line with jurisdictional legal frameworks. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as cyber security risk, as well as environmental and climate risks and impacts"</sub>
