## Addressing the decline in correspondent banking relationships: Progress report

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The decline in the number of correspondent banking relationships remains a concern for the international community: the number of active correspondent banks declined by 3.4% in 2018, taking the cumulative decline since 2011 to 19.3%. Concentration increased as fewer correspondent banks handle payments, and access to correspondent banking remains a critical issue in some regions and jurisdictions. (p. 1)[^original]

## Remittance service providers' access to banking services: Monitoring of the FSB's recommendations

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The reduction in correspondent banking relationships has had a significant impact on remittance service providers' ability to access banking services, particularly acute in developing countries where remittance flows are a key source of household funds. - **descriptive / finding or result**: The report assesses implementation of the FSB's March 2018 recommendations on remittance service providers' access to banking services. ## Notes to editors

- **descriptive / context**: The FSB coordinates internationally the work of national financial authorities and standard-setting bodies and promotes effective financial sector policies in the interest of financial stability. It brings together national authorities from 24 countries and jurisdictions plus international financial institutions, sector-specific regulator groupings and central bank expert committees, and conducts outreach with approximately 70 other jurisdictions through six Regional Consultative Groups. ## Notes to editors

- **descriptive / context**: The FSB began a four-point action plan in November 2015 against the decline in correspondent banking. Its four strands are: studying the scale and implications of the problem; making regulatory expectations clearer, including through FATF and BCBS guidance; building capacity domestically in jurisdictions home to affected respondent banks; and improving due diligence tools used by correspondent banks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FSB launched its four-point action plan in November 2015 to assess and address the decline in correspondent banking."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB's March 2018 recommendations on remittance service providers (RSPs) accessing banking services have four themes: encouraging dialogue between the banking and remittance sectors; international standards and oversight for the remittance sector; the role of innovation in the sector and whether it could widen RSPs' access to banking; and technical assistance on remittance-related subjects. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Promoting dialogue and communication between the banking and remittance sectors;"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB's chair is Randal K. Quarles of the US Federal Reserve (Vice Chairman for Supervision) and its vice chair is Klaas Knot of De Nederlandsche Bank (President). The FSB Secretariat sits in Basel, Switzerland, and is hosted by the Bank for International Settlements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FSB is chaired by Randal K. Quarles, Vice Chairman for Supervision, US Federal Reserve"</sub>
