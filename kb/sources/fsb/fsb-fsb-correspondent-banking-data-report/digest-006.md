## 4.2 Drivers of termination reported by banks in the FSB-CBCG survey

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Table 11 classifies by region the reasons respondent banks were given by correspondents for termination; as a reading example, 'changes to overall business model or business strategy' accounted for 22.50% of reasons mentioned by respondent banks in surveyed East Asia and Pacific jurisdictions. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The most cited reason for surveyed jurisdictions in Sub-Saharan Africa was"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "dormant relationships were less frequently a reason for termination for banks in lower middle income countries (5%)"</sub>

## 4.3 Survey of authorities: regulatory changes as a driver of termination of correspondent banking services

- **descriptive / limitation**: The available information is insufficient to fully assess the extent to which regulatory changes, or changes in implementation of existing regulation, might have affected the network structure of correspondent banking. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The information available is insufficient to fully assess the extent to which regulatory changes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the FSB-CBCG authorities survey, 12 countries cited 20 measures considered to have meaningfully negatively impacted correspondent banking services, 10 of them taking place in 2014 and 2015; only one was domestic; 11 affected services provided and 9 services received. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "12 countries cited 20 measures they considered meaningfully negatively impacted correspondent banking services,"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Ten of the 20 measures related to AML/CFT or sanctions: 4 on new AML/CFT legislation or standards (including 2 on the EU directive on customer due diligence), 2 on stricter AML/CFT and sanctions regime enforcement in a foreign jurisdiction, 2 on specific sanctions regimes (Iran and Russia) and 2 reflecting that the responding jurisdiction had at some point been listed by the FATF. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ten of these measures were related to AML/CFT or sanctions: 4 were about new AML/CFT legislation or standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Authorities also cited 5 measures related to financial reforms (two specifically citing the liquidity coverage ratio, one exposure limits, two more general), plus new regulations on MTOs and the Single European Payments Area (SEPA), which was seen as reducing the need for correspondent banking services. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities also cited 5 measures related to financial reforms, with two of them specifically citing the liquidity coverage ratio"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: When respondents and correspondents were asked for reasons for relationship termination, 'impact of internationally agreed financial regulatory reforms' was not among the main reasons mentioned by banks in either role. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "was not among the main reasons mentioned by banks in either role"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "22 jurisdictions cited 74 measures implemented49"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Forty-six of 72 measures were AML/CFT oriented."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Eleven of the AML/CFT measures aimed to strengthen and improve the jurisdiction's AML/CFT legal framework, for example improving definitions of correspondent banking services. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Eleven of these measures were aimed to strengthen and improve the jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Seven measures designated supervisory authorities, defining or extending their powers regarding AML/CFT compliance. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seven measures designated supervisory authorities, defining or extending their powers regarding AML/CFT compliance"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Three measures defined the framework or set expectations regarding account and transaction risk classification of both respondents (given their business activity) and final clients. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "three measures defined the framework or set expectations regarding account and transaction risk classification"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Six measures related to adoption of FATF recommendations, particularly prohibiting engagement with shell banks. (p. 52)[^original]

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Two measures set requirements for PEPs. (p. 52)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "Two measures set information requirements based on threshold of transaction"</sub>

## 6. Alternatives to correspondent banking

- **descriptive / context**: Withdrawal of correspondent banking services, with higher concentration, longer payment chains and related fragilities and risks, could encourage cross-border payment participants to use alternative methods more, or more intensively. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could encourage cross-border payment participants to use alternative methods more"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the FSB-CBCG survey, 73 alternatives to traditional correspondent banking channels were listed (few responses, from all 312 surveyed banks); nine cited European cross-border systems such as EBA Clearing's EURO 1 and STEP2-T and TARGET 2. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Among 73 alternatives listed, nine responses cited European cross-border systems or networks such as EBA"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Footnote 50: there were few responses to this section, only 73 alternatives provided by all 312 surveyed banks. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There were few responses to this section, only 73 alternatives provided by all 312 surveyed banks."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Other alternatives mentioned: eleven mentions of third party banks as indirect correspondents, six of remittance providers, four of settling only net amounts with correspondent banks and three of opening accounts with smaller banks. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there were eleven mentions of using third party banks as indirect correspondents, and six mentions of remittance providers"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "in the Philippines and Kenya, blockchainbased intermediaries offer money transfer services via Bitcoin"</sub>

## 6. Alternatives to correspondent banking

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nine survey responses named European cross-border systems (EBA Clearing's EURO 1 and STEP2-T, and TARGET 2). The report says these three Euro payment systems give pan-European reach and lessen the need for banks elsewhere to hold several correspondents in Euro area countries, yet they do not replace correspondent banking worldwide. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "European reachability and reduce the need for banks in other countries to have multiple correspondents in Euro area countries"</sub>

## Information from other sources

- **descriptive / limitation**: According to the World Bank's 2015 report, operations via cash management companies are geographically limited and the high operating costs may lead to shutdown of MTO services because continuing may no longer be profitable. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "operations via cash management companies are geographically limited and the high operative costs may lead"</sub>

## 7. Final considerations

- **descriptive / context**: The report aimed to give a comprehensive overview of the scale, effects and drivers of the decline in correspondent banking and potential implications for structural risks or fragilities in specific markets, with a focus on how new FSB-CBCG survey evidence complements existing evidence. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "comprehensive overview of the scale, effects and drivers, of the decline in correspondent banking"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB-CBCG will continue monitoring developments in correspondent banking; it sees an opportunity to gain more perspective on the coverage and representativeness of survey results through comparison with corridor-level data on SWIFT payment messages. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FSB-CBCG will continue monitoring evolutions in correspondent banking and has identified avenues"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Progress on other aspects of the FSB's four point action plan (clarifying regulatory expectations, strengthening tools for due diligence and capacity building in jurisdictions home to affected banks) is described in a separate progress report to the G20 Leaders Summit of July 2017. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to assess and address the decline in correspondent banking through a comprehensive framework of clarifying regulatory expectations"</sub>
