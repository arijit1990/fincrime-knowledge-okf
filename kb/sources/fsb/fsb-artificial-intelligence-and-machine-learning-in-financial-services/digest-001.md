## Press release

- **descriptive / context**: In a press release (Ref no 39/2017, 1 November 2017) the Financial Stability Board (FSB) announced publication of a report on the financial stability implications of the growing use of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning in financial services. - **descriptive / context**: Financial institutions are increasingly using AI and machine learning to assess credit quality, price and market insurance contracts and automate client interactions, and also to optimise scarce capital, back-test models and analyse the market impact of trading large positions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to assess credit quality, to price and market insurance contracts and to automate client interactions"</sub>

## FSB considers financial stability implications of artificial intelligence and machine learning

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Potential benefit: more efficient processing of information (for example in credit decisions, financial markets, insurance contracts and customer interactions) may contribute to a more efficient financial system, and use of AI and machine learning by regulators and supervisors can help improve regulatory compliance and increase supervisory effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may contribute to a more efficient financial system"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Potential risk: AI and machine learning applications could create new and unexpected forms of interconnectedness between financial markets and institutions, for instance through use by various institutions of previously unrelated data sources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "new and unexpected forms of interconnectedness between financial markets and institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Potential risk: network effects and scalability of new technologies may in future give rise to third-party dependencies, which could lead to new systemically important players that could fall outside the regulatory perimeter. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "new systemically important players that could fall outside the regulatory perimeter"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Potential risk: lack of interpretability or auditability of AI and machine learning methods could become a macro-level risk, and widespread use of opaque models may result in unintended consequences. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The lack of interpretability or auditability of AI and machine learning methods could become a macro-level risk."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FSB's analysis found several potential benefits and risks of AI and machine learning for financial stability, which it says should be monitored over the coming years as the technology is adopted and more data becomes available. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "potential benefits and risks for financial stability that should be monitored as the technology is adopted"</sub>

## Notes to editors

- **descriptive / context**: In June 2017 the FSB published a report on Financial Stability Implications from FinTech, which identified 10 regulatory and supervisory FinTech issues meriting authorities' attention, three seen as priorities for international collaboration: managing operational risk from third-party service providers; mitigating cyber risks; and monitoring macrofinancial risks that could emerge as FinTech activities increase. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identified 10 regulatory and supervisory FinTech issues that merit authorities’ attention, three of which"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FSB is chaired by Mark Carney, Governor of the Bank of England; its Secretariat is in Basel, Switzerland, hosted by the Bank for International Settlements. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FSB is chaired by Mark Carney, Governor of the Bank of England."</sub>

## FSB considers financial stability implications of artificial intelligence and machine learning

- **expected / expectation**: The FSB says that, as with any new product or service, it will be important to assess uses of AI and machine learning in light of their risks, including adherence to relevant protocols on data privacy, conduct risks and cybersecurity. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it will be important to assess uses of AI and machine learning in view of their risks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FSB says adequate testing and 'training' of AI and machine learning tools, using unbiased data and feedback mechanisms, is important to ensure that applications do what they are intended to do. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with unbiased data and feedback mechanisms is important to ensure applications do what they are intended to do."</sub>
