## Use and adoption

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the 2024 survey, 75% of firms are already using AI and a further 10% plan to use it over the next three years, up from 58% and 14% respectively in the 2022 joint Bank of England and FCA machine learning survey. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "75% of firms are already using artificial intelligence (AI), with a further 10% planning to use AI"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Foundation models make up 17% of all AI use cases, which the report says supports anecdotal evidence of rapid adoption of this complex type of machine learning. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Foundation models form 17% of all AI use cases supporting anecdotal evidence"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A third of all AI use cases are third-party implementations, up from 17% in the 2022 survey; the report says this supports the view that third-party exposure will keep rising as model complexity increases and outsourcing costs decrease. Third-party implementation is defined as the case where most of the development or deployment processes of the AI application are implemented by a third party. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A third of all AI use cases are third-party implementations."</sub>

## Third-party exposure

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The top three third-party providers account for 73% of reported cloud providers, 44% of model providers and 33% of data providers respectively. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The top three third-party providers account for 73%, 44%, and 33% of all reported cloud, model, and data providers respectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents report that 55% of all AI use cases have some degree of automated decision-making, and 24% of those are semi-autonomous, meaning they can make a range of decisions on their own but are designed to involve human oversight for critical or ambiguous decisions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "55% of all AI use cases have some degree of automated decision-making with 24% of those being semi-autonomous"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Only 2% of use cases have fully autonomous decision-making. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only 2% of use cases have fully autonomous decision-making"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Firms rate 62% of all AI use cases as low materiality and 16% as high materiality. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "62% of all AI use cases are rated low materiality by the firms that use them"</sub>

## Understanding of AI systems

- **descriptive / finding or result**: 46% of respondent firms reported only 'partial understanding' of the AI technologies they use, versus 34% reporting 'complete understanding'; this is largely due to third-party models, where firms noted a lack of complete understanding compared with models developed internally. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "46% of respondent firms reported having only ‘partial understanding’ of the AI technologies they use"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The highest perceived current benefits of AI are in data and analytical insights, anti-money laundering (AML) and combating fraud, and cybersecurity; the largest expected increases in benefit over three years are in operational efficiency, productivity and cost base, broadly in line with the 2022 survey. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The highest perceived current benefits are in data and analytical insights, anti-money laundering (AML) and combating fraud, and cybersecurity"</sub>

## Benefits and risks of AI

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Of the top five perceived current risks of AI, four relate to data: data privacy and protection, data quality, data security, and data bias and representativeness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Of the top five perceived current risks, four are related to data"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The risks expected to increase most over the next three years are third-party dependencies, model complexity, and embedded or 'hidden' models. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "third-party dependencies, model complexity, and embedded or ‘hidden’ models"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The expected increase in average perceived benefit over three years (21%) exceeds the expected increase in average perceived risk (9%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The increase in the average perceived benefit over the next three years (21%) is greater than"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Cybersecurity is rated the highest perceived systemic risk both now and in three years, and the largest increase in systemic risk over that period is expected from critical third-party dependencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cybersecurity is rated as the highest perceived systemic risk both currently and in three years"</sub>

## Constraints

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The largest perceived regulatory constraint on AI use is data protection and privacy, followed by resilience, cybersecurity and third-party rules and the FCA's Consumer Duty. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The largest perceived regulatory constraint to the use of AI is data protection and privacy"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The largest perceived non-regulatory constraint is safety, security and robustness of AI models, followed by insufficient talent and access to skills. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The largest perceived non-regulatory constraint is safety, security and robustness of AI models"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: 84% of firms reported having an accountable person for their AI framework, and over half of firms reported having nine or more AI-specific governance components (frameworks, controls and/or processes). (p. 1)[^original]

- **descriptive / finding or result**: 72% of firms said executive leadership were accountable for AI use cases, but accountability is often split, with most firms reporting three or more accountable persons or bodies. (p. 1)[^original]

## 1.1: Context and objectives

- **descriptive / context**: AI use in UK financial services has grown in number and types of use cases; while it brings benefits such as operational efficiency and personalised customer services, it can also challenge the safety and soundness of firms, fair treatment of consumers and financial stability, so the Bank and the FCA consider it important to maintain an understanding of AI capabilities, development, deployment and use. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it can also present challenges to the safety and soundness of firms, the fair treatment of consumers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Earlier Bank and FCA work includes the machine learning surveys of 2019 and 2022, the AI Public-Private Forum, discussion paper DP5/22 and feedback statement FS2/23; in response to the UK Government's AI White Paper the Bank published a response to DSIT/HMT and the FCA an AI Update. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the machine learning in UK financial services surveys from 2019 and 2022"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Survey 2024 continues the 2019 and 2022 surveys by giving ongoing insight into AI use by Bank and/or FCA-regulated firms, and added generative AI questions given its growth since 2022. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In view of generative AI’s growth since the 2022 survey, the 2024 survey incorporated related questions."</sub>

## 1.2: Methodology

- **descriptive / method**: Results are anonymised and aggregated, with respondents grouped into six sectors (Table A): UK banks (UK deposit-takers, retail banks, building societies); international banks operating in the UK (responses refer to UK operations); insurance (general, health, life, personal and commercial lines insurers, insurance services providers); non-bank lending (credit brokers, consumer credit lenders, non-bank lenders); investment and capital markets (alternatives, asset, fund and wealth managers, stockbrokers, wholesale brokers); and financial market infrastructures, payments and other (FMI firms, payments, credit reference agencies, e-money issuers, exchanges, multilateral trading facilities). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The results presented in this report are anonymised and aggregated with respondents grouped into the sectors"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The survey received 118 responses. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The survey received 118 responses with the number of respondents from each sector shown"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For the survey and report, artificial intelligence is defined as the simulation of human intelligence by machines, including computer systems able to perform tasks demonstrating learning, decision-making, problem solving and other tasks that previously required human intelligence. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "artificial intelligence is defined as the simulation of human intelligence by machines"</sub>

## 2.1: AI use and adoption

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Overall, 75% of responding firms use AI and a further 10% plan to within three years, an increase from the 2022 survey, which showed 58% using and 14% planning. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "75% of firms that responded to the survey said they are already using AI with a further 10%"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Insurance reported the highest share of firms currently using AI at 95%, closely followed by international banks at 94%; financial market infrastructure firms were lowest at 57%. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The insurance sector reported the highest percentage of firms currently using AI at 95%"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents expect the median number of AI use cases to more than double over three years, from 9 to 21; large UK banks and large international banks have medians of 39 and 49 use cases respectively, versus an overall median of nine. 'Large' denotes PRA Category 1 firms, the most significant firms whose size, interconnectedness, complexity and business type give them capacity to cause very significant disruption to the UK financial system by failing or by operating unsafely. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Respondents expect the median number of use cases to more than double over the next three years"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: 56% of respondents currently using AI have 10 or fewer use cases and 10% have more than 50; looking three years out, 31% of firms expect 10 or fewer and nearly a quarter expect over 50. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "56% of those that currently use AI) reported having 10 or fewer use cases, with 10% having more than 50"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Operations and IT is the largest business area for AI use cases at around 22% of all reported use cases, twice the next largest area, retail banking (11%); general insurance is third with 10%. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the largest such area, with around 22% of all reported use cases, is operations and IT"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Foundation models account for 17% of all use cases, and operations and IT has the largest number of them, around 30% of all foundation model use cases. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The results show that foundation models account for 17% of all use cases"</sub>

## 2.3: Use of foundation models

- **descriptive / finding or result**: By business area, legal functions have the highest proportion of foundation models at 29% of all models, with human resources second at 28% of use cases. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms’ legal functions have the highest proportion of foundation models at 29% of all models"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The area with the highest percentage of respondents using AI is optimisation of internal processes (41%), followed by cybersecurity (37%) and fraud detection (33%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "optimisation of internal processes (41% of respondents). This is followed by cybersecurity (37%) and fraud detection (33%)."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: 55% of AI use cases have some degree of automated decision-making, 24% of those semi-autonomous (able to make a range of decisions but designed to involve human oversight for critical or ambiguous decisions); automation with dynamic models accounts for 2% of AI applications and fully autonomous decision-making for a further 2%. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Automation with dynamic models accounted for 2% of AI applications, and fully autonomous decision-making for a further 2%."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Gradient boosting models are by far the most popular model type, at 32% of all reported use cases, and transformer-based models are second with 10%. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "gradient boosting models are by far the most popular model type, comprising 32% of all reported use cases"</sub>

## 2.6: Use cases by model type

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Other model types are spread fairly evenly, with random forests and decision trees the closest followers; other notable examples are linear models (including generalised linear models), traditional neural networks (multilayer perceptron/feedforward, convolutional and recurrent), clustering and prototype models (e.g. K-means, K-Nearest Neighbours), and third-party or proprietary models. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the closest followers being random forests and decision trees"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: 81% of firms currently using AI employ some kind of explainability method; feature importance (72%) and Shapley additive explanations (64%) are the most popular, and both aim to explain how much each input feature contributes to a model's predictions. Feature importance gives a general ranking of features, while Shapley values consider all possible combinations of features to assess each one's impact. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A high proportion of firms currently using AI (81%) employ some kind of explainability method."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A third of current AI use cases are third-party implementations, significantly higher than the 17% in 2022 and in line with the increased perceived third-party dependency risk; the highest shares are in human resources (65%), risk and compliance (64%), and operations and IT (56%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The business areas with the highest percentages of third-party implementations were human resources (65%)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The top three providers of cloud, models and data account for 73%, 44% and 33% of all named providers respectively; the cloud share is somewhat lower than in 2022, the model share is significantly higher than the 2022 figure of 18%, and the data share is up from 25% in 2022. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accounted for 73%, 44%, and 33% of all named providers respectively"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Asked how evaluation and integration of third-party AI products differs from non-AI products, some respondents reported using existing frameworks for third-party AI systems, and some also had additional AI-specific conditions or considerations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some respondents reported use of existing frameworks for the evaluation and integration of third-party AI systems."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The survey defined materiality as a rating of a use case's impact, which could include (a) quantitative size-based measures such as exposure, book or market value, or number of customers a model applies to, and (b) qualitative factors on the model's purpose and relative importance to business decisions, considering potential impact on the firm's solvency and financial performance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We defined materiality as a rating of the use case's impact"</sub>

## 2.9: Materiality of applications

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Of all reported use cases (internal and third-party), 62% were rated low materiality, 22% medium and 16% high; low and medium materiality were most common in operations and IT, and high materiality most common in general insurance, risk and compliance, and retail banking. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "62% were rated as low materiality, 22% as medium and 16% as high."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One third (33%) of AI use cases are external or third-party implementations; of these, 62% are low materiality, 22% medium and 16% high, with a significant proportion of the high materiality ones in operations and IT, and in risk and compliance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One third (33%) of AI use cases are reported as external or third-party implementations."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Of foundation model use cases (17% of the total), 71% are rated low materiality, 17% medium and 12% high; among the high-materiality ones, the largest shares are in operations and IT (25%), retail banking (18%), and 11% each in research and in risk and compliance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "71% are rated as low materiality, 17% medium and 12% high materiality"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Firms were given a list of 16 AI governance approaches (multiple answers allowed). The most common was an accountable person or persons responsible for the AI framework (84% of firms using AI), followed by an AI framework, principles, guidelines or best practice (82%) and data governance (79%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We provided a list of 16 approaches, and asked firms which of them they used specifically for AI applications."</sub>

## 3.1: Governance and accountability

- **descriptive / finding or result**: 72% of firms using or planning to use AI allocate accountability for AI use cases and outputs to executive leadership, followed by developers and data science teams (64%) and business area users (57%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "72% of firms using or planning to use AI stated that they allocate accountability for AI use cases"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Data management practices are mostly not AI-specific: change management was cited by 87% (71% not AI-specific, 16% AI-specific); data privacy and security by 19% AI-specific and 66% non-AI-specific; data architecture and infrastructure 16% AI-specific and 68% non-AI-specific; data ethics, bias and fairness has the highest AI-specific share at 34%, with 40% non-AI-specific. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Change management practices were cited by 87% of respondents with 71% being not AI-specific and 16% being AI-specific."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Firms consider a broad range of factors when assessing AI model complexity, including business need (how appropriate a model type is to the objective); more than half use complexity tests, some built into existing processes and some AI-specific. AI-specific tests tend to consider methodology, data, complexity of code, interpretability, parameter count and frequency of use, and data complexity is a central factor, particularly for large, multi-dimensional or multi-modal data sets. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "More than half of respondents use complexity tests, some of which are built into existing processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The most common metrics for monitoring model effectiveness are accuracy, precision, recall and sensitivity (88% of firms using AI), operational efficiency (74%), and model robustness and stability (72%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accuracy, precision, recall and sensitivity (reported by 88% of firms using AI)"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Of the firms using or planning to use AI over the next three years, 46% reported having only ‘partial understanding’"</sub>

## 4.1: Benefits now and in three years

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Benefits are expected to grow in all areas, with the largest expected increases in operational efficiency, productivity and cost base; the average benefit is rated slightly lower than medium now and slightly higher than medium in three years. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the areas with the largest expected increase over the next three years are operational efficiency, productivity, and cost base"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: On a 1 to 5 scale, four of the top five risks relate to data use; the three biggest current risks are data privacy and protection, data quality and data security; the risks expected to increase most are third-party dependencies, model complexity and embedded or 'hidden' models. The average risk level is slightly above medium and expected to increase somewhat. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "four of the top five risks are related to the use of data"</sub>

## 4.2: Risks for firms and consumers

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The increase in average expected benefits over three years (21%) is greater than the increase in average expected risk (9%). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the increase in average expected benefits over the next three years (21%) is greater than"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents ranked cybersecurity the highest potential systemic risk, expected to remain highest in three years; critical third-party dependencies ranked second and common data sets and/or models third, with the greatest expected increase in risk in critical third-party dependencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Critical third-party dependencies were ranked the second highest risk with common data sets and/or models in third place."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Data protection and privacy is the greatest regulatory constraint (23% large, 29% medium, 10% small); resilience and cyber security rules are 12% large, 22% medium, 17% small; FCA Consumer Duty and conduct are 5% large, 21% medium, 23% small. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with 23% identifying it as a large constraint, 29% as medium, and 10% as a small constraint"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: High regulatory burden is the main type of regulatory constraint (33% of firms for data protection and privacy, 23% for FCA Consumer Duty, 20% for other FCA regulations). Lack of clarity is cited by 18% for intellectual property rights, 13% for Consumer Duty and 11% for resilience and cyber security rules; only 5% see lack of alignment between UK and international regulation as a constraint for data protection and privacy. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "High regulatory burden is considered the main type of regulatory constraint"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The top three non-regulatory constraints are safety, security and robustness (19% large, 32% medium, 30% small), insufficient talent/access to skills (25% large, 32% medium, 24% small), and appropriate transparency and explainability (16% large, 38% medium, 25% small). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Safety, security and robustness was considered by 19% of firms to be a large constraint"</sub>
