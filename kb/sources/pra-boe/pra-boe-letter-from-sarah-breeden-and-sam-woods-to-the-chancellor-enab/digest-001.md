## Sam Woods

- **descriptive / context**: The letter responds to two requests: (1) to work closely with HMT and DSIT to publish a plan on how the PRA/Bank will help enable safe AI innovation, including agreeing a suitable timeline in the first half of 2026, and (2) to report annually on how the regulatory approach has enabled AI-driven innovation and growth in the financial sector. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "report annually on how our regulatory approach has enabled innovation and growth driven by AI in the financial sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The authors state that, if adopted responsibly, AI offers significant opportunities for innovation and economic growth. Given the Bank's statutory objectives, they want an environment in which responsible AI adoption can thrive and contribute to innovation, competition, competitiveness and growth while safeguarding the integrity of the financial system. They describe a proactive, forward-looking approach that is kept under continuous review given the rapid pace of AI development and adoption. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we want to create an environment in which responsible adoption of AI can thrive"</sub>

## 1. Our plans to enable safe AI innovation, including planned work for 2026

- **descriptive / context**: In 2022 the PRA and FCA published joint Discussion Paper 5/22, setting out how rules applied to the use of AI and seeking views on how the regulatory framework might need to evolve. Feedback indicated that many respondents thought there were no regulatory barriers to safe and responsible AI adoption in the UK. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many respondents thought there were no regulatory barriers to the safe and responsible adoption of AI in the UK"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Other themes from DP5/22 feedback noted in the letter: live guidance or best practice could help given rapidly changing capabilities; coordination among regulators domestically and internationally is important; there are concerns about increasing use of third party models and data; and ongoing industry engagement is needed. The authors say this feedback continues to inform their approach. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "live guidance or best practice could be helpful"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In 2023 the PRA issued Model Risk Management Principles for banks, described as technology-agnostic and outcomes-focussed but deliberately including factors relevant to the use of AI models. The authors intend to build on this further in 2026, including by using insights on good practice gained through supervisory dialogue and broader engagement with firms. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which are technology-agnostic and outcomesfocussed but deliberately included factors relevant to the use of AI models"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The PRA has highlighted AI adoption in its 2026 supervisory priorities, so AI will be a key topic of exploration in its supervisory dialogues with firms. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the PRA has highlighted AI adoption in its 2026 supervisory priorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The authors say active monitoring through ongoing engagement with practitioners and experts is crucial to understanding the state and trajectory of AI adoption so as to manage emerging risks. In 2026 they will run the fourth edition of the Bank and FCA's biennial survey of AI adoption by the financial sector, which gives insight into AI use by Bank and FCA-regulated firms. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This year we will run the fourth edition of the Bank and FCA’s biennial survey of AI adoption"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In May of the previous year the Bank and FCA established the AI Consortium, a platform for public-private engagement gathering stakeholder input on the capabilities, development, deployment and use of AI in UK financial services. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we and the FCA established the AI Consortium, a platform for public-private engagement"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: AI Consortium members are exploring approaches to better manage key AI risks to the financial system, focusing on (i) concentration risks including from third-party model providers, (ii) the evolution of AI 'edge cases' as adoption moves into use cases more relevant to financial stability such as credit risk assessment and trading, (iii) explainability and transparency in generative AI, and (iv) AI-accelerated contagion in financial markets. Discussions also cover emerging trends such as the rise of agentic AI, and the Consortium will publish a report on its work this year. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "concentration risks including from third-party model providers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank and PRA co-chair the Cross Market Operational Resilience Group (authorities and industry) and support its AI Taskforce, created in 2024 in response to concerns about sector-level risk from rapid adoption of Generative AI. In May of the previous year the AI Taskforce published its AI Baseline Guidance Review, covering government and regulatory approaches, risk management principles and frameworks, technical implementation, third party and legal considerations, and education and awareness. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI Taskforce published its AI Baseline Guidance Review"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Late in the previous year the authors held three roundtables with regulated firms to understand constraints on AI adoption and how the Bank and PRA can support it, one each for (1) challenger banks and UK-focussed larger banks, (2) global systemically important banks and (3) insurers. Insights included industry support for the PRA framework as it relates to AI and the need for risk management practice to evolve as more complex AI is deployed. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "three roundtables with participants from regulated firms"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most roundtable participants did not yet see a need for detailed AI-specific regulatory guidance or rules, and most could not see a case for a Bank or PRA AI sandbox at this time, regarding the FCA's Supercharged Sandbox and AI Live Testing initiatives as sufficient for testing. The authors intend to repeat the AI roundtables in 2026. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most couldn’t see a case for a Bank or PRA AI sandbox at this time"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In October the PRA held two roundtables with Chief Risk Officers from some of the largest PRA-regulated firms on adopting AI and machine learning (ML) in the context of implementing the SS1/23 'Model risk management principles for banks' as supervisory expectation. The PRA presented thoughts on risk appetite, model tiering, explainability and interpretability, data and overfitting, independent validation, and ongoing model monitoring. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "risk appetite, model tiering, explainability and interpretability, data and overfitting, independent validation, and ongoing model monitoring"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank continues ad hoc meetings with market participants, including firms beyond its regulatory perimeter, through Market Intelligence activities to understand the latest AI uses in financial markets; this also informs its financial stability work. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly to seek the perspective of firms beyond our regulatory perimeter"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: On international and domestic collaboration: in 2026 the G20 Financial Stability Board, chaired by Governor Andrew Bailey, is prioritising work with international standard-setters on sound practices for AI adoption, use and innovation by financial institutions. The PRA co-chairs the IAIS AI workstreams. The Bank works with the G7 via its cyber expert group on AI cybersecurity risks, and domestically works with the AI Security Institute on potential financial stability risks from frontier AI agents and with the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF). (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The PRA acts as a co-chair of the International Association of Insurance Supervisors’ (IAIS) AI workstreams"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank will use AI where appropriate to enhance its own capabilities in 2026, for example predictive analytics, study of non-linear interactions between variables and analysis of larger, richer data sets, which can potentially help forecast GDP growth and bank distress. It has rolled out off-the-shelf AI assistants (summarisation, note-taking, code generation) and is building bespoke solutions; the PRA is exploring LLMs for supervisory tools, such as data extraction and natural language querying. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the PRA is actively exploring the use of LLMs to enhance supervisory tools"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The authors say they are keeping under review their technology-agnostic approach to regulation and whether further action or guardrails might be needed to promote responsible AI adoption, given how rapidly the technology, use cases and adoption levels are advancing. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "keeping under review our technology-agnostic approach to regulation"</sub>
