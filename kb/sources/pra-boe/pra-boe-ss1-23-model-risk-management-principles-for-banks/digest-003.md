## Principle 4.1 The independent validation function

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.1(a): firms should have a validation function giving an objective, unbiased and critical opinion on (a) the suitability and soundness of models for intended use; (b) the design and integration of the system supporting the model; (c) the accuracy, relevance and completeness of development data; and (d) the output and reports used to inform decisions. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should have a validation function to provide an objective, unbiased, and critical opinion on"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.1(b): the validation function should be responsible for independent review and periodic re-validation of models and should give its recommendations on model approvals to the appropriate model approval authority. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "responsible for the (i) independent review and (ii) the periodic re-validation of models"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.1(c): while model owners are responsible for model performance, model users, owners and validators share responsibility for (i) ongoing model performance monitoring and (ii) process verification. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "model users, owners, and validators share the responsibility for"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.1(d): the validation function should operate independently from the model development process and from model owners. Firms with approval to use internal models to calculate capital requirements are expected to demonstrate independence through separate reporting lines for model validators and for model developers and owners, applicable across the MRM framework. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are expected to demonstrate independence through separate reporting lines for model validators and model developers and owners"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.1(e): the validation function should have sufficient organisational standing to provide effective challenge and appropriate access to the board and/or board committees to escalate concerns about model usage and MRM promptly. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sufficient organisational standing to provide effective challenge"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.2(a): all models should be subject to independent review to ensure they are suitable for their intended use. The review should (i) cover all model components, including inputs, calculations and reporting outputs; (ii) assess the conceptual soundness of the underlying theory and suitability for intended use; (iii) critically analyse the quality and extent of development evidence, including relevance and completeness of development data with respect to the portfolios, products, assets or customer base the model is intended for. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All models should be subject to an independent review to ensure that models are suitable for their intended use."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.2(a)(iv)-(v): the independent review should also (iv) evaluate qualitative information and judgements used in development, ensuring they were made appropriately and systematically and are supported by documentation; and (v) conduct additional testing and analysis as necessary so potential limitations are identified and addressed promptly, and review developers' sensitivity analysis evidence to confirm the impact on outputs of key assumptions and variable choices made at model selection. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conduct additional testing and analysis as necessary to enable potential model limitations to be identified"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.2(b): the nature and extent of independent review should be determined by the model tier and, where validation concerns a model change, be commensurate with the materiality of the change. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The nature and extent of independent review should be determined by the model tier"</sub>

## Principle 4.3 Process verification

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.3(a): firms should conduct appropriate verification of model processes and systems implementation to confirm all model components operate effectively and are implemented as intended, including verification of (i) model inputs: internal or external data used are representative of the portfolios, products, assets or customer base the model is intended for and comply with internal data quality control and reliability standards. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should conduct appropriate verification of model processes and systems implementation"</sub>

## Principle 4.4 Model performance monitoring

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.4(a): model performance monitoring should assess performance against thresholds for acceptable performance based on the model testing criteria used during development. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assess model performance against thresholds for acceptable model performance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.4(b): firms should conduct ongoing performance monitoring to (i) ensure parameter estimates and model constructs are appropriate and valid; (ii) ensure assumptions are applicable for the intended use; (iii) assess whether changes in products, exposures, activities, clients or market conditions should be addressed through adjustments, recalibration, redevelopment or replacement; and (iv) assess whether the model has been used beyond its intended use and whether that use delivered acceptable results. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assess whether the model has been used beyond the intended use and whether this use has delivered acceptable results"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.4(c): a range of tests should form part of model monitoring, including those determined by developers: (i) benchmarking, comparing model estimates with comparable alternative estimates; (ii) sensitivity testing, reaffirming robustness and stability; (iii) analysis of overrides, evaluating the performance of model adjustments made; and (iv) parallel outcomes analysis, assessing whether new data should be included in calibration. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "parallel outcomes analysis – assessing whether new data should be included in model calibration"</sub>

## Principle 4.3 Process verification

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.3(a)(ii)-(iii): process verification should also confirm that (ii) for calculations, the systems implementation (code), the integration (processing) and any user developed applications are accurate, controlled and auditable; and (iii) for reporting outputs, reports built from model outputs are accurate, complete, informative and suitably designed for the purpose they are meant to serve. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "systems implementation (code), integration (processing), and user developed applications are accurate, controlled and auditable"</sub>

## Principle 4.4 Model performance monitoring

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.4(d)-(e): monitoring should be ongoing with a frequency determined by the model tier. Firms should produce timely and accurate performance monitoring reports that are independently reviewed, with results incorporated into the procedures for measuring model performance (per Principle 2.3(c)(vi)). (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Model monitoring should be conducted on an ongoing basis with a frequency determined by the model tier."</sub>

## Principle 4.5 Periodic revalidation

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.5(a): firms should undertake regular independent revalidation of models (typically less detailed than validation during initial development) to determine whether the model has operated as intended and whether previous validation findings remain valid, should be updated, or validation should be repeated or augmented with additional analysis. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "typically less detailed than the validation applied during initial model development"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 4.5(b): periodic revalidation should be carried out with a frequency consistent with the model tier. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "carried out with a frequency that is consistent with the model tier"</sub>

## Principle 5 – Model risk mitigants

- **expected / expectation**: High-level principle (restated in the excerpt): firms should have established policies and procedures for the use of model risk mitigants when models are under-performing, and procedures for the independent review of post-model adjustments. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should have procedures for the independent review of post-model adjustments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.1(b): PMA processes may vary across model types but the intended outcomes should be similar, focused on a clear rationale for using PMAs to compensate for model limitations and an approach suitable for their intended use. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The processes for applying PMAs may vary across model types but the intended outcomes of each process should be similar"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.1(c)-(d): PMAs for material models or portfolios should be documented, supported by senior management and approved by the appropriate authority (for example senior management, risk committee, audit committee). PMAs should be applied systematically and transparently, and their impact made clear by presenting model results with and without PMAs when reported for decision making. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with model results being presented with and without PMAs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.1(e): all PMAs should be independently reviewed with intensity commensurate with their materiality. At a minimum the review scope should include (i) the continued relevance of PMAs to the underlying portfolio; (ii) qualitative reasoning, ensuring assumptions are relevant and reasoning is sound and logically and conceptually sound from a business perspective; (iii) inputs, ensuring integrity of data used to calculate the PMA and its representativeness of the portfolio; (iv) outputs, ensuring plausibility; and (v) root cause analysis, ensuring clear understanding of underlying model limitations and whether significant deficiencies require remediation. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All PMAs should be subject to an independent review with intensity commensurate to the materiality of the PMAs."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 21: post-model adjustments (PMAs) refer to all model overlays, management overlays, model overrides or any other adjustments to model output where risks and uncertainties are not adequately reflected in existing models. Footnote 22: expert judgement uses more qualitative and expert reasoning to reach an estimate because there is a lack of empirical evidence for a quantitative calculation. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Post-model adjustments (PMAs) will refer to all model overlays, management overlays, model overrides"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.1(f): PMAs should be supported by appropriate documentation, which at a minimum includes (i) a clear justification for applying PMAs; (ii) criteria for how PMAs are calculated and when they are reduced or removed; and (iii) triggers for prolonged use of PMAs to activate validation and remediation. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "triggers for prolonged use of PMAs to activate validation and remediation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.1(a): firms should operate a consistent firm-wide process for applying post-model adjustments (PMAs) to deal with model limitations, where risks and uncertainties are not adequately captured in models or handled during model development. The process should be written into firms' policies and procedures and include a governance and control framework covering review and support of PMA use, implementation of decisions on how PMAs are calculated, the completeness of PMAs, and when PMAs should be reduced or removed. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consistent firm-wide process for applying post-model adjustments (PMAs) to address model limitations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.1(g): firms should have a process to consider whether the materiality of PMAs, or a trend of recurring PMAs for the same model limitations, indicates flawed model design or misspecification of the construct, and consider remedial action up to recalibration or redevelopment to remediate limitations and reduce reliance on PMAs. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reduce reliance on PMAs"</sub>

## Principle 5.2 Restrictions on model use

- **recommended / recommendation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.2(a): firms should consider placing restrictions on model use when significant deficiencies and/or errors are identified in validation, or when performance tests show a significant breach has occurred or is likely to occur, including (i) permitting use only under strict controls or mitigants and (ii) placing limits on use, including prohibiting use for specific purposes. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should consider placing restrictions on model use when significant model deficiencies and/or errors are identified"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.2(b): the process of managing significant model deficiencies or inadequate performance should be adequately documented and reported to key stakeholders (model owners, users, validation staff and senior management), including recording the nature of issues and tracking remediation status in the model inventory. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "tracking the status of remediation in the model inventory"</sub>

## Principle 5.3 Exceptions and escalations

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.3(a): for material models, firms should formulate the exceptions they would allow for model use and model performance and implement formally approved policies and procedures setting out the escalation procedures for managing them. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should formulate the exceptions23 they would allow for model use and model performance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.3(a)(i): exceptions for model use should be temporary, subject to post-model adjustments (PMAs), and reported to and supported by stakeholders and senior management. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Exceptions for model use should be temporary, should be subject to post-model adjustments (PMAs)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.3(a)(ii): for model performance exceptions, firms should have clear guidelines for a maximum tolerance on deviation from expectation, subject to appropriate risk controls (for example alternative models, heightened review and challenge, more frequent monitoring of post-model adjustments) and mitigants (for example recalibrating or redeveloping the methodology) once defined triggers and thresholds are breached. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should have clear guidelines for determining a maximum tolerance on model performance exceptions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 5.3(b): firms should have escalation processes so key stakeholders (model owners, users, validation staff and senior management) are promptly made aware of a model exception. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should have escalation processes in place so that the key stakeholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 23: 'exceptions' means using a model not approved for usage by the appropriate oversight entity or not validated for use; using a model outside its intended purpose; continuing to use a model that persistently breaches performance metrics; or back testing suggesting results are inconsistent with actual outcomes. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a model that displays persistent breach of performance metrics continues to be used"</sub>
