## Principle 1.1 Model definition

- **descriptive / context**: SS1/23 Principle 1.1: a formal definition of a model sets the scope of an MRM framework and promotes consistency across business units and legal entities. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A formal definition of a model sets the scope of an MRM framework and promotes consistency"</sub>
- **expected / definition**: SS1/23 Principle 1.1(a): firms should adopt as the basis for MRM scope the definition that a model is a quantitative method, system or approach applying statistical, economic, financial or mathematical theories, techniques and assumptions to process input data into output; inputs may be quantitative and/or qualitative or expert judgement-based, and outputs quantitative or qualitative.   <sub>anchor: "Firms should adopt the following definition of a model as the basis for determining the scope of their MRM frameworks"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.1(b): where material deterministic quantitative methods, such as decision-based rules or algorithms, that are not classified as models have a material bearing on business decisions and are complex in nature, firms should consider whether to apply the relevant aspects of the MRM framework to them. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should consider whether to apply the relevant aspects of the MRM framework to these methods"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 5: 'business decisions' means all decisions on general business and operational banking activities, strategic decisions, financial, risk, capital and liquidity measurement and reporting, and any other decisions relevant to the safety and soundness of firms. ## Principle 1.2 Model inventory

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.2(b): although each line of business or legal entity may maintain its own inventory, firms should maintain a firm-wide model inventory that helps identify all direct and indirect model inter-dependencies, for a better understanding of aggregate model risk. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should maintain a firm-wide model inventory which would help to identify all direct and indirect"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.2(c): the model inventory should capture (i) the purpose and use of a model, for example the product or portfolio, intended versus actual use, and the model operating boundaries under which performance is expected to be acceptable; (ii) model assumptions and limitations, for example risks not captured and limitations in calibration data; (iii) findings from validation, for example indicators that models are functioning properly, when they were last updated and outstanding remediation actions; and (iv) governance details, for example names of those responsible for validation, dates of last validation and frequency of future validation. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The types of information the model inventory should capture include"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 7: 'operating boundaries' is the sample data range (including empirical variance-covariance relationships in the multivariate case) used to estimate a statistical model's parameters. It is not a measure of model performance per se, but extrapolating beyond operating boundaries (such as macroeconomic indices in stressed conditions) should be assumed to involve increased model risk. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be assumed to involve increased model risk"</sub>

## Principle 1.3 Model tiering

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3: risk-based model tiering should be used to prioritise validation activities and other risk controls through the model lifecycle and to identify and classify models posing most risk to a firm's business activities and/or safety and soundness. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Risk-based model tiering should be used to prioritise validation activities and other risk controls"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3(a): firms should implement a consistent, firm-wide model tiering approach assigning a risk-based materiality and complexity rating to each model. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consistent, firm-wide model tiering approach that assigns a risk-based materiality and complexity rating"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3(b): when firms assess a model's materiality for tiering, they should look at two things: (i) quantitative size-based measures, for example exposure, book or market value, or the number of customers a model applies to; and (ii) qualitative factors tied to the model's purpose and its relative importance in informing business decisions, taking into account the potential impact on the firm's solvency and financial performance. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "quantitative size-based measures. For example, exposure, book or market value, or number of customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: SS1/23 footnotes 10 to 12 to the Principle 1.3 complexity factors define terms: interpretability is how easy or hard it is to predict what a model will do, i.e. the extent to which the cause of a decision can be understood; explainability is the extent to which a model's workings can be understood in non-technical terms; and bias refers to cases where parts of a dataset (or the outcome of model design) are weighted or represented more heavily than others, giving results with possible ethical and/or social implications. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ease or difficulty of predicting what a model will do"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3(c): the assessment of model complexity should consider risk factors affecting inherent risk within each component of the modelling process, for example nature and quality of input data, choice of methodology (including assumptions), requirements and integrity of implementation, and frequency and/or extensiveness of use. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nature and quality of the input data, the choice of methodology (including assumptions)"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3(c): where necessary, in particular with newly advanced approaches or technologies, the complexity assessment may also consider risk factors related to (i) use of alternative and unstructured data and (ii) measures of a model's interpretability, explainability, transparency and the potential for designer or data bias to be present. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in particular with the use of newly advanced approaches or technologies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3(d): the firm-wide tiering approach should be subject to periodic validation or other objective and critical review by an informed party to ensure its continued relevance and accuracy, including an assessment of the relevance and availability of the information used to determine tiers and accurate recording and maintenance of tiering in the model inventory. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subject to periodic validation, or other objective and critical review by an informed party"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 1.3(e): individual model tier assignments, including materiality and complexity ratings, should be independently reassessed as part of model validation and revalidation, including a review of the accuracy and relevance of the information used to assign tiers. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be independently reassessed as part of the model validation and revalidation process"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 8: inherent risk is risk in the absence of any management or mitigating actions to alter its likelihood or impact. Footnote 9: alternative and unstructured data is data, usually unstructured and non-financial, not traditionally used in financial modelling, including satellite imagery, telemetric or biometric data and social-media feeds, unstructured in that it has no defined data model or pre-existing organisation. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inherent risk is the risk in the absence of any management or mitigating actions"</sub>

## Principle 2 – Governance

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2: firms should have strong governance oversight with a board that promotes an MRM culture from the top by setting a clear model risk appetite; the board should approve the MRM policy and appoint an accountable individual responsible for implementing a sound MRM framework. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should have strong governance oversight with a board that promotes an MRM culture from the top"</sub>

## Principle 2.1 Board of directors’ responsibilities

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.1: the firm-wide MRM framework should be subject to leadership from the board of directors to ensure it is effectively designed. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be subject to leadership from the board of directors to ensure it is effectively designed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.1(a): the board should establish a comprehensive firm-wide MRM framework that is part of its broader risk management framework and proportionate to the firm's size and business activities, the complexity of its models, and the nature and extent of model use. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should establish a comprehensive firm-wide MRM framework that is part of its broader risk management framework"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.1(b): the framework should promote understanding of model risk both for individual models and in aggregate, promote management of model risk as a risk discipline in its own right, and clearly define roles and responsibilities for model risk across business, risk and control functions. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "promote the management of model risk as a risk discipline in its own right"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.1(d): the board should receive regular reports on the model risk profile against appetite, including qualitative measures on the effectiveness of the control framework and model use, significant model risks from individual models or in aggregate, significant changes in model performance over time, and the extent of compliance with the MRM framework. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The board should receive regular reports on the firms’ model risk profile against its model risk appetite."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.1(e): the board is expected to challenge the outputs of the most material models and to understand model capabilities and limitations, the operating boundaries within which performance is expected to be acceptable, the potential impact of poor performance, and the mitigants in place should performance deteriorate. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The board is expected to provide challenge to the outputs of the most material models"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.1(c): the board should set a model risk appetite stating the level and types of model risk the firm is prepared to accept, proportionate to the nature and type of models in use. The appetite should include measures for (i) how effectively the MRM framework is designed and operated; (ii) identifying models and approving their use in decision making; (iii) limits on model use, exceptions and overall compliance; (iv) thresholds for acceptable model performance and tolerance for errors; and (v) how effectively model risk mitigants are used and expert judgement is overseen. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The board should set a model risk appetite that articulates the level and types of model risk"</sub>

## Principle 2.2 SMF accountability for model risk management framework

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.2: an accountable SMF should be empowered to have overall oversight to ensure the effectiveness of the MRM framework. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An accountable SMF should be empowered to have overall oversight"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.2(a): firms should identify the SMF(s) most appropriate to their organisational structure and risk profile to hold overall responsibility for the MRM framework, its implementation, execution and maintenance. The relevant SMF(s) should be the most senior individual responsible for the risks from models the firm operates, and the Statement of Responsibilities should reflect specific accountability for overall MRM. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The relevant SMF(s) should be the most senior individual with the responsibility for the risks resulting from models"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.2(c): identifying an SMF with overall MRM responsibility does not prejudice the responsibilities of business, risk and control functions for developing and using individual models. Model owners, developers and users remain responsible for ensuring individual models are developed, implemented and used in accordance with the MRM framework, model risk appetite and limitations of use. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Model owners, model developers, and model users remain responsible for ensuring that individual models are developed"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.2(b): the MRM responsibilities of the accountable SMF(s) may cover (i) setting up policies and procedures that put the MRM framework into operation and ensure compliance; (ii) allocating the framework's roles and responsibilities; (iii) ensuring effective challenge; (iv) ensuring independent validation; (v) evaluating and reviewing model results together with validation and internal audit reports; (vi) taking prompt remedial action when necessary so that the firm's aggregate model risk stays within the board-approved risk appetite; and (vii) ensuring sufficient resourcing, adequate systems and infrastructure for data and system integrity, and effective controls and testing of model outputs to support effective MRM practices. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The accountable SMF(s)’s responsibilities regarding MRM may include"</sub>

## Principle 2.3 Policies and procedures

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.3: firms should have comprehensive policies and procedures that formalise the MRM framework and ensure effective and consistent application across the firm. (p. 16)[^original]

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.3(a): firms should have clearly documented policies and procedures formalising the MRM framework. - **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 2.3(c): firms should establish policies and procedures across all aspects of the model lifecycle so models are suitable for their proposed usage at the outset and on an ongoing basis and model risks are identified and addressed promptly. ## Principle 3.2 The use of data

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.2(a): the development process should demonstrate that data used are suitable for the intended use, consistent with the chosen theory and methodology, and representative of the underlying portfolios, products, assets or customer base the model is intended for.   <sub>anchor: "are representative of the underlying portfolios, products, assets, or customer base the model is intended to be used for"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.2(b): the development process should ensure there is no inappropriate bias in the development data and that data usage complies with data privacy and other relevant data regulations. (p. 21)[^original]

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.2(c): when development data are not representative of the portfolios, products, assets or customer base the model will be used for, the potential impact should be assessed and the limitation reflected in the model's tier classification to reflect higher uncertainty; model users and owners should be made aware of any limitations.   <sub>anchor: "use of proxies to compensate for the lack of representative data should be clearly documented and subject to validation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.2(e): interconnected data sources and alternative and unstructured data should be identified and recorded in the model inventory, and the complexity from interconnected data and added uncertainty from alternative and unstructured data should be reflected in the model's tier classification so appropriate rigour is applied in independent validation. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be identified and recorded in the model inventory"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 17: alternative and unstructured data is data, usually unstructured and non-financial, not typically used in financial modelling, such as social-media feeds, unstructured in that it has no defined data model or pre-existing structure. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not typically used in financial modelling such as socialmedia feeds"</sub>

## Principle 3.3 Model development testing

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.3(a): development testing should demonstrate a model works as intended, with clear criteria (tests) to measure model quality in development and to select between candidate models. Developers should provide the key outline of the monitoring pack (set of tests or criteria) that will monitor ongoing performance during use. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Model developers should provide the key outline of the monitoring pack"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.3(b)(i)-(ii): development testing should assess performance against design objective(s) using a range of tests: backward-looking tests using actual observations across varied economic and market conditions relevant to intended use; and forward-looking tests using plausible scenarios assessing whether the model can handle changes in conditions and in the portfolio, products, assets or customer base without performance falling below acceptable levels, including sensitivity analysis to determine the operating boundaries. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "performance tests should be conducted using plausible scenarios that assess the extent to which the model can take into consideration changes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.3(b)(iii): where practicable, performance tests should include comparing model output with available challenger models (alternative implementations of the same theory, or implementations of alternative theories and assumptions). The extent of challenger or benchmark comparison should be considered in the model's tier classification to reflect higher uncertainty. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "performance tests should also include comparisons of the model output with the output of available challenger models"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.3(c): development testing should also be conducted for material model changes, including material changes over time in dynamic models (able to adapt, recalibrate or change autonomously with new inputs), and should compare output before and after the change to actual observations and outcomes (parallel outcomes analysis). (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dynamic models (ie models able to adapt, recalibrate, or otherwise change autonomously in response to new inputs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 18: sensitivity analysis is evaluating a model's output over a range of input values or parameters. Footnote 19: dynamic-model changes include where parameters and/or hyperparameters are automatically recalculated over time. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Evaluating a model's output over a range of input values or parameters."</sub>

## Principle 3.4 Model adjustments and expert judgement

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.4(a)-(b): firms should be able to demonstrate that risks from model limitations and uncertainties are adequately understood, monitored and managed, including through expert judgement. The development process should consider the need for expert judgement to make model adjustments modifying any part of a model (input, assumptions, methodology or output) to address limitations. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should be able to demonstrate that risks relating to model limitations and model uncertainties"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.4(c): where adjustments are needed they should be adequately justified and clearly recorded in the model inventory, which should record the reasons for adjustments and how they should be calculated over time. Implementation should be documented and subject to proper governance, including ongoing independent validation. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the adjustments should be adequately justified and clearly recorded in the model inventory"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.4(d): where adjustments are made to a feeder model or a sub-model of a system of models, the impact on related models should be assessed and relevant owners and users made aware; where the adjustment is material it should be subject to independent validation of both models. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the impact of those adjustments on related models should also be assessed"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.4(e)-(f): where firms use conservatism to address model uncertainties, it should be intuitive from a business and economic perspective, adequately justified and documented, and consistent with design objectives. Model owners or developers should demonstrate a clear link between model limitations and reasons for adjustments and be responsible for developing and implementing clear remediation plans. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should be intuitive from a business and economic perspective, adequately justified"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.4(g): firms should have a process to consider whether the materiality of model adjustments, or a trend of recurring adjustments for the same limitations, indicates flawed design or misspecification of the model construct, and consider the need for remedial action up to recalibration or redevelopment. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a trend of use of recurring model adjustments for the same model limitations"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 20: model uncertainty is the inherent uncertainty in the parameter estimates and results of statistical models, including uncertainty from model choices or model misuse. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inherent uncertainty in the parameter estimates and results of statistical models"</sub>

## Principle 3.5 Model development documentation

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.5(a): firms should have comprehensive, up-to-date documentation of the design, theory and logic behind model development, detailed enough that an independent third party with relevant expertise can understand how the model operates, identify key assumptions and limitations and replicate parameter estimation and results. Documentation of third-party vendor models should be detailed enough to validate the firm's use of the model. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an independent third party with the relevant expertise would be able to understand how the model operates"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.5(b): model documentation should include (i) use of data: sources, proxies and results of data quality, accuracy and relevance tests; (ii) choice of methodology: techniques, assumptions or approximations, processing components, mathematical specification, numerical and statistical techniques; (iii) performance testing: tests or criteria for ongoing monitoring and the rationale for choosing them; and (iv) model limitations and expert judgement: nature and extent of limitations, justification for adjustments and how they are calculated over time. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the use of data: a description of the data sources, any data proxies"</sub>

## Principle 3.6 Supporting systems

- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.6(a): models should be implemented in information systems or environments thoroughly tested for the intended model purposes and/or systems for which models have been validated and approved. Systems should be subject to rigorous quality control and change control, and the findings of system and implementation tests documented. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Models should be implemented in: information systems or environments that have been thoroughly tested"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 Principle 3.6(b): firms should periodically reassess the suitability of systems for model purposes and take appropriate remedial action as needed. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should periodically reassess the suitability of the systems for the model purposes"</sub>

(p. 25)[^original]
