## Expanding mandatory ISO 20022 enhanced data in CHAPS from 2027

- **descriptive / context**: The Bank sets out its position on expanding its mandatory ISO 20022 enhanced data requirements for CHAPS from November 2027, reflecting feedback to its 2024 consultation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This sets out our position on expanding our mandatory ISO 20022 enhanced data requirements for CHAPS from November 2027"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The intended industry benefits of ISO 20022 enhanced data for CHAPS payments include global harmonisation, improved payment prioritisation, improved straight-through processing (raising resilience and lowering cost), greater information and analysis capabilities, and improved fraud prevention. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "global harmonisation, improved payment prioritisation, improved straight-through processing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank has encouraged use of enhanced data since CHAPS migrated to ISO 20022 in June 2023, and has mandated Purpose Codes and Legal Entity Identifiers (LEIs) in certain CHAPS payments since May 2025. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we have mandated Purpose Codes and Legal Entity Identifiers (LEIs) in certain CHAPS payments since May 2025"</sub>

## 1: Overview

- **descriptive / context**: The 2024 consultation proposed expansions of enhanced data requirements to take effect in 2027 and received over 20 responses from CHAPS Direct Participants (DPs), an indirect participant, a trade association, an end-user, a standards group and an LEI issuer. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We received over 20 responses to our 2024 consultation."</sub>

## Policy position for November 2027

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents identified lack of readiness across the payment chain as an obstacle to including enhanced data in payments initiated outside the control of CHAPS DPs; CHAPS DPs have a leading role in driving change across the sector. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an obstacle to including enhanced data in payments initiated outside the control of CHAPS DPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank states its policy aims for the optimal trade-off between supporting the benefits of enhanced data and minimising implementation burden for industry where it is disproportionate, and it altered its proposal for payment initiation channels outside CHAPS DPs' control for this reason. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "optimal trade-off between supporting all the benefits that enhanced data can bring"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank did not consult on changes to its LEI policy but received feedback on including LEIs in ISO 20022 payment messages, and took specific steps with other organisations in response (set out in the Annex). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While we did not consult specifically on any changes to our LEI policy"</sub>

## Support for expanding purpose code requirements to all CHAPS payments from November 2027

- **descriptive / finding or result**: There was broad support for expanding purpose code requirements to all CHAPS payments from November 2027; several respondents saw Purpose Codes for financial institution and property transactions as a good pathfinder, and one noted the Bank and Pay.UK produced a shorter purpose code list for UK use. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There was broad support on expanding purpose code requirements to all CHAPS payments from November 2027."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Three respondents had already enabled Purpose Codes for all their payments (cross-border and customer); another is working with clients and channels to include them in all CHAPS payments by November 2027 and expects to meet requirements by then; another said requiring a purpose code in customer channels is a relatively easy step as the data was already introduced for FI-to-FI transactions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Three respondents reported that they have already enabled Purpose Codes for all their payments"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One respondent would prefer changes to accommodate the full range of enhanced data fields even though only a small fraction is mandated early, to prevent rework if other elements are later mandated and to lay foundations should retail payments systems mandate enhanced data; it noted much work on Purpose Codes and structured addresses was done with Pay.UK. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This will prevent rework should other elements be mandated for CHAPS"</sub>

(p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "how payment service providers (PSPs) implement Purpose Codes in their customer channels is in the competitive space"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Respondents noted greater benefit if Purpose Codes were also mandated on Swift CBPR+; the Bank agrees, follows CPMI best-practice on international harmonisation including use of globally standardised External code sets, welcomes Swift's aim to validate FINplus services and ISO messages against External code sets, and encourages CHAPS DPs to reinforce its communications on enhanced data benefits and CHAPS requirements to the international community. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Respondents noted that there would be greater benefit if Purpose Codes are also mandated on Swift CBPR+."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Bank agrees with respondents supporting alignment with other domestic retail payment systems and expects industry to reinforce its communications and requirements to realise the benefits of purpose codes; it is working with its Standards Advisory Panel on payments standards at a strategic level, including UK retail payments with Pay.UK and a strategic discussion on payment fraud standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we expect industry to reinforce our communications and requirements, to realise the benefits of purpose codes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Respondents called for a unified approach towards payments without purpose codes; the Bank supports CHAPS DP feedback on customer communications and an approach of monitoring and following up when Purpose Codes are not provided, and supports the general importance of data protection. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an approach of monitoring and following up when Purpose Codes are not provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An end-user respondent said some CHAPS DPs communicate requirements to customers with too little time before deadlines; the Bank publishes policy with at least 18 months' notice to allow DPs to communicate with sufficient time, continues to engage end-users, and follows up with DPs not giving customers sufficient time. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some CHAPS DPs are communicating CHAPS requirements to customers with too little time before deadlines"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Almost all respondents confirmed the stated change management timeframe of 18 months should enable industry to prepare for expanded purpose code requirements. (p. 1)[^original]

## 2.1: Proprietary regulatory reporting codes elsewhere

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Several respondents raised the need for consumer education on the differences between the purpose code field and the regulatory reporting code field (a proprietary code for the Country/Countries involved in the payment), as payers could misunderstand the data required for each. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "differences between the purpose code and regulatory reporting code fields"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents noted consumers may be unaware a payment will go through CHAPS and so may misuse purpose of payment fields; CHAPS DPs will need to change software to collect both the purpose code and the regulatory reporting code where required, and suppliers will need time to change systems and educate customers; one respondent suggested work on how the UK recommended list aligns with other country-specific purpose codes on international payments, e.g. a GBP payment to the UAE. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CHAPS DPs will need to change software to collect both the purpose code and the regulatory reporting code"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank's response: it will support CHAPS DPs to deliver sufficient education to customers and software suppliers on correct use of the purpose code and regulatory reporting code fields, and as CHAPS operator supports the PMPG's August 2024 best practice on populating them. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we will support CHAPS DPs to deliver sufficient education to customers and software suppliers"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents flagged the large number of Purpose Codes in the External code sets; given the range of options and overlaps making more than one code relevant to some payments, they thought PSPs and end-users would likely use them inconsistently. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "instances where some Purpose Codes overlap making more than one relevant to some payments"</sub>

## 2.2: The number of Purpose Codes

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Industry feedback: one respondent saw simplification opportunities; another said PSPs with highly defined markets could rationalise to a smaller set of Purpose Codes but this is harder for PSPs with broad retail and corporate bases; a couple warned of diminishing value if firms use different Purpose Codes for the same transaction, as inaccurate data reduces usefulness and analytics. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is a risk of diminishing value if firms adopt different Purpose Codes for the same transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One respondent believed all payments should have a purpose code but only a subset should be used by end-customers; another said there are too many codes for personal CHAPS payments, with over 300 Purpose Codes in the international ISO 20022 set and over 100 in the UK list; both reported that with long or unclear lists customers pick the quickest option, the wrong option or drop out of the journey. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "over 300 Purpose Codes available to use in the international ISO 20022 set and over 100 in the UK list"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Respondents warned wrong or poor purpose code selection could contradict the aims of Purpose Codes, such as harming a firm's ability to use purpose code data to tackle or prevent Authorised Push Payment scams, and making Purpose Codes less effective for incident management, financial crime compliance and operational resilience. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "negatively impacting on a firm’s ability to use purpose code data to tackle/prevent Authorised Push Payment scams"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank's response: the Bank and Pay.UK provided a draft segmented list in Annex B of the Purpose Codes consultation response and welcome feedback; Pay.UK's approach to enhanced data and Purpose Codes will include further alignment and, where possible, simplification; the Bank agrees on the importance of segmented market guidance, has begun convening experts for the property segment, and stands ready to work with DPs and experts on other segments. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We, along with Pay.UK, agree with the importance of segmented market guidance."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The Bank is open to exploring best practice implementation guidance with industry where it is not in the competitive space, for example agreeing an industry standard for segmenting recommended Purpose Codes to specific customer groups. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we are open to exploring the potential for best practice implementation guidance with the industry"</sub>

## 2.3: Clarity of Purpose Codes for customers

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents wanted purpose code collection to be user friendly since many customers rarely make CHAPS payments; one respondent said the key value-driving use cases are fraud mitigation, payments recovery and prioritisation, but these benefits are unclear to end-users so input quality may vary; the Bank has seen Purpose Codes used effectively to segment business alongside other enhanced data. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the key use cases driving value are fraud mitigation, payments recovery and prioritisation"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Feedback: consumers may challenge sharing information for mortgage transactions, manageable with clear rationale and Purpose Codes; unconsidered or 'bad data' could harm a firm's ability to use Purpose Code data to tackle or prevent fraud; and CHAPS DPs must also educate corporate customers about the implications of ISO 20022 for their CHAPS payments. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Unconsidered or ‘bad data’ could also negatively impact a firm’s ability to use Purpose Code data to tackle/prevent fraud"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Another respondent said the existing list of 127 Purpose Codes is unrealistic to present to a customer but accepted it will be responsible for advising customers; it said standardisation should give clear, consistent customer guidance, engagement with corporates on unstructured data use, clear UK guidance aligned to international practice on edge cases such as continuous linked settlement (CLS) payments, and work on how Purpose Codes can help prevent fraud where correctly categorised payments go to inappropriate accounts. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the existing list of 127 Purpose Codes is unrealistic to present to a customer when they make a payment"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Two respondents said that while risk of payment rejection may change customer behaviour, they support simplified end-user Purpose Code options to deter overuse of the 'other' code and support correct use, and that clear definitions and usage guidelines are needed so codes can be relied on for treatment and analytical strategies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "support simplified end-user Purpose Code options to deter overuse of ‘other’ code"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A separate respondent believed the number of codes routinely asked of customers in digital channels would be very limited, perhaps seven or eight; compared with transaction reasons asked when setting up a new Faster Payment payee, the existing list has too many codes for some headings (e.g. investments), codes not precise enough for others (e.g. payments to friends and family) and no code for some (e.g. crypto). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "perhaps seven or eight – especially in digital channels"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The respondent said it is important that a purpose code's definition and guidance accurately reflect transaction purpose if relied on for a treatment strategy, and that codes are populated accurately and reliably, which can depend on who populates them and their responsibility to do so accurately. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the definition and guidance around a purpose code accurately reflects the transaction purpose"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A final respondent was concerned about friction in customer interfaces for cross-border payments: the UK recommended Purpose Code list is a condensed version of the External code sets, so if the UK code does not match the exact reason a customer may need to give more than one purpose code, potentially discouraging customers. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The UK recommended Purpose Code list is a condensed version of the External code sets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank's response: the External code sets are a globally standardised list open to requests for new Purpose Codes; the Bank stands ready to support industry requests with strong business justification; the sets can be updated every quarter though not every update adds codes; Bank and Pay.UK can then reflect changes in the UK recommended list. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The external code sets can be updated every quarter – though not every quarterly update adds new purpose codes."</sub>

## 2.4: International alignment

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Respondents noted indirect participant clients instructing via Swift CBPR+ would need to provide Purpose Codes for CHAPS by November 2027, whereas under the November 2026 Swift specification these are optional with no mandatory adoption date; they sought information on Swift CBPR+ Network validation of the Purpose Code and said aligning CBPR+ and CHAPS requirements would ease client education, make the field mandatory for file-submitted payments and streamline IT development and testing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these requirements are optional and there is no date for mandatory adoption"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One respondent flagged the substantial documentation needed to meet country-specific international payment requirements and that CHAPS requirements add overhead for international payments with a CHAPS domestic leg; one respondent asked that 2028 be the date to extend the rejection period to, ideally aligned with Swift timelines; another asked the Bank to weigh international adoption and compliance rates against the extent to which payments must be rejected by CHAPS infrastructure. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "2028 would be a suitable date to extend the rejection period to"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank's response: it believes richer, structured data is a better outcome worldwide and follows CPMI best-practice on international harmonisation including the globally standardised External code sets; it welcomes Swift's aim to validate FINplus services and ISO messages against External code sets in 'non-blocking mode' in Standards Release 2025 and 'blocking mode' in Standards Release 2026, at which point messages containing unregistered external codes will be rejected (NAK'd) with intervention required from FINplus users. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At that point messages that contain external codes that are not registered at ISO 20022 will be rejected"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The Bank agrees it should continue to support ISO 20022 benefits within its control while other payment systems progress, and encourages CHAPS DPs to reinforce its communications on enhanced data benefits and CHAPS requirements to the international community, especially now that they have built capabilities for CHAPS. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we encourage CHAPS DPs to reinforce our communications on the benefits of enhanced data"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: On payment rejection, the Bank will not reject CHAPS payments solely due to incomplete or incorrect enhanced data before end-2028 at the earliest. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we will not reject CHAPS payments solely due to incomplete or incorrect enhanced data before end-2028 at the earliest"</sub>

- **descriptive / context**: Industry feedback under Section 2.1 also records the respondents' view that CHAPS DPs must optimise their channels to encourage correct use of the different fields (this is feedback, not a Bank requirement). (p. 1)[^original]

## 2.2: The number of Purpose Codes

- **descriptive / context**: Industry feedback under Section 2.2: a couple of respondents did not expect the total number of Purpose Codes to fall, and proposed splitting the list into segments with market guidance for each, reflecting the nature of payments in the segment and the sophistication of payers. One respondent said its retail customers choose from nine payment purposes and receive fraud warnings tailored to the purpose chosen. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their retail customers can choose from nine payment purposes and are provided tailored fraud warnings"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The respondents add that segmented guidance for different end-user cohorts would not stop users picking a purpose outside the guidance list but would give a manageable number of options, which could raise the chance of the right purpose being chosen. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Providing a segmented list in the guidance for different cohorts of end-users would not prevent end-users"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In its response under Section 2.2, the Bank, despite not extending requirements to channels outside DP control, says it is expecting industry support to realise the benefits of Purpose Codes in all CHAPS payments. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we are expecting industry support to realise the benefits of Purpose Codes in all CHAPS payments"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In the same response the Bank repeats that, as operator, it can convene groups, but CHAPS DPs have closer expertise on end-user data and must contribute expertise and advise on benefit cases. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CHAPS DPs have closer expertise on end-user data and must contribute expertise and advise on benefit cases"</sub>

## 2.3: Clarity of Purpose Codes for customers

- **descriptive / context**: Industry feedback under Section 2.3: one respondent said any proposal to ask consumers for Purpose Codes would need a purpose code list whose entries do not overlap; where there are several levels ('pick down' lists), a non-overlapping hierarchy whose top level shows what each class contains (a 'scent trail'). It noted that an overall class of 'general' would not help and that the existing category codes are not consumer focused. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A mutually exclusive purpose code list and if more than one level were involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The same respondent's further conditions for asking consumers for Purpose Codes: a limited number of choices to avoid confusion; operation alongside existing payment-process questions such as authorised push payment fraud warnings; consumer testing to check the options are understood; and consumer education on why Purpose Codes are requested and why accurate population matters. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To operate alongside any existing questions in the payments process"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: That respondent wanted collaborative work between stakeholders and CHAPS DPs to get maximum value from Purpose Codes sent by consumers (which it believes could number no more than seven or eight) and by businesses, for example for salaries and pensions, so that received data is accurate, understood and reliable. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For this reason, the respondent would like to see a piece of collaborative work involving stakeholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The respondent argued that Purpose Codes could be implemented in simplified form first and built up over time as automated population methods develop, with an initial focus on scam prevention and priority payment types, and that PSPs could over time aim for automatic system-driven controls such as spotting out-of-character payments to an account. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A focus on scam prevention and priority payment types may enable a more operational and value-add first-phase implementation."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In its response under Section 2.3 the Bank reports having recently settled several closest-match purpose code queries with CHAPS DPs. The agreed matches are: LIPS settlement uses NETT; funding reserves and other RTGS accounts use LIMA; controlled start and managed start payments use CASH; and CLS payments use FXNT. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CASH for controlled start and managed start payments."</sub>

## 2.5: UK alignment

- **descriptive / context**: Industry feedback under Section 2.5: a final respondent concluded that industry should keep collaborating and sharing information within the community, with Swift and with the Bank, so that the control build-out covers all the preventative, identifier and reactive controls for the enhanced data requirements, and it requested more industry-wide communications, especially for critical third parties, for effective and cost-efficient planning and delivery. (p. 1)[^original]
