## Summary of guidance issued / plan to issue

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Annex paragraph 8: the Bank and PRA's technology-agnostic approach does not usually mandate or prohibit specific technologies, they may issue guidance or other policy tools to clarify how existing rules apply, and thus far they have not issued AI-specific guidance or other policy tools. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Thus far, we have not issued AI-specific guidance or other policy tools."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Proportionality informs the Bank and PRA's approach to AI including potential future interventions; it is one of the FSMA 2000 regulatory principles the PRA and Bank must have regard to in discharging general functions including rulemaking, and Section 30E of the Banking Act 1998 applies these principles to the Bank's FMI functions, but only to FMI entities (CCPs and CSDs), not payment systems or firms. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The principle of proportionality also informs our thinking and approach to AI"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Chapter 4 of DP5/22 set out the legal requirements and guidance seen as most relevant to mitigating AI/ML risks; appendices cover Data (Appendix 3), Model Risk Management (Appendix 4) and selected relevant publications from international bodies and UK institutions (Appendix 5). (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They were broken down into Data – Appendix 3 – and Model Risk Management – Appendix 4."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Data management: BCBS 239 contains principles to strengthen prudential risk data aggregation, such as accuracy, integrity, completeness, timeliness and adaptability of data; the PRA expects the UK's globally systemically important banks to adhere to these principles. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The PRA expects the UK’s globally systemically important banks to adhere to these principles."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: For Solvency II insurers, Rule 12.1 of the Technical Provisions Part and Rule 4.3 of the Conditions Governing Business Part of the PRA Rulebook require firms to have internal processes and procedures ensuring completeness, accuracy and appropriateness of data used to calculate technical provisions. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require firms to have internal processes and procedures in place to ensure the completeness, accuracy, and appropriateness of the data"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Section 6.1(c) of the Conditions Governing Business Part requires insurers to assess the sufficiency and quality of data used in calculating technical provisions, and Rule 11.4 of the Solvency Capital Requirements - Internal Models Part states that data used for the internal model must be accurate, complete and appropriate. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "data used for the internal model must be accurate, complete, and appropriate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Where firms use AI to process personal data they may have obligations under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, with the ICO responsible for enforcing data protection compliance. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms may have regulatory obligations under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms also need to consider their obligations under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017; the Payment Services Regulations 2017 (PSRs) focus on security and quality of data transfers to third parties. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms also need to consider their obligations under The Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Other relevant requirements and guidance include Corporate governance principles for banks (BCBS 328) and the Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (PFMI). (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Corporate governance principles for banks (BCBS 328); Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (PFMI)"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: SS1/23 has expectations on data quality management procedures, including rules and standards for data quality, accuracy and relevance and specific risk controls and criteria reflecting higher uncertainty from alternative or unstructured data sources; SS11/13 (IRB approaches) sets expectations on data used in IRB models feeding credit regulatory capital, including data management approaches, data and reporting systems for IRB model management and capital reporting, and internal audit or independent materials on IT or data systems. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "specific risk controls and criteria applicable to reflect the higher level of uncertainty"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The AIPPF final report noted that MRM is increasingly becoming important as a primary framework for some firms to manage potential AI-related risks. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "MRM is increasingly becoming important as a primary framework"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SS1/23 created a unified approach to MRM under one supervisory statement where there was previously fragmentation, designed to identify, monitor and control risks of simple and complex models including AI; it is relevant to all banks and building societies with internal model approvals (a limited number of firms, 23), and includes expectations on dynamic models able to adapt, recalibrate or change autonomously in response to new inputs. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "created a unified approach to MRM under a single supervisory statement"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The SS1/23 principles are technology-agnostic and should cover future AI/ML developments; they apply only to banks with internal model approval and not to insurers, and the PRA will consider later whether they could be extended to insurers and banks not currently in scope. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These principles only apply to banks with internal model approval, and they do not apply to insurers."</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: BCBS 328 states a bank's risk management function is responsible for ensuring the board and management are aware of the assumptions used in, and potential shortcomings of, the bank's risk models and analyses (paragraph 119), and stresses that risk identification and measurement should include both quantitative and qualitative elements (paragraph 114). (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "risk identification and measurement should include both quantitative and qualitative elements"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SS5/18 (Algorithmic Trading) and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589 set existing expectations and requirements for algorithmic trading, including implementing 'kill switch' functionality in the control framework. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including the implementation of ‘kill switch’ functionality in the control framework"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Rule 6 of the PRA's Fundamental Rules states that a firm must organise and control its affairs responsibly and effectively. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must organise and control its affairs responsibly and effectively"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: SYSC 4.1.1 and Rule 2.1 of the PRA Rulebook General Organisational Requirements Part state that a firm must have robust governance arrangements: a clear organisational structure with well defined, transparent and consistent lines of responsibility, effective processes to identify, manage, monitor and report risks, and internal control mechanisms including sound administrative and accounting procedures and effective control and safeguard arrangements for information processing systems; the Rulebook also has provisions on compliance, internal audit, financial crime, risk control, outsourcing and recordkeeping, and insurers' governance rules are in Conditions Governing Business; these general rules are relevant to a firm's use of AI. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must have robust governance arrangements, which include a clear organisational structure with well defined"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Solvency II Article 258 (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35), applying to all insurers, requires firms to ensure that members of the administrative, management or supervisory body collectively possess the necessary qualifications, competency, skills and professional experience in the relevant areas of the business to manage and oversee the undertaking professionally. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collectively possess the necessary qualifications, competency, skills, and professional experience"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank, PRA and FCA have developed a coordinated framework to strengthen operational resilience of the UK financial sector; SS1/21 sets PRA expectations for operational resilience of important business services. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "coordinated regulatory and supervisory framework to strengthen the operational resilience"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Annex paragraph 10: DP5/22 responses did not display clear evidence of a need for an AI-specific framework, but respondents said clarification on how existing guidance applies to AI/ML would be useful in Data Management, Model Risk Management, Governance, and Operational Resilience and Third-Party Risks (summarised in FS2/23); the Bank has addressed some areas and will continue to consider the rest. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the responses did not display clear evidence of the need for an AI-specific framework"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Data management: the regulatory landscape is fragmented; for large banks there are of the order of 10 pieces of regulation touching on data governance variously but no overarching data governance requirements, and the Bank is considering options to address this. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there are no overarching data governance requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under SS1/21 on operational resilience and impact tolerance for important business services, the PRA expects firms to identify their important business services by weighing the risk that disruption to them poses to financial stability (where applicable) and to the firm's safety and soundness, and, for insurers, the risk to policyholder protection. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The PRA expects firms to identify important business services considering the risk their disruption poses"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SS1/23 on model risk management principles for banks will come into effect in May 2024. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will come into effect in May 2024"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: On governance, respondents to DP5/22 said existing structures such as the SM&CR suffice for AI risks and no dedicated AI SMF is needed; SS1/23 includes governance expectations in which firms must identify the relevant SMF(s) to take overall responsibility for the MRM framework and requires firms to provide a model inventory including AI/ML models. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must identify the relevant SMF(s) to assume overall responsibility for the firms MRM framework"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank, PRA and FCA are assessing their approach to critical third parties (CTPs), including a consultation paper; the proposed requirements aim to manage risks to the stability of, or confidence in, the UK financial system from failure or disruption of services a CTP provides to firms or FMIs. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "critical third parties to the financial sector, which has included publishing a consultation paper"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The CTP regime is not AI/ML-specific, but the concept of CTP services is broad enough to cover systemic use of a common AI/ML model (e.g. data bias, model robustness); ML adoption may give rise to critical third-party AI providers, which could be captured by the regime if designated by HM Treasury. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could be captured by the proposed regime for CTPs if they were designated by HM Treasury"</sub>

## Current and future work we are doing to understand, assess, and manage risks posed by AI/ML

- **descriptive / context**: AI/ML risks are highlighted in joint publications with the FCA: Machine Learning in UK financial services (2019 and 2022), DP5/22, FS2/23 and the AI Public-Private Forum final report. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Machine Learning in UK financial services (2019); Machine Learning in UK financial services (2022)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In December 2023 the FPC was briefed on continued AI/ML adoption including LLMs; engagement with firms shows many are experimenting with LLMs but most identified use cases are relatively low risk, such as information search and retrieval and generating outputs for internal use. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most use cases identified to date are relatively low risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Wider adoption of AI/ML could pose system-wide financial stability risks, e.g. by amplifying herding or broader procyclical behaviours or increasing cyber-risk and interconnectedness; the impact needs careful monitoring and is ongoing Bank work in 2024, with the Bank seeking with other authorities to ensure UK financial system resilience. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "amplifying herding or broader procyclical behaviours or increasing cyber-risk and interconnectedness"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Bank also analyses wider technology developments such as quantum computing; advances could pose risks to public key cryptography widely used to secure data and systems, and the excerpt states quantum computers are able to decrypt messages without access to the private key, presenting cyber security risks that could interact with AI-related cyber-risks. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "advances in quantum computing could pose risks to public key cryptography"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank will keep building on its framework for cyber and operational resilience, including the FPC's cyber stress tests, SS1/21 and participation in the Cross Market Operational Resilience Group (CMORG), and recently published Financial Stability in Focus on the FPC's macroprudential approach to operational resilience, which references AI's impact. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FPC’s cyber stress tests, SS1/21 – Operational Resilience: Impact tolerance for important business services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In 2024 the Bank, PRA and FCA plan the third instalment of the 'ML in UK financial services' survey and are considering a follow-up industry consortium to the AIPPF, with details forthcoming. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "considering establishing a follow-up industry consortium to the AIPPF"</sub>
