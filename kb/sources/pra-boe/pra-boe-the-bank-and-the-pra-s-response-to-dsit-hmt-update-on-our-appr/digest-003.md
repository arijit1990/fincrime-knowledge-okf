## Interactions with our remit and other regulators, including collaboration

- **descriptive / context**: Annex paragraph 15: the Bank has worked closely with the FCA on several initiatives exploring AI/ML adoption by UK financial firms and implications for their remits, particularly where these overlap or are complementary. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we have worked closely with the FCA on several initiatives exploring the adoption of AI/ML"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 60: public key cryptography uses a pair of public and private keys to encrypt messages in a way which is all but impossible to decrypt with conventional computers without the private keys. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all but impossible to decrypt using conventional computers without access to the private keys"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2019 Bank-FCA joint survey on ML in UK financial services found firms increasingly using ML and needing effective and evolving risk management controls to use it safely and harness benefits. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "needed effective and evolving risk management controls if they were to use it safely"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Bank and FCA launched the AIPPF in 2020 with experts from financial services, tech and academia plus public sector observers; its final report (February 2022) explored barriers, challenges and risks of AI in financial services and identified that the primary drivers of AI risk relate to three lifecycle stages: data, models and governance. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "three key stages of the AI lifecycle: data, models and governance"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A second Bank-FCA survey in 2022 found that UK financial services firms are continuing to adopt machine learning at pace. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "UK financial services firms are continuing to adopt machine learning at pace"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Alongside the 2022 survey the Bank, PRA and FCA published DP5/22 seeking views on gaps and how additional intervention might support safe and responsible AI adoption; FS2/23 summarising responses was published in October 2023. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sought views on whether there are gaps and/or how additional intervention might support the safe and responsible adoption of AI"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Regulatory coordination: the Bank is represented on the DRCF quarterly regulators' roundtable and will work with the DRCF on selected AI/ML projects in 2024; it is a member of the ICO's AI and Regulators Working Group and represented on the Alan Turing Institute's Standards Hub Regulators' Forum; the Bank and PRA are also members of several international fora discussing AI/ML with peer regulators. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "member of the Information Commissioner’s Office's AI and Regulators Working Group"</sub>

## Current and future capabilities to address AI risks within our regulatory remit

- **descriptive / context**: Annex paragraph 16: prudential AI/ML policy development is primarily led by the Bank's Fintech Hub and Prudential Policy Directorate (PPD), but draws on staff across the institution (Model Risk Management, Data, Operational Resilience and Third-Party Management, Governance, supervision, financial stability analysis); as the Bank is technology-agnostic, its AI/ML work benefits most from general policy development. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "primarily led by the Bank’s Fintech Hub and Prudential Policy Directorate (PPD)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The 2022 joint Bank-PRA-FCA Discussion Paper was supported by a cross-Bank and PRA working group of around 40 colleagues with expertise in prudential policy, data, banking supervision, insurance supervision, FMI supervision, supervisory risk, advanced analytics and legal. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a cross-Bank and PRA working group of around 40 colleagues"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Bank and PRA use AI where appropriate to enhance capabilities: the Bank uses AI for predictive analytics, non-linear interactions and larger datasets, potentially helping forecast GDP growth, bank distress and financial crises; it is exploring AI text analysis of newspapers for economic forecasting and 'faster indicators' for real-time analysis; the PRA introduced a cognitive search tool with AI capabilities helping supervisors extract patterns from unstructured, complex firm management information and is developing other AI tools for staff. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The PRA successfully introduced a cognitive search tool with AI capabilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank established the Fintech Hub in 2018 to examine how technology is changing financial services; AI/ML is a key focus and the Hub led the ML surveys, the AIPPF, DP5/22 and FS2/23. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Bank established the Fintech Hub to examine how technology is changing financial services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FMID supervises UK FMIs and owns and analyses some of the Bank's largest structured datasets, informing the FPC, PRC and MPC, the Financial Stability Report, research and speeches; it collaborates with data scientists in other business areas, making it possible to apply basic machine learning tools to FMID data. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which makes it possible to apply basic machine learning tools on FMID data"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank established the Advanced Analytics (AA) Division in 2014 (now part of DAT) as a centre of expertise for ML and AI working with all Bank areas to ensure research functions use modern analytical methods; close to 20 FTE work in AA. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Bank established the Advanced Analytics (AA) Division"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As of September 2023 the Bank had 82 data scientists in total. Most were in the Monetary Policy area, which includes the Data and Analytical Transformation (DAT) Directorate (37 of them); the PRA is listed separately alongside with 30. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there were 82 data scientists across the Bank most of which were in the Monetary Policy area"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A further 10 data scientists work in the FMI Directorate (FMID), in addition to the 82 across the Bank. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There are a further 10 data scientists in the FMI Directorate (FMID)."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In 2021 the PRA established the RegTech, Data and Innovation Division (RDID) to bolster efficiency, effectiveness and data culture through phased investment in tools, technology, processes and skills, including building targeted ML tools for front-line supervision (e.g. processing large unstructured data); it has around 35 FTE, roughly one quarter building specialist ML tools and digital skills work for all PRA staff. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the PRA established a dedicated RegTech, Data and Innovation Division (RDID)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: PRA staff outside RDID also help build these capabilities, supported by the Bank's Data & Analytics agenda; the work helps staff develop and maintain skills needed to understand AI tools used by firms. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "skills needed to understand AI tools used by firms"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank and PRA resourcing model aims to make AI/ML knowledge mainstream: a dedicated, mandatory curriculum teaches supervisors the fundamentals of emerging technologies such as AI, with optional courses on the technology and use-cases and practical training on using AI in day-to-day work. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dedicated and mandatory curriculum in which supervisors learn the"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank/PRA hold 'fintech weeks' with regulated firms and other companies; the next, 'PRA Innovation Week', is in Q2 2024 and will include some talks on AI adoption in financial services. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "“PRA Innovation Week”, in Q2 2024"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Annex paragraph 18: the Bank recently set up a cross-organisation AI taskforce to use AI, particularly generative AI, effectively, safely and responsibly, with three aims: identify promising AI/ML use cases and run pilots; develop guardrails to control AI risks, including an ethical framework for responsible use of AI/ML and other quantitative models; and identify training needs and grow a culture where models are understood and used effectively; its work is scrutinised by internal management and risk committees. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cross-organisation AI taskforce to ensure that it makes progress using AI"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank recognises it has much to learn from others and will keep engaging external organisations and consider a technology environment for experimentation; at this stage it does not anticipate a change in resourcing related to regulating firms' AI/ML use, but will reprioritise analytical and supervisory resources as warranted. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we do not anticipate a change in resourcing related to the regulation of the use of AI/ML"</sub>
