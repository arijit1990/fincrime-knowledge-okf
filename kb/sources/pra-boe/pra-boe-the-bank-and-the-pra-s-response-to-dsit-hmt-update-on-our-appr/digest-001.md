- **descriptive / context**: In a letter dated 22 April 2024 from Sam Woods and Sarah Breeden (Bank of England Deputy Governors) to the Secretary of State for Science, Innovation and Technology and the Economic Secretary to the Treasury, the Bank, including the PRA, responds to a joint letter of 1 February 2024 on its work on delivering safe and responsible AI and ML within its regulatory remit; an Annex gives supporting detail.   <sub>anchor: "better detect fraud and money laundering, and enhance data and analytics capabilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank states it has so far been able to meet its statutory objectives while supporting safe and responsible AI/ML adoption, and will keep its approach under continuous review given the pace of innovation and evolving use cases; it has engaged and will continue to engage with the tech sector, academia and firms. (p. 2)[^original]

## The Bank and the PRA’s statutory objectives and approach to regulation

- **descriptive / context**: The Bank's statutory objectives are to maintain monetary and financial stability in the UK; the MPC and FPC have secondary objectives to support the Government's economic policy including growth and employment. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain monetary and financial stability in the UK"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The PRA's objectives are to promote the safety and soundness of the firms it regulates and to contribute to securing an appropriate degree of protection for insurance policyholders; its two secondary objectives are to facilitate effective competition between firms and to facilitate the international competitiveness of the UK economy and its medium to long term growth. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "promote the safety and soundness of the firms we regulate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank and PRA focus on how to support safe and responsible AI/ML adoption from a macro-financial and prudential perspective, given its potential benefits including driving innovation. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from a macro-financial and prudential perspective"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank and PRA adopt a technology-agnostic approach to supervision and regulation of AI/ML; core principles, rules and regulations do not usually mandate or prohibit specific technologies, but technology-agnostic does not mean technology-blind, since risks may relate to specific technologies and adversely affect statutory objectives. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "However, technology-agnostic does not mean technology-blind; risks may arise"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Where technologies raise novel challenges making it difficult for firms to understand how existing rules apply, the Bank and PRA may issue guidance or use other policy tools to clarify how existing rules and expectations apply. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we may issue guidance or use other policy tools to clarify how the existing rules"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: As of 2 January 2024, the PRA is responsible for the prudential regulation and supervision of around 1,330 firms, a group that spans banks, building societies, credit unions, insurers and major investment firms. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "around 1,330 banks, building societies, credit unions, insurers, and major investment firms"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank also regulates certain financial market infrastructures (FMIs); under the Financial Services and Markets Act (FSMA) 2023 it has a secondary objective to facilitate 'innovation in the provision of FMI services', with further details given in the Annex. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Bank also regulates certain financial market infrastructures6 (FMIs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Having worked with the FCA over many years on AI/ML risks, the Bank and PRA believe the regulatory framework has proven well equipped to capture regulated firms' use of AI/ML; feedback to DP5/22 confirms this and was summarised in FS2/23. They believe the approach is consistent with the Government's principles-based framework but will keep it under review. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "our regulatory framework has proven to be well equipped to capture regulated firms’ use of AI/ML"</sub>

## Our current and future work on the safe and responsible adoption of AI/ML in financial services

- **descriptive / context**: AI/ML could make financial services and markets more efficient, accessible and tailored to consumers, but greater adoption could also pose risks to the Bank and PRA's objectives, so they have a close interest in encouraging safe and responsible adoption. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "greater AI/ML adoption could also pose risks to the Bank and the PRA’s objectives"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Timeline of work: ML adoption reports first published in 2019 with a follow-up in 2022; the Bank and FCA ran the AI Public-Private Forum from 2020 to 2022; DP5/22 was published in 2022 seeking views on whether the existing framework suffices; FS2/23 was published in October 2023; in December 2023 the FPC noted it had been briefed on continued AI/ML adoption and would further consider the risks in 2024. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "From 2020 to 2022, the Bank and the FCA ran the AI Public-Private Forum"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank and PRA are exploring four areas where further clarification of the regulatory framework could be beneficial for AI/ML: (1) Data Management; (2) Model Risk Management; (3) Governance; and (4) Operational Resilience and Third-Party Risks. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(1) Data Management; (2) Model Risk Management; (3) Governance; and (4) Operational Resilience and Third-Party Risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Planned work: a third instalment of the 'ML in UK financial services' survey; deeper analysis during the year of the financial stability implications of AI/ML, to be considered by the FPC; and continued direct stakeholder engagement, which may include establishing a new AI Consortium. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "third instalment of the comprehensive ‘ML in UK financial services’ survey"</sub>

## collaboration

- **descriptive / context**: The Bank welcomes the Government's five principles to guide AI/ML regulation (in the initial guidance for regulators), which it considers broadly consistent with its approach; some principles are more relevant to its remit than others. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which we consider are broadly consistent with our approach to regulating the use of AI/ML"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank agrees regulatory collaboration on AI/ML is essential, given that matters within one regulator's purview can affect another's objectives; it works closely with the FCA and in 2024 will work with the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF) on selected AI/ML projects, such as joint research on cross-sector adoption of generative AI. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regulatory collaboration in this space is essential"</sub>

## Conclusion

- **descriptive / context**: The Bank and PRA support the Government's aim for an AI regulatory framework that is pro-innovation, proportionate, trustworthy, adaptable, clear and collaborative, and believe their current framework, grounded in statutory objectives, appropriately supports AI/ML benefits while addressing risks. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pro-innovation, proportionate, trustworthy, adaptable, clear, and collaborative"</sub>

## Yours sincerely,

- **descriptive / context**: Annex paragraph 1: the Bank's FPC, subject to the Financial Stability objective of protecting and enhancing UK financial system stability, exercises its functions with a view to supporting the Government's economic policy including growth and employment objectives. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "protect and enhance the stability of the financial system of the UK"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Bank supervises certain FMIs (payment systems, CCPs, CSDs). FSMA 2023 introduced a new secondary objective requiring the Bank, so far as reasonably possible, to act in a way that facilitates innovation in provision of FMI services, to improve quality, efficiency and economy; and requires the Bank, when exercising FMI functions in a way that advances the Financial Stability objective, to have due regard to section 30E Banking Act 1998 principles, effects on financial stability of other countries where CCPs/CSDs are established or provide services, and the desirability of regulating CCPs and CSDs irrespective of whether users are in the UK or elsewhere. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Bank must have due regard to: (1) specified regulatory principles in section 30E of the Banking Act 1998"</sub>

## Steps taken to address the AI principles set out in the guidance

- **descriptive / context**: Annex paragraph 2: the Government's five principles emphasise (1) safety, security and robustness; (2) appropriate transparency and explainability; (3) fairness; (4) accountability and governance; and (5) contestability and redress; the Bank considers them broadly consistent with its regulatory approach. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(1) safety, security, and robustness; (2) appropriate transparency and explainability; (3) fairness; (4) accountability and governance"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex paragraph 3: the Government defines safety, security and robustness as AI systems functioning in a robust, secure and safe way throughout the AI life cycle, with risks continually identified, addressed and managed. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI systems should function in a robust, secure, and safe way throughout the AI life cycle"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Bank, PRA and FCA flagged outsourcing and third-party risk management concerns in DP5/22; policies on outsourcing/TPRM put the onus on regulated firms to manage risks from suppliers where third-party services support important business services, and address specific cloud computing risks (SS2/21), cloud often being the infrastructure on which AI applications are developed and deployed. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "put the onus on regulated firms to manage risks from their suppliers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Bank expects firms and FMIs to identify and manage risks from 'off-the shelf' machine learning models, including samples of data used to train and test them, open-source software and ML libraries developed by third-party providers; the FMI outsourcing and third-party risk management policy similarly notes that ML libraries from third parties may be non-outsourcing third party arrangements. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We expect firms and FMIs to identify and manage the risks from"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex paragraph 4: the guidance says AI systems should be appropriately transparent and explainable. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI systems should be appropriately transparent and explainable"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SS1/23 (Model Risk Management principles for banks) lists explainability and transparency as factors to consider when assessing model complexity; increased complexity requires more oversight, prioritising validation and risk controls on more complex models; the principles apply to all models used by banks, not just AI, and to third-party models. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lists explainability and transparency as factors that should be considered when assessing the complexity of a model"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SS1/23 uses a broad model definition including qualitative model outputs, intended to bring recommendation systems in client services and other AI/ML delivering qualitative outputs within its scope. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including qualitative model outputs in the definition"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 does not require banks to make ML algorithms more interpretable or explainable and does not define either concept; it expects regulated banks to define them and to establish policies and procedures determining how this is done using risk-based model tiering. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We also do not define either concept, but rather expect regulated banks to do so."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under UK GDPR, personal data processing must be fair and transparent; data controllers must give data subjects information about processing including profiling and the existence of automated decision-making (Articles 13 and 14); where decisions have legal or similarly significant effects, the notice must also include meaningful information about the logic involved and the significance and envisaged consequences for the data subject. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data controllers must provide data subjects with various information about their data processing activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The Bank recognises that the growing complexity of AI/ML models such as LLMs challenges the concepts of explainability and transparency. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the growing complexity of AI/ML models, such as LLMs, challenge the concepts of explainability and transparency"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex paragraph 5: the guidance says AI systems should not undermine legal rights of individuals or organisations, discriminate unfairly or create unfair market outcomes, and actors at all life cycle stages should consider descriptions of fairness appropriate to the system's use, outcomes and relevant law. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI systems should not undermine the legal rights of individuals or organisations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The fairness principle is more relevant to the FCA and consumer-facing regulators; where fairness is deemed relevant to the Bank's remit (e.g. prudential soundness), it would expect firms to define fairness for themselves, with justification. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we would expect firms to define fairness for themselves, with justification"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Bank is subject to the Equality Act 2010, which requires it to have due regard to the need to eliminate discrimination between those with protected characteristics and others, whenever carrying out a public function (e.g. monetary policy, prudential regulation, financial stability, issuing bank notes). (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Bank is subject to the Equality Act 2010"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex paragraph 6: the guidance says governance measures could be put in place to ensure effective oversight of supply and use of AI systems, with clear lines of accountability across the AI life cycle; these requirements are principally covered by the SM&CR (implemented through the PRA Rulebook and Supervisory Statements), with SS1/23 setting specific model risk expectations. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These requirements are principally covered by the Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The SM&CR is an individual accountability regime; regulated firms are required to ensure one or more Senior Managers have overall responsibility for the firm's main activities, business areas and management functions, so any material use of AI/ML in relation to those should be set out as within a Senior Manager's responsibilities. Senior Managers can be held accountable for breaches in their area and could face enforcement action if they failed to take reasonable steps to prevent them. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regulated firms are required to ensure that one or more of their Senior Managers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SMR requirements are in the PRA Rulebook (Allocation of Responsibilities and Senior Management Functions for CRR firms; Insurance - Allocation of Responsibilities and Insurance - Senior Manager Functions for Solvency II firms); SS28/15 and SS35/15 set PRA expectations on individual accountability in banking and insurance, and SS5/21 states expectations on SMF accountability for international bank branches and subsidiaries. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SS28/15 – Strengthening individual accountability in banking"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Currently technology systems are the responsibility of SMF24 (Chief Operations Function), while SMF4 (Chief Risk Function) is responsible for overall risk management including setting and managing risk exposures (PRA Rulebook Senior Management Functions Part Rules 3.4 and 3.8). (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "technology systems are the responsibility of the SMF24 (Chief Operations Function)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In DP5/22 the Bank and PRA sought feedback on whether there should be a dedicated SMF for AI; respondents said existing firm governance structures and frameworks such as the SM&CR are sufficient to address AI risks, as outlined in FS2/23. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "explicitly sought feedback on whether there should be a dedicated SMF for AI"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The response cites supervisory statement SS5/16 on corporate governance and Board responsibilities as setting expectations for Boards, in particular Section 4 on risk appetite, risk management and internal controls, Section 6 on the roles of executive and non-executive directors, and Section 9 on management information and transparency. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SS5/16 – Corporate governance: Board responsibilities37 establishes expectations for Boards, particularly Section 4"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: PRA Rulebook sections General Organisation Requirements (banks) and Conditions Governing Business (insurers) give governance rules: General Organisation Requirements Section 2.1 sets high-level governance, risk management and controls requirements and Section/Part 5.1 sets governance requirements for management bodies; Risk Control Section 2.1A covers high level governance for risk management procedures; insurers' risk management is in Conditions Governing Business Section 3. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "General Organisation Requirements Section 2.1 establishes high-level requirements on governance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SS1/23 establishes that the PRA expects strong governance oversight with a board that promotes a MRM culture from the top by setting clear model risk appetite. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strong governance oversight with a board that promotes a ‘MRM’ culture from the top"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Banks should identify relevant SMF(s), appropriate to their structure and risk profile, to assume overall responsibility for the MRM framework, its implementation, execution and maintenance. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks should identify a relevant Senior Management Function/Functions (SMF(s))"</sub>
- **permissive / expectation**: The accountable SMF(s)' MRM responsibilities may include establishing policies and procedures to operationalise the MRM framework and ensure compliance; assigning roles and responsibilities; ensuring effective challenge and independent validation; evaluating model results, validation and internal audit reports; taking prompt remedial action to keep aggregate model risk within board-approved risk appetite; and ensuring sufficient resourcing, systems and infrastructure. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "taking prompt remedial action when necessary to ensure the bank’s aggregate model risk remains within the board approved risk appetite"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: SS1/23 requires banks to provide a comprehensive model inventory, which includes AI/ML models. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SS1/23 requires banks to provide a comprehensive model inventory"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex paragraph 7: the guidance says that where appropriate, users, impacted third parties and AI life cycle actors should be able to contest an AI decision or outcome that is harmful or creates material risk of harm. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be able to contest an AI decision or outcome"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Contestability and redress sits more within consumer-facing regulators' domain, so the Bank and PRA have not taken action to implement it in practice at this point; under UK GDPR Article 22 consumers have the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, with legal or similarly significant effect. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Bank and the PRA have not taken action to implement it in practice at this point"</sub>
