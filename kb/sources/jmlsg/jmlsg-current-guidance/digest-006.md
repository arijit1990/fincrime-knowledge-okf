## RISK-BASED APPROACH

- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 4.43 (SYSC 6.3.6 G): to implement a reasonable RBA, firms should identify criteria to assess potential money laundering risks. Identifying the ML or TF risks (to the extent TF risk can be identified) of customers or categories of customers, and transactions, allows firms to design and implement proportionate measures and controls. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should identify criteria to assess potential money laundering risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.44: ML and TF risks may be measured using a number of factors, and applying risk categories to customers/situations gives a strategy for managing risk through proportionate controls and oversight. The key risk criteria are country or geographic risk, customer risk, and product/services risk. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The key risk criteria are: country or geographic risk; customer risk; and product/services risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.44: the weight given to these criteria (individually or in combination) may vary from one institution to another, so firms have to make their own determination of risk weights, although parameters set by law or regulation may limit a firm's discretion (Regulations 33(7), 37(4)). (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms have to make their own determination as to the risk weights"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.46 (Regulation 28(13)): when assessing ML/TF/PF risks relating to types of customers, countries or geographic areas, and products, services, transactions or delivery channels, a firm should take into account risk variables for those categories. Variables, singly or in combination, may increase or decrease risk and so affect the appropriate level of CDD. Examples are the purpose of an account or relationship, the level of assets to be deposited or the size of transactions, and the regularity or duration of the business relationship. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm should take into account risk variables relating to those risk categories"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.45 refers firms to Annex 4-II, which sets out a longer list of illustrative risk factors that a firm may look at when judging the ML/TF/PF risk presented by customer situations. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Annex 4-II contains a fuller list of illustrative risk factors a firm may address"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.48: a risk assessment will often result in a stylised categorisation such as high/medium/low, with criteria attached to each category to allocate customers and products and to determine different treatments of identification, verification, additional customer information and monitoring, in a way that minimises complexity. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A risk assessment will often result in a stylised categorisation of risk"</sub>

## Weighting of risk factors

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.49: when weighting risk factors, firms should make an informed judgment about the relevance of different factors in the context of a particular customer relationship or occasional transaction. This often results in different 'scores' for different factors; for example, a customer's personal links to a higher-risk jurisdiction may be less relevant given the features of the product sought. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should make an informed judgment about the relevance of different risk factors"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.50: the weight given to each factor is likely to vary from product to product, customer to customer (or category) and firm to firm. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the weight given to each of these factors is likely to vary from product to product"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.50: when weighting factors, firms should ensure that weighting is not unduly influenced by just one factor, and that economic or profit considerations do not influence the risk rating. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Weighting is not unduly influenced by just one factor"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.50: firms should ensure weighting does not lead to a situation where it is impossible for any business to be classified as high risk, and that situations identified by national legislation or risk assessments as always presenting a high money laundering risk cannot be over-ruled by the firm's weighting. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Weighting does not lead to a situation where it is impossible for any"</sub>

## RISK-BASED APPROACH

- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 4.47: in assessing risk, firms should consider all the relevant risk factors before settling on the overall risk category and the level of mitigation to apply. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should consider all relevant risk factors before determining what is the overall risk category"</sub>

## Weighting of risk factors

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.52: firms should be aware of the FCA's published enforcement findings on individual firms and of its actions in response; this information is available on the FCA website. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should be aware of the FCA’s published enforcement findings in relation to individual firms"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.50: firms should ensure they are able to override any automatically generated risk scores where necessary, and the rationale for the override should be documented appropriately. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The rationale for the decision to override such scores should be documented appropriately"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.51: where a firm uses automated systems purchased from an external provider to allocate overall risk scores, it should understand how the systems work and how they combine risk factors. A firm must always be able to satisfy itself that the scores reflect its understanding of ML/TF/PF risk, and should be able to demonstrate this to the FCA if necessary. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should understand how such systems work and how it combines risk factors to achieve an overall risk score"</sub>

## Lower risk/simplified due diligence

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 4.53: many customers, by nature or from what the firm already knows, carry a lower ML or TF risk. These might include employment-based customers or those with a regular, known source of income supporting the activity (equally pensioners, benefit recipients, or those whose income comes from a partner's employment); customers with a long-term and active relationship with the firm; and customers represented by those whose appointment is subject to court approval or ratification (such as executors). (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Many customers, by their nature or through what is already known about them by the firm, carry a lower money laundering or terrorist financing risk"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Paragraph 4.54 (Regulation 37(1)): there are circumstances beyond those in 4.53 where ML/TF risk may be lower. If the country or the firm has adequately analysed the risk, taking account of the risk factors in Regulation 37(3), the firm may apply reduced CDD measures, where local law or regulation permits. Part I paragraphs 5.4.1ff give further guidance on simplified due diligence. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm may (if permitted by local law or regulation) apply reduced CDD measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.57: firms need to be aware that allowing a higher risk customer to take a lower risk product or service on a verification standard suited to that lower risk product can mean further verification is later required, particularly if the customer afterwards wants a higher risk product or service. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allowing a higher risk customer to acquire a lower risk product or service on the basis of a verification standard"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Paragraph 4.56: lower ML/TF risk for identification and verification purposes does not automatically mean the same customer is lower risk for all types of CDD measures, in particular ongoing monitoring of transactions. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not automatically mean that the same customer is lower risk for all types of CDD measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.57: firms should not judge risk solely on the nature of the customer or the product. Where the customer and/or the product is considered higher risk, the overall risk of the customer should be considered carefully. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should not, however, judge the level of risk solely on the nature of the customer or the product"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.58: further considerations for risk assessment are set out in the sectoral guidance in Part II. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further considerations to be borne in mind in carrying out a risk assessment are set out in the sectoral guidance in Part II"</sub>

## Higher risk/enhanced due diligence

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.59: potentially higher risk situations may be influenced by customer risk factors, country or geographic risk factors, and product, service, transaction or delivery channel risk factors. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "potentially higher risk situations may be influenced by"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.62: where ML/TF/PF risks are higher, firms must conduct enhanced due diligence measures consistent with the risks identified. In particular they must, as far as reasonably possible, examine the background and purpose of the transaction (Regulation 33(4)). (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where the risks of ML/TF/PF are higher, firms must conduct enhanced due diligence measures consistent with the risks identified"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 4.62(b): examples of further EDD measures that could be used for higher risk business relationships, depending on the case, include obtaining (and where appropriate verifying) additional customer information and updating the identification of the customer and any beneficial owner more often; obtaining more information about the intended nature of the business relationship; obtaining information on the customer's source of funds or source of wealth; and obtaining information on the reasons for intended or completed transactions. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could be applied for higher risk business relationships include"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 4.62(b), continued: further example EDD measures are obtaining senior management approval to start or continue the business relationship; enhanced monitoring of the relationship by increasing the number and timing of controls and selecting transaction patterns that need closer examination; and requiring the first payment to be made through an account in the customer's name with a bank subject to similar CDD standards. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Obtaining the approval of senior management to commence or"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.62(a)/(Regulation 33(5)): firms must also increase the degree and nature of monitoring of the business relationship to determine whether transactions or activities appear unusual or suspicious. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "relationship, in order to determine whether these transactions or activities appear unusual or suspicious"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.60 (Regulation 33(1)): where higher risks are identified, firms are required to take enhanced measures to manage and mitigate them. Politically Exposed Persons and correspondent relationships have been specifically identified by the authorities as higher risk, as have business relationships with customers established in a high risk third country and relevant transactions where either party is established in a high risk third country. Specific EDD guidance is in section 5.5. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms are required to take enhanced measures to manage and mitigate the risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.61: where a customer is assessed as higher risk, then depending on the product it will be necessary to seek additional information to judge whether the perceived higher risk is likely to materialise. Such information may include an understanding of where the customer's funds and wealth have come from; types of additional information are set out in section 5.5. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it will be necessary to seek additional information in respect of the customer"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 4.62(b) (continued): further example EDD measures are obtaining senior management approval to commence or continue the relationship; enhanced monitoring by increasing the number and timing of controls and selecting transaction patterns needing further examination; and requiring the first payment to be made through an account in the customer's name with a bank subject to similar CDD standards. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Obtaining the approval of senior management to commence or"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.63: Annex 4-II contains a fuller list of illustrative risk factors for ML/TF/PF risk posed by customer situations. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Annex 4-II contains a fuller list of illustrative risk factors a firm may address when considering the ML/TF/PF risk posed by customer situations"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.64 (Regulation 33(1)(f), (4)): where EDD measures are applied, firms must look into the background and purpose of all complex or unusually large transactions, unusual transaction patterns, and transactions with no apparent economic or legal purpose, as far as is reasonably possible. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must as far as reasonably possible examine the background and purpose of all complex or unusually large transactions"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.64: where EDD measures are applied, firms must also step up the degree and nature of monitoring of the business relationship in which such transactions occur, to establish whether the transactions or the relationship look suspicious. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They must also increase the degree and nature of monitoring of the business relationship in which such transactions are made"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 4.65: for some situations assessed as high risk or outside the firm's risk appetite, the firm may wish not to take on the customer or to exit the relationship, for example in relation to particular customer types, customers from or transactions to or through particular high-risk countries or areas, or combinations of other risk factors. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm may wish not to take on the customer, or may wish to exit from the relationship"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 4.66: although jurisdictions may be subject to economic sanctions, in some situations for humanitarian or other reasons a firm may, under licence, take on or continue with the customer, business or transaction in, to or through such high-risk jurisdictions. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for humanitarian or other reasons a firm may, under licence, take on or continue with the customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.67: the firm must decide, on the basis of its assessment of risks posed by different customer/product combinations, the level of verification to apply at each level of risk. Consideration should be given to all information gathered about a customer in normal business and vetting processes, and the overall information held may alter the customer's risk profile. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must decide, on the basis of its assessment of the risks posed by different customer/product combinations"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Paragraph 4.68: identifying a customer as higher risk does not automatically mean they are a money launderer or terrorist financier, and identifying a customer as low risk does not mean they are not. Staff need to be vigilant, using experience and common sense in applying the firm's risk-based criteria and rules (see Chapter 7). (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not automatically mean that he is a money launderer, or a financier of terrorism"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 4.69: when the FCA publishes a relevant thematic review report or updates its Financial Crime Guide as part of its ongoing review of its controls, a firm should consider how its own systems, controls and procedures measure up against the report's self-assessment questions. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm should consider how its systems, controls and procedures appear in relation to the self- assessment questions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.69: firms should be aware of the FCA's published enforcement findings on individual firms and its actions in response, available on the FCA website. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should be aware of the FCA’s published enforcement findings in relation to individual firms"</sub>

## A risk-based approach – Monitor and improve the effective operation of the firm’s controls

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.71: testing of, and reporting on, the development and effective operation of the firm's RBA should be done independently, for example by an internal audit function (where one exists), external auditors, specialist consultants or other qualified parties who play no part in implementing or running the firm's AML/CTF compliance programme. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Independent testing of, and reporting on, the development and effective operation of the firm’s RBA should be conducted"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.72 (SYSC 6.3.3 R): the firm will need some means of assessing whether its risk mitigation procedures and controls are working effectively and, if not, where they need improving. Its policies, controls and procedures will need to be kept under regular review. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm will need to have some means of assessing that its risk mitigation procedures and controls are working effectively"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.72: aspects the firm will need to consider include appropriate procedures to identify changes in customer characteristics that emerge in normal business, and reviewing how different products and services may be used for ML/TF and how those ways may change, supported by typologies and law enforcement feedback. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate procedures to identify changes in customer characteristics, which come to light in the normal course of business"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.72, continued: the firm will also need to consider the adequacy of staff training and awareness; compliance monitoring arrangements such as internal audit, quality assurance or external review; where appropriate, setting up an internal audit function; the balance between technology-based and people-based systems; capture of appropriate management information; upward reporting and accountability; and the effectiveness of liaison with other parts of the firm and with regulatory and law enforcement agencies. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the balance between technology-based and people-based systems"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.70 (Regulation 19(2)(b), SYSC 6.3.8 R): the policies, controls and procedures should be approved by senior management, and measures to manage and mitigate risks (whether higher or lower) should be consistent with national requirements and guidance from competent authorities. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The policies, controls and procedures should be approved by senior management"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 4.73: when the FCA publishes a relevant thematic review report or updates its Financial Crime Guide as part of its monitoring of its ML/TF/PF controls, a firm should consider whether any of the examples of poor practice resonate with the firm. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm should consider whether any of the examples of poor practice have any resonance within the firm"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.73: firms should be aware of the FCA's published enforcement findings on individual firms and its actions in response, available on the FCA website. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should be aware of the FCA’s published enforcement findings in relation to individual firms"</sub>

## A risk-based approach – Record appropriately what has been done and why

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.74 (SYSC 6.3.3 R, Regulation 18(4)): firms must record their risk assessments so they can show the basis for them, keep the assessments up to date, and have appropriate mechanisms for giving appropriate risk assessment information to competent authorities. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must document their risk assessments in order to be able to demonstrate their basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.75: Annex 4-III contains illustrative examples of systems and controls a firm might have in place to keep its risk assessments up to date. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Annex 4-III contains illustrative examples of systems and controls a firm might have in place"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Paragraph 4.76: documentation of the firm's responses should enable it to demonstrate to its regulator and/or a court how it assesses threats/risks of being used for money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing; how it agrees and implements appropriate systems and procedures including due diligence requirements in light of its risk assessment; how it monitors and, as necessary, improves their effectiveness; and the arrangements for reporting to senior management on the operation of its control processes. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Documentation of those responses should enable the firm to demonstrate to its regulator and/or to a court"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.77: on a case-by-case basis, firms should document the rationale for any additional due diligence measures undertaken (or any waived) compared to the standard approach, in view of the risk assessment of a particular customer. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should document the rationale for any additional due diligence measures it has undertaken"</sub>

## Risk management is dynamic

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 4.78 (SYSC 6.3.3 R): risk management is a continuous, dynamic process and an ML/TF/PF risk assessment is not a one-time exercise. Firms must ensure their risk management processes for ML, TF and PF risks are kept under regular review. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must therefore ensure that their risk management processes for managing money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risks are kept under regular review"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.79: there is a need to monitor the firm's operating environment. Success in preventing ML/TF/PF in one area tends to drive criminals to migrate to another area, business or product stream, so periodic assessment should be made of activity in the firm's market place. If evidence suggests displacement, or customer behaviour is changing, the firm should be considering what it should do differently. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Success in preventing ML/TF/PF in one area of operation or business will tend to drive criminals to migrate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.80: in a stable business change may occur slowly; customers' activities change (without always notifying the firm) and the firm's products, services and distribution change. Products/transactions attacked by prospective launderers, terrorist financiers and proliferation financiers vary as perceptions of relative vulnerability change. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Customers’ activities change (without always notifying the firm)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.82: a firm should keep its risk assessment(s) up to date. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm should therefore keep its risk assessment(s) up to date"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.82: a formal reassessment every year might be too frequent in most cases, though it would still suit a dynamic, growing business. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An annual, formal reassessment might be too often in most cases"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 4.82: it is recommended that a firm re-examine its assessment at least once a year, even where it concludes no revision is warranted. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is recommended that a firm revisit its assessment at least annually"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 4.82: firms should put details of the assessment, and any changes resulting from it, in the MLRO's annual report (see paragraphs 3.37 to 3.45). (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should include details of the assessment, and any resulting changes, in the MLRO’s annual report"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4.81: there is a balance to be achieved between responding promptly to environmental changes and maintaining stable systems and procedures. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is, however, a balance to be achieved between responding promptly to environmental changes"</sub>
