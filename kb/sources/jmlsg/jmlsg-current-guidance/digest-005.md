## Monitoring effectiveness of money laundering controls

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(1), where appropriate with regard to the size and nature of its business, a firm must establish an independent internal audit function responsible for examining and evaluating the adequacy and effectiveness of its ML Regulations policies, controls and procedures, making recommendations on them, and monitoring the firm's compliance with those recommendations. (p. 36)[^original]

- **descriptive / context**: Effectiveness of systems and controls is driven by a combination of features, including: policies and procedures reflecting current legal and regulatory developments; appropriate monitoring processes with timely follow-up of findings; adequacy of resources; appropriate monitoring of outsourced compliance arrangements; adequately trained, up-to-date staff; quality control/internal review processes; appropriate management information for senior management and those with supervisory responsibilities; and the work of any internal audit function. (p. 36)[^original]

## RISK-BASED APPROACH

- **mandatory / requirement**: The Chapter 4 summary lists core obligations: identify and assess the ML/TF risks of the business; appropriate systems and controls must reflect the degree of risk associated with the business and its customers; determine CDD measures on a risk-sensitive basis depending on type of customer, business relationship, product or transaction; and take into account situations and products that can by nature present higher risk, specifically correspondent relationships and business relationships and occasional transactions with PEPs. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Appropriate systems and controls must reflect the degree of risk associated with the business and its customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The Chapter 4 summary lists actions to keep under regular review: a formal and regular ML/TF/proliferation financing risk assessment including market changes and changes in products, customers and the wider environment; internal policies, controls and procedures (including staff awareness) reflecting the assessment; customer identification and acceptance procedures reflecting customer risk characteristics; and robust monitoring arrangements reflecting those characteristics. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Carry out a formal, and regular, money laundering/terrorist financing/proliferation financing risk assessment"</sub>

## General

- **descriptive / method**: Paragraph 4.1 sets out discrete steps for managing ML, TF and proliferation financing risks cost-effectively and proportionately: identify the relevant risks; assess the risks presented by the firm's customers and underlying beneficial owners, products or services, transactions, delivery channels and geographical areas of operation; design and implement controls to manage and mitigate the assessed risks in the context of the firm's risk appetite; monitor and improve the controls; and record appropriately what has been done and why. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "design and implement controls to manage and mitigate these"</sub>

## Risk Assessment

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulations 18 and 18A, the ML Regulations require firms to take appropriate steps to identify and assess the ML, TF and proliferation financing risks to which the business is subject, taking into account information made available by the FCA and risk factors relating to customers, countries or geographic areas of operation, products, services, transactions and delivery channels, and taking into account the size and nature of the business. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must take into account the size and nature of its business"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Firms that do not offer complex products or services and have limited or no international exposure may not need an overly complex or sophisticated business risk assessment. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may not need an overly complex or sophisticated business risk assessment"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The risk assessments carried out must be documented, kept up to date and made available to the FCA on request. The FCA may decide a documented risk assessment is not required for a particular firm where the specific risks inherent in its sector are clear and understood. (p. 40)[^original]

(p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A stand-alone PF risk assessment is not required."</sub>

## Obligation to adopt a risk-based approach

- **descriptive / context**: Senior management of most firms manage affairs with regard to risks inherent in the business environment and jurisdictions, the risks inherent in the business, and the effectiveness of controls put in place to manage them. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "manage the firm’s affairs with regard to the risks inherent in the business environment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A risk-based approach recognises that the ML/TF/PF threat varies across customers, jurisdictions, products and delivery channels; allows management to differentiate between customers in a way matching the risk in their business; allows senior management to apply its own approach to procedures, systems and controls; and helps produce a more cost-effective system. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allows management to differentiate between their"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A firm uses its assessment of the risks inherent in its business to inform its risk-based approach to identifying and verifying individual customers, which in turn drives the level and extent of due diligence appropriate to that customer. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which will in turn drive the level and extent of due diligence appropriate to that customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: No system of checks will detect and prevent all ML, TF and proliferation financing. A risk-based approach balances the cost burden on firms and customers with a realistic assessment of the threat, focusing effort where it is needed and will have most impact. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No system of checks will detect and prevent all money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing."</sub>

## Risk assessment - identification and assessment of business risks

- **descriptive / context**: The firm's risk environment includes the wider context: risks posed by jurisdictions where it and its customers operate, the relative attractiveness of its products, and the nature of transactions. Risk arises not only from whether CDD was carried out or who the customer or beneficial owner is, but also from the activities the customer undertakes, in the normal course of business or through products used and transactions undertaken. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but also in relation to the activities undertaken by the customer"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Where a firm's product and customer base is relatively simple, with few products and most customers in similar categories, a simple approach building on product risk may be appropriate, focusing on customers who fall outside the 'norm'. Firms with a greater volume of predominantly retail customers served through delivery channels allowing a standardised approach may likewise take a straightforward approach for most customers, building on product risk. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a simple approach, building on the risk the firm’s products are assessed to present, may be appropriate for most customers"</sub>

## Risk assessment - identification and assessment of business risks

- **descriptive / context**: For firms operating internationally or with customers based or operating abroad, there are additional risk considerations relating to the jurisdictions involved, their reputation and standing as regards inherent ML/TF/PF risk, and the effectiveness of their AML/CTF enforcement regime.   <sub>anchor: "their reputation and standing as regards the inherent ML/TF/PF risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The ML Regulations state that a high-risk third country is a country named on either FATF list: High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action, or Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "(i) High- Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action; (ii) Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring"</sub>

- **expected / expectation**: In identifying its money laundering risk an FCA-regulated firm should consider a range of factors, including its customer, product and activity profiles; distribution channels; complexity and volume of transactions; processes and systems; and operating environment (SYSC 6.3.6 G). ## New technologies

- **mandatory / requirement**: In identifying and assessing ML/TF/PF risks, firms must take account of whether new products and new business practices (including new delivery mechanisms) are involved, and the use of new or developing technologies for new and pre-existing products. Such a risk assessment should take place prior to launch, and appropriate measures should be taken to manage and mitigate those risks, including where relevant enhanced due diligence. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must take account of whether new products and new business practices are involved"</sub>

## A risk-based approach - Design and implement controls to manage and mitigate the risks

- **mandatory / requirement**: Once the firm has identified and assessed its ML, TF and proliferation financing risks, senior management must establish and maintain policies, controls and procedures to mitigate and manage effectively the risks identified in its risk assessment, taking account of the size and nature of the business (Regulations 19(1) and 19A(1)). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "senior management must establish and maintain policies, controls and procedures to mitigate and manage effectively"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Policies, controls and procedures designed to mitigate assessed ML/TF/PF risks should be appropriate and proportionate to those risks and designed to provide an effective level of mitigation. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be appropriate and proportionate to these risks, and should be designed to provide an effective level of mitigation"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulations 19(2)(b) and 19A(2)(b), firms must obtain approval from senior management for the policies, controls and procedures they put in place and, where appropriate, for monitoring and enhancing the measures taken. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must obtain approval from their senior management for the policies, controls and procedures"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A risk-based approach requires the full commitment and support of senior management and active co-operation of business units. It needs to be part of the firm's philosophy and reflected in its procedures and controls, with clear communication of policies across the firm and robust mechanisms to ensure they are carried out effectively, weaknesses are identified and improvements made. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires the full commitment and support of senior management, and the active co-operation of business units"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The nature and extent of AML/CTF/PF controls depend on factors including the nature, scale and complexity of the business; diversity of operations including geographical diversity; customer, product and activity profile; distribution channels; volume and size of transactions; the extent of dealing directly versus through intermediaries, third parties, correspondents or non face to face access; and the degree of outsourcing of procedures to other (Group) entities. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The nature and extent of AML/CTF/PF controls will depend on a number of factors"</sub>

## A risk-based approach - Design and implement controls to manage and mitigate the risks

- **descriptive / context**: How a risk-based approach is implemented depends on the firm's operational structure, for example multiple business units versus a single business, branches or subsidiaries, face-to-face versus online business, a high staff/customer ratio and changing customer base versus a small group of relationship managers and a stable base, and an international (especially high net worth) versus largely domestic customer base.   <sub>anchor: "How a risk-based approach is implemented will depend on the firm’s operational structure."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Senior management should decide the appropriate approach in light of the firm's structure. The firm may start at business area or business stream level, and may start with customer assessments overlaid with product and delivery channel risks, or start with product risk overlaid with customer and delivery channel risks, taking account of geographical considerations. (p. 45)[^original]

## General

- **descriptive / context**: Based on the risk assessment, a firm will determine the level of CDD for each customer and beneficial owner; it is likely there will be a standard level of CDD applying to the generality of customers, based on the firm's risk appetite (Regulation 28(12)). ## Customer risk assessments

  <sub>anchor: "a firm must consider its customer risks in the context of the wider ML/TF/PF environment"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A firm may also have to adjust its risk assessment of a particular customer based on information received from a competent authority. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adjust its risk assessment of a particular customer based on information received from a competent authority"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some firms, often (but not exclusively) those dealing in wholesale markets, offer a more 'bespoke' service to customers, many already subject to extensive due diligence by lawyers and accountants for reasons other than AML/CTF/PF. Identifying the customer is then more complex but takes account of the considerable additional information that already exists. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many of whom are already subject to extensive due diligence by lawyers and accountants"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Some business lines carry an inherently higher risk of being used for ML/TF/PF purposes than others"</sub>
