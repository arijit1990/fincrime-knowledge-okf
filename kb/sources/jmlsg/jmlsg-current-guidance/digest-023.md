## APPENDIX I ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING RESPONSIBILITIES IN THE UK

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I describes the Joint Money Laundering Steering Group as an industry body made up of 14 financial sector trade bodies which produces guidance on compliance with legal and regulatory requirements and good practice. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Industry body made up of 14 financial sector trade bodies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I lists the Home Office's responsibilities as UK primary legislation (POCA 2002, Terrorism Act 2000 and Anti-terrorism, Crime and Security Act 2001), police strategy and resourcing, and asset recovery strategy; it jointly with HM Treasury chairs the Money Laundering Advisory Committee (MLAC), a forum for key stakeholders to coordinate the AML regime and review its efficiency and effectiveness. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Chairs (jointly with HM Treasury) Money Laundering Advisory Committee (MLAC)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I lists the National Crime Agency's responsibilities: as the UK's financial intelligence unit it receives SARs (about money laundering and terrorist financing) and sends cleared intelligence to law enforcement agencies; it assesses organised crime threats, exercises POCA powers to recover proceeds of crime through criminal, civil or tax recovery processes, supports law enforcement agencies and trains financial investigators. (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "receives suspicious activity reports (about money laundering and terrorist financing)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I lists the Financial Conduct Authority as the UK's financial regulator whose statutory objectives under FSMA 2000 include reduction of financial crime; it approves persons to perform controlled functions (including the money laundering reporting officer function), makes, supervises and enforces rules on money laundering among other things, and has power to prosecute firms under the Money Laundering Regulations (except in Scotland). (p. 208)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Power to prosecute firms under the Money Laundering Regulations (except in Scotland)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I lists other regulators as HM Revenue and Customs, the Gambling Commission and 22 professional body supervisors listed in Schedule 1 of the Money Laundering Regulations, and notes the police (43 forces in the UK) investigate crime, including money laundering and terrorism. (p. 209)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "22 professional body supervisors, listed in Schedule 1 of the Money Laundering Regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I lists HM Treasury's responsibilities as representing the UK in the EU and FATF, implementing EU Directives principally through the Money Laundering Regulations, approving industry guidance under POCA, the Terrorism Act and the Money Laundering Regulations, and implementing and administering the UK financial sanctions regime through the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation. (p. 209)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Approves industry guidance under POCA, Terrorism Act and Money Laundering Regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG Appendix I lists HM Revenue and Customs as investigating money laundering, drug trafficking and certain tax offences and licensing money service businesses and dealers in high value goods; the Revenue and Customs Prosecutions Office prosecutes those offences investigated by HMRC.   <sub>anchor: "Prosecutes crime, money laundering and terrorism offences in Scotland"</sub>

## SUMMARY OF UK LEGISLATION

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG's summary of UK legislation states that POCA 2002 (as amended) consolidates and extends existing UK money laundering legislation, covers all crimes and any dealing in criminal property, with no exceptions and no de minimis. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with no exceptions and no de minimis"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states POCA empowers the NCA to conduct an investigation (s 341(2)) to discover whether a person holds criminal assets and to recover the assets in question. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "empowers the NCA, to conduct an investigation31 to discover whether a person holds criminal assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states POCA creates five investigative powers for law enforcement agencies: a production order (s 345), a search and seizure warrant (s 352), a disclosure order (s 357), a customer information order (s 363) and an account monitoring order (s 370). (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "creates five investigative powers for the law enforcement agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states POCA (ss 330 and 331) creates a criminal offence for persons working in the regulated sector of failing to report, as soon as reasonably practicable after the information came to their attention in the course of regulated business, where they have knowledge or suspicion, or reasonable grounds for it, that another person is laundering the proceeds of any criminal conduct. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for persons working in the regulated sector of failing to make a"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states POCA (s 333A) creates a criminal offence for anyone to take action likely to prejudice an investigation by informing the person who is the subject of a suspicion report, or anybody else, that a disclosure has been made to a nominated officer or the NCA, or that the police or customs authorities are carrying out or intending to carry out a money laundering investigation. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for anyone to take any action likely to prejudice an investigation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG legislative summary says POCA (ss327-329) makes it a criminal offence to acquire, use, possess, conceal, disguise, convert or transfer criminal property, or to take it out of the jurisdiction, and also to enter into or become concerned in an arrangement that helps another person acquire, retain, use or control criminal property. (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to acquire, use, possess, conceal, disguise, convert, transfer or"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The summary adds in a note that POCA's reporting duty carries no materiality or de minimis threshold, although deposit-taking firms may, in certain circumstances, carry out a transaction under £250 without consent (see paragraph 6.73). (p. 210)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There are no provisions governing materiality or de minimis thresholds for having to report under POCA"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states POCA creates an offence (s 341(2)(b)) of destroying or disposing of documents relevant to an investigation, and an offence by a firm (s 366) of failing to comply with a customer information order requirement, or of knowingly or recklessly making a false or misleading statement in material particular in purported compliance. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "of destroying or disposing of documents which are relevant to an"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states the maximum penalty for the offence of money laundering under POCA is 14 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "of 14 years’ imprisonment and/or an unlimited"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary notes that an offence is not committed if a person reports the property involved to the NCA or under approved internal arrangements, either before the prohibited act is carried out or as soon afterwards as is reasonably practicable. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An offence is not committed if a person reports the property involved to the National Crime Agency (NCA)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states POCA maximum penalties of five years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine for failing to make a report of suspected money laundering, two years and/or an unlimited fine for tipping off, and five years and/or an unlimited fine for destroying or disposing of relevant documents. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for “tipping off” of two years’ imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states the Terrorism Act 2000 establishes offences related to involvement in arrangements for facilitating, raising or using funds for terrorism purposes, and makes it a criminal offence for any person not to report the existence of terrorist property where there are reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting it. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "makes it a criminal offence for any person not to report the existence of terrorist property"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states the Terrorism Act (s 39) makes it a criminal offence to take action likely to prejudice an investigation by tipping off the subject of a suspicion report, or anybody else, that a disclosure has been made to a nominated officer or the NCA or that police or customs are carrying out or intending a terrorist financing investigation, and (s 38A, Schedule 6A) grants law enforcement a power to make an account monitoring order similar to POCA's. (p. 211)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "grants45 a power to the law enforcement agencies to make an account monitoring order"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG summary states the Terrorism Act maximum penalty for failure to report is five years' imprisonment and/or a fine, and for the offence of actual money laundering is 14 years' imprisonment and/or a fine. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the maximum penalty for the offence of actual money laundering is 14 years’"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 3 states that the definition of terrorist property includes resources of a proscribed organisation, and that the primary source of information on proscribed organisations, including up-to-date aliases, is the Home Office. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The primary source of information on proscribed organisations, including up-to-date information on aliases, is the Home Office."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 4 states the Anti-terrorism, Crime and Security Act 2001 gives the authorities power to seize terrorist cash, freeze terrorist assets and direct firms in the regulated sector to provide specified information on customers and their terrorism-related activities. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "gives the authorities power to seize terrorist cash, to freeze terrorist assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 4 states that under the Anti-Terrorism, Crime and Security Act 2001 HM Treasury may issue a freezing order for individuals, entities or organisations outside the UK where there is reasonable belief they have taken or are likely to take action to the detriment of the UK economy or threatening the life or property of one or more UK nationals or residents. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "HM Treasury may issue a freezing order in respect of individuals, entities or organisations outside of the UK"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 5 states that Schedule 7 to the Counter-terrorism Act 2008 gives HM Treasury power to issue directions to financial sector firms, with requirements relating to customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring, systematic reporting, and limiting or ceasing business. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Schedule 7 to the CTA gives power to HM Treasury to issue directions to firms in the financial sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 6 states the CDD and ongoing monitoring requirements build on the similar obligation under the ML Regulations, while the requirements for systematic reporting and limiting or ceasing business are new. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The requirements for systematic reporting and limiting or ceasing business are new."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 7 states the Treasury may give a Schedule 7 direction in relation to a non-EEA country if the FATF has advised that measures be taken because of the risk of terrorist financing or money laundering activities in the country, by its government, or by persons resident or incorporated there. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that the Financial Action Task Force has advised that measures should be taken in relation to the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 7 states the Treasury may also give a direction if it reasonably believes there is a risk of terrorist financing or money laundering activities being carried on in the non-EEA country, by its government or by persons resident or incorporated there, posing a significant risk to UK national interests; or that development or production of nuclear, radiological, biological or chemical weapons in the country, or anything facilitating it, poses a significant risk to UK national interests. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "poses a significant risk to the national interests of the UK."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 9 of the summary's financial sanctions section says firms need an appropriate way of monitoring payment instructions so that no payments reach sanctions targets or their agents, and that in the regulated sector this applies to all firms rather than banks only. (p. 212)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms therefore need to have an appropriate means of monitoring payment instructions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 8 states HM Treasury maintains a Consolidated List of targets listed by the UN, EU and UK under current financial sanctions regimes, including all individuals and entities subject to financial sanctions in the UK. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "HM Treasury maintains a Consolidated List of targets listed by the United Nations, European Union and United Kingdom"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 9 states it is a criminal offence to make payments, or allow payments to be made, to targets on the HM Treasury list, including dealing directly or through intermediaries such as lawyers or accountants. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is a criminal offence to make payments, or to allow payments to be made, to targets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 10 states that guidance on compliance with the financial sanctions regime is set out in paragraphs 5.3.54 to 5.3.61. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Guidance on compliance with the financial sanctions regime is set out in paragraphs 5.3.54"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 11 states the ML Regulations specify arrangements which must be in place within firms within their scope in order to prevent operations relating to money laundering or terrorist financing. (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ML Regulations specify arrangements which must be in place within firms within the scope of the Regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 12 states the ML Regulations apply to the regulated activities of all financial sector firms (banks, building societies and other credit institutions; individuals and firms engaging in regulated investment activities under FSMA; issuers of electronic money; insurance companies undertaking long-term life business including Lloyd's life business), and to money service businesses (bureaux de change, cheque encashment centres, money transmission services). (p. 213)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks, building societies and other credit institutions;"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 12 states the ML Regulations also apply to trust and company service providers, casinos, dealers in high-value goods (including auctioneers) accepting cash payment of €10,000 or more (single or linked transactions), estate agents and letting agents, legal and accountancy services providers when undertaking relevant business, art market participants, cryptoasset exchange providers and custodian wallet providers. (p. 214)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who accept payment in cash of €10,000 or more (either single or linked transactions)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 13 states the ML Regulations require firms to appoint a nominated officer to receive internal reports relating to knowledge or suspicion of money laundering. (p. 214)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ML Regulations require firms to appoint a nominated officer to receive internal reports"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 15 states the FCA may institute proceedings (other than in Scotland) for offences under prescribed money laundering regulations (FSMA s 402(1)(b)), and this power is not limited to firms or persons regulated by the FCA. (p. 214)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This power is not limited to firms or persons regulated by the FCA."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 15 states that whether a breach of the ML Regulations has occurred does not depend on whether money laundering has taken place, so firms may be sanctioned for not having adequate AML/CTF systems, and that where failure to comply with a requirement constitutes an offence the punishment is a maximum of two years' imprisonment, or a fine, or both. (p. 214)[^original]

## FCA-regulated firms – the FCA Handbook

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 16 states FSMA makes the prevention of financial crime integral to the discharge of the FCA's functions, so the FCA is concerned that regulated firms and their senior management are aware of the risk of their businesses being used for financial crime and take appropriate measures to prevent it, facilitate its detection and monitor its incidence. (p. 214)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FSMA makes the prevention of financial crime integral to the discharge of the FCA’s functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 17 states firms may only engage in a regulated activity in the UK if they are a regulated or exempt person, which can result from being given a Part 4A permission by the FCA or qualifying for authorisation under FSMA itself, for example an EEA firm establishing a UK branch or providing cross-border services under FSMA Schedule 3. (p. 214)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms may only engage in a regulated activity49 in the UK if it is a regulated or exempt person."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 17 states that such EEA firms are generally regulated by their home state regulator but are regulated by the FCA in connection with the regulated activities carried on in the UK. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are regulated by the FCA in connection with the regulated activities carried on in the UK"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 18 states that a firm may only carry on regulated business in accordance with its permission; a Part 4A firm may apply to vary its permission, and before giving or varying a permission the FCA must ensure the firm will satisfy and continue to satisfy the threshold conditions for all the regulated activities, and may vary or cancel permission if a firm is failing or likely to fail them. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm may only carry on regulated business in accordance with its permission."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 19 (COND 2.5.7G) states the FCA will consider whether a firm is ready, willing and organised to comply on a continuing basis with the requirements and standards of the regulatory system, and in particular whether it has in place systems and controls against money laundering of the sort described in SYSC 6.1.1 R to SYSC 6.3.10 G. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has in place systems and controls against money laundering of the sort described in SYSC 6.1.1 R"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: JMLSG summary paragraph 20 states that SYSC requires FCA-regulated firms (subject to specified exceptions, see paragraph 1.35) to have effective systems and controls for countering the risk that a firm might be used to further financial crime, with specific provisions on money laundering risks, and to ensure that approved persons exercise appropriate responsibilities in relation to these AML systems and controls. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SYSC requires FCA-regulated firms (subject to some specified exceptions: see paragraph 1.35 above)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 20 states that APER Principle 5 requires an approved person to take reasonable steps to ensure the business of the firm for which he is responsible is organised so that it is controlled effectively, and PRIN Principle 3 requires a firm to take reasonable care to organise and control its affairs responsibly and effectively, with adequate risk management systems. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "APER - Principle 5 requires an approved person to take reasonable steps to ensure"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG summary paragraph 20 states that COND specifically requires a firm to have systems and controls against money laundering of the sort in SYSC 6.1.1 R to SYSC 6.3.10 G in assessing fitness and properness, and that SYSC chapters 2, 3 and 6 set out requirements on senior management responsibilities and systems and controls, including the risk the firm may be used to further financial crime. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SYSC - Chapters 2, 3 and 6 set out particular requirements relating to senior management responsibilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 19 of the summary explains that Threshold Condition 5 (Suitability) obliges a firm to show the FCA it is fit and proper to hold Part 4A permission, taking account of all circumstances such as its links with other persons, the range and nature of its regulated activities, and the overall need to be satisfied that its affairs are and will stay soundly and prudently run. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Threshold condition 5 (Suitability) requires the firm to satisfy the FCA that it is"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 20 of the summary says that under DEPP, when the FCA weighs disciplinary action for breach of the money laundering rules in SYSC 3.2 or SYSC 6.3, it looks at whether the firm followed the relevant parts of the JMLSG guidance for the financial sector. (p. 215)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCA will have regard to whether a firm has followed relevant provisions in the JMLSG guidance"</sub>
