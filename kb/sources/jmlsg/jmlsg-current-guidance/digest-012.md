## C – MITIGATION OF IMPERSONATION RISK

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.122: where a firm has concluded it should treat a customer as financially excluded for customer identification, and the customer is identified by means other than standard evidence, the reasons for doing so should be documented. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the reasons for doing so should be documented"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.123: firms may wish to consider whether enhanced due diligence (section 5.5) or monitoring (section 5.7) of the size and expected volume of transactions would be useful for some financially excluded categories, based on the firm's own experience of their operation. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms may wish to consider whether enhanced due diligence"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.124: where available identity evidence is limited, the firm judges the individual cannot reasonably be expected to provide more, but the relationship should go ahead, it should consider instituting enhanced monitoring over the customer's transactions and activity (section 5.7), and should consider whether restrictions should be placed on the customer's ability to migrate to other, higher risk products or services. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should consider instituting enhanced monitoring arrangements over the customer’s transactions and activity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.125: where an applicant produces non-standard or incomplete documentation, staff should not cite the ML Regulations (or other AML/CTF regulation) as an excuse for not opening an account without proper consideration of the available evidence, referring up the line for advice as necessary. A considered judgment that the evidence does not give sufficient confidence the applicant is who they claim to be may fully justify a decision not to open the account. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "staff should not cite the ML Regulations (or other regulation relating to the prevention of money laundering and/or terrorist financing) as an excuse"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.121 refers to Part II, Sector 1 (Retail banking), paragraphs 1.38-1.41, for more guidance on verifying the identity of financially excluded persons. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further guidance on verifying the identity of financially excluded persons is given in Part II, Sector 1: Retail banking"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.121: firms will need to apply a proportionate, risk-based approach when deciding whether the evidence available gives reasonable confidence in a customer's identity. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A proportionate and risk-based approach will be needed to determine whether the evidence available gives reasonable confidence"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.123: the 'financially excluded' do not form one group with uniform risk, so firms should consider the risk in each particular case. Some may pose higher money laundering risk whether their identity tokens are standard or non-standard, for example a passport holder who qualifies only for a basic account on credit grounds. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not a homogeneous category of uniform risk, and firms should consider the risk presented in any particular case"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.125: firms should keep in mind that the ML Regulations do not spell out what is or is not acceptable evidence of identity. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should bear in mind that the ML Regulations are not explicit as to what is and is not acceptable"</sub>

## Customers other than private individuals

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.126: a relationship or transaction with a customer that is not a private individual may be set up in the customer's own name or in the names of specific individuals or other entities acting for it. Beneficial ownership can nonetheless sit with others, either because the legal owner acts for the beneficial owner or because the law requires ownership to be registered in a particular way. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Beneficial ownership may, however, rest with others"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.127 (Regulation 28(4)): in deciding who the beneficial owner is for a customer that is not a private individual, the firm's objective must be to know who has ownership or control over the funds forming or relating to the relationship, and/or forms the controlling mind and/or management of any legal entity involved in the funds. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm’s objective must be to know who has ownership or control over the funds which form or otherwise relate to the relationship"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.128: firms have obligations under the UK financial sanctions regime requiring collection of information on trustees, directors or equivalent (Part III paragraphs 4.83-4.85), so in determining the information to collect firms should take account of their information needs for sanctions compliance. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should take account of their information needs in relation to sanctions compliance"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.129: certain other information about the entity should be obtained as a standard requirement. Thereafter, on the basis of the ML/TF risk assessed for the customer/product/delivery channel combination, a firm should decide the extent to which the entity's identity should be verified, and what additional information on the entity and potentially some of the individuals behind it should be obtained (see section 5.5). (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm should decide the extent to which the identity of the entity should be verified"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.129A (Regulation 30A(1),(1A),(2),(2A)): firms must obtain an excerpt of the register of the company, unregistered company, limited liability partnership, eligible Scottish partnership, in-scope trust or registrable overseas entity before establishing a business relationship. It may be obtained from the customer, Companies House or a third-party provider. For in-scope trusts an excerpt from the Trust Registration Service (TRS) must be obtained via the agent or trustee, and for registrable overseas entities an excerpt of the Register of Overseas Entities (ROE) must be obtained. See also 5.3.129C. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must obtain an excerpt of the register of the company, unregistered company, the limited liability partnership"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.129A: firms must also obtain such an excerpt while carrying out CDD and ongoing monitoring of an established relationship. Firms should apply a risk-based approach to obtaining up-to-date excerpts, for example when they become aware that the beneficial ownership information held is outdated or has changed, or that an overseas entity has become registrable (which could occur when the nature and purpose of the relationship changes and the firm starts providing products/services facilitating the purchase, sale or transfer of UK land or property). (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should apply a risk-based approach to obtaining up-to-date excerpts"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 28(3A): where the customer is a legal person, company, trust, foundation or similar legal arrangement, reasonable steps have to be taken to understand how the entity is owned and controlled. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reasonable measures must be taken to understand the ownership and control structure of the entity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.127: verification of the beneficial owner(s) of a customer that is not a private individual is done on a risk-based approach following paragraphs 5.3.8 to 5.3.16. It takes account of how many individuals are involved, the nature and spread of their interests in the entity, and the nature and extent of any business, contractual or family relationship among them. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nature and distribution of their interests in the entity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.129B (Regulation 30(2B), 3, Schedule 3AZA): if the firm finds a material discrepancy between beneficial ownership information of the company collected from the register and information available to it while conducting CDD (including onboarding and ongoing monitoring), the material discrepancy must be reported to Companies House, or to HMRC if it relates to a trust. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the material discrepancy must be reported to Companies House, or to HMRC if it relates to a trust"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: beneficial ownership in this context (excluding trusts and overseas entities) means a person of significant control (PSC) per the PSC register, not as defined in the ML Regulations, so PSC register information may differ from other beneficial ownership information without being inaccurate. Discrepancies resulting solely from differences between the PSC and 'beneficial owner' definitions are not material and not reportable. For trusts it means trustees, settlors, beneficiaries, protectors or other individuals exercising control (see 5.3.261); for overseas entities it means registrable beneficial owners, not PSCs. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Discrepancies that result solely from differences between the definition of PSCA and 'beneficial owner' in the ML Regulations are not material"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: identifying a discrepancy does not in itself trigger the reporting obligation, as discrepancies must be material to be reportable. For example, a material discrepancy arises when there is a missing or different person (legal or natural) recorded, comparing PSC register, ROE or TRS information with beneficial ownership information obtained during CDD. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a material discrepancy would arise when there is a missing or different person (legal or natural) recorded"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: a discrepancy does not necessarily prohibit onboarding or require exit from the relationship, but firms should assess its nature and relevance within their CDD process and risk-based approach in considering whether there are reasonable grounds for suspicion of ML/TF. A discrepancy report is not a substitute for a suspicious activity report (SAR), and the requirement to submit a SAR where appropriate remains. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A discrepancy report is not a substitute for a suspicious activity report (SAR)"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: material discrepancies should be considered with an overarching requirement of proportionality and reasonableness, taking into account the ML/TF risk the firm has determined the customer poses and the reasonable justification provided for the discrepancy. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Material discrepancies should be considered with an overarching requirement of proportionality and reasonableness"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: firms should consider whether a material discrepancy in beneficial ownership information points to a link with ML/TF or to concealment of details of the customer's business, either of which could be a potential indicator of suspicion of ML/TF. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should consider whether the material discrepancy is indicative of being linked to ML/TF"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: in assessing a material discrepancy for possible concealment, firms do not have to decide whether the concealment is deliberate. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms need not consider whether any such concealment is deliberate or not"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: discrepancies caused by administrative or clerical errors, or by timing factors that delay register updates, are unlikely to amount to concealment and so are unlikely to need reporting. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Discrepancies arising from administrative or clerical errors, or due to chronological factors"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.129B: the material discrepancy report should be made as soon as reasonably possible when discovered. Further guidance (including circumstances where Companies House considers firms need not report, and what HMRC considers a material discrepancy for in-scope trusts) is available via the referenced GOV.UK pages. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The material discrepancy report should be made as soon as reasonable possible when discovered"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.129C: excerpts should only be those of the legal entity or trust that is the firm's customer, not other undertakings, entities or trusts within the customer's ownership and control structure, unless the firm has a direct business relationship with them. Excerpts need only be obtained if the customer is registered or registrable, which may be determined through the ordinary course of business and existing CDD measures. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Excerpts should only be those of the legal entity or trust that is the firm's customer"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.129C: there are exemptions to registration (for TRS and ROE) and firms can apply a risk-based approach to this determination; depending on risk, this could include obtaining attestation from the customer in lower risk scenarios, or independent professional advice where ML/TF risk is assessed as higher. For trusts, firms may wish to resolve discrepancies with the customer and only report if unresolved within a reasonable time. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtaining attestation from the customer in lower risk scenarios"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.129C: if a firm determines that a customer is registrable but has not registered, that is a material discrepancy and must be reported. If a firm is reasonably satisfied an unregistered trust is not required to register, it need not make a report but should document its decision. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If a firm determines that a customer is registrable but has not registered, it is a material discrepancy and must be reported"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.129C: the firm only needs to check the ROE and obtain ROE excerpts where the nature and purpose of the relationship involves, or changes so that it involves, the firm providing products or services that facilitate buying, selling or transferring UK land or property, subject to a risk-based approach and proportionality. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ROE only needs to be checked and excerpts of the ROE obtained when the nature and purpose"</sub>
- **mandatory / context**: Paragraph 5.3.129C footnote: overseas entities have to be registered with the ROE in order to purchase, sell or transfer UK land or property, and UK and non-UK trusts that are liable for UK tax have to register with the TRS. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Overseas entities must be registered with ROE to purchase, sell or transfer UK land or property"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.130 (Regulation 27(9)(c), 33(1)(g)): where an entity is known to be linked to a PEP (the PEP being a beneficial owner) or to a jurisdiction assessed as higher ML/TF risk, it is likely to be placed in a higher risk category and enhanced due diligence measures should therefore be applied (sections 5.5 and 5.7). (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enhanced due diligence measures should therefore be applied"</sub>
- **expected / limitation**: Paragraph 5.3.131: many entities operate websites containing information about the entity; firms should bear in mind that this information, though helpful for company, directors and business material, is not independently verified before being made public. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is not independently verified before being made publicly available in this way"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.132: guidance on verifying non-personal entities covers regulated financial services firms (5.3.133-5.3.138), other firms subject to the ML Regulations (5.3.139-5.3.142), corporate customers (5.3.143-5.3.176), partnerships and unincorporated businesses (5.3.177-5.3.191), public sector bodies, governments, state-owned companies and supranationals (5.3.192-5.3.203), Sovereign Wealth Funds (5.3.204-5.3.227), pension schemes (5.3.228-5.3.237), charities, church bodies and places of worship (5.3.238-5.3.257), other trusts and foundations (5.3.258-5.3.282), and clubs and societies (5.3.283-5.3.293). (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section provides guidance on verifying the identity of a range of non- personal entities"</sub>

## Regulated financial services firms subject to the ML Regulations (or equivalent)

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.133 (Regulation 37(3)(a)): in determining whether a business relationship presents a low degree of ML/TF risk, and so the extent to which SDD is appropriate, a firm must take into account, inter alia, whether the customer is a credit institution or financial institution subject to the ML Regulations' requirements. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether the customer is a credit institution or a financial institution which is subject to the requirements in the ML Regulations"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.135 (Regulation 37(1)): if the firm determines that the situation with another regulated financial services firm presents a low degree of ML/TF risk, simplified due diligence may be applied (see section 5.4). (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "simplified due diligence may be applied (see section 5.4)"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.136: when applying SDD measures firms must continue to comply with Regulation 28 of the ML Regulations, although the extent, timing or type of measures may be adjusted to reflect the low-risk determination under 5.3.135. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must continue to comply with the requirements of Regulation 28 of the ML Regulations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.137: firms should record the steps taken to check the status of the other regulated firm; take appropriate steps to be reasonably satisfied that the person they deal with is properly authorised by the customer; and document the rationale for the decision to apply SDD. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "document the rationale for the decision to apply SDD"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.138: firms must continue to monitor business relationships and transactions to detect unusual or suspicious transactions. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must continue to monitor business relationships and transactions to detect unusual or suspicious transactions"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.134 (Regulation 37(3)): when firms decide whether a relationship is low risk, they also have to take into account whether the customer's country of residence, establishment, registration or operation is the UK or an assessed low risk jurisdiction. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is the UK or an assessed low risk jurisdiction"</sub>
