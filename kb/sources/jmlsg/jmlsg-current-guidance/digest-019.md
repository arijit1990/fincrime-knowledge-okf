## POOLED CLIENT ACCOUNTS

- **descriptive / definition**: Suspense accounts held by respondent institutions are not PCAs (refer to Part II Sector 16 on Correspondent Relationships). (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Suspense accounts held by respondent institutions are not PCAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: There are two primary vectors of risk for PCAs: the customer's clients misusing a PCA for ML/TF without the customer's knowledge; and the customer being complicit in using its PCAs for ML/TF, either willingly or under duress. (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There are two primary vectors of risk"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: PCA guidance 1.2 (Purpose): firms should take reasonable measures to establish and document the purpose of PCAs. (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should take reasonable measures to establish and document the purpose of PCAs."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Factors to consider include whether the funds are backed by government schemes with enforcement powers through a judicial body (for example UK letting/property/estate agents and property management agents); the PCA serves a limited, domestic purpose; the customer is subject to the ML Regulations or equivalent (for example UK legal and accountancy practitioners under professional body AML supervision); the customer is subject to other regulatory or professional conduct obligations (client identification rules, professional conduct rules on funds in PCAs, client money protection regulations); and the PCA is used for low risk activity outside the ML Regulations' scope (for example managing assets of individuals in care, UK litigation, property management agents). (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The funds in the PCA are backed by government schemes with enforcement powers through a judicial body"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Firms should also consider whether they have taken reasonable measures to satisfy themselves that the customer applies robust and risk-sensitive CDD (where relevant to regulated activity) to its own clients and their beneficial owners (for example by obtaining copies of external or internal audit reports, appropriate representations from the customer, or reviewing the customer's relevant procedures), and whether the customer is unnecessarily and/or unreasonably reluctant to provide information on the PCAs. (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The customer is unnecessarily and/or unreasonably reluctant to provide information on the PCAs."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Pooled Client Account guidance 1.1 defines a Pooled Client Account (PCA) as a bank account that a customer, such as a legal practitioner or a letting or estate agent, opens with the firm to look after money belonging to the customer's own clients; the clients' money is mixed together and those clients cannot directly tell the firm to carry out transactions. (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A Pooled Client Account (PCA) is a bank account opened with the firm by a customer"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: PCA guidance 1.2 says that, although it may be evident from the nature and purpose of the business relationship, firms may need to establish matters such as which types of client the PCA funds belong to, how much is deposited and how large the transactions are, and how far the funds touch industries and geographies recognised as vulnerable to money laundering, corruption or terrorist financing. (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the types of clients whose funds are held in the PCAs, the level of assets deposited"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: PCA guidance 1.3 (Risk Assessment): as part of the documented customer risk assessment (see 4.33ff), firms should consider whether offering PCAs affects the customer's ML/TF risk. (p. 166)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should consider whether the provision of PCAs impacts the customer’s ML/TF risk"</sub>

## 1.4 Written Agreement

- **mandatory / requirement**: PCA guidance 1.4: the firm must enter into a written agreement with the customer in which the customer agrees to provide, on request, information on the identity (including verification documents/data where the customer undertakes CDD under the ML Regulations) of the owners of the funds held in the PCAs. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must enter into a written agreement with the customer"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Firms may decide to obtain this agreement through, for example, an appropriate clause in the product terms and conditions, an attestation letter or similar. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms may decide to obtain this agreement through, for example, the inclusion of an appropriate clause"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The timescale agreed with the customer should be proportionate to the ML/TF risk, reasonable within the context of the business relationship, and sufficient to meet the needs of a court order should one be issued to the firm in relation to the PCAs. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be proportionate to the ML/TF risk, be reasonable within the context of the business relationship"</sub>

## 1.5 Due Diligence

- **mandatory / requirement**: Where the firm concludes the customer presents a degree of ML/TF risk other than low (so SDD cannot be applied), the firm must either take reasonable measures to identify and verify the identity of the owners of the funds in the PCA (for example by a formal reliance agreement as per 5.6.4), or take measures to decrease the ML/TF risk until SDD can be applied. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm must either take reasonable measures to identify and verify the identity of the owners of the funds held in the PCA"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Examples of risk-decreasing measures include subjecting the PCAs and/or wider relationship to enhanced ongoing monitoring; requesting that the customer enhances its practices so it can provide, on request, information on the identity of the owners of funds in the PCA (including customers not subject to the ML Regulations); and restricting the type of the customer's clients whose funds are held in the PCAs to those posing lower risk. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Subjecting the PCAs and/or wider business relationship to enhanced ongoing monitoring"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should take reasonable measures to confirm that the customer has enhanced its practices as requested (for example, sample testing the customer's ability to provide CDD or client identity information on request). (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should take reasonable measures to confirm that the customer has done so"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: PCA guidance 1.5: where the firm decides that the customer and its use of the PCA carry a low ML/TF risk, it may use simplified due diligence on the PCA, so that the firm does not have to identify or verify who owns the funds in the account. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may apply simplified due diligence measures on the PCA"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: PCA guidance 1.5: when asking a customer to put risk-reducing measures in place, firms should give it a reasonable time to do so, having regard to factors including the ML/TF risk level; how complex the relationship is; whether the customer is low risk enough not to fall under the ML Regulations; whether it is otherwise low risk yet has no CDD obligations; whether it knows who its clients are and why they transact; whether it meets its own local AML/CTF obligations; how cooperative it is; and whether there are legitimate privacy difficulties. (p. 167)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should allow the customer a reasonable period to implement any such measures"</sub>

## SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITIES, REPORTING AND DATA PROTECTION

- **mandatory / requirement**: Core obligations: all staff must raise an internal report where they have knowledge or suspicion, or reasonable grounds for knowledge or suspicion, that another person is engaged in money laundering or that terrorist property exists; the nominated officer (or appointed alternate) must consider all internal reports. (p. 168)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All staff must raise an internal report where they have knowledge or suspicion"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Core obligations: the nominated officer (or appointed alternate) must make an external report to the National Crime Agency (NCA) as soon as is practicable if he considers there is knowledge, suspicion, or reasonable grounds for knowledge or suspicion that another person is engaged in money laundering or that terrorist property exists. (p. 168)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must make an external report to the National Crime Agency (NCA) as soon as is practicable"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Core obligations: the firm must seek consent from the NCA before proceeding with a suspicious transaction or entering into arrangements. (p. 168)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must seek consent from the NCA before proceeding with a suspicious transaction or entering into arrangements"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Core obligations: it is a criminal offence for anyone, following a disclosure to a nominated officer or to the NCA, to do or say anything that might 'tip off' another person that a disclosure has been made or prejudice an investigation; the nominated officer must report suspicious approaches even if no transaction takes place. (p. 168)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must report suspicious approaches, even if no transaction takes place"</sub>

## General legal and regulatory obligations

(p. 168)[^original]

## General legal and regulatory obligations

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 6.3: when the nominated officer concludes that a report gives grounds for knowledge or suspicion, the matter must be passed to the NCA; under POCA the nominated officer must do so as soon as practicable if there are grounds to suspect that another person, customer or not, is engaged in money laundering, and the Terrorism Act sets similar conditions for disclosure of suspected terrorist financing. (p. 169)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they must report the matter to the NCA"</sub>

## Attempted offences

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 6.7 (POCA s330, Terrorism Act s21A(2)): a disclosure has to be made where there are grounds to suspect that a person is engaged in money laundering or terrorist financing; POCA's meaning of money laundering covers attempting an offence under ss327-329, and under the Terrorism Act disclosure is required where someone knows or suspects that another person committed or attempted an offence under sections 15-18. (p. 169)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "POCA and the Terrorism Act provide that a disclosure must be made where there are grounds for suspicion"</sub>

## Financial sanctions legislation

- **mandatory / requirement**: There is also a requirement to report to OFSI both details of funds frozen and where firms have knowledge or suspicion that a customer, or a person with whom the firm has had business dealings, is a listed person or entity, a person acting on behalf of one, or has committed an offence under the sanctions legislation. (p. 170)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is also a requirement to report to OFSI both details of funds frozen"</sub>

## Attempted offences

- **descriptive / limitation**: There is no duty under s330 of POCA or s21A of the Terrorism Act to disclose information about a person who unsuccessfully attempts to commit fraud, because the attempt was to commit fraud rather than an offence under those Acts. (p. 170)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no duty under s330 of POCA or s21A of the Terrorism Act to disclose information"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 6.8: as soon as the firm has reasonable grounds to know or suspect that any benefit has been acquired (by the fraudster or a third party) so that criminal property or terrorist property exists, then, subject to 6.9, knowledge or suspicion of ML/TF must be reported to the NCA (see 6.40ff). (p. 170)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "knowledge or suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing must be reported to the NCA"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 6.8 states that who committed the criminal conduct, who benefited, and whether the conduct took place before or after POCA was passed make no difference to the obligation to disclose. (p. 170)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Who carried out the criminal conduct, and who benefited from it"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.8 adds that the identity of the person who carried out the conduct and who benefited should nevertheless be included in the report where it is known. (p. 170)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "but should be reported if known"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: POCA s330(3A) / 6.9: where neither the fraudster's identity nor the location of any related criminal property is known or likely to be discovered, limited useable information is available (for example theft of a chequebook or card leading to multiple low-value fraudulent transactions); there is then no obligation to report to the NCA where none of the following is known or suspected: the identity of the person engaged in money laundering; the whereabouts of any laundered property; or that any available information would help identify that person or the property. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no obligation to make a report to the NCA where none of the following is known or suspected"</sub>

## What is meant by "knowledge" and "suspicion"?

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 6.10: having knowledge means actually knowing something to be true; in a criminal court it must be proved the individual in fact knew a person was engaged in money laundering, though knowledge can be inferred from surrounding circumstances (for example a failure to ask obvious questions may be relied upon by a jury to imply knowledge). (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Having knowledge means actually knowing something to be true."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The knowledge must have come to the firm (or staff member) in the course of business, or (for a nominated officer) as a consequence of a disclosure under s330 POCA or s21A Terrorism Act; information coming in other circumstances is outside the regulated sector reporting obligation, though this does not preclude a report if staff choose to make one or are obliged to by other parts of the Acts. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information that comes to the firm or staff member in other circumstances does not come within the scope"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 6.11: suspicion is more subjective and falls short of proof based on firm evidence; the courts have defined it as being beyond mere speculation and based on some foundation. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Suspicion is more subjective and falls short of proof based on firm evidence."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 6.11 quotes court descriptions of suspicion: it is a degree of satisfaction, not necessarily amounting to belief, that goes at least beyond speculation about whether an event has occurred; and although suspicion needs a lesser factual basis than belief, it must still rest on some foundation. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A degree of satisfaction and not necessarily amounting to belief but at least extending beyond speculation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 6.13 states that a staff member, the nominated officer included, who regards a transaction or activity as suspicious is not necessarily expected to know or work out the precise nature of any underlying crime, or that the funds or property definitely come from crime or terrorist financing. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would not necessarily be expected either to know or to establish the exact nature of any underlying criminal offence"</sub>

## What is meant by "reasonable grounds to know or suspect"?

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.15 says that reasonable grounds for suspicion depend on the circumstances and that staff should weigh factors such as the nature or origin of the transaction, how the funds, cash or assets were found, the amounts or values, where they are meant to go, how they reached the customer, and whether the customer or the owners of the assets (if different) seem linked to criminals, terrorists, terrorist groups or sympathisers, in the UK or abroad. (p. 171)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the member of staff should take into account such factors as the nature/origin of the transaction"</sub>

## What is meant by "knowledge" and "suspicion"?

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 6.12: a transaction which appears unusual is not necessarily suspicious; even customers with stable profiles have periodic unusual transactions and many have erratic patterns for good reasons, so the unusual is in the first instance only a basis for further enquiry, which may require judgment as to whether it is suspicious. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A transaction which appears unusual is not necessarily suspicious."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A transaction or activity may not be suspicious at the time, but if suspicions are raised later, an obligation to report then arises. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "if suspicions are raised later, an obligation to report then arises"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 6.14: transactions or proposed transactions such as '419' scams are attempted advance fee frauds, not money laundering, and are therefore not reportable under POCA or the Terrorism Act unless the fraud is successful and the firm is aware of resulting criminal property. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are attempted advance fee frauds, and not money laundering"</sub>

## What is meant by "reasonable grounds to know or suspect"?

- **descriptive / context**: POCA s330(2)(b), s331(2)(b), Terrorism Act s21A / 6.15: POCA and the Terrorism Act create criminal liability for failing to disclose information where reasonable grounds exist for knowing or suspecting that a person is engaged in ML/TF, introducing an objective test of suspicion. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This introduces an objective test of suspicion."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.17: where a court order about a customer is served on a firm, this may, depending on circumstances, create reasonable grounds for suspicion, and the firm should then look at what it holds on that customer across the whole firm to decide whether such grounds exist. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should review the information it holds about that customer across the firm"</sub>

## Internal reporting

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.19: if a firm lets staff consult a line manager before reporting to the nominated officer, it should make sure the arrangement is never used to stop reports reaching the nominated officer when staff have said they know or suspect that a transaction or activity may involve ML/TF. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should ensure that they are not used to prevent reports reaching the nominated officer"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 6.21 says that having few people between the person who knows or suspects and the nominated officer gives speed, confidentiality and quick access to the nominated officer. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Short reporting lines, with a minimum number of people between the person with the knowledge or suspicion"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.22: every suspicion reported to the nominated officer should be written down or recorded electronically, giving full details of the customer concerned and as complete a statement as possible of the information behind the knowledge or suspicion. (p. 172)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All suspicions reported to the nominated officer should be documented, or recorded electronically."</sub>

## What is meant by "reasonable grounds to know or suspect"?

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 6.16: to defend against a charge of failing the objective test, staff would need to demonstrate that they took reasonable steps in the circumstances, in the context of a risk-based approach, to know the customer and the rationale for the transaction, activity or instruction; in practice a jury may decide with hindsight whether the objective test has been met. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "staff within financial sector firms would need to be able to demonstrate that they took reasonable steps"</sub>

## Internal reporting

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 19(4)(d), POCA s330(5) / 6.18: the obligation to report to the nominated officer where there are grounds for knowledge or suspicion of ML/TF is placed on all relevant employees in the regulated sector; financial sector firms therefore need to ensure all relevant employees know whom to report suspicions to. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is placed on all relevant employees in the regulated sector"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 6.19: firms may wish to set up internal systems allowing staff to consult their line manager before reporting to the nominated officer; the POCA obligation is to report 'as soon as is reasonably practicable', so consultations should take this into account. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms may wish to set up internal systems that allow staff to consult with their line manager"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 6.20: whether or not a staff member consults colleagues, the legal obligation remains with the staff member to decide for themself whether a report should be made, and they must not allow colleagues to decide for them; a consulted colleague themself then has knowledge on the basis of which they must consider whether to report. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they must not allow colleagues to decide for them"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In such circumstances firms should make arrangements so that the nominated officer receives only one report in respect of the same information giving rise to knowledge or suspicion. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nominated officer only receives one report in respect of the same information"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: All internal enquiries made in relation to the report should also be documented or recorded electronically, as the information may be required to supplement the initial report or as evidence of good practice and best endeavours if an investigation later confirms or disproves the suspicions. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All internal enquiries made in relation to the report should also be documented"</sub>

## Non-UK offences

(p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The nominated officer is not, therefore, obliged to report everything that comes to their attention from outside of the UK"</sub>

## Internal reporting

## Evaluation and determination by the nominated officer

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 21(5), paragraph 6.29: the firm's nominated officer must consider every report and decide whether it gives rise to knowledge or suspicion, or reasonable grounds for knowledge or suspicion. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm’s nominated officer must consider each report and determine whether it gives rise to knowledge or suspicion"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 6.29: the firm must allow the nominated officer access to whatever relevant information it holds, 'know your customer' information included. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must permit the nominated officer to have access to any information"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 6.29 adds that the nominated officer may ask for further information to be obtained, from the customer where necessary or from the introducing intermediary so far as it still holds it, bearing in mind the intermediary's own record keeping requirements. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The nominated officer may also require further information to be obtained, from the customer if necessary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.30: in dealing with an internal suspicion report, and having regard to the risk of the transaction or activity, the nominated officer needs to balance making a timely disclosure to the NCA, especially where consent is required, against delays that could result from searching several unlinked systems and records. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strike the appropriate balance between the requirement to make a timely disclosure to the NCA"</sub>

## Non-UK offences

- **mandatory / requirement**: In deciding whether to make a disclosure, the nominated officer must bear in mind the need to avoid involvement in an offence under ss327-329 of POCA. (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nominated officer must bear in mind the need to avoid involvement in an offence under ss327-329 of POCA"</sub>

## Evaluation and determination by the nominated officer

- **expected / expectation**: Any approach to the customer or intermediary should be made sensitively, and probably by someone other than the nominated officer, to minimise the risk of alerting them that a disclosure to the NCA may be being considered. (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any approach to the customer or to the intermediary should be made sensitively"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 6.31: as part of the review, other known connected accounts or relationships may need to be examined, with connectivity arising commercially (linked accounts, introducers) or through individuals (third parties, controllers, signatories). (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "other known connected accounts or relationships may need to be examined"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Given the need for timely reporting, it may be prudent for the nominated officer to consider making an initial report to the NCA before completing a full review of linked or connected relationships, which may or may not subsequently need to be reported. (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may be prudent for the nominated officer to consider making an initial report to the NCA"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.32: if the nominated officer decides not to make a report to the NCA, the reasons should be clearly documented or recorded electronically and retained with the internal suspicion report. (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the reasons for not doing so should be clearly documented, or recorded electronically"</sub>

## External reporting

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 19(4)(d), POCA s331 and Terrorism Act s21A, paragraph 6.33: the nominated officer must report to the NCA any transaction or activity that, once evaluated, they know or suspect, or have reasonable grounds to know or suspect, may be linked to money laundering, terrorist financing, or an attempt at either, and must do so as soon as reasonably practicable after the information reaches them. (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must report to the NCA any transaction or activity that, after their evaluation, they know or suspect"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: POCA s339, paragraph 6.34: under POCA the Secretary of State has power to set by order the form and manner in which a disclosure under s330, s331, s332 or s338 is made. (p. 174)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Secretary of State may by order prescribe the form and manner in which a disclosure"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Paragraph 6.35: the NCA prefers that SARs are submitted electronically via the secure internet system SAR Online, or via a dedicated bulk reporting facility. (p. 175)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The NCA prefers that SARs are submitted electronically via the secure internet system SAR Online"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 6.36: to maintain an informed overview, all contact between particular departments/branches and law enforcement agencies should be controlled through, or reported back to, a single contact point, typically the nominated officer; alternatively it may be appropriate to route communications through an appropriate member of staff in the firm's legal or compliance department. (p. 175)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be controlled through, or reported back to a single contact point"</sub>
