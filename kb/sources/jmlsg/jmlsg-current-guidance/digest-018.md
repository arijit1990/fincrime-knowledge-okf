## 2 CONFIRMATION

- **descriptive / definition**: In the third-country pro-forma confirmation, the introducing 'regulated firm' is one that carries on business outside the UK and is (i) subject to CDD and record keeping requirements equivalent to those in the 4th MLD, and (ii) supervised for compliance with those requirements in a manner equivalent to section 2 of Chapter VI of the 4th MLD. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supervised with compliance with those requirements in a manner equivalent to section 2 of Chapter VI of the 4th MLD"</sub>

## Explanatory notes

- **mandatory / requirement**: Explanatory note 1: a separate confirmation must be completed for each customer (for example joint holders, trustee cases and joint life cases); where a third party such as a payer of contributions differs from the customer, that person's identity must also be verified and a confirmation provided. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A separate confirmation must be completed for each customer"</sub>

## 3 DETAILS OF INTRODUCING FIRM

- **descriptive / limitation**: Explanatory note 2: the third-country form cannot be used for customers exempt from verification as an existing client of the introducing firm prior to adoption of the national legislation implementing the EU Fourth Money Laundering Directive. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prior to the adoption of our national legislation that implements the EU Fourth Money Laundering Directive"</sub>

## 1 DETAILS OF CUSTOMER

- **descriptive / data or feature**: The customer-details section of the confirmation forms for corporate and other non-personal entities asks for: the customer's full name; the type of entity (corporate, trust and so on); where the business is located (full operating address); the registered office in the country of incorporation; the registered number, if any; the relevant company registry or regulated market listing authority; the names of directors (or equivalent); and the names of principal beneficial owners holding over 25%, with dates of birth for the individuals named if known. (p. 152)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Type of entity (corporate, trust, etc) Location of business (full operating address) Registered office in country of incorporation"</sub>

## INTRODUCTION BY A UK-REGULATED FIRM

- **descriptive / context**: In the UK-regulated-firm corporate introduction form, the introducing firm confirms that the section 1 information was obtained by it in relation to the customer, and ticks one option: the verification evidence meets the standard evidence in the JMLSG guidance for the UK Financial Sector, or exceeds it (with written details of further verification evidence attached). (p. 153)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exceeds the standard evidence (written details of the further verification evidence taken are attached to this confirmation)"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Previous address if customer has changed address in the last three months"</sub>

(p. 157)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "of the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (as amended), and the guidance for standard evidence"</sub>

(p. 157)[^original]

## Simplified Due Diligence

- **permissive / context**: Risk factor guidelines: firms may apply simplified due diligence (SDD) measures in situations where the ML/TF risk associated with a business relationship is low. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms may apply simplified due diligence (SDD) measures in situations where the ML/TF risk"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: SDD measures firms may apply include, but are not limited to, adjusting the timing of CDD, for example where the product or transaction has features that limit its use for ML/TF purposes, such as verifying identity during establishment of the relationship, or once transactions exceed a defined threshold or a reasonable time limit has lapsed. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adjusting the timing of CDD, for example where the product or transaction sought has features that limit its use"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The SDD risk factor guidelines state that simplified due diligence does not waive any of the CDD measures; what firms may do is vary the amount, timing or type of some or all of those measures so that they fit the low risk the firm has identified. (p. 160)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SDD is not an exemption from any of the CDD measures"</sub>

## RISK FACTOR GUIDELINES

- **mandatory / requirement**: Where firms defer verification to a threshold or time limit, they must make sure that this does not result in a de facto exemption from CDD (the identity will ultimately be verified); the threshold or time limit is set at a reasonably low level; systems detect when it has been reached; and CDD is not deferred or delayed where applicable legislation does not permit this. (p. 161)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this does not result in a de facto exemption from CDD"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Firms may adjust the quantity of information obtained for identification, verification or monitoring, such as verifying identity on the basis of one document only, or assuming the nature and purpose of the relationship because the product is designed for one particular use only, such as a company pension scheme or a shopping centre gift card. (p. 161)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adjusting the quantity of information obtained for identification, verification or monitoring purposes"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Firms may adjust the quality or source of information obtained, for example accepting information obtained from the customer rather than an independent source when verifying the beneficial owner's identity; this is not permitted in relation to verifying the customer's identity. (p. 162)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "note that this is not permitted in relation to the verification of the customer’s identity"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Where the risk associated with all aspects of the relationship is determined to be very low, firms may rely on the source of funds to meet some CDD requirements, for example where funds are state benefit payments or have been transferred from an account in the customer's name at an EEA firm. (p. 162)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "relying on the source of funds to meet some of the CDD requirements"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Firms may adjust the frequency of CDD updates and reviews, for example only when trigger events occur (such as the customer seeking a new product or service, or a transaction threshold being reached); firms must make sure this does not result in a de facto exemption from keeping CDD information up-to-date. (p. 162)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adjusting the frequency of CDD updates and reviews of the business relationship"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Firms may adjust the frequency and intensity of transaction monitoring, for example by monitoring only transactions above a certain threshold; where they do so they must ensure the threshold is set at a reasonable level and that they have systems to identify linked transactions which, taken together, would exceed it. (p. 162)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they must ensure that the threshold is set at a reasonable level"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The information obtained when applying SDD must enable the firm to be reasonably satisfied that the risk associated with the relationship is low, and must be sufficient to give enough information about the nature of the relationship to identify any unusual or suspicious transactions; SDD does not exempt an institution from reporting suspicious transactions to the FIU. (p. 162)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must enable the firm to be reasonably satisfied that the risk associated with the relationship is low"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where there are indications that the risk may not be low, for example grounds to suspect that ML/TF is being attempted or doubts about the veracity of information obtained, SDD must not be applied. (p. 162)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where there are indications that the risk may not be low, for example where there are grounds to suspect"</sub>

## Unusual transactions

- **expected / expectation**: Firms should put in place adequate policies and procedures to detect unusual transactions or patterns of transactions. (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should put in place adequate policies and procedures to detect unusual transactions or patterns of transactions."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Transactions are unusual where they are larger than the firm would normally expect based on its knowledge of the customer, the relationship or the customer's category; have an unusual or unexpected pattern compared to the customer's normal activity or that of similar customers, products or services; or are very complex compared to other similar transactions by similar customer types, products or services. (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are larger than what the firm would normally expect based on its knowledge of the customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: These EDD measures should be sufficient to help the firm determine whether the transactions give rise to suspicion and must at least include taking reasonable measures to understand their background and purpose (for example establishing the source and destination of funds or learning more about the customer's business to ascertain the likelihood of such transactions), and monitoring the relationship and subsequent transactions more frequently and with greater attention to detail. (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "give rise to suspicion and must at least include"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: A firm may decide to monitor individual transactions where this is commensurate with the risk it has identified. (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm may decide to monitor individual transactions where this is commensurate with the risk it has identified."</sub>

## High-risk third countries

- **mandatory / requirement**: When dealing with individuals or entities established or residing in a high risk third country in FATF's lists identifying countries with strategic deficiencies in AML, counter terrorist financing or counter proliferation financing, EDD measures must be applied (see 5.5.11). (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "EDD measures must be applied (see 5.5.11)"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: When adjusting the extent of EDD measures (including timing of existing customer reviews), the following may inform the firm's risk-based approach: the constitution, industry sector and overall ML/TF risk of the firm's customer base, for example different risk-based approaches to EDD for local or expatriate private individuals, corporate customers, regulated financial institutions, and other customer types outlined in 5.3.177-5.3.293. (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the following may inform the firm’s risk-based approach"</sub>

## Unusual transactions

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under the EDD risk factor guidelines, where a firm detects transactions that are unusual because they exceed what it would expect given what it knows of the customer, the relationship or the customer's category, or follow an unusual or unexpected pattern compared with the customer's norm or with similar customers, products or services, or are very complex compared with comparable transactions, and the firm sees no economic rationale or lawful purpose or has doubts about the information it was given, the firm is required to apply EDD measures. (p. 163)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and the firm is not aware of an economic rationale or lawful purpose or doubts the veracity"</sub>

## High-risk third countries

- **permissive / recommendation**: Further factors that may inform the risk-based approach: the presence of other AML/CTF regulated entities in the relationship structure that undertake due diligence or provide management oversight and are established in lower-risk jurisdictions; whether the firm has a branch or subsidiary in the high-risk third country subject to equivalent group-wide policies and procedures; and the specific strategic deficiencies, jurisdictional typologies and compliance ratings per FATF recommendation behind the country's designation on either FATF list. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The presence of other AML/CTF regulated entities in a relationship or relationship structure"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Existing customers already subjected to EDD through the firm's customer risk assessment (considering the Annex 4-II risk factors) may be treated as already subject to mandatory EDD, so firms may conclude no additional EDD is required. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms may conclude that existing customers are already subjected to mandatory EDD measures and therefore no additional EDD is required."</sub>

## Other high-risk situations

- **expected / expectation**: In all other high-risk situations, firms should take an informed decision on which EDD measures are appropriate; the appropriate type of EDD (including the extent of additional information sought and increased monitoring) will depend on the reason a relationship was classified as high risk. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should take an informed decision which EDD measures are appropriate for each high-risk situation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Firms will not need to apply all listed EDD measures in all cases; for example, in certain high-risk situations it may be appropriate to focus on enhanced ongoing monitoring during the business relationship. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms will not need to apply all EDD measures listed below in all cases."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD measures firms should apply may include increasing the quantity of information obtained for CDD, about the customer's or beneficial owner's identity or the ownership and control structure, to be satisfied the risk is well known; this may include assessing reputation and negative allegations, with examples being information about family members and close business partners, past and present business activities, and adverse media searches. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "increasing the quantity of information obtained for CDD purposes"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD may also include increasing information on the intended nature of the relationship to ascertain that its nature and purpose are legitimate and to build a more complete risk profile, including the number, size and frequency of transactions likely to pass through the account (requesting evidence in some cases) to spot deviations that may give rise to suspicion. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the number, size and frequency of transactions that are likely to pass through the account"</sub>

## High-risk third countries

- **descriptive / context**: The guidelines say that once a country is taken off the FATF lists, the duty in 5.5.11 to apply EDD to a customer established in that country comes to an end. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When a country is removed from the FATF lists, the obligation to apply EDD measures"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: After a country is removed from the FATF lists, firms must keep deciding how far their CDD measures should go on a risk-sensitive basis, depending on the type of customer, business relationship, product or transaction. (p. 164)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must continue to determine the extent of their CDD measures on a risk sensitive basis"</sub>

## Other high-risk situations

- **permissive / recommendation**: Further information on the intended nature of the relationship may cover why the customer seeks a specific product or service (particularly where it is unclear why needs cannot be better met another way or in a different jurisdiction), the destination of funds, and the nature of the customer's or beneficial owner's business. (p. 165)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "why the customer looks for a specific product or service, in particular where it is unclear"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD may include increasing the quality of information to confirm identity, including requiring the first payment to be carried out through an account verifiably in the customer's name with a bank subject to UK CDD standards. (p. 165)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring the first payment to be carried out through an account verifiably in the"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD may include establishing that the customer's source of wealth and source of funds are not proceeds of criminal activity and are consistent with the firm's knowledge of the customer and the nature of the relationship; where risk is particularly increased, verifying source of wealth and funds may be the only adequate risk mitigation tool, and can be done by reference to VAT and income tax returns, copies of audited accounts, pay slips, public deeds or independent and credible media reports. (p. 165)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "verifying the source of wealth and the source of funds may be the only adequate risk mitigation tool"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD may include increasing the frequency of reviews, to be satisfied the firm can still manage the risk or conclude it no longer corresponds to its risk appetite, including more frequent reviews to ascertain whether the risk profile has changed and the risk remains manageable, and reviewing the relationship more regularly so changes in risk profile are identified, assessed and acted upon where necessary. (p. 165)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "increasing the frequency of reviews, to be satisfied that the firm continues to be able to manage the risk"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD may include obtaining senior management approval to commence or continue the relationship, so senior management are aware of the risk exposure and can take an informed decision on whether the firm is equipped to manage it. (p. 165)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "obtaining the approval of senior management to commence or continue the"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: EDD may include conducting more frequent or in-depth transaction monitoring to identify unusual or unexpected transactions that may give rise to suspicion of ML/TF, which may include establishing the destination of funds or ascertaining the reason for certain transactions. (p. 165)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducting more frequent or in-depth transaction monitoring to identify any"</sub>
