## Politically exposed persons (PEPs)

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraphs 5.5.13-5.5.14 (Regulation 35(3)(a), 35(4)(b)): individuals who have or have had a high political profile, or hold or have held public office, can pose a higher money laundering risk because their position may make them vulnerable to corruption; the risk also extends to their immediate family members and known close associates. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their position may make them vulnerable to corruption"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: PEP status itself does not incriminate individuals or entities, but it puts the customer or beneficial owner into a higher risk category. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PEP status itself does not, of course, incriminate individuals or entities."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulation 35(12)(a); (d-e) / 5.5.15: a PEP is an individual entrusted with prominent public functions, other than as a middle-ranking or more junior official. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A PEP is defined as an individual who is entrusted with prominent public functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulation 35(3A): a domestic PEP is a PEP entrusted with prominent public functions by the UK. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A domestic PEP is a PEP entrusted with prominent public functions by the UK."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A domestic PEP, or a family member or known close associate of a domestic PEP, has an inherently lower risk than a non-domestic PEP, and if no enhanced risk factors are present the extent of EDD applied will be less than for a non-domestic PEP; 'enhanced risk factors' means risk factors other than the person's position as a domestic PEP or as a family member or known close associate of one (Regulation 12(f)). (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has an inherently lower risk than a non- domestic PEP"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.5.14 states that the risk attached to any PEP, family member or close associate, and with it the extent of EDD measures to be applied, must be assessed individually in each case. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be considered on a case-by-case basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.5.14 notes that the FCA has to issue guidance to the firms it supervises about the EDD measures the ML Regulations call for in relation to PEPs, their family members and known close associates. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FCA is required to give guidance to the firms it supervises"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.5.14 says firms should have regard to the FCA's guidance on EDD for PEPs, their family members and known close associates. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should have regard to this guidance."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Regulation 35(9), paragraph 5.5.16: within the PEP definition, the duty to apply EDD to an individual comes to an end once a year has passed since they left office, or after any longer period the firm judges suitable to deal with the ML/TF risks that person poses. (p. 134)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ceases after they have left office for one year, or for such longer period as the firm considers appropriate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulation 35(14) / 5.5.17: individuals entrusted with prominent public functions include heads of state, heads of government, ministers and deputy or assistant ministers; members of parliaments or similar legislative bodies; members of supreme courts, constitutional courts or other high-level judicial bodies whose decisions are not subject to further appeal except in exceptional circumstances; members of courts of auditors or central bank boards; ambassadors, charges d'affaires and high-ranking armed forces officers (other than in respect of relevant positions at Community and international level); members of administrative, management or supervisory boards of State-owned enterprises; and directors, deputy directors and board members (or equivalent) of an international organisation. (p. 135)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "members of the administrative, management or supervisory boards of State-owned enterprises"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: These categories do not include middle-ranking or more junior officials. (p. 135)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These categories do not include middle-ranking or more junior officials."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.5.18: public functions exercised at levels lower than national should normally not be considered prominent; however, when political exposure is comparable to similar national positions (for example a senior official at state level in a federal system), firms should consider, on a risk-based approach, whether persons exercising those functions should be considered PEPs. - **descriptive / definition**: Regulation 35(12)(b) / 5.5.19: family members of a PEP include a spouse or partner; children and their spouses or partners; and parents. (p. 135)[^original]

## Risk-based procedures

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.5.24: the nature and scope of a firm's business will generally determine whether PEPs in the customer base are an issue and whether the firm needs to screen all customers; it would be appropriate for resources to be focused on products and transactions characterised by a high risk of money laundering. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "whether or not the firm needs to screen all customers for this purpose"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.5.26: establishing whether individuals qualify as PEPs, and so the appropriate EDD level, is not always straightforward; there is a hierarchy or continuum of PEPs, from those just above 'middle ranking or junior official' level to those with significant or even absolute control over the levers, patronage and resources in an area or jurisdiction. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is clearly a hierarchy, or continuum, of PEPs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Regulation 35(3), (4) / 5.5.27: firms' analysis should employ an appropriate risk-based approach to assess where on the PEP continuum an individual lies. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms’ analysis should therefore employ an appropriate risk- based approach"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Firms are under a legal requirement to conduct EDD on PEPs, their family members and known close associates; the ML/TF risk presented varies case by case. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms are under a legal requirement to conduct EDD on PEPs, their family members and known close associates."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The higher up the risk scale a PEP is, the more extensive the EDD measures that should be carried out; conversely, lower down the risk scale it may be appropriate for firms to take less intrusive and less exhaustive EDD measures. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The higher up the risk scale a PEP is, the more extensive the EDD measures that should be carried out."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.5.28: where firms need to carry out specific checks, they may be able to rely on an internet search engine, or consult reports and databases on corruption risk published by specialised national, international, non-governmental and commercial organisations; resources such as the Transparency International Corruption Perception Index (ranking approximately 180 countries) may help assess risk, and the IMF, World Bank and some NGOs also publish relevant reports. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they may be able to rely on an internet search engine"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Regulation 35(3) and 35(4)(b), paragraph 5.5.25: when conducting transactions or business relationships with PEPs, firms should adopt a proportionate, risk-based approach that differs according to where each PEP falls on the risk scale. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should take a proportional, risk-based and differentiated approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Paragraph 5.5.26 notes that judging the status of close relatives of a PEP, such as children and their spouses, is especially hard, because in reality they may be remote from, or even estranged from, their parent or other relative who holds PEP status. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This process can be particularly difficult when seeking to form a view on the status of close family members"</sub>

## Source of wealth

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.5.31: wherever possible, firms should consult sources such as asset and income declarations, which some jurisdictions expect certain senior public officials to file and which frequently describe an official's source of wealth and current business interests. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should, where possible, refer to information sources such as asset and income declarations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.5.31 adds that firms should note that such declarations are not all open to the public and that a PEP customer could have legitimate grounds for not supplying a copy, and should also be aware that some jurisdictions restrict their PEPs from holding foreign bank accounts or other office or paid employment. (p. 136)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not all declarations are publicly available and that a PEP customer may have legitimate reasons for not providing a copy"</sub>

## Reliance on third parties

- **descriptive / requirement**: The relying firm retains responsibility for any failure to comply with a requirement of the Regulations, as this responsibility cannot be delegated. (p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The relying firm, however, retains responsibility for any failure to comply with a requirement of the Regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.6.5 gives examples of reliance: firm A enters a business relationship with, or undertakes an occasional transaction for, the underlying customer of firm B (for example by accepting instructions given through firm B); or firms A and B both act for the same customer in a transaction (for example A as executing broker and B as clearing broker), in which case A may rely on B to carry out CDD while remaining ultimately liable for compliance with the ML Regulations.   <sub>anchor: "firm A may rely on firm B to carry out CDD measures, while remaining ultimately liable"</sub>

(p. 139)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In this context, Firm B must be:"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "The ML Regulations expressly permit a firm to rely on another person to apply any or all"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "it must immediately obtain from the third party all the information needed to identify the customer or beneficial owner"</sub>

(p. 140)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must enter into arrangements with the firm being relied on which"</sub>

## Basis of reliance

  <sub>anchor: "Part of the firm’s AML/CTF policy statement should address the circumstances where reliance may be placed on other firms"</sub>

## Group introductions

- **descriptive / requirement**: Regulation 39(5), paragraph 5.6.25: when one part of a financial sector group introduces a customer to another part, the customer's identity need not be verified again, provided the introducing part verified it in line with AML/CTF standards in the UK, the EU or an assessed low risk jurisdiction and the group entity that did the CDD can be relied on as a third party under Regulation 39(3). (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not necessary for their identity to be re-verified, provided that"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.6.27: where UK firms can access all group customer information and records day to day, no group introduction confirmation is needed if the customer's identity was earlier verified to AML/CTF standards in the EU or an assessed low risk jurisdiction. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no need to obtain a group introduction confirmation"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.6.27 adds that if the customer's identity was not verified earlier (for instance, because the group relationship predates anti-money laundering regulations) or the verification evidence is inadequate, whatever verification evidence is lacking will need to be obtained. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any missing verification evidence will need to be obtained"</sub>

## Use of pro-forma confirmations

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.6.29: pro-forma confirmations for customer identification and verification are attached as Annex 5-I; paragraph 5.6.30: pro-forma confirmations for group introductions are attached as Annex 5-II. (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pro-forma confirmations in respect of group introductions are attached as Annex 5-II to this chapter."</sub>

## Situations which are not reliance

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.6.31: at one end of the spectrum a firm may act solely as an introducer between the customer and the product/service provider with no further relationship with the customer, playing no part in the transaction and having no relationship with either party that would constitute a business relationship; name-passing brokers in inter-professional markets are an example (Part II, Sector 19). (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one firm may act solely as an introducer between the customer and the firm providing the product or service"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.6.32: where the introducer neither gives advice nor plays any part in negotiating or executing the transaction, the identification and verification obligations under the ML Regulations lie with the product/service provider. (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the identification and verification obligations under the ML Regulations lie with the product/service provider"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: This does not preclude the introducing firm from carrying out identification and verification of the customer on behalf of the product/service provider, as agent for that firm (see 5.6.34 - 5.6.35). (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This does not, of course, preclude the introducing firm carrying out identification and verification"</sub>

## Where the intermediary is the agent of the product/service provider

- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.6.33: an intermediary that is an agent or appointed representative of the product/service provider is an extension of that firm; it may obtain the verification evidence, but the provider is responsible for specifying what should be obtained and for ensuring records of the verification evidence are retained. (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is an extension of that firm"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.6.34: when the product or service provider has its own direct sales force, those staff form part of the firm even if they work through a separate group legal entity; the firm remains responsible for stating what is needed and for making sure records of the verification evidence are kept. (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where the product/service provider has a direct sales force, they are part of the firm"</sub>

## Where the intermediary is the agent of the customer

- **expected / expectation**: Regulation 37(1), paragraph 5.6.36: a firm should work out and record who its customer is and which of the underlying parties it has a business relationship with, meaning those customers who need risk-based CDD measures. (p. 143)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm should understand and document who its customer is and which of the underlying parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.6.35: from the provider's viewpoint, the position of an intermediary acting as agent of the customer is influenced by several factors: it may be subject to the ML Regulations, the EU Fourth Money Laundering Directive or similar legislation in an assessed low risk jurisdiction; it may be regulated; and it may be based in the UK or elsewhere, in a FATF member country or not (Annex 4-I gives guidance on assessing low risk jurisdictions). (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The intermediary may be subject to the ML Regulations, or otherwise to the EU Fourth Money Laundering Directive"</sub>
