## CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.17: firms must apply CDD measures at appropriate times to existing customers on a risk-sensitive basis, and must apply CDD to any anonymous accounts, passbooks or anonymous safe-deposit boxes before they are used. The obligation to report suspicions of ML or TF, and the UK financial sanctions regime, apply to all of the firm's customers. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must apply CDD measures at appropriate times to its existing customers on a risk-sensitive basis"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.17: firms must apply CDD measures when they have any legal duty (for example ML Regulation 28(3A)) to contact an existing customer to review beneficial ownership information, or to fulfil any duty under the International Tax Compliance Regulations 2015 (for example FATCA, CRS, DAC2). Firms should consider whether information received as a result contains changes that require CDD to be applied on a risk-based approach. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must apply CDD measures when they have any legal duty"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.18 (Regulation 27(9)): as risk dictates, firms must take steps to ensure they hold appropriate information to demonstrate that they are satisfied they know all their customers. Where an existing customer's identity was verified to a previously applicable standard, then absent circumstances indicating the contrary the risk is likely to be low; trigger events such as an existing customer applying for a new account or relationship might prompt a firm to seek evidence. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must take steps to ensure that they hold appropriate information to demonstrate that they are satisfied that they know all their customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.19: firms that do not seriously address risks (including the risk that they have not confirmed the identity of existing customers) expose themselves to action for breach of the FCA Rules or the ML Regulations. Paragraph 5.3.20: for customers of some years' standing the issue may be more one of collating and assessing information already held than approaching customers for more data. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are exposing themselves to the possibility of action for breach of the FCA Rules, or of the ML Regulations"</sub>

## Acquisition of one financial services firm, or a portfolio of customers, by another

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.21: when a firm acquires another firm's business and customers (as a whole or as a portfolio), it is not necessary to re-verify the identity of all existing customers provided all underlying customer records are acquired with the business, or a warranty is given by the acquired firm (or the vendor of a portfolio) that customer identities have been verified. ## Nature and purpose of proposed business relationship

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.23 (Regulation 28(2)(c)): a firm must understand the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship or transaction to assess whether it is in line with the firm's expectation and to provide a meaningful basis for ongoing monitoring. Whether and to what extent the customer has contact or relationships with other parts of the firm or wider group can be relevant, especially for higher risk customers, as the customer may have different risk profiles in different parts of the business or group. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm must understand the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship or transaction"</sub>

## Acquisition of one financial services firm, or a portfolio of customers, by another

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.22: if sample testing shows the acquired firm's identification procedures were not done to an appropriate standard, or the procedures cannot be checked, or the acquirer cannot access the customer records, the acquirer will need to verify identity as soon as practicable for all transferred customers who are not already verified customers of the transferee. This follows the acquirer's risk-based approach and the rules for existing customers opening new accounts. (p. 79)[^original]

## Keeping information up to date

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.27 (Regulation 28(11)(b)): documents or information obtained for CDD and held about customers must be kept up to date. Once identity has been satisfactorily verified there is no obligation to re-verify unless doubts arise as to the veracity or adequacy of the evidence previously obtained; as risk dictates, firms must take steps to ensure they hold appropriate up-to-date information. Trigger events such as an existing customer applying for a new account or relationship might prompt a firm to seek evidence. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Documents or information obtained for the purposes of applying CDD measures, held about customers, must be kept up to date"</sub>

## Characteristics and evidence of identity

Other facts accumulate over time, such as family circumstances and addresses, employment and business career, contacts with authorities or other financial firms, and physical appearance. (p. 80)[^original]

## Evidence of identity

- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.32: for individuals, much weight is placed on identity documents such as passports and photocard driving licences, which are often the easiest way of being reasonably satisfied. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "written assurances from persons or organisations that have dealt with the customer for some time"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.33 (Regulation 28(19)): evidence of identity can also be obtained through a digital identification process. Electronic sources vary in integrity, reliability and independence, so firms should be satisfied that any process is secure from fraud and misuse and capable of providing an appropriate level of assurance that the person claiming an identity is that person, and firms should document steps taken. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should be satisfied that any process from which such information is obtained is secure from fraud and misuse"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.31 (Regulation 28(2)(a)(b),(18)): the ML Regulations require CDD to rest on documents or information from a reliable source that is independent of the customer. (p. 81)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "customer due diligence must be carried out on the basis of documents or information obtained from a reliable source"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "It is therefore important that the evidence used to verify identity meet this test"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.34 (Regulation 28(12)): how much identity information or evidence to request, how to balance documents against electronic or digital identification, and what to verify, so as to be reasonably satisfied of identity and guard against impersonation, are for the firm's judgment, which must be exercised on a risk-based approach under Chapter 4. Factors to take into account include the nature of the product or service sought (and any other products the customer can migrate to without further identity verification), the nature and length of any existing or previous relationship, the nature and extent of assurances from other regulated firms that may be relied on, and whether the customer is physically present. - **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.66: a shell bank is an entity incorporated in a jurisdiction where it has no physical presence involving meaningful decision-making and management, and which does not belong to a financial conglomerate. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A shell bank is an entity incorporated in a jurisdiction where it has no physical presence"</sub>

## Obtain standard evidence

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.72 (Regulation 28(18)(b)): verification of the information obtained must be based on reliable sources independent of the customer, which might be a document or documents produced by the customer, or electronically by the firm, or a combination. Documents issued or made available by an official body are regarded as independent of the customer even if provided by the customer. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Verification of the information obtained must be based on reliable sources, independent of the customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.72: where business is conducted face-to-face, firms should see originals of any documents involved in verification, and customers should be discouraged from sending original valuable documents by post. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should see originals of any documents involved in the verification"</sub>
