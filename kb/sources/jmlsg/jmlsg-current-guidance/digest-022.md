## RECORD KEEPING

- **descriptive / context**: The JMLSG chapter 8 summary lists the relevant law/regulation as the Data Protection Act 2018, Regulations 18, 19 and 39-41, and SYSC Chapter 3. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Data Protection Act 2018 ▪ Regulations 18, 19 and 39-41 ▪ SYSC Chapter 3"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The JMLSG chapter 8 core obligations state that firms must retain for five years after the end of the customer relationship, or five years after completion of an occasional transaction, copies of or references to the evidence of a customer's identity and details of customer transactions. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must retain for five years after the end of the customer relationship or five years after"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The JMLSG chapter 8 core obligations state that firms should retain details of actions taken on internal and external suspicion reports, and details of information considered by the nominated officer on an internal report where no external report is made. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "details of actions taken in respect of internal and external suspicion reports"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The JMLSG chapter 8 core obligations state that firms must delete any personal data relating to CDD and client transactions in accordance with Regulation 40. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must delete any personal data relating to CDD and client transactions in accordance with Regulation 40"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The JMLSG chapter 8 summary says firms should maintain appropriate systems for retaining records, and for making records available when required within the specified timescales. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should maintain appropriate systems for making records available when required"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.1 states that chapter 8 provides guidance on record keeping that will meet firms' obligations on preventing money laundering and terrorist financing, and is not intended to replace or interpret wider obligations to maintain appropriate records and controls in relation to their business. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this guidance is not intended to replace or interpret such wider obligations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.2 states that record keeping is an essential component of the audit trail that the ML Regulations and FCA Rules seek to establish to assist financial investigations and to ensure criminal funds are kept out of the financial system or, if not, can be detected and confiscated by the authorities. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Record keeping is an essential component of the audit trail"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.3 (Regulations 18(4), 19(1)(b), 39(2)(b)) states that, besides record keeping on customer identification and transactions, firms are obliged to document their risk assessment and their policies, controls and procedures, and must have written arrangements with any third party on which they rely to apply CDD measures. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm is also required to have written arrangements with any third party on which they rely"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.4 (Regulation 40, SYSC 3.2.20 R, SYSC 6.3.1 R) states that firms must retain records concerning customer identification and transactions as evidence of their compliance work and for use as evidence in any law enforcement investigation. (p. 196)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must retain records concerning customer identification and transactions as evidence of the work"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.4 states that FCA-regulated firms must take reasonable care to make and keep adequate records appropriate to the scale, nature and complexity of their businesses. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must take reasonable care to make and keep adequate records appropriate to the scale, nature and complexity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.5 (Regulation 39) states that where a firm has an appointed representative, it must ensure the representative complies with the ML Regulations record keeping obligations, and the principle also applies where record keeping is delegated to a third party such as an administrator or an introducer. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it must ensure that the representative complies with the record keeping obligations under the ML Regulations"</sub>

## What records have to be kept?

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.6 states the precise nature of the records required is not specified in the legal and regulatory regime; the objective is to ensure the firm meets its obligations and, so far as practicable, can provide the authorities in any later investigation with its section of the audit trail. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The precise nature of the records required is not specified in the legal and regulatory regime."</sub>

## Customer information

- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.8 (Regulation 40(2)) states that, in relation to evidence of a customer's identity, firms must keep a copy of any documents or information obtained to satisfy the CDD measures required under the ML Regulations; where a firm has received a confirmation of identity certificate, that certificate will in practice be the evidence of identity that must be kept. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must keep a copy of any documents or information it obtained to satisfy the CDD measures"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.8 states some identification documents are more sensitive than others (for example Armed Forces Cards and Firearms certificates), and firms should deal with such evidence with care. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should deal with such evidence with care"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.9 says that when a firm has concluded it should treat a client as financially excluded for customer identification purposes, it should keep a record of the reasons for doing so. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should keep a record of the reasons for doing so"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.10 states that a firm may often hold additional information on a customer obtained for enhanced customer due diligence or ongoing monitoring. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm may often hold additional information in respect of a customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.12 (Regulation 40(3)(b)(ii)) states that records of identification evidence must be kept for five years after the business relationship with the customer has ended, i.e. the closing of the account or accounts. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Records of identification evidence must be kept for a period of five years after the business"</sub>

## Transactions

- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.18 (Regulation 40(3)(a)(b)) states that records of all transactions relating to a customer must be retained for five years from the date an occasional transaction is completed or, in other cases, from the date the business relationship ended, i.e. the closing of the account or accounts. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Records of all transactions relating to a customer must be retained for a period of five years from"</sub>

## Customer information

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 40(5), paragraph 8.13: when the five year period in paragraph 8.12 ends, firms must erase any personal data, unless one of three exceptions applies: the firm is obliged by or under an enactment, or for court proceedings, to keep records containing personal data; the firm has reasonable grounds to believe the records are needed for legal proceedings; or the data subject has agreed to the data being kept. (p. 197)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must delete any personal data unless"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.13 states the exceptions to deletion after the five year period also include where the firm has reasonable grounds for believing the records need to be retained for the purpose of legal proceedings, or the data subject has given consent to retention of that data. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the data subject has given consent to the retention of that data"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.14 (Regulation 40(6)) states that a firm which is relied on by another firm for CDD purposes must keep the records referred to in paragraph 8.8 for five years from the ending of the business relationship with the customer. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm which is relied on by another firm for the purposes of customer due diligence must keep the records"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.15 states that where documents verifying a customer's identity are held in one part of a group, they do not need to be held in duplicate in another. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they do not need to be held in duplicate form in another"</sub>

## Transactions

- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.17 states that all transactions carried out on behalf of or with a customer in the course of relevant business must be recorded within the firm's records. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All transactions carried out on behalf of or with a customer in the course of relevant business must be recorded"</sub>

## Customer information

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 8.15 says that although identity verification documents held in one part of a group need not be duplicated elsewhere in it, the records need to be reachable by the nominated officer, the MLRO and every area dealing with the customer, and obtainable on request where those areas want to rely on the evidence or may be asked by law enforcement to produce it. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The records do, however, need to be accessible to the nominated officer and the MLRO"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 8.16 says that where an introducing branch or subsidiary stops trading or stops having a business relationship with a customer while the customer's dealings with other group members go on, particular care is needed to keep or pass on the proper customer records; similar arrangements are needed if a company holding relevant records leaves the group, and the same issue arises where record keeping has been delegated to a third party. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particular care needs to be taken to retain, or hand over, the appropriate customer records"</sub>

## Transactions

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 8.17 says that the transaction records supporting account entries, in whatever form (for example credit or debit slips, or cheques), should be kept in a way that lets a satisfactory audit trail be put together when needed and that can show the financial profile of any suspect account or customer. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be maintained in a form from which a satisfactory audit trail may be compiled where necessary"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 8.19: for managers of investment funds and issuers of electronic money, where there may be no business relationship as the ML Regulations define it but the customer could still carry out occasional transactions later, it is recommended that all records are kept for five years after the investment has been completely sold or the funds paid out. (p. 198)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the case of managers of investment funds or issuers of electronic money"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.18 (Regulation 40(4)) states that a firm is not required to retain records relating to transactions occurring in a business relationship for more than 10 years. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm is not required to retain records relating to transactions occurring in a"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.20 (Regulation 40(5)) states that on expiry of the period in 8.18, firms must delete any personal data unless the firm is required to retain it by or under any enactment or for court proceedings, has reasonable grounds for believing it needs to be retained for legal proceedings, or the data subject has consented to retention. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Upon the expiry of the period referred to in paragraph 8.18, firms must delete any personal data unless"</sub>

## Internal and external reports

- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.21 says a firm should make and retain records of actions taken under the internal and external reporting requirements, and, when the nominated officer has considered information about possible money laundering but not reported to the NCA, a record of the other material considered. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm should make and retain"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.22 says copies of any SARs made to the NCA should be retained. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "copies of any SARs made to the NCA should be retained"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.23 says records of all internal and external reports should be retained for at least five years from the date the report was made. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Records of all internal and external reports should be retained for at least five years from the date"</sub>

## Other

- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.24 says a firm's records should include, for training, the dates AML training was given, the nature of the training, the names of staff who received it, and the results of tests undertaken by staff, where appropriate. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "➢ dates AML training was given; ➢ the nature of the training; ➢ the names of the staff"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.24 says a firm's records should include, for compliance monitoring, reports by the MLRO to senior management and records of consideration of those reports and of any action taken as a consequence. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reports by the MLRO to senior management"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.25 (Regulation 21(8),(9)) states that a firm must establish and maintain systems enabling it to respond fully and rapidly to enquiries from financial investigators accredited under s3 of POCA, persons acting for the Scottish Ministers as an enforcement authority, or constables, on whether it maintains, or has maintained during the previous five years, a business relationship with any person and the nature of that relationship. (p. 199)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm must establish and maintain systems which enable it to respond fully and rapidly to enquiries"</sub>

## Form in which records have to be kept

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.26 states that most firms have standard procedures kept under review and seek to reduce the volume and density of records stored while complying with statutory requirements; retention may be by original documents, photocopies, microfiche, scanned form, or computerised or electronic form. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will seek to reduce the volume and density of records which have to be stored"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.27 states that record retention requirements are the same regardless of the format in which records are kept or whether the transaction was undertaken by paper or electronic means. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The record retention requirements are the same, regardless of the format in which they are kept"</sub>

## Location

- **descriptive / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.30 states that where identification records are held outside the UK, it is the responsibility of the UK firm to ensure the records available meet UK requirements. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is the responsibility of the UK firm to ensure that the records available do in fact meet UK requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.30 states no secrecy or data protection legislation should restrict access to the records either by the UK firm freely on request or by UK law enforcement under court order or mutual assistance procedures; if such restrictions exist, copies of the underlying records of identity should, wherever possible, be sought and retained within the UK. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No secrecy or data protection legislation should restrict access to the records"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: JMLSG paragraph 8.31 says firms should take account of the scope of AML/CTF legislation in other countries, and should ensure group records kept in other countries that are needed to comply with UK legislation are retained for the required period. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should take account of the scope of AML/CTF legislation in other countries"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.32 states there can sometimes be tension between the ML Regulations and data protection legislation, and the nominated officer and the MLRO must have due regard to both sets of obligations. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nominated officer and the MLRO must have due regard to both sets of obligations"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 8.33 states that in setting document retention policy, financial sector businesses must weigh the statutory requirements and the needs of the investigating authorities against normal commercial considerations. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial sector businesses must weigh the statutory requirements and the needs of the investigating authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.33 states that where original vouchers are used for account entry and not returned to the customer, keeping them assists law enforcement forensic analysis and firms' own internal investigations, but this is not a requirement of the AML legislation and retaining electronic/digital copies may be a more realistic storage method. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this is not a requirement of the AML legislation"</sub>

## Form in which records have to be kept

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 8.28 says firms involved in mergers, take-overs or internal reorganisations need to make sure that records of identity verification and of transactions can still be readily retrieved for the required periods while computer systems and physical storage are being rationalised. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "need to ensure that records of identity verification and transactions are readily retrievable"</sub>

## Location

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 8.29 notes that the ML Regulations are silent on where relevant records must be kept, and that the key aim is that firms can get at relevant information promptly. (p. 200)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the overriding objective is for firms to be able to retrieve relevant information without undue delay"</sub>

## Sanctions and penalties

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 8.34 (Regulation 86(1)) states that where ML Regulations record keeping obligations are not observed, a firm or person is open to prosecution, including imprisonment for up to two years and/or a fine, or regulatory censure. (p. 201)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "imprisonment for up to two years and/or a fine, or regulatory censure"</sub>

## GLOSSARY OF TERMS

- **descriptive / definition**: The JMLSG glossary defines an Annex I Financial Institution as an undertaking (other than a credit institution or consumer credit institution) carrying out one or more of the operations listed in Schedule 2 to the ML Regulations, other than trading on its own account where its only customers are group companies. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An undertaking (other than a credit institution or a consumer credit institution)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The JMLSG glossary defines an 'appropriate person' as someone in a position of responsibility who knows, and is known by, a customer and may reasonably confirm the customer's identity; no definitive list can be given, but examples might include members of a local authority, staff of a higher or further education establishment, or a hostel manager. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Someone in a position of responsibility, who knows, and is known by, a customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The JMLSG glossary defines beneficial owner(s) as the individual who ultimately owns or controls the customer on whose behalf a transaction or activity is being conducted, with special rules for bodies corporate, partnerships, trusts, and entities or arrangements administering and distributing funds and estates of deceased persons (ML Regulations 5 and 6). (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The individual who ultimately owns or controls the customer on whose behalf a transaction or activity is being conducted"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The JMLSG glossary defines criminal property (POCA s 340(3)) as property which constitutes a person's benefit from criminal conduct or represents such a benefit (in whole or part, directly or indirectly), where the alleged offender knows or suspects it constitutes or represents such a benefit. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Property which constitutes a person’s benefit from criminal conduct or which represents such a benefit"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The JMLSG glossary defines criminal conduct (POCA s 340(2)) as conduct which constitutes an offence in any part of the United Kingdom, or would constitute an offence in any part of the UK if it occurred there. (p. 202)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Conduct which constitutes an offence in any part of the United Kingdom"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The JMLSG glossary defines a customer, in relation to an FCA-regulated firm, as a person who is using, or may be contemplating using, any of the services provided by the firm, and notes this is not the definition of customer that applies in SYSC.
