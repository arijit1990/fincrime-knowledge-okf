- **descriptive / context**: Examples of classes given are 'Homeless persons in London', 'Deaf and blind people' and 'Children in the village of Ambridge'; in other charities no individuals benefit directly, for example charities for animals, wildlife or flora, or conservation or preservation of buildings, habitats or environment. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In other charities, no individuals benefit directly from the charity’s objects."</sub>

## Obtain standard evidence

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.246: the firm should obtain for the charity or church body its full name and address, the nature of its activities and objects, the name(s) of any settlor(s), the names of all trustees (or equivalent), and the names or classes of beneficiaries. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm should obtain the following in relation to the charity or church body"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.247: the existence of the charity can be verified from several sources, depending on whether it is registered or not, a place of worship, or an independent school or college. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The existence of the charity can be verified from a number of different sources"</sub>

## Registered charities – England and Wales, and Scotland

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.249: details of registered charities can be accessed on the Charity Commission and OSCR websites, or checked by telephone to the regulator's enquiry line. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Details of all registered charities can be accessed on the Charity Commission website"</sub>

## Charities in Northern Ireland

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.250: applications from or on behalf of charities in Northern Ireland should be dealt with under the private company procedures in paragraphs 5.3.163 to 5.3.169 if limited by guarantee, and for clubs and societies under paragraphs 5.3.283 to 5.3.293. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be dealt with in accordance with procedures for private companies set out in paragraphs 5.3.163 to 5.3.169"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Verification of the charitable status of Northern Ireland charities can normally be obtained through HMRC. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Verification of the charitable status can normally be obtained through HMRC."</sub>

## Independent schools and colleges

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.254: for independent schools and colleges, firms should take appropriate steps to be reasonably satisfied that the individual they deal with has the customer's proper authority. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "5.3.254 Firms should take appropriate steps to be reasonably satisfied"</sub>

## Other considerations

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.255: when assessing risk across different charities, a firm might need to distinguish suitably between charities with a restricted geographical remit and those with unrestricted geographical reach, such as medical and emergency relief charities. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm might need to make appropriate distinction between those with a limited geographical remit"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.257 notes that non-profit organisations have been known to be misused to channel funds to terrorist financing and other criminal activities, and that in June 2015 FATF published a best practices paper titled 'Combating the abuse of non-profit organisations', in support of Recommendation 8. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Non-profit organisations have been known to be abused, to divert funds to terrorist financing"</sub>

## Church bodies and places of worship

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.251: certain church bodies are excepted by law from registering as charities and may not have a registered number; for tax purposes they may notify HMRC of charitable status, and verification may be met by sight of HMRC's confirmation of the church's application for charitable status. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Certain church bodies are excepted by law from registering as charities"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The identity of individual churches may be verified through the headquarters or regional organisation of the denomination or religion. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be verified through the headquarters or regional organisation of the denomination, or religion"</sub>

## Unregistered charities or church bodies

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.252: other than those in 5.3.251, the identities of unregistered charities or church bodies, in the UK or elsewhere, cannot be verified by reference to registers maintained by independent bodies. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cannot be verified by reference to registers maintained by independent bodies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Applications from or on behalf of unregistered charities should be dealt with under the procedures for private companies (paragraphs 5.3.163 to 5.3.169), for trusts (5.3.258 to 5.3.282), or for clubs and societies (5.3.283 to 5.3.293); firms should take particular note of those paragraphs addressing customers where ML/TF risk is greater. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Applications from, or on behalf of, unregistered charities should therefore be dealt with in accordance with the procedures"</sub>

## Independent schools and colleges

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.253: an independent school or college that is a registered charity should be treated in accordance with the guidance for charities; one not registered as a charity should be treated under the private company guidance in paragraphs 5.3.163 to 5.3.169. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should be treated in accordance with the guidance for charities"</sub>

## Other considerations

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 5.3.256: charities with a defined area of benefit can only expend funds within it; if that area is an overseas country the charity can properly transfer funds there, but otherwise it would be less clear why the organisation is transferring funds to a third country and this would be unusual, leading to the charity being regarded as higher risk. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such activity would lead to the charity being regarded as higher risk."</sub>

## Other trusts and foundations

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.258: trusts range from large, nationally and internationally active organisations with a high degree of public interest and quasi-accountability, through testamentary trusts, to small local trusts funded by small donations. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is a wide variety of trusts, ranging from large, nationally and internationally active organisations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.258 states that, when setting up proportionate AML/CTF processes and carrying out risk assessments, firms need to allow for the differing ML/TF risks posed by trusts of different sizes, areas of activity and types of business. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms take account of the different money laundering or terrorist financing risks that trusts of different sizes"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.259: on a risk-based approach, where a trust or foundation is a large, well known and accountable organisation, firms may treat as customers only those trustees who instruct the firm; the other trustees are verified as beneficial owners using paragraphs 5.3.8 to 5.3.16. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in the case of a large, well known and accountable organisation firms may limit the trustees considered customers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.260: the majority of trusts are not separate legal persons, and for AML/CTF purposes they should be identified in the way set out in paragraphs 5.3.267 to 5.3.271. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most trusts are not separate legal persons, and for AML/CTF purposes should be identified"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Under Regulation 6(2), in paragraph 5.3.263, where someone other than the trustees, settlor and beneficiaries controls the trust property (a trust protector is given as an example), that person counts as a beneficial owner; this applies only in exceptional cases. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are to be considered as beneficial owners"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.265 observes that some trusts have no individual who benefits directly, for instance trusts for animals, wildlife or flora, or for conserving or preserving buildings, habitats or the environment. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In some trusts, no individuals may benefit directly"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.259: for trusts or foundations with no legal personality, the trustees (or equivalent) who enter into the business relationship in their capacity as trustees are the firm's customers on whom the firm must carry out full CDD measures. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are the firm’s customers on whom the firm must carry out full CDD measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulations 6(1) and 42(2)(b) / 5.3.261: the beneficial owner of a relevant trust means each of: the settlor; the trustees; the beneficiaries, or where the individuals benefiting have not been determined, the class of persons in whose main interest the trust is set up or operates; and any individual with control over the trust. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the class of persons in whose main interest the trust is set up, or operates"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Regulation 6(3) / 5.3.262: for a foundation or other legal arrangement similar to a trust, the beneficial owners are those who hold equivalent or similar positions to those in paragraph 5.3.261. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the beneficial owners are those who hold equivalent or similar positions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Regulation 42(2)(b) / 5.3.264: for the vast majority of relevant trusts there will be clearly identified beneficiaries (who are beneficial owners) or a class of beneficiaries, self-evident from the trust's constitution or proof of registration from the Trust Registration Service. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or proof of registration document from the Trust Registration Service"</sub>
