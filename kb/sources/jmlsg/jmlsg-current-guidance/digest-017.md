## Where the intermediary is the agent of the customer

- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.6.37: depending on jurisdiction, where the customer is an intermediary carrying on appropriately regulated business and acting on behalf of another, and the firm determines the situation presents a low degree of ML/TF risk, the product provider may decide to carry out SDD measures on both the customer and the underlying party (see 5.3.134). (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the product provider may decide to carry out SDD measures on both the customer and on the underlying party"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.6.38: where the firm takes instruction from the underlying customer, or acts on the underlying customer's behalf (for example as a custodian), the firm has an obligation to carry out CDD measures in respect of that customer, although the reliance provisions (5.6.4ff) may be applied. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm then has an obligation to carry out CDD measures in respect of that customer"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Where the underlying party is a low risk customer of the firm, SDD may be applied. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where the underlying party is a low risk customer of the firm, SDD may be applied."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.6.39: in verifying the underlying customer's identity the firm should take a risk-based approach, assessing the AML/CTF regime in the intermediary's jurisdiction, the level of reliance that can be placed on the intermediary and its verification work, and consequently the amount of evidence to be obtained directly from the customer. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm should take a risk-based approach. It will need to assess the AML/CTF regime in the intermediary’s jurisdiction"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.6.40: where the intermediary is in a higher risk jurisdiction, or a country listed as having material deficiencies, the risk-based approach should be aimed at ensuring the business does not proceed unless the identity of the underlying customers has been verified to the product/service provider's satisfaction. (p. 144)[^original]

## The requirement to monitor customers' activities

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 28(11) / 5.7.1: firms must conduct ongoing monitoring of the business relationship with their customers. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must conduct ongoing monitoring of the business relationship with their customers."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Ongoing monitoring of a business relationship includes scrutiny of transactions throughout the relationship (including, where necessary, the source of funds) to ensure they are consistent with the firm's knowledge of the customer, its business and risk profile; and ensuring that documents or information obtained for CDD are kept up to date. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ensuring that the documents or information obtained for the purposes of applying customer due diligence are kept up to date."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.1 states that a firm's monitoring arrangements should follow a risk-based approach, be shaped by the nature, scale and complexity of its business, and form a component of its financial crime control framework. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Its monitoring arrangements should be risk based, driven by the nature, size and complexity of the firm’s business"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.2 explains that watching customer activity brings unusual activity to light, and that unusual activity with no rational explanation may be tied to money laundering or terrorist financing; monitoring across the whole relationship helps firms know their customers, assess risk, and be more confident that they are not being used for financial crime. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If unusual activities cannot be rationally explained, they may involve money laundering or terrorist financing."</sub>

## What is monitoring?

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.7.3 sets out three essentials of any monitoring system: it raises transactions or activities for closer examination; the appropriate person or people review those alerts without delay; and suitable action follows from what the further examination finds. (p. 144)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these reports are reviewed promptly by the right person(s)"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 5.7.4: monitoring can be in real time (transactions or activities reviewed as they take place or are about to take place) or after the event, through independent review of customer transactions/activities, possibly over a reasonable time period to identify patterns/trends; in either case the objective is to identify or flag unusual transactions or activities for further examination. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in real time, in that transactions and/or activities can be reviewed"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 5.7.5: monitoring may be by reference to specific types of transactions, to the customer's profile, to networks of connected persons, or by comparing activity or profile with a similar peer group of customers, or through a combination of these approaches. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Monitoring may be by reference to specific types of transactions, to the profile of the customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.6: firms should also have systems and procedures to deal with customers who have not had contact with the firm for some time where regular contact might be expected, and with dormant accounts or relationships, to be able to identify future reactivation and unauthorised use. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should also have systems and procedures to deal with customers who have not had contact"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.8: monitoring is not a mechanical process and does not necessarily require sophisticated electronic systems; its scope and complexity are influenced by the firm's business activities and size. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Monitoring is not a mechanical process and does not necessarily require sophisticated electronic systems."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.7 states that, when firms design monitoring arrangements, they should give due weight to how often customers transact and to the volume and size of those transactions, set against the assessed risk of the customer and product. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate account be taken of the frequency, volume and size of transactions with customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.8 describes the key elements of any monitoring system as holding current customer information, knowing about developing financial crime risks and typologies relevant to the firm so that unusual activity can be recognised, and putting relevant questions to find out why unusual transactions or activities occur, so as to decide whether they may be suspicious. (p. 145)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "being aware of evolving financial crime risks and typologies that are relevant to the firm"</sub>

## 5.7.8A

- **expected / expectation**: Transaction monitoring is a dynamic process, so monitoring arrangements, including automated monitoring system rules and thresholds, should be reviewed regularly to ensure they remain effective. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be reviewed regularly to ensure that they remain effective"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: This may include reallocating resources from less productive or less efficient monitoring arrangements (activity that never or seldom contributes to the management of financial crime risk) to higher priority risks, to ensure monitoring provides more effective outcomes. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reallocating resources from less productive or less efficient monitoring arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: These arrangements and any changes to them should be documented appropriately and be subject to regular review. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These arrangements and any changes to them should be documented appropriately"</sub>

## Nature of monitoring

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.9: some financial services business involves customers about whom the firm has a good deal of information, while other types need only limited information; the nature of monitoring will depend on the firm's business, the frequency of customer activity and the types of customers. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will therefore depend on the business of the firm, the frequency of customer activity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.11: the arrangements should include training of staff on procedures to spot, and deal specially (for example by referral to management) with, situations suggesting a heightened money laundering risk. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The arrangements should include the training of staff on procedures to spot and deal specially"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: The arrangements could involve exception reporting by reference to objective triggers (for example transaction amount). (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they could involve arrangements for exception reporting by reference to objective triggers"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Staff training is not a substitute for having in place some form of regular monitoring activity. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Staff training is not, however, a substitute for having in place some form of regular monitoring activity."</sub>

## Manual or automated?

- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 5.7.13: a monitoring system may be manual, or automated to the extent that a standard suite of exception reports is produced, or a combination; one or other of these may suit most firms. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A monitoring system may be manual, or may be automated to the extent that a standard suite"</sub>

## Nature of monitoring

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 5.7.10 says effective monitoring is likely to rest on carefully identified transaction characteristics: an unusual transaction (for instance abnormal size or frequency for that customer or peer group, or early surrender of an insurance policy); the nature of a run of transactions (for instance several cash credits); where a payment goes to or comes from (for instance a high-risk country); who the parties are (for instance a payment request involving someone on a sanctions list); known threats or typologies, including those in the public domain; and, in line with the firm's nature, size and complexity, networks of linked accounts, counterparties, customers or beneficial owners. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective monitoring is likely to be based on a considered identification of transaction characteristics"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 33(1) and 33(5)(d), paragraph 5.7.12: higher risk accounts and customer relationships call for enhanced ongoing monitoring, which will in general be more frequent or more intensive, set on a risk basis. (p. 146)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Higher risk accounts and customer relationships require enhanced ongoing monitoring."</sub>

## Manual or automated?

- **descriptive / context**: In firms with major volume issues, or other factors making a basic exception report regime inappropriate, a more sophisticated automated system may be necessary. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a more sophisticated automated system may be necessary"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where manual monitoring is in place, firms should have procedures to manage the risk of manual error. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where manual monitoring is in place, firms should have procedures to manage the risk of manual error."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.15: an automated system may add value to manual systems and controls provided the parameters determining its outputs are appropriate; firms should understand the workings and rationale of an automated system and the reasons for its alerts, as the firm may be asked to explain this to its regulator. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should understand the workings and rationale of an automated system"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.16: the greater the volume of transactions, the less easy it is to monitor them without automation; anti-fraud monitoring systems (used by many firms, particularly those offering credit) are not specifically designed to identify ML/TF, but their output can often indicate possible ML/TF. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the output from these anti-fraud monitoring systems can often indicate possible money laundering or terrorist financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.17: many automated transaction monitoring systems are available using techniques ranging from artificial intelligence to simple rules; they are not designed to detect ML/TF but can detect and report unusual/uncharacteristic behaviour and patterns characteristic of ML/TF, which after analysis may lead to suspicion. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These techniques can range from artificial intelligence to simple rules."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Implementing transaction monitoring systems is difficult because of the complexity of the underlying analytics and their heavy reliance on customer reference data and transaction data; ongoing effectiveness also depends on system parameters (for example rules/thresholds). (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The implementation of transaction monitoring systems is difficult due to the complexity of the underlying analytics"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should ensure that the thresholds used are relevant and applicable to their business and customer activities. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should ensure that the thresholds used are relevant and applicable to their business and customer activities."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.18: monitoring systems vary considerably in approach; it is important for firms to ask questions of the supplier of an automated system, and internally, to help select a solution best meeting their business needs. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important for firms to ask questions of the supplier of an automated system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Questions that should be addressed include: how the solution enables a risk-based approach to customers, third parties and transactions; how system parameters aid the risk-based approach and affect the quality of alerts; which ML/TF typologies the system addresses, which component addresses each, whether the included typologies are complete and relevant to the firm's line of business, and how often they are updated; what functionality exists to implement new typologies, how quickly they can be commissioned and how their validity can be tested before activation in the live system; whether the system gives the reason a transaction is alerted with a full evidential process behind it; and whether it has robust mechanisms to learn from experience and to continually monitor and reduce unproductive alerts/'false positives'. (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Questions that should be addressed include:"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.14 states that it is essential to recognise how much staff alertness matters, since staff intuition, direct face-to-face or telephone contact with a customer, and the practical experience to see transactions that make no sense for that customer cannot be automated (see Chapter 8). (p. 147)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is essential to recognise the importance of staff alertness."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Although monitoring processes may be outsourced, firms remain responsible for their regulatory obligations. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Although monitoring processes may be outsourced, firms remain responsible for their regulatory obligations."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.20: the effectiveness of a monitoring system, automated or manual, in identifying unusual activity depends on the quality of the parameters determining its alerts and the ability of staff to assess and act on outputs; each firm's needs and each system's capabilities vary with scale, nature and complexity of business. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will depend on the quality of the parameters which determine what alerts it makes"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should establish an appropriate governance mechanism for oversight, review and approval of monitoring processes and parameters, including documenting monitoring arrangements and rationale. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should establish an appropriate governance mechanism for the oversight, review and approval of monitoring processes and parameters"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: This may include consideration of: defining responsibilities for the governance mechanism; measuring the effectiveness and relevance of monitoring arrangements; supporting changes to systems to address evolving ML/TF risks; and approach and governance for reallocating resource (for example turning off or dialling down less efficient monitoring parameters or introducing different parameters). (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Measuring the effectiveness and relevance of monitoring"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.7.21: monitoring also involves keeping information held about customers up to date as far as reasonably possible; guidance is at paragraphs 5.3.27 - 5.3.28. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Monitoring also involves keeping information held about customers up to date, as far as reasonably possible."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.19: because the system itself often decides what counts as unusual or uncharacteristic customer behaviour, it will be important to choose a system whose definition of that term suits the nature of the firm's business. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It will be important that the system selected has an appropriate definition"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.7.20 states that it is important to get the balance right when setting the level at which alerts are generated: it is not sufficient to set it so the system yields only as much output as existing staff can handle, yet the system should also not produce large numbers of unproductive alerts or 'false positives' that take excessive resources to investigate. (p. 148)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important that the balance is right in setting the level at which an alert is generated"</sub>

## INTRODUCTION BY A UK-REGULATED FIRM

- **descriptive / context**: Annex pro-forma confirmation (introduction by a UK-regulated firm), section 1: the form records the individual customer's full name, current address, previous address if changed in the last three months, and date of birth. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Previous address if individual has changed address in the last three months"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The introducing firm confirms that the section 1 information was obtained by it in relation to the customer, and ticks one option: that the identity verification evidence meets the standard evidence set out in the JMLSG Guidance for the UK Financial Sector, or exceeds it (with written details of further verification evidence attached). ## Explanatory notes

- **mandatory / requirement**: Explanatory note 1: a separate confirmation must be completed for each customer (for example joint holders, trustee cases and joint life cases); where a third party is involved, such as a payer of contributions different from the customer, that person's identity must also be verified and a confirmation provided.
