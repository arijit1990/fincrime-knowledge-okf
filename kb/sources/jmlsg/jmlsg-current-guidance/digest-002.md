## SENIOR MANAGEMENT RESPONSIBILITY AND GOVERNANCE

- **mandatory / requirement**: The Chapter 1 summary of core obligations states that senior management in all firms must identify, assess and manage effectively the risks in their businesses, and, if in the regulated sector, must appoint a nominated officer to process disclosures. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management in all firms must: o identify, assess, and manage effectively, the risks in their businesses"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Chapter 1 summary states that senior management in FCA-regulated firms must appoint individual(s), including an MLRO, with certain responsibilities; adequate resources must be devoted to AML/CTF; and there is potential personal liability if legal obligations are not met. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management in FCA-regulated firms must appoint individual(s) (including an MLRO) with certain responsibilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The Chapter 1 summary lists actions to be kept under regular review: prepare a formal policy statement on preventing, and risk assessing, money laundering/terrorist financing; ensure adequate resources for AML/CTF; and commission an annual MLRO report and remedy any deficiencies it identifies in a timely manner. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Commission annual report from the MLRO and take any necessary action to remedy deficiencies identified by the report"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / expectation**: Being used for ML or TF involves firms in reputational, legal and regulatory risks. Senior management is responsible for ensuring the firm's policies, controls and procedures are appropriately designed, implemented and effectively operated to reduce the risk of the firm being used in connection with ML or TF. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management has a responsibility to ensure that the firm’s policies, controls and procedures are appropriately designed and implemented"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 18, the ML Regulations require firms to take appropriate steps to identify and assess the ML and TF risks to which their business is subject, taking into account information made available by the FCA and risk factors relating to customers, countries or geographic areas of operation, products, services, transactions and delivery channels. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms to take appropriate steps to identify and assess the risks of money laundering and terrorist financing"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In considering what steps are appropriate to identify and assess risks, a firm must take into account the size and nature of its business. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must take into account the size and nature of its business"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Regulation 16(2), the firm's risk assessment should be informed by relevant findings in the National Risk Assessment. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The assessment should be informed by relevant findings in the National Risk Assessment."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: A firm must take a proportionate, risk-based approach to the risk of being used for money laundering or terrorist financing, as senior management in financial firms is accustomed to doing for other aspects of its business. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm must take such an approach to the risk of being used for the purposes of money laundering"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under a risk-based approach firms start from the premise that most customers are not money launderers or terrorist financiers, but they must have systems in place to highlight customers who, on criteria established by the firm, may present a higher risk. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must have systems in place to highlight those customers who, on criteria established by the firm"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Senior management must be fully engaged in decision-making and must take ownership of the risk-based approach, since they will be held accountable if it is inadequate. Senior management approval is specifically required for the firm's policies, controls and procedures for mitigating and managing the ML/TF risks identified in its risk assessment. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management must be fully engaged in the decision-making processes, and must take ownership of the risk-based approach"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The firm's ML/TF policies, controls and procedures must be kept up to date and should reflect changes in the ML and/or TF risks the firm faces. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such policies, controls and procedures must be kept up- to-date"</sub>

## International AML/CTF standards and legislation

- **descriptive / context**: FATF issues the International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation (the FATF Recommendations), which set minimum standards so that AML/CTF efforts are consistent internationally. FATF also maintains an International Co-operation Review Group (ICRG) and publishes a regularly updated list of countries and jurisdictions with strategic deficiencies. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "aimed at setting minimum standards for action in different countries, to ensure that AML/CTF efforts are consistent internationally"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An EU Directive on money laundering prevention was implemented in the UK mainly through the Money Laundering Regulations 2017, to implement the revised FATF Standards published in February 2012. From 1 January 2021 the UK has had its own standalone list. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "From 1 January 2021, the UK has had its own standalone list."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In terms of the ML Regulations, a high-risk third country is a country named on either of the FATF lists identifying high-risk third countries with strategic deficiencies in AML, counter terrorist financing or counter proliferation financing: High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action, and Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a high-risk third country is a country named on either of the lists published by FATF"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The extent of ML/TF risk associated with individual countries may also be assessed through other sources, for example HM Treasury Sanctions, FATF high-risk and non-cooperative jurisdictions, FATF Mutual Evaluation Reports, the Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index, the FCDO Human Rights Report, and Department for International Trade overseas country risks. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also be assessed through other sources, for example, HM Treasury"</sub>

## Introduction

- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 1.8 says senior management must know how much money laundering risk the firm faces and judge whether the firm is equipped to mitigate it effectively. This implies that decisions on entering into or keeping high-risk business relationships must be escalated to senior management. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this implies that decisions on entering or maintaining high-risk business relationships must be escalated to senior management"</sub>

## International AML/CTF standards and legislation

- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Recommendations, the Basel paper on sound management of ML/TF risks, IAIS Guidance Paper 5 and the IOSCO Principles paper encourage national supervisors to require firms to follow specific due diligence procedures on customers, and explicitly envisage a risk-based approach to AML/CTF. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These organisations explicitly envisage a risk-based approach to AML/CTF being followed by firms."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some international groupings, such as Transparency International and the Wolfsberg Group, publish material that may be useful as context and background in informing firms' approaches to AML/TF. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some international groupings, official or informal, publish material that may be useful as context and background"</sub>

## The UK legal and regulatory framework

- **descriptive / context**: The UK approach to fighting financial crime is a public-private partnership. Objectives are specified in legislation and FCA Rules, but there is usually no prescription on how they must be met, leaving UK businesses a degree of discretion. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The UK approach to fighting financial crime is based on a partnership between the public and private sectors."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Key elements of the UK AML/CTF framework are POCA 2002 (as amended), the Terrorism Act 2000 (as amended by the Anti-terrorism, Crime and Security Act 2001), the Counter-Terrorism (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (as amended), Counter-terrorism Act 2008 Schedule 7, HM Treasury Sanctions Notices, Guidance and News Releases, and the FCA Handbook. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Counter-Terrorism (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019"</sub>

## International AML/CTF standards and legislation

- **descriptive / context**: The United Nations and the EU maintain sanctions that deny a range of named individuals and organisations, and nationals of certain countries, access to the financial services sector. In the UK, HM Treasury, through the Office for Financial Sanctions Implementation, issues a sanctions notice whenever a new name is added to the list or details are amended. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "HM Treasury (through the Office for Financial Sanctions Implementation) issues sanctions notices whenever a new name is added"</sub>

## The UK legal and regulatory framework

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG provides implementation guidance for the financial services industry. Multiple UK bodies share responsibility for combating ML and TF (set out in Appendix I). HMRC, as supervisory and law enforcement authority, may use the UK AML regime to gather information for tax purposes. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "HMRC may use the UK anti-money laundering regime to gather information for tax purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The ML Regulations apply to a range of specified firms undertaking business in the UK. POCA and the Terrorism Act apply UK AML/CTF legislation to any dealings in criminal or terrorist property, and the UK financial sanctions regime imposes additional obligations, so firms need to think in terms of involvement with any crime or terrorist activity. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms need to think in terms of involvement with any crime or terrorist activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Action Plan for anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist finance (April 2016) sets four priority areas. The first is a stronger partnership with the private sector: law enforcement, supervisors and the private sector working together to target resources at the highest ML/TF risks, new means of information sharing to strengthen the risk-based approach, and a collaborative approach to preventing individuals becoming involved in money laundering. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A stronger partnership with the private sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The second Action Plan priority is enhancing the law enforcement response, through new capabilities and legal powers to build the intelligence picture, disrupt money launderers and terrorists, recover criminal proceeds and protect the integrity of the UK financial system. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Enhancing the law enforcement response New capabilities and new legal powers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should be aware of the Home Office's Serious and Organised Crime Strategy, issued in October 2013. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should be aware of the Home Office’s Serious and Organised Crime Strategy, issued in October 2013"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The third Action Plan priority is improving the effectiveness of the supervisory regime: investigating the current regime and considering radical options so that a risk-based approach is fully embedded, starting with understanding specific risks and spotting criminal activity rather than tick-box compliance. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "rather than a focus on tick-box compliance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The fourth Action Plan priority is increasing international reach: increasing the international reach of law enforcement and international information sharing to tackle ML and TF threats. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Increase the international reach of law enforcement agencies and international information sharing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: HM Treasury and the Home Office jointly published the first UK national risk assessment (NRA) of ML and TF in October 2015, the second in 2017, and the third in 2020. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the first UK national risk assessment (NRA) of money laundering and terrorist financing in October 2015"</sub>

## General legal and regulatory obligations and expectations

- **descriptive / context**: Obligations are placed on all firms subject to the ML Regulations, POCA, the Terrorism Act and the UK financial sanctions regimes, and fulfilling them falls to senior management as a whole (summarised in Appendix II). For FCA-regulated firms, senior management's specific responsibilities and the FCA's expectations are set out in FSMA and the FCA Handbook. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fulfilling these responsibilities falls to senior management as a whole"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: After thematic and other reviews the FCA may clarify its expectations of firms in the relevant areas, and firms should be aware of those expectations. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCA may clarify their expectations of firms in the relevant areas; firms should be aware of these expectations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FCA's Financial Crime Guide (FCG) gives firms practical assistance and information on the FCA's expectations of actions they can take to counter the risk of being used to further financial crime, and it consolidates examples of good and poor practice published with FCA thematic reviews. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which provides practical assistance and information for firms on FCA’s expectations of actions they can take"</sub>

## Relationship between money laundering, terrorist financing and other financial crime

- **mandatory / requirement**: Firms must consider how best to assess and manage their overall exposure to financial crime. Fraud, market abuse, ML and TF prevention and financial sanctions obligations need not be addressed by a single function, but there must be close liaison between those responsible for each. The guidance relates only to preventing ML and TF. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must consider how best they should assess and manage their overall exposure to financial crime"</sub>

## Obligations on all firms

- **mandatory / requirement**: The ML Regulations place a general obligation on firms in scope to establish adequate and appropriate policies, controls and procedures to prevent ML and TF. Failure to comply risks a prison term of up to two years and/or a fine, and may also attract regulatory sanction depending on the nature and extent of the failure. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to comply with this obligation risks a prison term of up to two years and/or a fine."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(1)(a), where appropriate with regard to the size and nature of its business, a firm must appoint a board member (or equivalent) or senior manager as the officer responsible for the firm's compliance with the ML Regulations. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as the officer responsible for the firm’s compliance with the ML Regulations"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 92, where a firm is a body corporate, an officer (director, manager, secretary, chief executive, committee of management member, or person purporting to act as such) who consents or connives in an offence by the firm, or to whose lack of supervision or control the offence is attributable, commits a criminal offence and may be prosecuted. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who consents or connives in the commission of an offence by the firm"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where the firm is a partnership, a partner who consents to or connives in offences under the ML Regulations, or where the offence is attributable to his neglect, is individually liable to prosecution; a similar rule applies to officers of unincorporated associations. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a partner who consents to or connives in the commission of offences under the ML Regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The ML offences under POCA and the obligation to report knowledge or suspicion of possible money laundering affect members of staff of firms, as do similar offences and obligations under the Terrorism Act. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "affect members of staff of firms"</sub>

## Obligations on FCA-regulated firms subject to the Senior Managers and Certification Regime

- **permissive / requirement**: The firm may allocate this prescribed responsibility to the MLRO but is not required to. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm may allocate this responsibility to the MLRO, but does not have to"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: If the prescribed responsibility is allocated to an SMF Manager other than the MLRO, it includes responsibility for supervising the MLRO. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this prescribed responsibility includes responsibility for supervision of the MLRO"</sub>

## Obligations on all FCA-regulated firms

- **descriptive / context**: A number of FCA-regulated financial sector firms are 'common platform' firms because they are subject to both MiFID and the Capital Requirements Directive. FCA rules on systems and controls against financial crime are in two parts: SYSC 6.1.1 applies to most firms and SYSC 3.2.6 applies to non-common platform firms. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those which apply to most firms, set out in SYSC 6.1.1"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The JMLSG guidance is written in terms of the requirements of SYSC 6.1.1. Non common platform firms should follow it, interpreting it as referring, as necessary, to the relevant parts of SYSC 3.2.6. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "non common platform firms should follow this guidance, interpreting it as referring as necessary"</sub>

## Obligations on all firms

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under the ML Regulations (Regulation 24), firms must take appropriate measures to ensure that employees and agents are made aware of the law relating to ML and TF (and data protection) and are regularly given training in recognising and dealing with transactions and activities that may be related to ML or TF. Chapter 7 covers staff training. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are regularly given training in how to recognise and deal with transactions and other activities"</sub>

## Obligations on FCA-regulated firms subject to the Senior Managers and Certification Regime

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under SMCR (SYSC 4.5.4 R, SYSC 25.2.1 R), deposit takers, insurers and investment banks are required to maintain a Management Responsibilities Map allocating prescribed responsibilities to individual SMF Managers. The map of a small and non-complex firm is likely to be simple and short, possibly no more than a single sheet. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "required to maintain a Management Responsibilities Map, which allocates prescribed responsibilities to individual SMF Managers"</sub>

## Obligations on all FCA-regulated firms

- **descriptive / context**: FSMA makes the prevention of financial crime integral to the FCA's functions and objectives. The FCA is concerned that regulated firms and their senior management are aware of the risk of their businesses being used for financial crime and take appropriate measures to prevent it, facilitate its detection and monitor its incidence. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FSMA makes the prevention of financial crime integral to the discharge of the FCA’s functions and fulfilment of its objectives"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Senior management has operational responsibility for ensuring that the firm has appropriate systems and controls in place to combat financial crime. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management has operational responsibility for ensuring that the firm has appropriate systems and controls"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In FCA-regulated firms (with an exception for general insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries under paragraph 1.49), a director or senior manager must be allocated overall responsibility for establishing and maintaining the firm's anti-money laundering systems and controls (SYSC 6.3.8 R). (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a director or senior manager must be allocated overall responsibility for the establishment and maintenance"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In FCA-regulated firms (with the same general insurance and mortgage intermediary exception), an individual, the MLRO, must be allocated responsibility for oversight of compliance with the FCA's Rules on systems and controls against money laundering. The FCA requires the MLRO to have sufficient seniority to carry out the function effectively; where the MLRO is not part of senior management, they are directly responsible to someone who is. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FCA requires the MLRO to have a sufficient level of seniority within the firm"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Senior management of FCA-regulated firms must appoint an appropriately qualified senior member of staff as MLRO and must provide direction to, and oversight of, the firm's AML/CTF strategy. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must appoint an appropriately qualified senior member of the firm’s staff as the MLRO"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Although the FCA rule requires overall responsibility for AML/CTF systems and controls to be allocated to a single individual, this may be difficult in practice, especially in larger firms; firms may therefore allocate this responsibility among a number of individuals, provided the division of responsibilities is clear. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms may allocate this responsibility among a number of individuals, provided the division of responsibilities is clear"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The relationship between the MLRO and the director/senior manager with overall AML/CTF responsibility (where they are different people) is one of the keys to an effective regime. It is important that it is clearly defined and documented so each knows the extent of their own and the other's role and day-to-day responsibilities. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important that this relationship is clearly defined and documented"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Where the firm must appoint a board member or senior manager as the officer responsible for ML Regulations compliance, it is important that this individual, the MLRO and the director/senior manager with overall AML/CTF responsibility (where different) are all clear as to each other's responsibilities. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are all clear as to the responsibilities of each"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: At least once in each calendar year, an FCA-regulated firm should commission a report from its MLRO on the operation and effectiveness of its systems and controls to combat money laundering (SYSC 6.3.7(2) G). Senior management should determine the depth and frequency of information it needs, and the MLRO may report more frequently than annually as circumstances dictate. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At least once in each calendar year, an FCA-regulated firm should commission a report from its MLRO"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: When senior management receives reports from the firm's MLRO, it should consider them and take any necessary action, in a timely manner, to remedy any deficiencies identified. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should consider them and take any necessary action to remedy any deficiencies identified in a timely manner"</sub>
