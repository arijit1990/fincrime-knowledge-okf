## 5.3.138A

## Partnerships and unincorporated bodies

- **descriptive / definition**: Regulation 5(3), paragraph 5.3.178: the beneficial owner of a partnership other than an LLP is any individual who ultimately is entitled to, or controls (directly or indirectly), over 25% of the partnership's capital or profits or over 25% of its voting rights, or who otherwise has ultimate control over its management. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "more than a 25% share of the capital or profits of the partnership"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.180: because partnerships and unincorporated businesses vary widely in size, reputation and number of partners or principals, firms need to judge where a given partnership or business sits on the risk spectrum. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms need to make an assessment of where a particular partnership or business lies on the associated risk spectrum"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.181: the firm's obligation is to verify the customer's identity using evidence from a reliable source independent of the customer. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm’s obligation is to verify the identity of the customer using evidence from a reliable source"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.184: for identification, Scottish partnerships and LLPs should be handled as corporate customers. For limited partnerships, the general partners' identity should be verified and other partners treated as beneficial owners. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Scottish partnerships and limited liability partnerships should be treated as corporate customers"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.185: firms should take suitable steps to be reasonably satisfied that the person they deal with is properly authorised by the customer. (p. 112)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "5.3.185 Firms should take appropriate steps to be reasonably satisfied that the person the firm is dealing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: If no individual owns or controls more than 25% of the capital, profits or voting rights of the partnership, firms should use judgement in determining whether an individual owning or controlling a lower percentage exercises effective control. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should use judgement in determining whether an individual owning or controlling a lower percentage exercises effective control"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.179: the firm should obtain as standard evidence for the partnership or unincorporated association its full name, business address, the names of all partners/principals who exercise control over management, and the names of individuals who own or control over 25% of its capital or profit, or of its voting rights. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm should obtain the following standard evidence in relation to the partnership or unincorporated association"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.181: the firm's obligation is to verify the customer's identity using evidence from a reliable source independent of the customer. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "verify the identity of the customer using evidence from a reliable source, independent of the customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Where partnerships or unincorporated businesses are well known, reputable, with long histories and substantial public information about them and their principals and controllers, confirmation of membership of a relevant professional or trade association is likely to provide such reliable and independent evidence; this does not obviate the need to verify the identity of the partnership's beneficial owners. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confirmation of the customer’s membership of a relevant professional or trade association"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.182: the firm must take reasonable measures to verify the identity of the individual beneficial owners who own or control more than 25% of the partnership's capital, profit or voting rights, or who otherwise exercise control over its management (see 5.3.8 to 5.3.16). Paragraph 5.3.183 is intentionally left blank. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must take reasonable measures to verify the identity of those individuals"</sub>

## Other considerations

- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.188: where ML/TF risk is at its lowest, the firm may be able to use source of funds as evidence of the customer's identity, following paragraphs 5.3.102 to 5.3.106. Beneficial owners, where they exist, must still be identity-verified. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm may be able to use the source of funds as evidence of the customer’s identity"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.189: where there is less transparency, less industry profile or less independent means of verifying the client entity, firms should consider the ML/TF risk it presents and so how many precautions beyond the standard evidence are needed. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the extent to which, in addition to the standard evidence, additional precautions should be taken"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.190: it is important to know and understand the entity's associations with other jurisdictions and the individuals who may influence its operations, such as through political connections. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important to know and understand any associations the entity may have with other jurisdictions"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.190: a visit to the place of business may help confirm that the business exists and what it does. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A visit to the place of business may be helpful to confirm the existence and activities of the business"</sub>

## Public sector bodies, governments, state-owned companies and supranationals (other than sovereign wealth funds)

- **permissive / recommendation**: Regulation 37(3), paragraph 5.3.192: for UK or overseas governments (in jurisdictions the firm has judged low risk) or their representatives, supranational organisations, government departments, state-owned companies and local authorities, identification and verification may be adapted to the customer's circumstances, reflecting the firm's view of ML/TF risk. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the approach to identification and verification may be tailored to the circumstances of the customer"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.192: where the firm decides the relationship carries a low degree of ML/TF risk, SDD measures may be applied. Public sector bodies include state-supported schools, colleges, universities and NHS trusts. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where the firm determines that the business relationship presents a low degree of risk of ML/TF"</sub>

## Partnerships and unincorporated bodies

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.186: some consideration should be given as to whether documents relied upon are forged; if in a foreign language, appropriate steps should be taken to be reasonably satisfied that they evidence the customer's identity. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "5.3.186 Some consideration should be given as to whether documents relied upon are forged."</sub>

## Other considerations

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.187: most partnerships and unincorporated businesses are smaller, less transparent and less well known, and are not subject to the same accountability requirements as, for example, companies listed on a regulated market. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most partnerships and unincorporated businesses are smaller, less transparent, and less well known entities"</sub>

## Principals and owners

- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.191: following its ML/TF risk assessment of the entity, the firm may decide to verify the identity of one or more of the partners/owners as customers; in that event verification is likely to be appropriate for partners/owners who have authority to operate an account or give instructions on the use or transfer of funds or assets. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm may decide to verify the identity of one or more of the partners/owners as customers"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Other partners/owners must be verified as beneficial owners, following the guidance in paragraphs 5.3.8 to 5.3.16. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "other partners/owners must be verified as beneficial owners"</sub>
