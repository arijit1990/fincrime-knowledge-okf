  <sub>anchor: "the use of non-resident accounts, companies or structures in"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.33 lists as a potentially unusual situation a transaction requested by the customer, or its size or pattern, that without reasonable explanation is out of the ordinary range of services normally requested or inconsistent with the firm's experience of that customer. (p. 192)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is, without reasonable explanation, out of the ordinary range of services normally requested"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.33 lists as potentially unusual situations dealing with customers not normally expected in that part of the business, and transfers to and from high-risk jurisdictions, without reasonable explanation, that are not consistent with the customer's declared foreign business dealings or interests. (p. 192)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dealing with customers not normally expected in that part of the"</sub>

## Training in the firm's procedures

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 7.31 states that relevant employees should also learn about the particular circumstances of customers who pose a higher ML/TF/proliferation financing risk or who are financially excluded, and how best to recognise them; training should cover how to verify identity in such cases, what extra steps to take and/or what local checks are possible. (p. 192)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Relevant employees should also be made aware of the particular circumstances of customers who present a higher risk"</sub>

## Staff alertness to specific situations

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.33 lists as potentially unusual situations a series of transactions structured just below a regulatory threshold, and a customer who enters a business relationship and uses it for a single transaction or only a very short period. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where a series of transactions are structured just below a regulatory"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.33 lists as potentially unusual situations unnecessary routing of funds through third party accounts, and unusual investment transactions without an apparently discernible profitable motive. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unusual investment transactions without an apparently discernible"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.34 lists customer identification questions that may raise concerns, including whether the customer has refused or appeared particularly reluctant, without reasonable explanation, to provide the information requested. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Has the customer refused, or appeared particularly reluctant, to"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.34 asks whether staff understand the legal and corporate structure of a client entity, its ownership and control, and whether the structure makes sense given the purpose of the transaction or business relationship. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Do you understand the legal and corporate structure of the client"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.34 asks whether there are inconsistencies between the information provided and what would be expected given the customer's location, whether the area of residence is consistent with other profile details such as employment, and whether an address appears vague or unusual (for example an accommodation agency, a professional 'registered office' or a trading address). (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Does an address appear vague or unusual"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.34 asks whether it makes sense for the customer to open the account or relationship in the requested jurisdiction, whether the information provided is consistent with the services sought, and whether supporting documentation adds validity to the other information. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Does it make sense for the customer to be opening the account or"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.34 asks whether the customer's information is consistent across other banking or financial relationships with the firm, whether the client wants to conclude arrangements unusually urgently against an unsatisfactorily explained promise to provide information later, and whether the customer has suggested changes to a proposed arrangement to avoid providing certain information. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Has the customer suggested changes to a proposed arrangement in"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.35 also lists large numbers of electronic transfers into and out of the account, significant/unusual/inconsistent deposits by third parties, and reactivation of dormant account(s). (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "large numbers of electronic transfers into and out of the account"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 7.36 states that staff awareness and training programmes may also include the nature of terrorism funding and terrorist activity so staff are alert to customer transactions or activities that might be terrorist-related. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Staff awareness and training programmes may also include the nature of terrorism funding and terrorist activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.37 lists examples of activity that, assessed in the context of the overall risk presented by the customer, might suggest potential terrorist activity: round sum deposits followed by like-amount wire transfers; frequent international ATM activity; no known source of income. (p. 193)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "round sum deposits, followed by like-amount wire transfers"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: JMLSG paragraph 7.37 further lists, as examples suggesting potential terrorist activity, use of wire transfers and the internet to move funds to and from high-risk countries and geographic locations, frequent address changes, purchases of military items or technology, and media reports on suspected or arrested terrorists or groups. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "purchases of military items or technology; and"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 7.38 points to FATF's regular typologies publications and to FATF's Guidance for Financial Institutions in Detecting Terrorist Financing issued in April 2002, which contains an in-depth analysis of methods used to finance terrorism and the financial activities constituting potential indicators. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF’s Guidance for Financial Institutions in Detecting Terrorist Financing issued in April 2002"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 7.39 states that illustrations, based on real cases, of how individuals and organisations might raise funds and use financial sector products for money laundering or terrorist financing are available on the FATF website. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Illustrations, based on real cases, of how individuals and organisations might raise funds"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: JMLSG paragraph 7.40 states the NCA publishes material such as threat assessments and risk profiles of which firms may wish to make their staff aware, and that information on its website could usefully be incorporated into firms' training materials. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "of which firms may wish to make their staff aware"</sub>

## Training methods and assessment

- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 7.42 states there is no single solution for delivering training and a mix of techniques may be appropriate; on-line learning can often be adequate for many employees but not for some classes of employees, focused classroom training for higher risk or minority areas can be more effective, and continually re-showing the same video may produce diminishing returns. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no single solution when determining how to deliver training"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JMLSG paragraph 7.43 states procedures manuals, paper or intranet based, are useful in raising staff awareness and supplementing dedicated training, but their main purpose is ongoing reference and they are not generally written as training material. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their main purpose is to provide ongoing reference"</sub>

## Staff alertness to specific situations

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 7.38 says it is important that staff are suitably informed about the changing behaviour and practices of money launderers and terrorist financiers. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important that staff are appropriately made aware of changing behaviour and practices"</sub>

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 7.41 states that where staff outside the UK process activities relating to UK business operations, they must be told about, and trained to follow, the AML/CTF policies and procedures that apply to the UK operations. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those staff must be made aware of and trained to follow the AML/CTF policies and procedures applicable"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "It is important that any local training and awareness obligations are also met, where relevant."</sub>

## Training methods and assessment

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 7.44 says ongoing training should be provided at suitable intervals to every relevant employee. (p. 194)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ongoing training should be given at appropriate intervals to all relevant employees."</sub>
