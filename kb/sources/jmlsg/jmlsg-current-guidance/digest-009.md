## CONSIDERATIONS IN KEEPING RISK ASSESSMENTS UP TO DATE

- **expected / expectation**: Firms should keep their assessment of ML/TF/PF risk for individual business relationships and occasional transactions, and the underlying factors, under review so the assessment remains up to date and relevant. They should assess information obtained through ongoing monitoring and consider whether it affects the risk assessment. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should keep their assessment of ML/TF/PF risk associated with individual business relationships and occasional transactions"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should ensure they have systems and controls to identify emerging ML/TF/PF risks and can assess and, where appropriate, incorporate them in business-wide and individual risk assessments in a timely manner. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "systems and controls in place to identify emerging ML/TF/PF risks"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Examples of systems and controls firms should put in place to identify emerging risks: processes to review internal information regularly for trends and emerging issues; processes to regularly review relevant sources, in particular media reports relevant to sectors or jurisdictions, law enforcement alerts and reports, changes to terror alerts and sanctions regimes as soon as they occur, and thematic reviews and similar publications by competent authorities. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regularly reviewing law enforcement alerts and reports"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Further examples for identifying emerging risks: processes to capture and review information on risks relating to new products; engagement with industry representatives and competent authorities (round tables, conferences, training) with feedback of findings to relevant staff; and a culture of information sharing within the firm and strong company ethics. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "processes to capture and reviewing information on risks relating to new products"</sub>
- **expected / method**: Examples of controls to keep individual and business-wide risk assessments up to date: setting a date for the next update (for example 1 March every year) so new or emerging risks are included, with a newly emerged or increased risk reflected as soon as possible; and carefully recording issues through the year that could bear on the assessment, such as internal suspicious transaction reports, compliance failures and intelligence from front office staff. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "setting a date at which the next risk assessment update takes place"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As with the original risk assessment, any update to a risk assessment, and any change to the accompanying CDD measures, should be in proportion to the ML/TF/PF risk. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any update of a risk assessment and adjustment of accompanying CDD measures should be proportionate and commensurate"</sub>

## CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE

- **mandatory / requirement**: Chapter 5 core obligations (summary): firms must carry out prescribed CDD measures for all customers not covered by exemptions; must have systems to deal with identification issues for those who cannot produce standard evidence; must take a risk-based approach to EDD given greater ML potential in higher risk cases, specifically PEPs and correspondent relationships; must not deal with some persons/entities; must have specific policies for the financially (and socially) excluded; must not proceed with the business relationship if satisfactory evidence of identity is not obtained; and must have some system for keeping customer information up to date. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If satisfactory evidence of identity is not obtained, the business relationship must not proceed further"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.1: the ML Regulations 2017 (as amended) specify the CDD measures required and their timing, actions required if CDD is not carried out, circumstances in which limited CDD ('Simplified Due Diligence') is permitted, customers and circumstances requiring enhanced due diligence, and provision for reliance on other regulated firms. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The ML Regulations 2017 (as amended) specify CDD measures that are required to be carried out, and the timing"</sub>

## What is customer due diligence?

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.1.5 (Regulation 28(1),(2)): the CDD measures that must be carried out are (a) identifying the customer and verifying identity; (b) identifying the beneficial owner, where relevant, and verifying identity; and (c) assessing, and where appropriate obtaining information on, the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship or transaction. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "assessing, and where appropriate obtaining information on, the"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.7: working out who is a beneficial owner may not be straightforward, and different rules apply to different forms of entity (see paragraphs 5.3.8ff). (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Working out who is a beneficial owner may not be a straightforward matter"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.1.8 (Regulations 33-38): for some relationships determined by the firm to present a low degree of ML/TF risk, simplified due diligence (SDD) may be applied; in higher risk situations, and specifically for PEPs or correspondent relationships with third country respondents, enhanced due diligence (EDD) measures must be applied on a risk sensitive basis. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "simplified due diligence (SDD) may be applied"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.10: the CDD and monitoring obligations under legislation and regulation are designed to make it more difficult for the financial services industry to be used for money laundering or terrorist financing. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designed to make it more difficult for the financial services industry to be used for money laundering or terrorist financing"</sub>

## CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.2: Schedule 7 to the Counter-terrorism Act 2008 allows HM Treasury to direct firms, in particular circumstances, to carry out enhanced CDD and monitoring. Details of any such directions are published on the HM Treasury website. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Schedule 7 to the Counter-terrorism Act 2008 gives HM Treasury power to require firms"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.1.4 (Regulation 28(12),(16)): the scope of a firm's CDD and ongoing monitoring has to be set on a risk-sensitive basis, according to the type of customer, business relationship, product or transaction. The firm must be able to show its supervisory authority that this scope is appropriate given the ML/TF risks. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must determine the extent of their CDD measures and ongoing monitoring on a risk-sensitive basis"</sub>

## What is customer due diligence?

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.1.6 (Regulation 28(4)(c), (5), (3A)): when the customer or beneficial owner is a legal person other than a company listed on a regulated market, or is a trust, company, foundation or similar legal arrangement, the firm must take reasonable steps to understand how that person or arrangement is owned and controlled. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms must take reasonable measures to understand the ownership and control structure"</sub>

## What is ongoing monitoring?

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.1.11: firms need to know who their customers are so as to guard against fraud, including impersonation fraud, and against the risk of committing money laundering or terrorist financing offences under POCA and the Terrorism Act. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms also need to know who their customers are to guard against fraud, including impersonation fraud"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.1.9 (Regulation 28(11)): firms must conduct ongoing monitoring of the business relationship with customers (section 5.7), including scrutiny of transactions throughout the relationship and keeping CDD information up to date. This is a separate but related obligation from applying CDD measures. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must conduct ongoing monitoring of the business relationship with their customers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.12 (Criminal Finances Act): tax evasion is a predicate offence leading to money laundering, and failing to report knowledge or suspicions relating to it is an offence committed by a firm. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Tax evasion is a predicate offence leading to money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.13: firms carry out CDD and monitoring for two broad reasons: to be reasonably satisfied, at the time of due diligence, that customers are who they say they are, whether they act on behalf of another, and that there is no legal barrier (for example government sanctions) to providing the product or service; and to enable the firm to assist law enforcement by providing available information on customers or activities under investigation. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to enable the firm to assist law enforcement, by providing available information on customers or activities being investigated"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.1.14: it may often be appropriate for the firm to know more about the customer than identity; for example it will often need to know the nature of the customer's business or activities to assess whether transactions are consistent with it. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it will, for example, often need to be aware of the nature of the customer’s business or activities"</sub>

## Other material, pointing to good practice

- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.1.15: FATF, the Basel Committee, IAIS and IOSCO have issued recommendations on identifying customers; FATF has issued guidance on high level principles on the risk-based approach. The Basel paper (banks), IAIS Guidance Paper 5 (insurance) and IOSCO Principles paper (securities) have principles worth considering by providers of other financial services. Firms are encouraged to use these bodies' websites to keep up to date; the Wolfsberg Group has also issued relevant material. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their principles are worth considering by providers of other forms of financial services"</sub>

## 5.2 Timing of, and non-compliance with, CDD measures

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.1 (Regulation 27(1)): a firm must apply CDD measures when it establishes a business relationship; carries out an occasional transaction; suspects money laundering or terrorist financing; or doubts the veracity of documents or information previously obtained for identification or verification. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm must apply CDD measures when it does any of the following"</sub>

## Timing of verification

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.2 (Regulation 30(2)), general rule: subject to exceptions, verification of the identity of the customer and, where applicable, the beneficial owner must take place before the establishment of a business relationship or the carrying out of a transaction. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must, subject to the exceptions referred to below, take place before the establishment of a business relationship"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.3 (Regulation 30(3)): verification may be completed during the establishment of a business relationship if (a) necessary not to interrupt the normal conduct of business and (b) there is little risk of ML/TF occurring, provided verification is completed as soon as practicable after contact is first established. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be completed during the establishment of a business relationship if (a) this is necessary not to interrupt the normal conduct of business"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.4 (Regulation 30(4),(5)): verification of a customer (or beneficial owner) opening an account may take place after the account (including one permitting transactions in transferable securities) has been opened, provided adequate safeguards ensure no transactions are carried out by or on behalf of the customer before verification is complete. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provided that there are adequate safeguards in place to ensure that no transactions are carried out by or on behalf of the customer before verification has been completed"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.5 (Regulation 30(6),(7)): where CDD is required for a trust, a legal entity (other than a body corporate) or a legal arrangement (other than a trust) whose beneficiaries are designated as a class or by reference to particular characteristics, the firm must establish and verify the beneficiary's identity before any payment is made to the beneficiary or before the beneficiary exercises vested rights. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm must establish and verify the identity of the beneficiary before"</sub>

## Requirement to cease transactions, etc

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.6 (Regulation 31(1)): where a firm is unable to apply CDD measures, it must not carry out a transaction through a bank account with or on behalf of the customer; must not establish a business relationship or carry out a transaction with the customer otherwise than through a bank account; must terminate any existing business relationship; and must consider whether it ought to make a report to the NCA under POCA and the Terrorism Act. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where a firm is unable to apply CDD measures in relation to a customer, the firm"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.8 (Regulation 31(1),(2)): if the firm concludes circumstances give reasonable grounds for knowledge or suspicion of ML or TF, a report must be made to the NCA (Chapter 6). The firm must then retain the funds until consent has been given to return them to the source from which they came. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm must then retain the funds until consent has been given to return the funds to the source from which they came"</sub>

## Electronic transfer of funds

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.2.10: to implement FATF Recommendation 16, the EU adopted Regulation 2015/847 (in force 26 June 2017, directly applicable in all member states), requiring payment service providers (PSPs) to include certain information in electronic funds transfers and ensure it is verified. The core requirement is that the payer's name, address and account number, and the payee's name and payment account number, are included, with a number of permitted exemptions, concessions and variations. Guidance is in Part III, Specialist Guidance 1: Wire transfers. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "payer's name, address and account number, and the name and payment account number of the payee, are included in the transfer"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.2.11 definitions under Regulation 2015/847: a 'payer' holds a payment account and allows a transfer from it (or, with no payment account, gives a transfer of funds order); a 'payee' is the intended recipient; a 'payment service provider' is a natural or legal person providing transfer of funds services; an 'intermediary payment service provider' is a PSP that is not the PSP of the payer or payee and receives and transmits a transfer on behalf of those PSPs or another intermediary PSP. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "‘Payee’ means a person that is the intended recipient of the transfer of funds"</sub>

## Requirement to cease transactions, etc

- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.2.7: firms should always consider whether being unable to apply CDD is down to the customer lacking the 'right' documents or information. If so, the firm should consider whether there are other ways to be reasonably satisfied of the customer's identity. Either way, it should consider whether anything gives grounds for making a report. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should always consider whether an inability to apply CDD measures is caused by the customer not possessing"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.2.9 (Regulation 31(2)): if the firm concludes there are no grounds for a report, it will need to decide what to do. It may keep the funds while looking for other ways to be reasonably satisfied of the customer's identity, or return the funds to where they came from. Returning funds in this situation forms part of ending the relationship, i.e. closing the account, rather than carrying out a transaction through a bank account. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it will need to decide on the appropriate course of action"</sub>

## Electronic transfer of funds

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.2.12: a financial sector business needs to consider which role it fulfils in a payment chain. A bank or building society effecting an electronic transfer on a customer's direct instructions is a PSP; when using an intermediary PSP it must provide the required information on its customer and payee to the intermediary PSP, including when inputting via an electronic banking product supplied by the intermediary. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a financial sector business needs to consider which role it is fulfilling when it is involved in a payment chain"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.2.13: when a financial sector business passes a transaction through an account in its own name (for example mortgages, documentary credits, insurance claims, financial markets trades), it may reasonably consider itself the payer rather than the PSP; it can input its own name if using an electronic banking product, and nothing in the Regulation prevents including the underlying client's name elsewhere in the transfer. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may reasonably consider itself under the above definitions as the payer, rather than the PSP"</sub>

## 5.3 Application of CDD measures

- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.1 (Regulation 28(1)): applying CDD involves verifying the identity of customers and, where applicable, beneficial owners, and assessing, and if appropriate obtaining information on, the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm is required to verify the identity of customers and, where applicable, beneficial owners"</sub>

## Identification and verification of the customer

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.2 (Regulation 28(2)(a)): the firm identifies the customer by obtaining a range of information; verification consists of verifying some of this information against documents or information from a reliable source independent of the customer. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The verification of the identity consists of the firm verifying some of this information against documents or information obtained from a reliable source"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraphs 5.3.3-5.3.4: 'customer' is not defined in the ML Regulations and is inferred from the definitions of 'business relationship' and 'occasional transaction', context and everyday meaning; for AML/CTF purposes it may be wider than the FCA Glossary definition. In general the customer is the party with whom the relationship is established or for whom the transaction is carried out, but where there are several parties to a transaction not all will necessarily be customers. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for AML/CTF purposes, a ‘customer’ may be wider than the FCA Glossary definition of ‘customer’"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.5 (Regulation 4): a 'business relationship' is a business, professional or commercial relationship between a firm and a customer, connected to the firm's business and expected at the time contact is established to have an element of duration. It need not involve an actual transaction; giving advice may often constitute establishing one. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "expected by the firm at the time when contact is established to have an element of duration"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.6 (Regulations 3(1), 27(1),(2)): an 'occasional transaction' for CDD purposes means a transfer of funds (within article 3.9 of the funds transfer regulation) exceeding EUR 1,000, or a transaction carried out other than in the course of a business relationship (for example a single foreign currency transaction or an isolated instruction to purchase shares) amounting to EUR 15,000 or more, whether in a single operation or several operations that appear to be linked. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "amounting to €15,000 or more, whether the transaction is executed in a single operation or in several operations which appear to be linked"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.7: factors linking transactions are inherent in their characteristics, for example several payments to the same recipient from one or more sources over a short period, or a customer regularly transferring funds to one or more sources. For lower-risk situations not otherwise giving rise to a business relationship, a three-month period for linking transactions might be appropriate, assuming this is not a regular occurrence. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a three-month period for linking transactions might be appropriate"</sub>

## Identification and verification of a beneficial owner

- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 18: 'transfer of funds' means any transaction at least partially carried out by electronic means on behalf of a payer through a PSP to make funds available to a payee through a PSP, irrespective of whether payer and payee are the same person or the PSPs are the same, including credit transfers and direct debits (Regulation (EU) No 260/2012), money remittance (Directive 2007/64/EC), and transfers using a payment card, electronic money instrument, mobile phone or similar digital/IT prepaid or postpaid device. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "‘transfer of funds’ means any transaction at least partially carried out by electronic means on behalf of a payer"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.8 (Regulation 6(9)): a beneficial owner is normally an individual who ultimately owns or controls the customer, or on whose behalf a transaction is carried out. For private individuals, the customer is the beneficial owner unless features of the transaction or surrounding circumstances suggest otherwise. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A beneficial owner is normally an individual who ultimately owns or controls the customer or on whose behalf a transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.8: in the case of private individuals, firms are not required to search proactively for beneficial owners. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no requirement on firms to make proactive searches for beneficial owners in such cases"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.8: firms should make appropriate enquiries where it appears that a private individual customer is not acting on their own behalf. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should make appropriate enquiries where it appears that the customer is not acting on their own behalf"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.9 (Regulation 5(1),(3)): under the ML Regulations, beneficial owners are individuals who own or control more than 25% of a body corporate or partnership, or who otherwise own or control the customer. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "owning or controlling more than 25% of body corporates or partnerships or otherwise owning or controlling the customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.9: such beneficial owners must be identified, and reasonable measures must be taken to verify who they are. The paragraph also cross-refers to 5.3.170. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These individuals must be identified, and reasonable measures must be taken to verify their identities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.10 (Regulation 6(1)): for a trust the beneficial owner is each of the settlor; the trustees; the beneficiaries or, where individuals benefiting have not been determined, the class of persons in whose main interest the trust is set up or operates; and any individual who has control over the trust. Paragraph 5.3.11 (Regulation 6(3)): for a foundation or similar legal arrangement, beneficial owners are those holding equivalent or similar positions. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the class of persons in whose main interest the trust is set up, or operates"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 5.3.12 (Regulation 6(7),(8)): for a legal entity or arrangement not covered by 5.3.8-5.3.10, beneficial owners are any individual who benefits from its property; where beneficiaries are yet to be identified, the class of persons in whose main interest it is set up or operates; and any individual who exercises control over its property. An individual who is the beneficial owner of a body corporate that benefits from or controls the property is regarded as benefiting from or controlling it. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any individual who exercises control over the property of the entity or"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.13: where a private individual customer is acting as a front for another individual who is the beneficial owner, the firm should collect the same information about that beneficial owner that it would collect for a customer. For customers who are not private individuals, see paragraphs 5.3.126 onwards. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm should obtain the same information about that beneficial owner as it would for a customer"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.15: in low-risk situations it may be reasonable for a firm to confirm the beneficial owner's identity from what the customer supplies. This could include information from the customer (including trustees or other representatives whose identities have been verified) about identity, and confirmation that they are known to the customer. It may be given orally or in writing. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may be reasonable for the firm to confirm the beneficial owner’s identity based on information supplied by the customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.15: where information on a beneficial owner is received orally, the firm should write it down. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any information received orally should be recorded in written form by the firm"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.14 (Regulation 28(2)(a),(b),(4)(b),(18)): the identity of a customer or beneficial owner must be verified on the basis of documents or information from a reliable source independent of the customer; documents issued or made available by an official body count as independent even if provided by or on behalf of the person. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be verified on the basis of documents or information obtained from a reliable source which is independent of the customer"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.14: the obligation to verify a beneficial owner's identity is for the firm to take reasonable measures so that it is satisfied it knows who the beneficial owner is. It is up to each firm, in light of the risk, to consider whether to use public records of beneficial owners, ask customers for data, require independent documentary evidence, or obtain the information in some other way. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm to take reasonable measures so that it is satisfied"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.16 (Regulation 6(1)(c)(d)): in some trusts and similar arrangements the beneficial owner may be a class of persons who may benefit; where only a class needs to be identified, it is sufficient for the firm to ascertain and name the scope of the class, and not necessary to identify every individual member. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is sufficient for the firm to ascertain and name the scope of the class"</sub>
