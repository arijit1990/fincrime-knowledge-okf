## Internal controls - specific requirements

- **mandatory / requirement**: FCA-regulated firms' systems and controls (excluding general insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries, see 1.49) are required to cover senior management accountability, including allocating to a director or senior manager overall responsibility for establishing and maintaining effective AML systems and controls, and appointing a person with adequate seniority and experience as MLRO. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including allocation to a director or senior manager of overall responsibility for the establishment and maintenance of effective AML systems"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The systems and controls should also cover appropriate money laundering training so employees understand their legal and regulatory responsibilities and their role in handling criminal property and ML/TF risk management. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate training on money laundering to ensure that employees are aware of, and understand"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The systems and controls should also cover appropriate provision of regular and timely information to senior management relevant to managing the firm's criminal property/ML/TF risks, and appropriate documentation of the firm's risk management policies and risk profile for money laundering, including how it applies those policies. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate provision of regular and timely information to senior management"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The systems and controls should also include appropriate measures to ensure money laundering risk is taken into account in the day-to-day operation of the firm, including in developing new products, taking on new customers and changes in the firm's business profile. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "money laundering risk is taken into account in the day-to-day operation of the firm"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: It is important that the firm's policies, controls and procedures are communicated widely throughout the firm to increase the effectiveness of their implementation. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "communicated widely throughout the firm, to increase the effectiveness of their implementation"</sub>

## Outsourcing and non-UK processing

- **expected / expectation**: Many firms outsource activities, including some systems and controls and/or processing, to agents or providers in the UK or other jurisdictions or to group companies. Involving other entities may give rise to additional risk which must be actively managed, and firms should obtain assurance that outsourcing providers meet the standards or requirements in the guidance. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should obtain assurance that outsourcing providers meet the standards or requirements set out in this Guidance."</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Under Regulation 39(7) and (8), the ML Regulations do not prevent a firm from applying CDD measures through an agent or an outsourcing service provider (but see paragraphs 5.3.51 to 5.3.53), as long as the arrangements between them leave the firm liable for any failure to apply those measures. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide for the firm to remain liable for any failure to apply such measures"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FCA-regulated firms cannot contract out of their regulatory responsibilities and remain responsible for systems and controls for outsourced activities, within the UK or abroad. The delegating firm bears ultimate responsibility, including ensuring that the provider has satisfactory AML/CTF systems, controls and procedures kept up to date with changes in UK requirements. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FCA-regulated firms cannot contract out of their regulatory responsibilities"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where a firm's UK AML/CTF operational activities are done by an outsourced provider or group companies in other jurisdictions (for example call centres abroad, transaction monitoring or CDD processing), they must be subject to AML/CTF policies and procedures equivalent to those applicable to the UK firm. Internal reporting procedures must ensure all suspicions relating to UK-related accounts, transactions or activities are reported to the firm's nominated officer in the UK. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be subject to equivalent AML/CTF policies and procedures that are applicable to the UK firm"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should be aware of local obligations for detecting and preventing financial crime in every jurisdiction to which they outsource functions, and should have procedures to meet local AML/CTF regulations and reporting requirements. Conflicts where meeting local requirements would give a lower standard than the UK should be resolved in favour of the UK requirements. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be resolved in favour of the UK requirements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Outsourcing can increase risk, for example outsourcing to businesses in jurisdictions with less stringent AML/CTF requirements than the UK. All financial services businesses that outsource functions and activities should assess the possible AML/CTF risk of the outsourced functions, record the assessment and monitor the risk on an ongoing basis. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should therefore assess any possible AML/ CTF risk associated with the outsourced functions, record the assessment"</sub>

## Intragroup and group companies

- **mandatory / requirement**: UK firms must ensure that all UK AML/CTF and sanctions obligations are complied with where UK branches or UK subsidiaries have intragroup and external outsourcing arrangements relevant to the UK firm's financial crime systems and controls. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "UK firms must ensure that all UK AML/CTF and sanctions obligations are complied with"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The MLRO/SMF 17 must have due authority to take action where material AML/CTF outsourcing risks arise, whether intragroup or external. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The MLRO/SMF 17 must have due authority to take action where material AML/CTF outsourcing risks arise"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where activities are outsourced intragroup to other group companies or to headquarters (for a UK branch), firms should have risk-based mechanisms such as service level agreements covering: reporting of AML/CTF management information such as KPIs indicating ML/TF risks to monitor compliance with policies and procedures. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should have risk-based mechanisms, such as service level agreements to cover"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such mechanisms could also cover reporting of management information in the form of key risk indicators (KRIs), which may include details of preventative mitigating controls or results of defective quality assurance controls. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "key risk indicators (KRIs) that may include details of preventative mitigating controls"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Such mechanisms could also cover information on AML/CTF training completed by staff of outsourcing service providers inside or outside the UK, information on results of compliance monitoring/testing or relevant audits where risk issues were identified, and escalation protocols to communicate operational events affecting the performance of the outsourced activity. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Provision of information on AML/CTF training completed by staff of the outsourcing service providers"</sub>

## NOMINATED OFFICER/MONEY LAUNDERING REPORTING OFFICER (MLRO)

- **mandatory / requirement**: The Chapter 3 summary states core obligations: a nominated officer must be appointed who must receive and review internal disclosures and is responsible for external reports; FCA approval is required for the MLRO (who may also be the nominated officer) as SMF 17; threshold competence is required; and the MLRO is responsible for oversight of the firm's AML systems and controls. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FCA approval required for MLRO (who may also be the nominated officer), as it is a designated"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Chapter 3 summary says the MLRO should be able to act on his own authority, and senior management are to ensure the MLRO has active senior management support, adequate resources, independence of action, access to information and an obligation to produce an annual report; the MLRO is to ensure continuing competence and monitor the effectiveness of systems and controls. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "MLRO should be able to act on his own authority"</sub>

## Legal obligations

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(3), all firms (other than sole traders) carrying out relevant business under the ML Regulations, whether or not FCA-regulated, must appoint a nominated officer responsible for receiving disclosures under Part 7 of POCA and Part 3 of the Terrorism Act, deciding whether to report them to the NCA, and, if appropriate, making external reports. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must appoint a nominated officer, who is responsible for receiving disclosures under Part 7 of POCA"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: A sole trader with no employees who knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds to know or suspect, that a customer or the person on whose behalf the customer acts is or has been engaged in or attempting ML or TF, must make a report promptly to the NCA. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A sole trader with no employees who knows or suspects"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(1)(a), where appropriate with regard to the size and nature of its business, a firm must appoint a board member (or equivalent) or senior manager as the officer responsible for the firm's compliance with the ML Regulations. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm must appoint a member of its board of directors (or equivalent"</sub>

## Regulatory obligations

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under SYSC 6.3.9(1) R, the MLRO is responsible for oversight of the firm's compliance with the FCA's Rules on systems and controls against money laundering. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The MLRO is responsible for oversight of the firm’s compliance with the FCA’s Rules on systems and controls"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: General insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries are not covered by the ML Regulations, s 330 of POCA, s 21A of the Terrorism Act or the SYSC provisions specific to money laundering, so have no obligation to appoint a nominated officer or MLRO or to allocate AML responsibility to a director or senior manager. They remain subject to general SYSC requirements (appropriate risk management systems and controls) and to ss 337 and 338 of POCA and s 19 of the Terrorism Act. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They therefore are under no obligation to appoint a nominated officer or an MLRO"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FCA-regulated firms must appoint an MLRO, except sole traders with no employees and the firms covered by paragraph 3.2. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is a requirement to appoint an MLRO"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The MLRO's responsibilities under SYSC differ from those of the nominated officer under the ML Regulations, POCA or the Terrorism Act, but in many FCA-regulated firms it is likely that the MLRO and the nominated officer will be the same person. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is likely that the MLRO and the nominated officer will be one and the same person"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where an individual carries out different legal and regulatory functions, it is important that he is aware which role he is acting in at any time. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important that the individual is aware which role he is acting in"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under Regulation 21(8) context, an MLRO should be able to monitor the day-to-day operation of the firm's AML/CTF policies and to respond fully and rapidly to information enquiries from the FCA or law enforcement. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "respond fully and rapidly to enquiries for information made by the FCA or law enforcement"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under FCA Principle 11 of its Principles for Businesses, an FCA-regulated firm must deal with its regulators openly and cooperatively and must appropriately disclose to the FCA anything about the firm of which the FCA would reasonably expect notice. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must deal with its regulators in an open and cooperative way"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The MLRO is personally required to deal with the FCA in the same open and cooperative way, under the FCA's Statement of Principle 4. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The MLRO is personally required to deal with the FCA similarly, under its Statement of Principle 4"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Firms with no legal obligation to do so may nevertheless choose to appoint a nominated officer for administrative convenience and to help staff meet POCA or Terrorism Act obligations; if they do, the nominated officer is subject to the reporting obligations in s 332 of POCA and s 19 of the Terrorism Act. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may nevertheless choose to appoint a nominated officer"</sub>

## Standing of the MLRO

- **mandatory / requirement**: The FCA has designated the MLRO role as a controlled/Senior Management function under s 59 of FSMA, so any person invited to perform it must be individually approved by the FCA, on the firm's application, before performing the function. The FCA expects the MLRO to be based in the UK. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be individually approved by the FCA, on the application of the firm, before performing the function"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Failure by the MLRO to discharge responsibilities imposed by SYSC 6.3.9 R is conduct that does not comply with Statement of Principle 7 for Approved Persons, under which an approved person performing an accountable higher management function must take reasonable steps to ensure the business they are responsible for complies with the relevant regulatory requirements and standards. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is conduct that does not comply with Statement of Principle 7 for Approved Persons"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: In FCA-regulated firms the MLRO oversees all aspects of the firm's AML/CTF activities and is the focal point for all AML activity in the firm. The MLRO must have sufficient seniority (paragraph 1.38). Their job description should clearly set out the extent of their responsibilities and objectives, and the MLRO will need to be involved in establishing the basis on which a risk-based approach is put into practice. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the MLRO is responsible for the oversight of all aspects of the firm’s AML/CTF activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Along with the SMF Manager appointed by the Board, an MLRO supports and co-ordinates senior management focus on managing ML/TF risk in individual business areas, and helps ensure the firm's wider responsibility for forestalling and preventing ML/TF is addressed centrally, allowing a firm-wide view of monitoring and accountability. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allowing a firm-wide view to be taken of the need for monitoring and accountability"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The relationship between the MLRO and the director(s)/senior manager(s) with overall AML/CTF responsibility is one of the keys to an effective regime and it is important that it is clearly defined and documented so each knows their own and the other's role and day-to-day responsibilities. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one of the keys to an effective AML/CTF regime"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Where a board member or senior manager must be appointed as the officer responsible for ML Regulations compliance, it is important that this individual, the MLRO and the director(s)/senior manager(s) with overall AML/CTF responsibility are all clear as to the responsibilities of each. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are all clear as to the responsibilities of each"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under SYSC 6.3.9(2) R, the MLRO must have authority to act independently in carrying out their responsibilities, and must be free to have direct access to the FCA and (where they are the nominated officer) appropriate law enforcement agencies including the NCA, so that suspicious activity is reported to the right quarter as soon as practicable. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The MLRO must have the authority to act independently in carrying out their responsibilities."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The MLRO must be free to liaise with the NCA, on their own authority, on any question of whether to proceed with a transaction in the circumstances. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They must be free to liaise with the NCA, on their own authority"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under SYSC 6.3.9(2) R, senior management must ensure that the MLRO has sufficient resources, including appropriate staff and technology, and arrangements to apply in their temporary absence. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management of the firm must ensure that the MLRO has sufficient resources available to them"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: A firm in a group may appoint as its MLRO an individual who performs that function for another group firm; it may then wish to let the MLRO delegate AML/CTF duties to suitably qualified individuals in the firm. Firms with multiple branches or offices may also wish to permit delegation. In larger firms one or more permanent Deputy MLROs of suitable seniority may be necessary. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may appoint as its MLRO an individual who performs that function for another firm within the group"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Where there are deputies or group arrangements, the principal or group MLRO needs to ensure roles and responsibilities within the group are clearly defined so staff in all business areas know exactly who they must report suspicions to. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "staff of all business areas know exactly who they must report suspicions to"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under SUP 10C.3.13 R, where an MLRO is temporarily unavailable no pre-approval for a deputy is required for temporary cover of up to 12 weeks in any consecutive 12-month period; for longer periods FCA approval must be sought. Smaller firms may prefer to rely on temporary cover rather than appoint a formal deputy. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "no pre-approval for a deputy will be required for temporary cover of up to 12 weeks"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Where AML/CTF tasks are delegated by a firm's MLRO, the FCA expects the MLRO to take ultimate managerial responsibility. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FCA will expect the MLRO to take ultimate managerial responsibility"</sub>

## Internal and external reports

- **mandatory / requirement**: A firm must require anyone in the firm to whom information comes in the course of business giving knowledge or suspicion, or reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting, that a person is engaged in ML or TF to comply with Part 7 of POCA or Part 3 of the Terrorism Act, including making an internal report to the nominated officer as soon as reasonably practicable. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm must require that anyone in the firm to whom information or other matter comes in the course"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Any internal report should be considered by the nominated officer, in light of all other relevant information, to determine whether it gives rise to knowledge or suspicion, or reasonable grounds for knowledge or suspicion, of ML or TF. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in the light of all other relevant information, to determine whether or not the information contained in the report"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A firm is expected to use its existing customer information effectively by making it readily available to its nominated officer. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm is expected to use its existing customer information effectively"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In most cases the nominated officer is likely to need access to the firm's relevant business information before deciding to report, so a firm should take reasonable steps to give access. Relevant information may include the financial circumstances of a customer, beneficial owner or person on whose behalf the customer acts. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm should therefore take reasonable steps to give its nominated officer access to such information."</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Relevant business information for the nominated officer may also include the features of transactions entered into with or for the customer (including, where appropriate, the jurisdiction in which the transaction took place) and the underlying CDD information and copies of source documentation for the customer. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the underlying CDD information, and copies of the actual source"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: The nominated officer may wish to consider the level of identity information held on the customer and any available information on their personal circumstances, and to review other transaction patterns and volumes through accounts in the same name, the length of the business relationship and identification records held. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to review other transaction patterns and volumes through the account or accounts in the same name"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: If the nominated officer (or appointed alternate) concludes that the internal report gives rise to knowledge or suspicion of ML or TF, they must report to the NCA as soon as practicable after making that determination. The decision must be their own and should not be subject to the direction or approval of other parties in the firm. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they must make a report to the NCA as soon as is practicable after they make this determination"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Guidance on reviewing internal reports and reporting to the NCA is set out in Chapter 6. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Guidance on reviewing internal reports, and reporting as appropriate to the NCA, is set out in Chapter 6."</sub>

## National and international findings in respect of countries and jurisdictions

- **expected / expectation**: An MLRO should ensure that the firm obtains, and makes appropriate use of, any government or FATF findings on the approach to money laundering prevention in particular countries or jurisdictions, especially where FATF has found the approach materially deficient. FATF mutual evaluation reports, and IMF and World Bank reports, are sources. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An MLRO should ensure that the firm obtains, and makes appropriate use of, any government or FATF findings"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The ML Regulations state that a high-risk third country is a country named on either FATF list identifying high-risk third countries with strategic deficiencies in AML, counter terrorist financing or counter proliferation financing: High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action, and Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a high-risk third country is a country named on either of the lists below published by FATF"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Countries may also be assessed using publicly available indices such as HM Treasury sanctions, FATF high-risk and non-cooperative jurisdictions, FATF Mutual Evaluation Reports, the Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index, the FCO Human Rights Report and Department for International Trade overseas country risk pages. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries may also be assessed using publicly available indices"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms considering business relations and transactions with individuals and firms located in higher risk jurisdictions (directly or through correspondents), or jurisdictions against which the UK has outstanding advisory notices, should take account of the background to the assessment or the specific recommendations in the advisory notices. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or jurisdictions against which the UK has outstanding advisory notices"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The NCA periodically produces intelligence assessments, forwarded to MLROs of the relevant sectors for internal dissemination only; no NCA material is published through an open source. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No NCA material is published through an open source."</sub>

## Monitoring effectiveness of money laundering controls

- **mandatory / requirement**: A firm is required to carry out regular assessments of the adequacy of its systems and controls to ensure they manage money laundering risk effectively. Oversight of implementing AML/CTF policies and procedures, including the risk-based approach, is primarily the MLRO's responsibility under delegation from senior management, and the MLRO must ensure appropriate monitoring processes and procedures are established and maintained across the firm. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm is required to carry out regular assessments of the adequacy of its systems and controls"</sub>
