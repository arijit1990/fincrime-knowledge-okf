## Obligations on all FCA-regulated firms

- **mandatory / requirement**: All firms, other than credit unions and certain firms with limited permissions and total revenues of less than GBP 5 million, must submit an Annual Financial Crime Report to the FCA for their financial year ending on the latest accounting reference date (see paragraphs 3.46-3.49). (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must submit an Annual Financial Crime Report to the FCA in respect of their financial year"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: JMLSG paragraph 1.46 says all firms, whether or not the FCA regulates them for AML purposes, must devote adequate resources to countering the risk of being used for financial crime. This includes setting up, and monitoring the effectiveness of, systems and controls to prevent ML/TF. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must apply adequate resources to counter the risk that they may be used for"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The level of resource a firm applies should reflect the size, complexity and geographical spread of its customer and product base. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The level of resource should reflect the size, complexity and geographical spread of the firm’s customer and product base"</sub>

## Exemptions from legal and regulatory obligations

- **descriptive / context**: General insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries are also subject to the POCA and Terrorism Act provisions that establish the primary offences. Those offences are not committed if a person's knowledge or suspicion of ML/TF is reported to the NCA and, where relevant, appropriate consent for the transaction or activity is obtained. Some of these firms may also be subject to Schedule 7 of the Counter-Terrorism Act 2008. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These offences are not committed if a person’s knowledge or suspicion of ML/TF is reported to the NCA"</sub>

## Obligations on all FCA-regulated firms

- **mandatory / requirement**: FCA-regulated firms required to appoint an MLRO are specifically required to provide the MLRO with adequate resources. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are specifically required to provide the MLRO with adequate resources"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The role, standing and competence of the MLRO, and the way internal processes for reporting suspicions are designed and implemented, impact directly on the effectiveness of a firm's ML/TF prevention arrangements. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "impact directly on the effectiveness of a firm’s money laundering/terrorist financing prevention arrangements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As well as supervisory expectations, firms should be aware of the FCA's published enforcement findings in relation to individual firms and its actions in response, available on the FCA website. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should be aware of the FCA’s published enforcement findings in relation to individual firms"</sub>

## Exemptions from legal and regulatory obligations

- **descriptive / context**: General insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries are FCA-regulated but are not covered by the ML Regulations or the SYSC provisions specifically on money laundering, so are under no obligation to appoint an MLRO. They remain subject to general SYSC requirements, including having appropriate risk management systems and controls to counter the risk of being used to further financial crime. Guidance for general insurers is in Part II Sector 7A. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not covered by the ML Regulations, or by the provisions of SYSC specifically relating to money laundering"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: For administrative convenience and to help staff meet obligations under POCA or the Terrorism Act, general insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries may choose to appoint a nominated officer. If they do, they become subject to the reporting obligations in s 332 of POCA and s 19 of the Terrorism Act (see Chapter 6). (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "general insurance firms and mortgage intermediaries may choose to appoint a nominated officer"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: E-money issuers and payment institutions are regulated under the Electronic Money Regulations and the Payment Services Regulations rather than FSMA, so are subject to the AML/CTF provisions in legislation but not to most FCA Handbook rules. The FCA has issued guidance on its expectations of their AML/CTF controls; guidance for e-money issuers is also in Part II Sector 3. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are subject to the AML/CTF provisions in legislation, but not to most of the FCA’s Handbook rules"</sub>

## Senior management should adopt a formal policy, and carry out a risk assessment, in relation to financial crime prevention

- **expected / expectation**: Senior management in FCA-regulated firms is responsible for ensuring policies, controls and procedures are appropriately designed, implemented and effectively operated, including taking appropriate steps to identify and assess the ML/TF risks of the business. The assessment should take into account relevant findings in the UK national risk assessments of ML and TF. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This assessment should take into account relevant findings in the UK national risk assessments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: SYSC 6.3.7(3) G says that an FCA-regulated firm should produce appropriate documentation of its risk management policies and risk profile in relation to money laundering, including documentation of how it applies those policies. Paragraph 1.55 notes exceptions for general insurance firms, mortgage intermediaries, e-money issuers and payment institutions. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate documentation of its risk management policies and risk profile in relation to money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A statement of the firm's AML/CTF policy and implementing controls clarifies how senior management intends to discharge its responsibility, provides a framework of direction, identifies named individuals and functions responsible, and sets out how senior management assesses and manages the ML/TF risks faced. Even in a small firm a summary of high-level policy focuses staff on being aware of such risks. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will identify named individuals and functions responsible for implementing particular aspects of the policy"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: A policy statement should be tailored to the circumstances of the firm; using a generic document might reflect adversely on the level of consideration senior management has given to the firm's particular risk profile. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A policy statement should be tailored to the circumstances of the firm."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The policy statement might include guiding principles: an unequivocal statement of the culture and values to be adopted throughout the firm toward preventing financial crime, and a commitment to satisfactorily verifying customers' identities before accepting them. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an unequivocal statement of the culture and values to"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The policy statement might include a commitment to the firm 'knowing its customers' appropriately, at acceptance and throughout the relationship, by taking appropriate steps to verify the customer's identity and business and reasons for seeking the relationship; and a commitment to training staff and making them aware of the law and their obligations, with procedures to implement these requirements. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a commitment to the firm ‘knowing its customers’"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 18, a firm's risk assessment must be documented, kept up to date and made available to the FCA on request. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm’s risk assessment must be documented, kept up-to-date and made available to the FCA on request."</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: The FCA may decide that a particular firm does not need a documented risk assessment where the specific risks inherent in the sector in which the firm operates are clear and understood. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FCA may decide that a documented risk assessment is not required in the case of a particular firm"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The policy statement might include recognition of the importance of staff promptly reporting their suspicions internally. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recognition of the importance of staff promptly"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The policy statement might include a risk mitigation approach section: a summary of the firm's approach to mitigating and managing identified ML/TF risks; allocation of responsibilities to specific persons and functions; a summary of controls and procedures for risk-based identification and monitoring checks; and a summary of monitoring arrangements ensuring policies and procedures are carried out. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a summary of the firm’s approach to mitigating and"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: It is important that the firm's policies, controls and procedures are communicated widely throughout the firm to increase the effectiveness of their implementation. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "communicated widely throughout the firm, to increase the effectiveness of their implementation"</sub>

## Application of group policies outside the UK

- **mandatory / requirement**: A firm that is a parent undertaking must ensure its policies, controls and procedures apply to all subsidiary undertakings and non-UK branches, and must establish and maintain throughout its group policies, controls and procedures for data protection and for sharing information for preventing ML and TF, including information about customers, accounts and transactions. Reporting processes must nevertheless follow local laws and procedures. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must ensure that its policies, controls and procedures apply to all subsidiary undertakings and non-UK branches"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 20(3) and (4), if a subsidiary or branch is established in a third country that does not impose AML/CTF requirements as strict as the UK's, the firm must ensure it applies measures equivalent to those required by the ML Regulations. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm must ensure that such subsidiary undertakings or branches apply measures equivalent to those required by the ML Regulations"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where the law of a third country does not permit equivalent measures, the firm must inform the FCA and take additional measures to handle the ML/TF risk effectively. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm must inform the FCA accordingly, and take additional measures"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 19(6), firms must communicate their policies, controls and procedures for preventing ML/TF-related activities to branches and subsidiary undertakings located outside the UK. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must communicate their policies, controls and procedures established to prevent activities related to money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Suspicions of ML/TF may be required to be reported within the jurisdiction where the suspicion arose and where related transaction records are held, and there may also be a requirement to report to the NCA (see paragraph 6.25). (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there may also be a requirement for a report to be made to the NCA"</sub>

## Extra-territoriality of some overseas jurisdictions' regimes

- **descriptive / context**: Where a firm has a listing, activities or links in certain overseas jurisdictions (through a branch, subsidiary, associate or correspondent relationship), or deals in another jurisdiction's currency, that jurisdiction's AML/CTF and financial sanctions regimes may apply to the firm's non-domestic activities. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or where a firm deals in another jurisdiction’s currency"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Senior management should take advice on the extent to which the firm's activities may be affected by overseas AML/CTF and financial sanctions regimes. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Senior management should take advice on the extent to which the firm’s activities may be affected in this way."</sub>

## INTERNAL CONTROLS

- **mandatory / requirement**: The Chapter 2 summary states the core obligation that firms must establish and maintain adequate and appropriate policies and procedures to forestall and prevent operations relating to money laundering, and that appropriate controls should take account of the risks faced by the firm's business. Actions to keep under review include maintaining appropriate control and oversight over outsourced activities. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms must establish and maintain adequate and appropriate policies and procedures to forestall and prevent operations relating to money laundering"</sub>

## General

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 19(1)(a), firms are required to establish and maintain policies, controls and procedures to mitigate and manage effectively the ML and TF risks identified in their risk assessment. FCA-regulated firms have similar regulatory obligations under SYSC. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms are required to establish and maintain policies, controls and procedures to mitigate and manage effectively the risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Chapter 2 gives guidance on internal controls that help firms meet ML/TF prevention obligations. There are wider general obligations to maintain appropriate records and controls in relation to the business, which the guidance is not intended to replace or interpret. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this guidance is not intended to replace or interpret these wider obligations"</sub>

## Appropriate controls in the context of financial crime prevention

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 19(1)(b), (c) and (2), a firm's policies, controls and procedures must be proportionate to the size and nature of its business, must be approved by senior management and kept under regular review. A firm must maintain a written record of them. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be proportionate with regard to the size and nature of its business, and must be"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The ML Regulations (Regulations 19 and 21(1)) specifically require adequate and appropriate policies, controls and procedures on: internal controls (including, where appropriate, employee screening and an internal audit function); risk management practices; customer due diligence and ongoing monitoring; record keeping; reporting of suspicions; monitoring and managing the effectiveness of and compliance with such policies; and internal communication of them, including staff awareness and training. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "internal controls, including where appropriate employee screening and the appointment of an internal audit function"</sub>

## Internal controls - specific requirements

- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(1), where appropriate with regard to the size and nature of its business, a firm must appoint a board member (or equivalent) or senior manager as the officer responsible for compliance with the ML Regulations. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Appoint a member of its board (or equivalent management body) or"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where appropriate with regard to the size and nature of its business, a firm must screen relevant employees and agents appointed by the firm, both before the appointment is made and at regular intervals during the appointment. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Carry out screening of relevant employees and agents appointed by"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Where appropriate with regard to the size and nature of its business, a firm must establish an independent internal audit function responsible for examining and evaluating the adequacy and effectiveness of the policies, controls and procedures adopted to comply with the ML Regulations, making recommendations on them, and monitoring the firm's compliance with those recommendations. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Establish an independent internal audit function with responsibility"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(3) and (4), an individual in the firm must be appointed as nominated officer, and their identity and any subsequent appointment must be notified to the firm's supervisor. The firm must also notify its supervisor of the name of the board member or senior manager responsible for ML Regulations compliance, and any subsequent appointment. Notifications must be made within 14 days of the appointment. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such notifications must be made within 14 days of the appointment."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Under Regulation 21(2)(a), screening of relevant employees means an assessment of the individual's skills, knowledge and expertise to carry out their functions effectively, and of their conduct and integrity. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the skills, knowledge and expertise of the individual to carry out"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Under Regulation 21(2)(b), a relevant employee is one whose work is relevant to the firm's compliance with any ML Regulations requirement, or is otherwise capable of contributing to identifying or mitigating the firm's ML/TF risks or to preventing or detecting ML/TF in relation to its business. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A relevant employee is one whose work is"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 19(4), a firm's policies, controls and procedures must provide for identification and scrutiny of complex or unusually large transactions, or an unusual pattern of transactions; transactions with no apparent economic or legal purpose; and any other activity the firm regards as particularly likely by its nature to be related to ML or TF. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "complex or unusually large transactions, or an unusual"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The firm's policies, controls and procedures must also specify additional measures, where appropriate, to prevent the use for ML/TF of products or transactions which might favour anonymity. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "products or transactions which might favour anonymity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The firm's policies, controls and procedures must ensure that when new products, new business practices or new technology are adopted, appropriate measures are taken to assess and, if necessary, mitigate any ML/TF risks this may cause. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "new products, new business practices or"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The firm's policies, controls and procedures must require anyone in the firm who knows or suspects (or has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting) ML or TF to report that knowledge or suspicion to the firm's nominated officer. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is required to report such knowledge or suspicion to the firm’s nominated officer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Firms should also have policies, controls and procedures to assess and mitigate the risks arising from remote booking arrangements. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to assess and mitigate the risks arising from remote booking arrangements"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under Regulation 21(8) and (9), firms must establish and maintain systems enabling them to respond fully and rapidly to enquiries from POCA s3 accredited financial investigators, persons acting for the Scottish Ministers as an enforcement authority, or constables, about whether it maintains or has maintained a business relationship with any person during the previous five years, and the nature of that relationship. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "respond fully and rapidly to enquiries from financial investigators accredited under s3 of POCA"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: FCA-regulated firms are required to have systems and controls appropriate to their business, varying with the firm's nature and characteristics, which must include measures for countering the risk that the firm might be used to further financial crime. This requires the firm to use its assessment of the financial crime risks it faces. Financial crime includes handling the proceeds of crime, that is money laundering or terrorist financing. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "‘for countering the risk that the firm might be used to further financial crime’"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: As well as considering the ML Regulations' provisions on what internal controls should comprise, it could be helpful to look to the FCA Handbook, which applies only to FCA-regulated firms but provides helpful commentary on overall systems requirements. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it could be helpful to look to the FCA Handbook, which although only applying to FCA-regulated firms"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The nature and extent of systems and controls depend on factors including the nature, scale and complexity of the business; the diversity of operations, including geographical diversity; the customer, product and activity profile; distribution channels; the volume and size of transactions; and the degree of risk associated with each area of operation. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the diversity of its operations, including geographical diversity"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: An FCA-regulated firm must ensure its systems and controls enable it to identify, assess, monitor and manage money laundering risk, and are comprehensive and proportionate to the nature, scale and complexity of its activities. The guidance notes these obligations are in effect similar to those on all obliged entities under the ML Regulations. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enable it to identity, assess, monitor and manage money laundering risk"</sub>
