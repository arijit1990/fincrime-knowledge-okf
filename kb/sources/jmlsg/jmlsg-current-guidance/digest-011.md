## A – DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.75: if identity is verified from documents, it should be based on either a government-issued document incorporating the customer's full name and photograph and either residential address or date of birth. Government-issued photo documents include a valid passport, valid photocard driving licence (full or provisional), national identity card, firearms certificate or shotgun licence, and identity card issued by the Electoral Office for Northern Ireland. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a government-issued document which incorporates"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.75 (alternative): or a government, court or local authority-issued document without a photograph incorporating the customer's full name, supported by a second document (government-issued, or issued by a judicial authority, public sector body or authority, regulated utility company, or another FCA-regulated UK financial services firm) incorporating full name and either residential address or date of birth. Government-issued documents without a photograph include a valid (old style) full UK driving licence, recent evidence of entitlement to a state or local authority-funded benefit, tax credit, pension, educational or other grant, an instrument of court appointment (such as liquidator or grant of probate), and a current council tax demand letter or statement. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supported by a second document, either government-issued, or issued by a judicial authority, a public sector body or authority"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "other evidence of identity may give the firm reasonable confidence in the customer’s identity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.73: when relying on such other evidence, the firm should weigh it against the risks involved. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm should weigh these against the risks involved"</sub>

(p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should normally only be accepted if it originates from a public sector body or another regulated financial services firm"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.76: examples of other documents supporting a customer's identity are current bank statements or credit/debit card statements issued by a regulated UK financial sector firm, and utility bills. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Examples of other documents to support a customer’s identity include current bank statements"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.76: a document obtained from the internet may be more reliable as a pdf (but see paragraph 5.3.45). ## B – ELECTRONIC EVIDENCE AND DIGITAL IDENTITY

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.79: when using an electronic source or digital identity, firms should ensure they can demonstrate both that the customer exists and that the individual seeking the relationship is in fact that customer (or beneficial owner). (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should ensure that they are able to demonstrate that they have both verified that the customer exists"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraphs 5.3.80-5.3.81: electronic verification may be carried out by the firm directly, using the customer's full name, address and date of birth, or through an organisation considered against the criteria in 5.3.51 and 5.3.52. A firm may approach an electronic source or digital identity provider of its own choosing, or the potential customer may offer access to a source they have already registered with that has accumulated verified evidence and has been considered per those criteria. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Electronic verification may be carried out by the firm either direct, using as its basis the customer’s full name, address and date of birth"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.82: some digital identity or electronic source providers focus on primary identity documents, sometimes using biometric data; others accumulate corroborative information separately available elsewhere. Some information is independent of the customer, while other information is under their control in that their approval is required for inclusion. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Other electronic sources accumulate corroborative information which in principle is separately available elsewhere"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.84: commercial providers display results in various ways (for example number of documents checked, or scoring), and some confirm a predetermined level of assurance or scored verification. Firms should ensure they understand the basis of the system used, to be satisfied that underlying data sources reflect the guidance in 5.3.46-5.3.50 and cumulatively meet an appropriate level of confirmation for the risk assessed in the relationship. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should ensure that they understand the basis of the system they use"</sub>

## C – MITIGATION OF IMPERSONATION RISK

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.87: how far non face-to-face customers must be verified depends on the nature of the product or service requested and the money laundering risk assessed for the customer. In some situations the customer is typically absent, such as many wholesale markets or purchases of some collective investments, and this does not in itself add to the risk of the transaction or activity. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would not in itself increase the risk attaching to the transaction or activity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.87: a firm should take such cases, where the customer is typically not physically present, into account when developing its systems and procedures. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm should take account of such cases in developing their systems and procedures"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.88: additional measures would also include assessing whether the customer is deliberately avoiding face-to-face contact. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Additional measures would also include assessing the possibility that the customer is deliberately avoiding face-to-face contact"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.88: it is important to be clear about the appropriate approach where a customer may be avoiding face-to-face contact. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is therefore important to be clear on the appropriate approach in these circumstances"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.89: where identity is checked electronically, copy documents are used, or the customer is not physically present, a firm should add a verification check that manages impersonation fraud risk by tying the customer directly to the claimed identity. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a firm should apply an additional verification check to manage the risk of impersonation fraud"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.89: for this additional check firms should consider verifying with the customer further aspects of identity held electronically; using biometric data (including facial recognition, for example via a UK government certified digital identity service provider, and liveness checks where appropriate); asking the applicant to confirm a secret code or biometric factor, set up within the identity or supplied once to a verified mobile phone or through a verified bank account; or following the guidance in paragraph 5.3.90. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "verifying with the customer additional aspects of their identity which are held electronically"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 5.3.86: internet applications and transactions may in themselves pose no greater risk than other non face-to-face business such as postal applications, but other factors may together aggravate typical risks: ease of access to the facility regardless of time and location; ease of making multiple fictitious applications without extra cost or risk of detection; the absence of physical documents; and the speed of electronic transactions. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ease of making multiple fictitious applications without incurring extra cost or the risk of detection"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote: with appropriate controls in place, non face-to-face may not pose higher risk. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "With appropriate controls in place non face-to-face may not pose higher risk"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 5.3.90: the additional verification check may be robust anti-fraud checks routinely undertaken, or may include requiring the first payment through an account in the customer's name with a UK or EU regulated credit institution or an assessed low risk jurisdiction; verifying additional aspects of identity (see 5.3.29); and telephone contact before opening the account on a verified home or business number, or a 'welcome call' before transactions are permitted to verify additional identity information provided at account set-up. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring the first payment to be carried out through an account"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Paragraph 5.3.90 (continued): further additional checks may include communicating with the customer at a verified address (for example direct mailing of account opening documentation required to be returned completed or acknowledged without alteration); internet sign-on using security codes, tokens or other secure authentication set up at account opening and sent by mail (or secure delivery) to the named individual at an independently verified address; other card or account activation procedures; and requiring copy documents to be certified by an appropriate person. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring copy documents to be certified by an appropriate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.91: the sources of information used to verify that an individual exists may differ from the sources used to verify that the potential customer is in fact that individual. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be different from those sources used to verify that the potential customer is in fact that individual"</sub>

Lawyers, probate practitioners and accountants acting as regulated firms who are not named as executors/administrators can be verified by reference to practising certificates or an appropriate professional register. (p. 92)[^original]

## Other considerations

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.92: the standard identification requirement, whether documentary or electronic, is likely to be enough in most situations. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The standard identification requirement (for documentary or electronic approaches) is likely to be sufficient for most situations"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.92: where the customer, product or delivery channel is assessed as higher ML/TF risk, because of the customer, their business, location or product features, the firm will need to decide whether to ask for extra identity information and/or verify more aspects of identity. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm will need to decide whether it should require additional identity information to be provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.93: where the standard verification check raises concern or uncertainty about identity, or other risk considerations apply, more matches will be needed before the firm can be reasonably satisfied of the individual's identity. (p. 92)[^original]

## Court of Protection orders and court-appointed deputies

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.97: under the Mental Capacity Act 2005, the Court of Protection can make an order concerning a single decision for someone lacking capacity, and can appoint a deputy or deputies (previously receivers) where a series of decisions needs to be made for that person. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Court can also appoint a deputy or deputies"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.97A (MLRs 28.10): when the Court of Protection (or equivalent in Scotland and Northern Ireland) appoints a deputy (for example a local authority/NHS trust or professional deputy such as a solicitors' firm), that deputy acts on behalf of the customer, and firms should verify the deputy's identity and authority to act. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should verify the deputy's identity and their authority to act on the customer's behalf"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.97A: the deputy may nominate an authorised officer (such as a professional carer) to access or close an account. A firm may, under its risk-based approach and as an anti-fraud check, take appropriate and proportionate measures to verify the officer's identity and authority. Firms should focus due diligence on the authorised officer in their professional capacity (for example recording business rather than residential address) unless personal capacity is considered relevant, and should take account of FCA guidance on good outcomes for customers with characteristics of vulnerability. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms should focus due diligence on the authorised officer in their professional capacity"</sub>

## Attorneys

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.99: a person dealing with assets under a power of attorney is also a customer of the firm, so the identity of the attorney should be verified as well as that of the donor. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the identity of holders of powers of attorney should be verified, in addition to that of the donor"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.99: under a joint and several power of attorney, the identity of a person acting alone may be verified by itself, without verifying everyone when they are not acting jointly. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the identity of the person acting separately may be verified on its own"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.100: other than where the donor or grantor of a power of attorney is an existing customer, their identity must be verified. Many may lack standard identity documents so firms may have to accept some documents in paragraph 5.3.115, and some may be unable to do face-to-face identification (for example disabled, home bound, remote location, severe loss of mental capacity), in which case due consideration should be given to their circumstances. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Other than where the donor or grantor of a power of attorney is an existing customer of the firm, their identity must be verified"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.101: new Enduring Powers of Attorney can no longer be entered into, but where one is registered with the Office of the Public Guardian the firm will know the donor has lost or is losing capacity. A Lasting Power of Attorney cannot be used until registered but, subject to restrictions, may be registered at any time including while the donor can still manage their affairs, so the firm will not necessarily know whether the donor has lost capacity. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the firm will not necessarily know whether the donor has lost capacity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.99, continued: where someone holds a power of attorney in a professional capacity, such as a solicitor, firms should direct their due diligence at that person as a professional rather than as an individual, unless the personal side is considered relevant. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should focus due diligence on that person in their professional capacity, rather than in their personal capacity"</sub>

## Source of funds as evidence

- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.102: under certain conditions, where the ML/TF risk in a product is at its lowest, a payment from an account at a UK or EU regulated credit institution (or one from an assessed low risk jurisdiction) in the customer's sole or joint name may meet the standard identification requirement. Part II, Sector 7 (life assurance and life-related pensions and investment products) has an exception for direct debits. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may satisfy the standard identification requirement"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.102: whether the payment is made between regulated firms' accounts, by cheque or by debit card, the accepting firm must be able to confirm that it comes from a bank or building society account in the customer's sole or joint name(s). (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the accepting firm must be able to confirm that the payment (by whatever method) is from a bank"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 5.3.103: it does not matter whether the transaction is remote or face-to-face, but each type of relationship or transaction entered into must be considered before deciding that source of funds is an appropriate method of verification. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each type of relationship or transaction that is entered into must be considered before determining"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.103: in a particular case, firms will need to be able to show why they thought it reasonable to treat source of funds as evidence of identity. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Firms will need to be able to demonstrate why they considered it to be reasonable"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.103: Part II, Sector 3 (Electronic Money) covers accepting the instrument used to fund a purse as a form of initial verification in low risk situations, subject to compensating monitoring controls and turnover limits and to establishing that the customer has rightful control over the instrument. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Part II, Sector 3: Electronic Money includes guidance on accepting the funding instrument used to load a purse"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.104: one restriction on a product qualifying for source of funds as evidence is an inability to make payments direct to, or receive from, third parties; if the firm later allows such a payment or receipt it should verify the third party's identity. A further restriction is that cash withdrawals should not be permitted other than by the customers themselves face-to-face where identity can be confirmed. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should verify the identity of the third party"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.105: if a firm proposing to rely on source of funds has reasonable grounds for believing the identity of the customer has not been verified by the firm on which the payment was drawn, it should not permit source of funds to be used as evidence and should verify identity to the standard requirement. Paragraph 5.3.106: if a firm suspects the motives behind a transaction or believes the business is structured to avoid the standard identification requirement, it should not permit use of source of funds as evidence. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should not permit the source of funds to be used as evidence, and should verify the customer’s identity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.107: Part II, Sector 8 (Non-life providers of investment fund products) gives additional guidance to investment fund managers for customers whose identity may not need to be verified until redemption. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "customers whose identity may not need to be verified until the time of redemption"</sub>

## Customers who cannot provide the standard evidence

- **expected / typology or red flag**: Paragraph 5.3.108: some customers may be unable to produce standard identification, for example low-income customers in rented accommodation, customers with a legal, mental or physical inability to manage their affairs, individuals dependent on others' care, dependant spouses/partners or minors, students, refugees and asylum seekers, migrant workers and prisoners. The firm will need an approach compensating for the difficulties such customers face. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The firm will therefore need an approach that compensates for the difficulties that such customers may face"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.109: the FCA Rules adopt a broad view of financial exclusion, ensuring that people who cannot reasonably be expected to produce standard evidence of identity are not unreasonably denied access to financial services. Financial exclusion is sometimes used more narrowly, for example by the Financial Inclusion Task Force for those lacking access to mainstream services (lower income, socially/financially disadvantaged, in receipt of benefits, or those who choose not to seek products believing they will be refused). (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not unreasonably denied access to financial services"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Paragraph 5.3.110: firms offering financial services directed at the financially aware may wish to consider whether any apparent inability to produce standard identification evidence is consistent with the targeted market for these products. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may wish to consider whether any apparent inability to produce standard levels of identification evidence is consistent"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.111: before concluding a customer cannot produce evidence of identity, firms will have established that the guidance on initial identity checks in paragraphs 5.3.71 to 5.3.107 cannot reasonably be applied given the customer's circumstances. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As a first step, before concluding that a customer cannot produce evidence of identity"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.112: the guidance at 5.3.75 does not require address verification in all cases (standard verification is name plus a choice between address or date of birth). Standard evidence of address is a particular difficulty for many new arrivals to the UK, and firms should have regard to this in deciding whether to insist on address verification and how it might be satisfied. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "firms should have regard to this fact in deciding whether, in particular cases, to insist on address verification"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.113: guidance on most categories of customers unable to provide standard evidence is in Part II, Sector 1: Retail banking; guidance of more general application is in paragraphs 5.3.115 to 5.3.125. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Guidance on verifying the identity of most categories of customers who cannot provide the standard evidence is given in Part II, Sector 1"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.114: where a firm concludes an individual cannot reasonably meet the standard requirement and the provisions in Part II, Sector 1, Annex 1-I cannot be met, it may accept as identification evidence a letter or statement from an appropriate person who knows the individual, indicating that the person is who he says he is. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it may accept as"</sub>

## Gender reassignment

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.117: a firm should satisfy itself (for example on documentary medical evidence) that a customer's gender transfer is genuine (as with a change of name). Such cases usually involve transferring a credit history to a reassigned gender, which is a data protection rather than money laundering issue, and the person's consent is necessary. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A firm should satisfy itself (for example, on the basis of documentary medical evidence) that the gender transfer of a customer is genuine"</sub>

## Students and young people

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 5.3.118: when opening accounts for students or other young people, the standard identification requirement should be followed as far as possible (5.3.71-5.3.107). Where it is not relevant or cannot be met, other evidence could be obtained through confirmation from the applicant's workplace, school, college, university or care institution, on appropriately headed notepaper, with firms having regard to how long the institution has existed and whether it is subject to regulatory oversight. UCAS maintains a subscription database of students with confirmed places. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the standard identification requirement should be followed as far as possible"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5.3.119: international students undergo rigorous immigration checks at home and abroad on identity and bona fides before leave to enter or remain as a student. Applicants must meet the Student Immigration Rules and provide documentation showing they intend to study and have been accepted on a course at a bona fide institution, including a course admission letter; without these documents they will not be given leave.
