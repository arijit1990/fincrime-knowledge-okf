## Financial sanctions enforcement: assessment and monetary penalties


[p. 1]

Guidance on UK financial sanctions in relation to enforcement, case assessments and monetary penalties.

From:

Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation

Published:

12 February 2024

Last updated:

9 February 2026 Show all updates 9 February 2026 Guidance has been updated to reflect the outcome of the Government's 2025 consultation on Improving civil enforcement processes for financial sanctions. 14 November 2024 Financial sanctions enforcement and monetary penalties guidance updated 16 May 2024 Guidance amended to include reference for inclusion of monetary penalties for financial sanctions breaches requirements in 56C of The Republic of Belarus Regulations. 2 May 2024 This update communicates that OFSI will now always apply the most recent iteration of its Enforcement guidance to cases. Chapter 3 of the guidance, which covers case assessment, gives details on the change. Other updates are also primarily to Chapter 3. The guidance now better explains how we apply and split the ‘case factors’ that OFSI uses to assess suspected breaches of financial sanctions. It introduces two new distinct case factors, “Knowledge, intention and reasonable cause to suspect” and “Cooperation” that were previously included more generally in the guidance. Chapter 6 also includes a small edit to the delegation of ministerial reviews of monetary penalties. 12 February 2024 First published.

### Updates to this page


- 9 February 2026 Guidance has been updated to reflect the outcome of the Government's 2025 consultation on Improving civil enforcement processes for financial sanctions.

- 14 November 2024 Financial sanctions enforcement and monetary penalties guidance updated

- 16 May 2024 Guidance amended to include reference for inclusion of monetary penalties for financial sanctions breaches requirements in 56C of The Republic of Belarus Regulations.

- 2 May 2024 This update communicates that OFSI will now always apply the most recent iteration of its Enforcement guidance to cases. Chapter 3 of the guidance, which covers case assessment, gives details on the change. Other updates are also primarily to Chapter 3. The guidance now better explains how we apply and split the ‘case factors’ that OFSI uses to assess suspected breaches of financial sanctions. It introduces two new distinct case factors, “Knowledge, intention and reasonable cause to suspect” and “Cooperation” that were previously included more generally in the guidance. Chapter 6 also includes a small edit to the delegation of ministerial reviews of monetary penalties.

- 12 February 2024 First published.
