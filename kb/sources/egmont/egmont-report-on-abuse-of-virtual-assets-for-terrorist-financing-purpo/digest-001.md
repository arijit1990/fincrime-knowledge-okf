## 1. Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: The report is an Egmont Group (IEWG) Public Summary dated June 2023 on abuse of virtual assets (VAs) for terrorist financing (TF). Its abbreviation list defines among others FIU, VA, VASP, TF, CFT, AML, ISIL, FININT, MSB, DNFBP, FTF, PF, LEA, KYC, STR, SAR, CDD, OSINT, RMVE, DEFI, NFT, CASP, ESW, TOR and I2P. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "REPORT ON ABUSE OF VIRTUAL ASSETS FOR TERRORIST FINANCING PURPOSES"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report says VAs can be instantly traded through peer2peer platforms and transferred on the Internet, which is generally characterized by non-face-to-face customer relationships and may permit anonymous funding. It adds that terrorist organizations and individual terrorists try to work in full conspiracy, which makes VAs a natural target for facilitating their financial activities. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "generally characterized by non-face-to-face customer relationships and may permit anonymous funding"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The IEWG project was designed to assess exposure to possible abuse and to record best practices used by Egmont member FIUs to prevent abuse of virtual assets for TF. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to record the best practices that are used by EG member FIUs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The project's key objectives were: understanding the regulation of VAs in different countries; identifying how VAs are defined in different countries; determining VA usage and detection; developing a framework, standard or best practices document for FIU use; and sharing case studies. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Develop a framework, standard or best practices document for the use by FIUs"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Citing earlier Egmont reports (Lone Actors and Small Cells), the report says FIUs usually face difficulties with TF cases, especially when excluded from the domestic CFT apparatus, not appropriately empowered or given the technical and analytical capacity to produce financial intelligence, or when information is missing for specific financial or other services. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs usually face difficulties with TF cases, especially when they are excluded from the domestic (CFT) apparatus"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Although jurisdictions are obliged by FATF Standards to collect and hold information on VASP activities on their territory and apply the FATF travel rule, the report says many VASPs remain under the radar of the domestic authorities responsible for registering and/or licensing them. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many remain under the radar of the competent domestic authorities engaged with the registration and/or licensing of VASPs"</sub>

## 2. Literature Review

- **descriptive / context**: The literature review notes that several research papers describe VAs as presenting a huge AML/CFT risk to the integrity of the financial system. The Egmont Group has previously examined TF through the ISIL Project and VAs through an e-Catalogue on regulated VASPs, and the FATF and IMF have produced public reports on VA abuse. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF and the IMF have produced public reports that examine the abuse of VAs"</sub>

## 2020 FATF Report Virtual Assets Red Flag Indicators

- **descriptive / context**: In October 2018 the FATF updated its Standards to clarify their application to VA activities and VASPs, and in June 2019 it adopted an Interpretative Note to Recommendation 15 clarifying application of FATF requirements to VA activities and VASPs, including with respect to suspicious transaction reporting. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In June 2019, the FATF adopted an _Interpretative Note to Recommendation 15_"</sub>

(p. 5)[^original]

## 1. Introduction

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report states that Egmont Group member FIUs are well placed to identify and trace suspicious transactions, activities, individuals and entities. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "EG member FIUs are well-positioned to identify and trace suspicious transactions, activities, individuals, and entities"</sub>

## IMF Work:

- **descriptive / context**: Describing the IMF's 2021 paper, the report says the 2018 and 2019 revisions to the standards clarified how VASP regulation and supervision should be carried out, which helped establish uniformity among jurisdictions and prevent regulatory arbitrage. The FATF ensured comparable risks are addressed equitably by subjecting VASPs to measures identical to those applicable to FIs and DNFBPs. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subjecting VASPs to measures identical to those already applicable to FIs and DNFBPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The likelihood of VASPs interacting with the traditional financial sector, including banks, has increased. Several VASPs have stated that the advantages of implementing reliable AML/CFT systems outweigh the drawbacks, particularly because it gave banks confidence that ML/TF/PF risks were sufficiently mitigated to allow banking activities. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "advantages of implementing reliable AML/CFT systems outweigh the drawbacks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The first IMF Fintech note clarified which assets and service providers should be subject to AML/CFT measures, the steps all jurisdictions should take, the action required for criminal misuse of VAs, and why VAs are vulnerable to misuse for ML/TF/PF (financing of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction). (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The first Fintech note clarified which assets and service providers should be subject to AML/CFT measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The second IMF Fintech note focused on AML/CFT regulation and supervision of VASPs, expanding on Fintech Note 1 to give competent authorities and policymakers a high-level overview of planned AML/CFT regulatory and supervisory frameworks for VAs and VASPs and the practical and legal concerns they raise. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The second Fintech note focused on the AML/CFT regulation and supervision of VASPs"</sub>

## 4.1 Regulatory Requirements in Terms of Virtual Asset Transactions

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Among responding FIUs, 55% state their jurisdictions have regulatory requirements for VA transactions and 45% state they do not. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "55% of FIUs state that their jurisdictions have regulatory requirements in terms of VA transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Although the FATF R.15 amendments introducing VA requirements were adopted in October 2018, around 45% of responding FIUs report their jurisdictions are still not regulating them. The report says this shows the need for regulation, which would let competent authorities collect relevant data and apply direct risk-mitigation measures. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "around 45% of the responding FIUs are still not regulating them"</sub>

## 4.1.2 Virtual Assets Defined

- **descriptive / finding or result**: On definitions of VAs: 36% of FIUs have a clear definition, 26% have no definition, 6% did not reply, and 32% gave alternative definitions. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "36% of FIUs have a clear definition of VA."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report says these results suggest a possible gap in the proper understanding of what VAs are and which should be precisely regulated, which may lead to gaps in overall reporting. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there might be a gap in the proper understanding of what VAs are"</sub>

## 5.1 Use of Virtual Assets in Illicit Markets

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most FIUs that answered have significant experience in identifying VA use in illicit markets (dark web); 16% indicated having no information on widespread use of VAs in illicit markets in their jurisdiction, though the potential for VAs to finance illicit activity is well acknowledged. One FIU noted virtual assets as such are illegal in its jurisdiction. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "16% of FIUs indicated not having information on the widespread use of virtual assets in illicit markets"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Several FIUs regularly receive suspicious transaction reports on VA activity in the darknet. Investigations led to darknet platforms where cryptocurrency is a common payment method, the most popular being Bitcoin. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the common payment method includes cryptocurrency, the most popular being Bitcoin"</sub>

## 6.1 Detecting and Analyzing Financial Transactions

- **descriptive / limitation**: Among challenges in investigations, the report notes difficulty in identifying VASPs and obtaining KYC information. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is a noted difficulty in identifying VASPs and obtaining KYC information"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report states that in 2015 the FATF expanded its risk-based approach guidance to operations involving virtual assets, and in 2018 amended Recommendation 15, extending AML standards to crypto businesses including exchanges and wallet providers. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In 2018, the FATF amended Recommendation 15, extending AML standards to crypto businesses"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In 2019 the FATF issued an Interpretive Note to Recommendation 15 addressing the definition of VASPs and their AML requirements, including customer due diligence, KYC, recordkeeping, transaction monitoring, suspicious transaction reporting and application of the risk-based approach.   <sub>anchor: "including customer due diligence, KYC, recordkeeping, transaction monitoring, suspicious transaction reporting, and applying the risk-based approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Some FIUs said strong international cooperation with partner FIUs is needed, especially where the VASP is registered in another jurisdiction. Cooperation with INTERPOL and overseas LEAs was also noted, for better understanding of the asset recovery system and legal frameworks in different jurisdictions. (p. 9)[^original]

## 7.1.2 Determining Red Flag Indicators

- **permissive / method**: Red flags can be used to cover many possible scenarios and can be used within transaction monitoring tools to automatically highlight potentially suspicious transactions. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These indicators can be used within transaction monitoring tools to automatically highlight any potential suspicious transactions."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Flagged transactions combined with facts or additional elements from client analysis determine whether there are sufficiently reasonable grounds to suspect an ML/FT offence was committed or attempted. A red flag indicator may not, on its own, be sufficient to determine that an operation is truly suspicious. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A red flag indicator may not, on its own, be sufficient to determine"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The report says that, to avoid an excess of irrelevant information to analyse, it is necessary to have red flag indicators that are as precise as possible and adapted to different scenarios, with the aim of the lowest possible number of false positives. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is necessary to have red flag indicators that are as precise as possible and adapted to different scenarios"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: For cryptocurrencies, TF indicators can be established, but the difficulty is that they can sometimes be the same as those used to detect suspicious transactions in ML. Some indicators are more oriented to TF, while others fit a broader context linked to both ML and TF. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these indicators can sometimes be the same as those used to detect suspicious transactions in the context of money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Additional elements, in particular those related to knowing the customer (KYC), are all the more important in determining the suspicious nature of a transaction in the TF context. (p. 11)[^original]

## 7.1.3 Modus Operandi Linked to Groups or Individuals

- **descriptive / limitation**: In TF contexts, detection is difficult because amounts transferred are often small and the financing is not often directly linked to an individual or group involved in terrorism. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the amounts transferred are often small and that this financing is not often directly linked"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Because of this indirect nature of funding, funds moved through virtual assets can pass from one virtual address to another without being detected. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can therefore move from one virtual address to another without being detected"</sub>

## 7.2 Identified Cases by the FIU Over the Past Five Years (2018-2022)

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Of surveyed FIUs, 29% identified cases in which VAs were linked to terrorism financing in 2018-2022, and 71% reported no cases. (p. 12)[^original]

## 7.3 Identified Terrorist Organizations

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Among FIUs identifying VA-based TF cases, domestic and international organisations were involved. Most often donors or supporters sent funds to addresses linked to terrorist organizations in conflict zones, such as ISIL, Al-Qaeda, and other extremist groups such as Hamas and the Al-Qassam Brigades. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as ISIL, Al-Qaeda, and other extremist groups, such as Hamas and the Al-Qassam Brigades"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Other cases involved sending funds directly to cryptocurrency exchange platforms located in the Middle East having links to TF. FIUs also observed VA abuse by individuals supporting right-wing organizations and individuals known to be rooted in radical religious behaviour. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sending of funds directly to cryptocurrency exchange platforms located in the Middle East having links to TF"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report concludes that the information suggests terrorist organizations and groups deliberately misuse VAs for their purposes. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "terrorist organizations and groups deliberately misuse VAs for their purposes"</sub>

## 8. Conclusion

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The conclusion states VAs pose a potential threat for ML/TF. A limited number of FIUs submitted replies and case studies, but the data represents sufficient regional diversity. A majority of responding FIUs have VA regulatory requirements, and compliance with jurisdictional AML/CFT laws is compulsory, yet a significant portion are still not regulating VAs despite the October 2018 R.15 amendments. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a significant portion of the responding FIUs are still not regulating it"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report says VAs currently cannot be widely used to purchase goods or services and so are not optimal as a medium of exchange. It says this matters in TF cases because most terrorists are deprived of their assets by sanctions regimes, so being able to convert VAs into fiat currency and vice versa anonymously would be of profound importance to terrorists and financiers. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cannot provide an optimal capacity to serve as a medium of exchange"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The conclusion repeats that most FIUs have experience identifying VA use in illicit markets and several regularly receive STRs on darknet VA activity. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Several FIUs regularly receive suspicious transaction reports related to the activity involving the use of Virtual Assets in the darknet."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Challenges include raising awareness of obliged entities and receiving STRs on VAs. FIUs mentioned that none of the STRs they received involving VAs incorporate a TF aspect, and strong international cooperation with partner FIUs is needed, especially where the VASP is registered in another jurisdiction. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "none of the STRs they have received involving VAs incorporate a TF aspect"</sub>
