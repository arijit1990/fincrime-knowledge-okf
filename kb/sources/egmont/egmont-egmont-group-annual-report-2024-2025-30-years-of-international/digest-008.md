## 2024 Best Egmont Case Award (BECA) Finalist FIU INDIA

- **descriptive / case example**: BECA finalist case (FIU-IND, India): FIU-IND played a central role in detecting and investigating a money laundering and cyber fraud network involving M/s ABC, an international online forex broker; the case showed exploitation of gaps in financial regulations, use of emerging technologies, and considerable international cooperation in tracing and freezing proceeds of crime. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exploitation of gaps in financial regulations, the use of emerging technologies for illicit gains"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Initial detection: FIU-IND produced an operational analysis report from STRs filed by a reporting entity, with grounds of suspicion including cryptocurrency transactions, service-based money laundering, KYC anomaly, debit freeze, cyber crime complaints, trade-based money laundering, high-value remittances and cash deposits; it was disseminated to the Directorate of Enforcement (ED). (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Operational Analysis developed by FIU IND was disseminated to the Directorate of Enforcement (ED)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: M/s ABC, operating globally since 2011, was identified as key beneficiary in multiple linked suspicious transactions; it operates in India through M/s ABC India Pvt. Ltd. but is not registered or regulated by national financial sector regulators, making its activities unauthorized. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is not registered or regulated by national financial sector regulators, making its activities unauthorized"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Modus operandi, promotion: M/s ABC used aggressive marketing including social media, IPL cricket sponsorships, influencer partnerships and a referral-based incentive model to attract Indian investors. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IPL cricket sponsorships, influencer partnerships, and a referral-based incentive model"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Modus operandi, collection and shells: investors were told to transfer funds directly to bank accounts of shell companies posing as e-commerce entities so as to be approved by payment gateways; ex-bankers involved in the illegal activity trained dummy directors of these shells to answer bank queries. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ex-bankers who were involved in the illegal activity trained dummy directors"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: B-book brokerage model: M/s ABC intermediated trades internally and often traded against its own users, profiting from their losses, and manipulated liquidity and market data to control outcomes. (p. 69)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "often trading against its own users, profiting from their losses"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Scale: over nine months (July 2022-April 2023) proceeds of crime totalled 80,000 million rupees (about USD 964 million); over five years of Indian operations suspected proceeds exceeded 500,000 million rupees (about USD 6 billion). (p. 69)[^original]

## MONEY LAUNDERING & LAYERING TACTICS

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Layering tactics: M/s ABC's network spanned eight countries across four continents, handling marketing, server hosting, payment gateway management and back-office functions overseas; shell companies registered as e-commerce collected investor funds, gave payment gateway access and concealed real ownership. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "spanned eight countries across four continents, handling marketing, server hosting, payment gateway management"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Further tactics: some laundered funds were invested in regulated Alternate Investment Funds (AIFs) to superficially legitimize them; payment aggregators were manipulated to bypass KYC norms and control payouts; funds were layered through bogus firms using fabricated invoices to remit money overseas as imports of services. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payment Aggregators: These were manipulated to bypass KYC norms and control payouts."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Reintegration: illicit proceeds were brought back to India as foreign direct investment (FDI) into M/s ABC India Pvt. Ltd., camouflaging their criminal origin. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Illicit proceeds were brought back to India as foreign direct investment (FDI)"</sub>

## METHODS & ANALYTICAL TOOLS USED

- **descriptive / method**: FIU-IND used FINnet 2.0, an AI/ML-powered analytical platform, to model topics and linkages from STRs, CTRs and wire transfer reports; in-house STR Topic Modelling and Fund Flow Analysis tools clustered related transactions and traced fund flows across bank accounts. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FINnet 2.0 (AI/ML-powered analytical platform) to model topics and linkages from STRs, CTRs, and wire transfer reports"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Open-source intelligence and registry databases identified foreign properties held by the accused; prompt sharing through Egmont Secure Web let FIU-IND communicate with seven foreign FIUs and gather critical evidence. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allowed FIU-IND to communicate with seven foreign FIUs and gather critical evidence"</sub>

## INVESTIGATION PROGRESS

- **descriptive / case example**: The ED opened a case under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, then filed under PMLA (Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002) after inputs from FIU-IND and registering a case for cheating and criminal conspiracy. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "then filed under PMLA (Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Property assets (bank accounts, crypto, demat and 19 immovable assets overseas) amounting to 16,080 million rupees (about USD 194 million), including 4,173 million rupees in foreign assets, were attached under MLAT provisions. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "amounting to ₹16,080 million (~$194 million), including ₹4,173 million in foreign assets"</sub>

## INTERNATIONAL & DOMESTIC COOPERATION

- **descriptive / case example**: Seven foreign FIUs replied to intelligence requests via ESW, helping trace key beneficiaries and mastermind entities; identified properties helped freeze assets; informal networks were set up with foreign police; Interpol and a foreign federal police agency joined efforts to track more assets. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Seven FIUs in foreign jurisdictions replied to intelligence requests via Egmont"</sub>

## OUTCOMES AND LEGAL ACTIONS

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Indian money laundering agency filed a prosecution complaint, the special court under PMLA took cognizance and trial began; the RBI directed authorized dealer banks to share information on accounts suspected of unauthorized forex trading with ED; M/s ABC's Indian revenue fell 60%; the case involved around 100 million users and laundering of about 500,000 million rupees. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "RBI issued a directive for authorized dealer banks to share information on accounts suspected of unauthorized forex trading"</sub>

## LESSONS LEARNED & BEST PRACTICES

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The ED and FIU-IND investigation, shared with the RBI, prompted a policy change mandating banks to report unauthorized forex trading, and M/s ABC was added to the RBI's Alert List; the case highlighted the need for robust domestic and international intelligence sharing, AI/ML-based financial intelligence platforms and convergence of cyber and financial crime investigations. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prompted a policy change mandating banks to report unauthorized forex trading"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Early, targeted intervention by FIU-IND expedited asset seizure and prosecution and is described as a best-practice example for multi-jurisdictional financial crime enforcement in India; the conclusion stresses effective financial intelligence, cross-border cooperation and technology-driven analysis. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Early, targeted intervention by FIU-IND expedited asset seizure and prosecution"</sub>
