## Information Exchange Working Group (IEWG)

- **descriptive / method**: Completed IEWG project, Effective Informal International Cooperation (joint with FATF, INTERPOL and UNODC): a handbook to identify and mitigate operational challenges to informal international co-operation faced by competent authorities, in order to enable high quality complex ML investigations and prosecutions, described as one of the least effective areas of global AML/CFT regimes. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one of the least effective areas of global AML/CFT regimes"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The informal-cooperation handbook is complemented by three operational brochures addressing practical challenges for financial intelligence units, law-enforcement authorities and prosecutors in co-operating internationally. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "three operational brochures addressing the practical challenges faced by three specific sets of actors"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Completed IEWG project, The Role of FIUs in Fighting Environmental Crimes: aims to raise FIU awareness and global understanding of illicit financial activity tied to six types of environmental crimes, their interlinkages with each other and with organized crime, and corruption as a facilitating factor; a survey assessed the domestic and international FIU role in prevention, detection and deterrence and gathered cases. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illicit financial activity associated with six different types of environmental crimes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Completed IEWG project, Increasing FIUs' Effectiveness in the Asset Recovery Process: a 2021 report highlighted the FIU role in asset tracing and securing; Phase II focused on asset identification and freezing, examining FIU powers, cooperation mechanisms, data access and analytical tools. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Phase II of the project focused on asset identification and freezing"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In 2021 the IEWG completed a report on the FIU role in asset tracing and securing within the asset recovery (AR) process; Phase II of the project then concentrated on asset identification and freezing, looking at FIU powers, cooperation mechanisms, data access and analytical tools. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Phase II of the project focused on asset identification and freezing, examining FIU powers"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the asset recovery project, participating FIUs displayed strong awareness of their role in fighting crime through AR, although challenges remain at national and international levels. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Participating FIUs showed strong awareness of their role in combating crime through AR"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The asset recovery report encourages wider FIU freezing powers, better information exchange, and full access to financial, administrative and law enforcement data. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report encourages expanding FIU freezing powers, improving information exchange"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The asset recovery project aims to put forward practical methods, tools and best practices that make FIUs more effective in asset recovery. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The project aims to propose practical methods, tools, and best practices"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF President Elisa de Anda Madrazo is quoted on the joint Handbook to Strengthen International Collaboration on Money Laundering, Protection, Investigation and Prosecution, saying it illustrates the central role of FIUs in putting FATF standards into practice. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illustrates the importance of our partnership and the central role that FIU"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Completed project, Amending the Egmont Group Principles for Information Exchange between FIUs: FIUs often start digital transformation with data collection to handle large volumes of STRs, CTRs, wire transfers and other threshold reports; the Principles aim to unify the rules, support flexible, effective data exchange, storage and protection, and discourage unjustified restrictions on international cooperation. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while discouraging unjustified restrictions on international cooperation"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The Principles project reviews them in light of recent FATF updates (Recommendations 4 and 40) and technological advances, proposing targeted amendments to enhance FIU information exchange. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recent FATF updates (Recommendations 4 and 40)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Completed project, Streamlined Data Integration for FIU Questionnaires (SDIFQ) Phase I: after the IEWG moved from traditional collection to Microsoft Forms, preprocessing and formatting remained a challenge; Phase One automates structured data: numerical data processing (algorithms detect and correct anomalies), categorical data management (cleaning and encoding, e.g. one-hot encoding) and multi-category handling (adds ordinality and applies encoding by column type without manual input). (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Responses are cleaned and encoded using techniques like one-hot encoding for statistical analysis"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The SDIFQ application is developed in Python and transforms Microsoft Forms exports into clean Excel datasets ready for tools like Power BI, Tableau and R; it supports files up to 200MB and includes low-frequency response grouping to simplify dashboards. It has IEWG endorsement, and a user manual and training materials are available. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It supports files up to 200MB and includes features like low-frequency response grouping"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In-progress project, joint IEWG/IMF updating the 2004 FIU Book: the IMF with the World Bank published a foundational FIU book in 2004; FIUs have since evolved, and the update will review current AML/CFT/CPF activities, challenges and ways to strengthen FIU effectiveness. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This joint project with the IMF will update the 2004 publication"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: PPP project deliverables (list starts mid-way in the excerpt): practical resources such as terms of reference templates, PPP model decision trees, info-sharing matrices, compliance checklists and metrics frameworks; consulting Egmont members and private-sector partners; delivering modular, scalable tools adaptable to different PPP maturity levels. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "terms of reference templates, PPP model decision trees, info-sharing matrices, compliance checklists, and metrics frameworks"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In-progress project, Strengthening Public-Private Partnerships for AML/CFT (best practice and toolkit): PPPs enable FIUs, law enforcement, regulators and private-sector entities to collaborate securely and improve intelligence quality, reporting and disruption of illicit networks; the PPP Best Practice Report and Toolkit will benchmark models from Egmont, FATF, RUSI, the Basel Institute on Governance and the financial industry and identify success factors such as governance, legal gateways, secure platforms and performance metrics. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identifying success factors like governance, legal gateways, secure platforms, and performance metrics."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In-progress project, SDIFQ Phase II: focus shifts to unstructured data: open text analysis using advanced NLP to extract themes and insights from open-text responses and turn qualitative data into quantifiable metrics, and complex response handling that separates mixed data types and applies tailored processing; aims to improve speed and accuracy of questionnaire analysis. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Using advanced NLP, the system extract"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In-progress project, The Role of FIUs in Preparation of Strategic Intelligence: explores FIU production and sharing of strategic financial intelligence, described as forward-looking analysis of financial flows, risks and typologies supporting AML/CFT policy and national security; FATF Recommendation 29 highlights the need for strategic analysis to detect emerging threats and systemic vulnerabilities. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF Recommendation 29 highlights the need for strategic analysis to detect emerging threats and systemic vulnerabilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Objectives of the strategic intelligence project: define strategic financial intelligence in the FIU context and distinguish it from operational outputs; assess FIU mandates, capacities and gaps in strategic analysis; document methodologies and data sources including big data, AI and OSINT; evaluate FIU contributions to NRAs and risk-based policy frameworks (FATF Recommendation 1); recommend legal, institutional and cooperative enhancements. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Evaluate FIU contributions to NRAs and risk-based policy frameworks (FATF Recommendation 1)."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The PPP best practice and toolkit project lists five objectives: consolidating global PPP best practices; providing a practical toolkit for setting up and improving PPPs; aligning with FATF and Egmont standards on governance and secure information-sharing; promoting knowledge exchange among FIUs; and strengthening trust and collaboration to improve detection and disruption of financial crime.   <sub>anchor: "Align with FATF and Egmont standards on governance and secure information-sharing."</sub>

- **descriptive / method**: Project, Global Risk Analysis - The FIU Perspective: compiles and synthesizes National Risk Assessments through the Egmont network to give a broader view of threats that may be overlooked at country level; the final report will identify and rank regional and global threats related to predicate offences and money laundering and serve as a strategic resource for FIUs, partners and international stakeholders.
