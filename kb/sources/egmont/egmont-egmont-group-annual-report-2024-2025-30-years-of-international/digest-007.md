## Best Egmont Case Award (BECA)

- **descriptive / definition**: The Best Egmont Case Award (BECA) is a TATWG initiative encouraging member FIUs to share cases where exchange of financial intelligence led to successful ML/TF investigations; each year two finalists are selected and a panel of judges picks the winner at the annual Plenary. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "encourages Egmont Group member FIUs to share cases where exchange of financial intelligence has led to successful ML/TF investigations"</sub>

## 2024 Best Egmont Case Award (BECA) Winner FIU PHILIPPINES

- **descriptive / case example**: BECA winner (FIU Philippines): the case shows how domestic and international collaboration combats money laundering, human trafficking and fraud; collective efforts dismantled criminal networks, seized approximately PHP5 billion in assets (around USD86.9 million) and led to prosecution for money laundering, human trafficking, corruption and tax evasion. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seized approximately PHP5 billion in assets (around USD86.9 million)"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: ZYTI operated under the guise of a legitimate offshore gaming corporation while running cryptocurrency investment scams and romance scams ('pig butchering'); trafficked workers posed as attractive men or women on social media, used scripted questions and answers on WhatsApp or Telegram, encouraged victims to invest in crypto with promised high returns, and communications and funds vanished when victims tried to withdraw. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "engaging in cryptocurrency investment scams and romance scams, commonly known as “pig butchering.”"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Twenty-seven cash vaults were found in buildings of the BF compound, and computers, mobile phones and SIM cards believed used in scams were seized under a warrant to search, seize and examine computer data. Nine foreign nationals and one Filipino were charged under the Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act and with kidnapping and serious illegal detention; suspicion then arose about links between the town mayor, ALG, and ZYTI. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Twenty-seven (27) cash vaults were also discovered in various buildings of the BF compound."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Role of the FIU and analysis: the AMLC analysed fund flows in the accounts of Mayor ALG, her family members and related businesses, based on Covered Transaction Reports (CTRs), Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs), KYC documents and beneficial ownership information from banks and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial analysis on the flows of funds in the accounts of Mayor ALG, her family members, and her related businesses"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Initial detection: on 13 March 2024 Philippine law enforcement agencies carried out a joint raid on ZYTI, described as a POGO operating at BF Compound in a small town about an hour from Manila, located behind the municipal hall; ZYTI was reportedly involved in human trafficking, severe cases of illegal detention and fraud. Execution of search warrants led to the rescue of 350 Filipinos and 280 foreign nationals. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "led to the rescue of 350 Filipinos and 280 foreign nationals"</sub>
