## 4. Reporting regimes and engagement with international counterparts

- **descriptive / context**: FinTech services operate across borders and in many jurisdictions; the report gives examples of consumers using FinTech to get a credit or debit card usable worldwide, invest in virtual assets, make payments via electronic money, and make mobile payments.   <sub>anchor: "FinTech services operate in a borderless world, in a multitude of jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The report identifies a real risk of criminal groups seeking out services to circumvent global reporting requirements, particularly where understanding of FinTech products is still emerging and national AML/CFT reporting regimes differ. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is a real risk of criminal groups seeking services to circumvent global reporting requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The report says ensuring FinTech entities actively report suspicious matters and other relevant customer transactions to one or more FIUs ensures they thoroughly address the risks of their products or services being misused. (p. 16)[^original]

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Best-practice examples from survey respondents showed results when FIUs proactively shared STR data received from FinTech entities that has a nexus to other jurisdictions with international counterparts; this lets other FIUs identify entities in their jurisdiction using foreign FinTech products to facilitate ML/TF or other serious crime and strengthens detection and disruption at national and international levels. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proactively share STR data received from FinTech entities with a nexus to other jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Spontaneous sharing of financial intelligence linked to FinTech entities lets jurisdictions build their understanding of risks and vulnerabilities posed by regulated and non-regulated FinTech entities' products and services, which can feed into national ML/TF risk assessments. ## 4. Reporting regimes and engagement with international counterparts

- **mandatory / requirement**: The report states that FIUs must be able to obtain information from FinTech entities that offer products or services within their jurisdiction, whether or not the FinTech is domiciled or registered there, and this includes access to financial transactions that were reported to other FIUs. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs must be able to access information from FinTech entities offering products or services"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Depending on applicable legislation, a FinTech entity may (a) file all its reports with the FIU in its country of incorporation, (b) file reports directly with the most relevant FIU (for example in the suspect's country of residence), or (c) file reports on the same suspicious activity in multiple jurisdictions (for example to both the FIU of its country of incorporation and the FIU of the suspect's country of residence). (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "file reports on the same suspicious activities or transactions in multiple jurisdictions"</sub>

## 5. Cooperation with FinTech entities

- **expected / expectation**: To analyze FinTech reports, FIUs need to understand FinTech services, how best to capture transaction data from them, their ML/TF risks and risk mitigation strategies; FIUs should, for example, be able to answer, for FinTech entities operating in their jurisdictions: how they operate; what risks are inherent to their services; what KYC/CDD measures they have in place; and how transaction records can be obtained in a format easily readable by the FIU's analysis tools. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should, for example, be able to answer these questions about FinTech entities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIUs and AML/CFT regulators should monitor the development of services offered by FinTechs in addition to initial contact. Two good practices cited: an FIU that took part in a regulator-initiated sandbox and contributed to assessing FinTech risks, and PPPs that fostered dialogue among participating FinTechs by structuring operational and strategic exchanges. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs and AML/CFT regulators should monitor the development of services offered by FinTechs"</sub>

## 6.1. Sharing information received from FinTechs

- **descriptive / context**: Under a centralized reporting regime, FinTechs may be required to submit all reports to the FIU in the jurisdiction where they are incorporated regardless of other territorial links such as the suspect's residence. This lets one FIU assess the risks from the FinTech's activity and the FinTech needs only implement the technical transmission solution required by that FIU. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is an example of a centralized reporting regime."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: In the centralized model the reporting burden on the reporting entity is reduced, but the receiving FIU carries the burden of processing and analysis and must ensure information is disseminated efficiently to national and international partners. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the receiving FIU carries the burden of the processing and analysis"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The FIU is responsible for ensuring information reaches the relevant partner FIU for timely identification of potential criminal activity. At European level, Article 53(1) point 3 of Directive (EU) 2015/849 (the Fourth Directive) is quoted as providing that when an FIU receives an SAR or STR concerning another EU Member State, it shall promptly forward it to the FIU of that Member State. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it shall promptly forward it to the FIU of that [EU] Member State"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: In the European context, the report recommends that an FIU understand the breadth of the global operations of each FinTech registered in its jurisdiction so all relevant transaction activity is reported, and the FIU can actively work to prioritize sharing with international counterparts. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is recommended that an FIU understands the breadth of the global operations of each FinTech"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Given high report volumes from some FinTechs and the substantial resources needed for basic dissemination, the report recommends that in jurisdictions with a centralized approach to reporting an automated dissemination mechanism be set up so information can be on-shared with relevant FIUs as required. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is recommended that an automated dissemination mechanism is set up"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FIU.net offers FIUs the 'Cross Border Dissemination' and 'Cross Border Reporting' solutions to share reports with peers, providing opportunities for automated dissemination. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU.net provides opportunities for FIUs to share reports with their peers"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most FIUs receive information from counterparts on the activity of FinTech entities domiciled elsewhere; 65% of survey respondents received spontaneous dissemination of information about FinTech entities from other FIUs. ## CASE STUDY 14:

- **descriptive / case example**: Case study 14: a foreign FIU informed an FIU of suspicious turnover and use of virtual assets by one of its nationals; the local FIU identified a personal account of the individual at a VASP, which per the VASP had carried out several transactions in favour of his personal bank account, with funds used to buy luxury cars and real estate. He had declared only his income as a private entrepreneur, so the initial suspicion was tax evasion or evasion of the income declaration abroad. (p. 28)[^original]

## 6.2. Obtaining information from FinTech

- **descriptive / context**: The report contrasts the digital world of FinTech with judicial cooperation, which often remains formal and lengthy; timely FIU-to-FIU cooperation is fundamental to collecting intelligence on online transactions, and as more jurisdictions make all relevant FinTech entities reporting entities, the global network can better use FinTech-held data to detect and disrupt illicit flows spread across jurisdictions. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Timely international cooperation between FIUs plays a fundamental role in collecting financial intelligence"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: While some FIUs send requests directly to FinTechs not registered in their jurisdiction, most survey respondents regularly contact foreign counterparts. The report says it is imperative that an FIU receiving a request from a counterpart FIU has the authority to contact the relevant reporting entities (FinTechs) to obtain the requested information and share it with the requesting FIU. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have the authority to contact the relevant reporting entities (in our case FinTechs)"</sub>
