## FRAUD AND EMBEZZLEMENT

- **descriptive / context**: Fraud victims lose life savings, jobs and investments; embezzlement of public and private assets is described as equally destructive and often intertwined with fraud, causing ineffective distribution of goods and services such as misdirected life-saving medical procurements, shoddy public works, misappropriated aid funds and environmental degradation. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countless people and organizations fall victim to fraud each year"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Fraud is always intentional, aiming to trick or deceive a person or company to obtain their wealth; it does not involve physical force like robbery but is a theft causing great misery. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraud is always intentional — it aims to trick or deceive a person or company"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Forms of fraud listed: Internet sales, website misdirection, charities fraud, payroll and employment scams, pyramid schemes, identity theft, credit card fraud, debt elimination, and contract and procurement fraud. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraud comes in various forms, such as Internet sales, website misdirection"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Fraudsters move proceeds from one scheme to another, hiding gains in multiple jurisdictions; cooperation among law enforcement, financial regulators and public-private partnerships is described as crucial, and joint work lets sectors analyze risks to better detect illicit activity and return stolen assets. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Successful cooperation among law enforcement agencies, financial regulators and public-private partnerships is crucial"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Fraud indicator: quick, successive money transfers to another account soon after deposit. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Quick, successive money transfers to another account soon after deposit"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Fraud indicator: transfer of funds without justifiable reason to countries known for opaque financial sector regulation or high perceptions of corruption. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transfer of funds to countries known for opaque financial sector regulation"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Fraud indicator: difficulty in verifying customer identification information. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Difficulty in verifying customer identification information"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Fraud indicator: unnecessarily complicated corporate ownership structuring and hidden beneficial ownership. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Unnecessarily complicated corporate ownership structuring and hidden beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Fraud indicator: schemes offering unusually large returns on investment. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Schemes that offer unusually large returns on investment"</sub>
