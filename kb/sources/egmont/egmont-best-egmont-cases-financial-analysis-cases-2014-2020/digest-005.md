## Cyber Attack Through a SWIFT Heist (Nepal, FIU-Nepal) 6

- **descriptive / case example**: Nepal case 6: cyberthieves routed 33 unauthorized transactions using eight different Nostro accounts of XYZ bank, a large Nepalese commercial bank, by hacking its SWIFT interbank messaging service in October 2017; funds of NPR 465.70 million (about USD 4.5 million) went to 21 international bank accounts in nine countries (China, Germany, Japan, Hong Kong, Malaysia, Turkey, Singapore, United States, United Kingdom). (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cyberthieves routed 33 unauthorized transactions using eight different Nostro accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Keywords: banking fraud, SWIFT hack; countries involved: Nepal, Japan, China, Turkey, Hong Kong, Malaysia, Germany, United States, United Kingdom and Singapore; sectors: banking, financial. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Nepal, Japan, China, Turkey, Hong Kong, Malaysia, Germany, United States, United Kingdom and Singapore"</sub>

## The Investigation

- **descriptive / case example**: XYZ bank filed an STR with FIU-Nepal immediately after the heist and informed the FIU head by telephone; FIU-Nepal informed the central bank and sought international support via Egmont. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "XYZ bank submitted a suspicious transaction report (STR) to Nepal’s Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) immediately after the heist happened."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: XYZ bank asked the Nostro account banks to stop payments and refund; FIU-Nepal opened a case file and tried the Egmont Secure Web but was stopped by technical issues, so emailed the FIUs of countries involved and phoned counterparts; the central bank sent an inspection team to XYZ bank at FIU request; the case was referred to the Nepal Police Central Investigation Bureau for joint recovery of funds. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "attempted to use the Egmont Secure Web to notify the Egmont Group, but was stopped due to technical issues"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Hackers initiated the transactions during the Deepawali/Tihar festival holiday (October 18-20, 2017); XYZ's IT team found the SWIFT server not working; Trade Operation staff found 31 SWIFT messages they had not executed; the Compliance team found 10 of the 31 blocked by the SWIFT sanction screening system and declined them; the Central Remittance team found some transactions already debited from Nostro accounts, confirming the SWIFT system was compromised. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "found 31 SWIFT mes- sages that were not executed by the Trade Operation team"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: KPMG, engaged by XYZ bank, found the fraudulent SWIFT transactions used bank user accounts compromised by external attackers in a highly complex, multi-layered cyberattack consisting of: executing scripts to gain entry to bank systems; using key loggers to install malware and spread mass infection; launching brute force attacks on the email infrastructure; and wiping key operating system files from the SWIFT servers after execution to make them inaccessible and avoid early blocking. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "wiping key operating system files from the bank’s SWIFT servers after execution of the transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The hackers compromised Windows credentials of multiple users including the network administrator, SWIFT request creator and verifier accounts, and USB keys used for AML checks and approvals; malware reverse connections identified IP addresses belonging to Romania; the KPMG report noted similarity to a SWIFT heist of a Taiwanese bank. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The KPMG report observed that the cyberattack on XYZ bank was similar to the SWIFT heist of a Taiwanese bank."</sub>

## FIU Action

- **descriptive / case example**: FIU-Nepal created the case file and notified the Egmont Group via Egmont Secure Web and through separate emails to affected FIUs, using all available means; within hours FinCEN mobilized all U.S. resources and called affected FIUs, countries and law enforcement; the bank then reported most funds were blocked in the respective accounts abroad; FIU-Nepal asked affected FIUs for refunds and coordinated with the Central Investigation Bureau. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Within a few hours of being notified, Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) mobilized all U.S. resources."</sub>

## Evolution of the Case

- **descriptive / case example**: The case summary states that in October 2017 hackers routed 31 unauthorized transactions from eight Nostro accounts via unauthorized SWIFT access, with funds sent to 21 accounts in nine countries (the excerpt elsewhere says 33 transactions); FIU-Nepal informed the central bank, asked it to send the inspection team, and reported to domestic law enforcement. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The hackers routed 31 unauthorized transactions from eight different Nostro accounts of the bank"</sub>

## Valuable indicators of the case

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators: extended statutory holiday attracts cybercrime, as criminals may exploit targets being disconnected during long holidays; crime involving several countries, where combating laundering is more effective if every country's resources are combined under a single umbrella. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Extended statutory holiday, which attracts cybercrime"</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Through joint efforts of XYZ Bank, FIU-Nepal, the central bank, law enforcement, foreign FIUs and other agencies, 85% of the funds were recovered; without quick handling about NPR 466 million might have been used in other crimes; timely detection and immediate action by the bank and stakeholders, mainly FIU-Nepal, were key factors. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "85% of the funds were suc- cessfully recovered"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case showed the importance of FIU close cooperation with domestic and international counterparts; the sizeable recovery would not have been possible without FIU-Nepal, foreign counterparts, banks, embassies and foreign officials; leveraging policy-level international connections will help lower the impact of financial crime. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Leveraging policy-level international connections for investigations will help lower the impact of financial crime."</sub>

## Global Money Laundering Related to Virtual Assets (Poland, FIU-Poland) 7

- **descriptive / case example**: Poland case 7 (the Bitfinex case): global financial operations related to virtual assets and a shadow banking ecosystem servicing criminal activities; several companies were set up to open accounts in small cooperative banks while conducting debit operations from a large international bank; using cryptocurrencies and sophisticated money pooling, suspects laundered funds from drug trafficking and other illicit activities; 16 FIUs cooperated. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Bitfinex case concerns global financial operations related to virtual assets and a shadow banking eco- system"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case's significance for Poland lies in the volume and value of operations (USD 380 million in frozen assets) and use of the weaker part of the banking sector, namely cooperative banks outside major financial centres. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "use of the weaker part of the banking sector for illegal activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Poland virtual-assets case (FIU-Poland) is tagged with the keywords drug trafficking, cybercrime and cryptocurrencies, and the sector involved is banking. The countries listed are, in the Americas, Brazil, Canada, Mexico and Panama; in Europe, Denmark, Germany, Gibraltar, Greece, Ireland, the Netherlands, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Switzerland and the United Kingdom; and Hong Kong. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Keywords drug trafficking, cybercrime, cryptocurrencies"</sub>

## The Investigation

- **descriptive / case example**: FIU-Poland's investigation of AAA SP. Z O.O. and BBB SP. Z O.O. began with seven SARs from two leading commercial banks and one local cooperative bank between July 2016 and January 2017; law enforcement was notified in January 2017; in May 2017 a non-EU foreign FIU requested information about several entities including AAA SP. Z O.O., mainly about DDD LLC, a fourth non-Polish company performing similar activity in the iFinex/Bitfinex/Tether group. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Seven SARs were filed related to the entities."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: FIU-Poland got a first foreign FIU request on suspect Mr. XXX in February 2015; the main case concerned Mr. XXX and two Polish companies registered in September 2016 that made many transfers for Bitfinex, began active transfers after two months, used 70 bank accounts in small cooperative banks outside Warsaw for incoming funds and a second big international bank in Warsaw for debit operations; between June and November 2016 he created another foreign company, CCC CORP, performing similar activities. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they used 70 bank accounts in small cooperative banks outside Warsaw"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In February 2018 the case was passed to the Organized Crime and Corruption Unit of the National Prosecutor's Office; in September 2017 FIU-Poland received a request from the District Prosecutor's Office on the key entities, following earlier law enforcement notification and/or notification by the local cooperative bank to the Local Prosecutor's Office. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the case was passed to the Organized Crime and Corruption Unit of the National Prosecutor’s Office"</sub>

## Methods of Gathering Information

- **descriptive / method**: In December 2017 FIU-Poland asked the non-EU FIU about three incoming transfers to AAA SP. Z O.O. totalling USD 14 million; the case required complex analysis of credit and debit transactions from many accounts (mass payment and 'traditional' customer accounts), and prompt data sharing with FIUs in many countries was critical because of extreme international fragmentation of the illegal activity. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prompt data sharing with FIUs in many countries due to extreme international fragmentation of the illegal activity"</sub>

## Analytical Process

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In January 2018 FIU-Poland froze four incoming transfers to AAA SP. Z O.O. totalling USD 20 million and the company's bank accounts; evidence showed the money's origin was diversified, partly from drug trafficking and illegal dark web trade, laundered using cryptocurrencies and sophisticated money pooling across many bank accounts. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU-Poland froze four incoming transfers to AAA SP. Z O.O. of a total value of USD 20 million"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A critical factor was international exchange of data and analysis: the initial milestone was the first foreign FIU request on Mr. XXX, then data exchange with the non-EU FIU, aggregated data from several financial institutions and exchange with Polish law enforcement; the geographic spread and many accounts demanded processing capacity and tools. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A critical factor for this case was the international exchange of data/analysis results."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: As part of freezing, FIU-Poland sent a complex notification to the District Prosecutor's Office describing evidence and alleged offences (money laundering related to drug trafficking and illegal trade in illicit goods on the dark web) and the same month requested information from 13 FIUs (Brazil, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Gibraltar, Hong Kong, Latvia, Lithuania, Mexico, Panama, Switzerland, Turkey, United Kingdom). (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU- Poland sent requests for information to 13 other FIUs"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The analysis was later updated with data from FIUs in Germany, Ireland, the Netherlands, Switzerland and the United Kingdom, and data from FIU-Brazil, Canada, Gibraltar, Hong Kong, Lithuania and Panama also contributed; FIU-Poland actively coordinated the financial intelligence data flow on the Polish legal entities; information exchange continues with Polish Police, prosecutors, foreign FIUs, tax authorities, banks and the Polish Financial Supervision Authority. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "significant contribution to the development of the case was active coordination of the financial intelligence data flow"</sub>

## Evolution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Fifteen FIUs plus Poland (16 in total) contributed; international cooperation was inevitable as the activity was fragmented geographically, with regular change of legal entities and many bank accounts including mass payment ones. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International cooperation was inevitable as the illegal activity was totally fragmented"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Prosecutor's Office investigation remains active and gathering evidence on the origin of funds; one main suspect was arrested in Greece near the Bulgarian border under a European arrest warrant and is in a Polish jail. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "arrested in Greece near the Bulgarian border under a European arrest warrant"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: New sources of funds have been identified (e.g. VAT carousel); separate investigations and lawsuits elsewhere concern Bitfinex as an exchange, with a question of possible misinformation about services and capital flow management within the iFinex/Bitfinex/Tether group; Bitfinex claims the roughly USD 380 million frozen is its capital and customer deposits. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "new sources of the funds have been identified (i.e., VAT carousel)"</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Domestic collaboration, including initiative of the Prosecutor's Office and law enforcement, contributed to USD 380 million in frozen funds and one suspect arrested; best practices identified include early warning based on requests and spontaneous information exchange, and prompt replies containing data and analytical outcome. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "frozen funds for the amount of USD 380 million and one suspect arrested"</sub>

## Effective Collaboration in Business Email Compromise (BEC) Scheme (South Africa, FIC) 8

- **descriptive / case example**: South Africa case 8: a transnational syndicate ran a BEC scheme targeting U.S. residents buying property, intercepting emails, carefully selecting victims mid-purchase and posing as a known party to the sale with alternate banking details, causing loss of funds. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "perpetrators carefully selected victims who were in the process of purchasing the property"</sub>

## Valuable indicators of the case

- **expected / typology or red flag**: Indicator on smaller financial institutions in the Poland virtual-assets case: special attention is required for smaller financial institutions; some analytical potential exists in horizontal change in an institution's balance sheet, which makes it possible to detect a sharp increase or change in smaller entities, for example in deposits. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Special attention is required for smaller financial institutions"</sub>
- **expected / typology or red flag**: Indicator on regulatory weaknesses in the Poland virtual-assets case: standards for effective regulations are required with respect to virtual, mass and special accounts and to data recovery and processing. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Standards for effective regulations are required with respect to virtual/mass/special accounts and data recovery/processing."</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **permissive / recommendation**: Lesson learned in the Poland virtual-assets case: cryptocurrency cases can require cooperation among several FIUs. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cryptocurrency cases can require cooperation among several FIUs."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Lesson learned in the Poland virtual-assets case: virtual business may be borderless, but prosecution is not. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Virtual business may be borderless, but prosecution is not."</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Lesson learned in the Poland virtual-assets case: based on meta analyses of the broader context of the case, special treatment is required against cryptocurrency intermediaries that are "pushed out into the shadows" and are looking for banking services including "a central bank." (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "special treatment is required against cryptocurrency intermediaries that are “pushed out into the shadows” and looking for banking services"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Lesson learned in the Poland virtual-assets case: in cases of this kind, prompt exchange of data and of analytical hypotheses and conclusions in a standardized format is critical. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The prompt exchange of data and analytical hypotheses/conclusions in a standardized format in these kinds of cases is critical."</sub>

## Effective Collaboration in Business Email Compromise (BEC) Scheme (South Africa, FIC) 8

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In each case victims got a legitimate attorney email requesting wire transfers to secure a property, then a second email from perpetrators posing as the same attorney saying banking details had changed; victims failed to notice the email address differed and transferred as instructed, then received emails saying funds arrived and to visit the office to close, before a call from their real attorneys saying no payments were received. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the victims failed to notice that the email address differed and effected the transfers as instructed"</sub>

## The Investigation

- **descriptive / case example**: Perpetrators directed victims to pay accounts at Bank of America and Wells Fargo Bank in the U.S. as the supposed attorneys' accounts; the case was triggered by FinCEN's Egmont Secure Web request to South Africa's FIC on January 20, 2016, followed by four further FinCEN requests. Countries: South Africa and United States; sectors: motor vehicles, international real estate. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "initially triggered by a request for information via the Egmont Secure Web"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The fraud and money laundering investigation concerned suspects of Nigerian, Ghanaian, Zimbabwean and South African descent; the activity was initially difficult to substantiate, but proceeds were used to buy many high-value vehicles, and cash withdrawals also contributed to disposing of proceeds. The FIC referred the fraud and theft allegation to domestic law enforcement after receiving U.S. complainants' statements, and a case was registered under a Johannesburg Commercial Crime Enquiry. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the proceeds from the FRAUD perpetrated were used to purchase numerous high-value vehicles"</sub>

## FIU Action

- **descriptive / case example**: The FIC identified the subjects, interlinked accounts, balances and assets bought; on January 20, 2016 it learned that USD 202,217.25 misappropriated from a U.S. citizen (Mr. A) went into a Bank of America account and on January 13, 2016 was transferred to three South African accounts of individuals and registered businesses totalling ZAR 3,529,693.76. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "USD 202,217.25 was misappropriated from Mr. A"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: After FinCEN's request the FIC contacted the local banks to verify transactions and balances and on January 20, 2016 instructed them to block the funds for five days; it contacted law enforcement to secure the remaining blocked funds (ZAR 1,701,111.92), seized assets valued at ZAR 1.6 million and issued a sworn statement supporting the asset forfeiture application. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FIC issued an instruction on January 20, 2016, to block the funds in the accounts for five days"</sub>

## Evolution of the Case

- **descriptive / case example**: Over the next two years four similar schemes with the same modus operandi occurred in August 2017, November 2017 and twice in January 2018, in which the FIC obtained preservation and forfeiture orders on several assets; the FIC requested bank statements, checked whether account holders had facilities at other institutions, and identified cash withdrawals and payments to high-value second-hand vehicle dealerships. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "four similar schemes using the same modus operandi took place in August 2017, November 2017, and twice in January 2018"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Dealerships were contacted for vehicle and buyer details, corroborated with eNatis (Electronic National Transport Information System) data, confirming offers to buy a Porsche, a BMW and a Mercedes-Benz; the FIC facilitated exchange between domestic law enforcement and FinCEN, giving FinCEN profile information on linked subjects and businesses and analysis of fund use, and giving law enforcement information, documents and financials to meet statutory forfeiture requirements. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Electronic National Transport Information System (eNatis)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Dealership inquiries confirmed the subject made cash payments aggregating ZAR 700,000.00 for a Mercedes-Benz; by linking this to the proceeds transferred from the U.S., the FIC supported a preservation order to seize the Mercedes-Benz and block ZAR 652,000.00 held in two dealerships' accounts for two other vehicles. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cash payments aggregating ZAR 700,000.00 to purchase a Mercedes-Benz"</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: For court-directed forfeiture the FIC gave sworn statements attesting the predicate offence, source of funds, flow into South Africa and domestic transfers; collaboration with the U.S. FBI quickly provided victims' sworn statements confirming the foreign crime, so the funds were deemed proceeds of crime in South Africa; preservation orders allowed repatriation to victims in certain instances. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Collaboration with the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation resulted in the quick provision of sworn statements"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIC's immediate actions blocked funds totalling ZAR 6,871,449.56 (about USD 453,600) and secured evidence leading to preservation orders; forfeiture orders were obtained, after which the secured funds with accumulated interest will be refunded to victims; FIC and victim statements led to registration of criminal cases and arrest warrants in South Africa. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "blocking of funds to the total value of ZAR 6,871,449.56 (about USD 453,600 )"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIC identified vehicles valued at ZAR 1,600,000.00 (about USD 105,600) bought with fraud proceeds and obtained preservation orders; one vehicle was seized, two circulated as sought, and the seized Mercedes-Benz was forfeited and proceeds paid to the U.S. victim. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seized Mercedes-Benz has been forfeited and the proceeds thereof paid to the victim of the fraud in the United States"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIC initiated meetings with domestic law enforcement and the U.S. Secret Service to prioritise investigations, and a Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty is being drafted for a joint investigation; the perpetrators are under joint U.S.-South African investigation, and the U.S. Secret Service has arrested subjects inside Bank of America suspected of assisting the syndicate. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has already arrested subjects operating within the Bank of America who are suspecting of having assisted the syndicate"</sub>
