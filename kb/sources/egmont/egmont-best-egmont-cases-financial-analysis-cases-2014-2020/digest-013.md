## CASE

- **descriptive / context**: Argentina (UIF-AR) case 'Successful Dismantling of a Human Trafficking and Money Laundering Organization': keywords trafficking, sexual exploitation, organized crime; sectors gaming industry, private urban transport (fixed-rate cab service), football club management and hospitality. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "gaming industry, private urban transport (fixed-rate cab ser- vice), football club management, hospitality industry"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The earlier criminal case ended in 2012 with acquittal of all defendants; the missing woman's mother then gave UIF-AR key elements of the organization's laundering scheme, kickstarting a nationwide multi-agency investigation. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The criminal investigation ended in 2012 with the acquittal of all the defendants of the case."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The scheme used three vehicles: a company running games of chance, a chauffeur-driven car agency and a football club management company; typologies included fake invoices, cash deposits into accounts with no economic background, fake prizes allegedly won through games of chance, and purchase of luxury goods; this triggered a new criminal investigation and supports an ongoing human trafficking investigation. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fake invoices, cash deposits into accounts with no economic background, fake prizes allegedly obtained"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In March 2013 UIF-AR received a formal Attorney General's Office request for assistance; its analysts supported several searches that seized key documents, firearms and significant cash, after which UIF-AR and Border Patrol agents combed through the evidence for about three weeks. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In March 2013, UIF-AR received a formal request from the Attorney General’s Office"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: On October 3, 2002 a 23-year-old woman was abducted by a human trafficking network in northern Argentina and is still missing. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "On October 3, 2002, a 23-year-old woman was abducted by a human trafficking network"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Using the new information, existing SARs and other agencies' information, UIF-AR established that the organization had a three-pronged laundering strategy: a games-of-chance company, a fixed-rate cab company and a football club management company. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this criminal organization had a three- pronged strategy to launder the proceeds of crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The mother's 2012 disclosure led to several SARs processed by analysts; combined with search material and inter-agency collaboration, UIF-AR produced intelligence reports establishing a money laundering hypothesis. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "UIF-AR produced intelligence reports that established a money laundering hypothesis"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Six individuals were remanded in custody in December 2013 and several others were sentenced to prison. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "six individuals were remanded in custody in December 2013"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: First vehicle: a games-of-chance and betting company issued payment papers for non-existing prizes to its shareholders (the criminals) and joined a 2008 voluntary tax amnesty for ARS 3 million, in effect declaring to tax authorities untaxed funds of ARS 3 million that its economic activity could not justify. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "payment papers related to non-existing prizes were issued to the shareholders of the firm"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UIF-AR analysts attended hearings and gave witness testimony; in December 2017 Ruben Eduardo Ale was convicted of money laundering, usury, extortion, profiting from sexual exploitation, drug trafficking and leading a conspiracy, receiving 10 years and an ARS 8 million fine; Adolfo Angel Ale was convicted of the same crimes with the same sentence and fine. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In December 2017, many key players of this criminal organization were convicted in Federal Court."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Five other defendants were convicted as accessories with sentences of 6 to 7 years, and six defendants were convicted of conspiracy to commit crime and drug trafficking with sentences of 3 to 4 years. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Five other defendants were convicted as accessories, receiving sentences that ranged between 6 and 7 years"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Second vehicle: a fixed-rate cab company acquired many assets and received many cash deposits into its accounts, made without documentation on the funds' origin. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These deposits were made without documentation on the origin of the funds."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The sentence is under appeal in the National Court of Cassation in Criminal Matters; key defendants initially got house arrest pending appeal, but it was revoked and they were returned to federal prison awaiting the ruling. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This sentence is under appeal in the National Court of Cassation in Criminal Matters."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Third vehicle: company GD managed a local football club run by organization members and raised flags including inconsistencies on business concessions and sales/purchases of players; a GD partner managed a hotel with over-invoicing of accommodation for the public; GD was also a shell company used to buy real estate, jewellery and luxury vehicles. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a company named GD managed a local football club and was run by members of the organization"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicators: SARs regarding large cash transactions; purchase of many real estate properties and luxury vehicles by individuals with little or no economic background. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SARs regarding large cash transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case began with raw material disclosed by a victim's mother; UIF-AR turned it into intelligence giving judicial authorities findings that propelled the investigation and enabled money laundering charges. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This case began with the raw material disclosed by the mother of one of this organization’s victims."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: large sums of cash deposited into accounts of recently created firms, whose shareholders filed first tax returns at the same time the firms were created. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Large sums of cash deposited into bank accounts for recently created firms."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: inconsistencies regarding tax filings. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inconsistencies regarding tax filings"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Luxury cars, real estate and jewellery were identified and seized along with firearms and large sums of cash, so millions of Argentine pesos of illicit proceeds could be applied to a good cause; sentences specified fines should be paid with the key players' assets until fully paid. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the sentences delivered specified that the fines should be paid with the assets of the key players"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: application for a tax amnesty benefit, where the fact of applying and especially the sum declared raised doubts about the economic source of the wealth and triggered in-depth FIU analysis. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Application for a tax amnesty benefit"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case led to several convictions and seizure of assets worth millions of Argentine pesos, halted development of a trafficking ring in northern Argentina, and the asset seizures create a deterrent for future launderers. (p. 115)[^original]

## CASE

- **descriptive / context**: Côte d'Ivoire (CENTIF CI) animal trafficking case: the government launched operation 'STOP a Ivoire' with EAGLE Ivory Coast on January 18-21 and March 21, 2018. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The government responded by launching operation “STOP à Ivoire,”"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: An investigation by the Transnational Crime Unit (UCT) led to arrest of the network's head, Vietnamese national Tran Van Tu, starting a long series of arrests and seizures. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "led to the arrest of the head of the criminal network, Tran Van Tu"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Animal trafficking often goes with other criminality; an operation against organized wildlife crime involving CENTIF CI became a full investigation of money laundering and international organized crime, involving several investigation units and some foreign FIUs and ending in arrests, asset seizures and convictions.   <sub>anchor: "Animal trafficking often goes hand in hand with other forms of criminality."</sub>
