## International Cooperation

- **descriptive / case example**: International cooperation in the Peru case: Country A and D counterparts' information let FIU-Peru identify the suspected bribe money flow and beneficiaries of two bank accounts; Country B and E counterparts identified the shareholders of the offshore companies; Country G showed the PEP and family had no other properties or accounts there; Country F helped locate minor transfers by the Uruguayan citizens. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allowed FIU-Peru to identify the shareholders of the offshore companies involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Peru case is significant for the sophistication of the laundering techniques and because it is part of wider investigations that led to regulatory change in Peru on criminal liability of legal persons (in public administration crimes) and the obligation of public officials to inform the public of the beneficial owners of their accounts; FIU-Peru's analysis identified new linkages that broadened the investigation, expanded with the Public Prosecutor's Office to more people and new illicit sources. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a regulatory change in Peru regarding the criminal liability of legal persons"</sub>

## Domestic Cooperation

- **descriptive / case example**: FIU-Peru requested information from the main Peruvian banks on transactions by those involved with or acting for the PEP; three banks submitted information and subsequently reported suspicious transactions, allowing identification of the money flow in Peru and funds used for real estate in Peru and Country G and for vehicles. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "three of these banks ultimately submitted information, and subsequently reported suspicious transactions to FIU-Peru"</sub>

## Evolution of the Case

- **descriptive / case example**: FIU-Peru issued two financial intelligence reports: the first identified financial operations abroad through offshore companies linked to the PEP; the second, built on information from the countries involved, completed the money trail including which local banks received the funds, the final beneficiaries in Peru and the final destination of funds. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU-Peru issued two financial intelligence reports by compiling, collating and analyzing the information received."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: After submitting the report to the Public Prosecutor's Office, FIU-Peru met the prosecutor to prompt a complementary evaluation extending the investigation to four companies linked to the PEP and to the original sources of the money, extending it to new parties and other sources of allegedly illicit money. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to extend the investigation to four companies linked to the PEP"</sub>

## Valuable Indicators of the Case

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators of the Peru case: establishment of an offshore company to receive alleged bribes; simulation of a private real estate sale contract to justify receipt of money from the Latin American transnational company; diversion of funds of unknown origin to finance a candidate's Andean Parliament campaign, moving funds from the family's BL 1 account to a PEP-owned company account and paying the candidate's advertising; transfer of funds to accounts mostly owned by the PEP's immediate family and companies in their names. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Simulation of a private real estate sale contract to justify the receipt of the money"</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The information was delivered to the Public Prosecutor's Office and included in the money laundering investigation of the PEP and Son 2; the judge ordered a restrictive measure preventing both from leaving the country for 12 months. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prevented the PEP and Son 2 from leaving the country for 12 months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case helped FIU-Peru identify specific weaknesses in the money laundering prevention system of one reporting entity, prompting it to strengthen supervision of reporting entities' prevention systems. (p. 20)[^original]

## The Pork Barrel Scheme (Philippines, AMLC) 3

- **descriptive / case example**: Philippines case 3: one of the biggest corruption cases in recent Philippine history stemmed from NBI complaints to the Ombudsman for plunder, malversation, direct bribery and graft related to legislators' pork barrel (PDAF) allocations; the AMLC (the FIU) played a vital role in tracing, freezing and seizing proceeds of corruption. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "plays a vital role in tracing, freezing and seizing proceeds of corruption"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The PDAF scam was alleged misuse by Congress members of their PDAF allocation by funding agricultural 'ghost' projects concocted by Ms. N and implemented through her companies with no tangible output; funds went through fake foundations and NGOs under her Group of Companies, with employees (including a nanny) named incorporators or directors; each NGO had bank accounts where PDAF funds were deposited, withdrawn by her employees and split among Ms. N, the lawmaker (40-60%), implementing agency officials who facilitated transfers (10-15%) and the local mayor or governor. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Funds would be processed through fake foundations and non-governmental organizations (NGOs)"</sub>

## The Investigation

- **descriptive / context**: The PDAF was a lump-sum discretionary fund for members of Congress: House members received PHP 70 million (about USD 1.5 million) annually and each senator PHP 200 million (about USD 4.4 million). (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "annual PDAF allocation of PHP 70 million (approximately USD 1.5 million)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Some of Ms. N's employees became whistleblowers and alleged complicit legislators were usually paid in cash through chiefs-of-staff or representatives; plunder and corruption charges were filed against Ms. N, her employees, agency officials and lawmakers including three prominent senators. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "usually paid in cash, through their chiefs-of-staff or other represen"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Keywords of the case: Philippines; AMLC; plunder; bribery; corruption; malversation; PDAF scam; NGOs; ultimate beneficial ownership; large cash transactions; money changers; MSBs; high-value real estate. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ultimate beneficial ownership; UBOs; large cash transactions; money changers; MSBs; high-value real estate"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: AMLC financial analysis based on the predicate offence of bribery and corruption confirmed that pork barrel funds were released to Ms. N's bogus NGOs through implementing agencies and that kickbacks to legislators were paid in cash to obscure the money trail; Ms. N denied involvement but bank documents belied her claim. Countries: Philippines and United States; sectors: banks, MSBs, insurance products, NPOs. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "kickbacks to the legislators were paid in cash in an attempt to obscure the money trail"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Bank documents showed cheques paid to the NGOs and withdrawal slips with handwritten bank employee notes that prior confirmation was sought from Ms. N; account transactions of Ms. N, her companies and employees were not commensurate with declared business, financial or earning capacity; NGO accounts were used merely as pass-through accounts with same-amount deposits and withdrawals on the same date or next banking day. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the bank accounts of the NGOs were used merely as pass-through accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: AMLC filed petitions for freeze orders in August and November 2013 (granted by the Court of Appeals) and civil forfeiture petitions in February and June 2014, leading to Asset Preservation Orders covering funds and investments over PHP 155 million (about USD 3.4 million), dollar accounts of about USD 697,000, 47 real properties and 16 motor vehicles. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Funds and investments totalling more than PHP 155 million (approximately USD 3.4 million)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: For one senator, cash deposits to various accounts and investments totalled more than PHP 87.6 million (about USD 1.95 million) from 2006 to 2010, made within 30 days of the dates he allegedly received PDAF commissions; more than PHP 27.7 million (about USD 615,000) in cash deposits also went to NCDR Corporation, owned and controlled by his wife, which apparently had no operations as it did not file financial statements with the Securities and Exchange Commission. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The cash deposits were made within 30 days from the dates the senator allegedly received PDAF commissions."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Ms. N laundered funds through bank deposits, variable insurance investments, prime real estate and expensive vehicles, and by using two money service businesses to remit more than USD 5.26 million to the United States to accounts of two companies owned by her daughter and brother. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ms. N also laundered the funds by using two money service businesses (MSBs) to remit more than USD 5.26 million"</sub>

## FIU Action

- **descriptive / case example**: AMLC coordinated with the NBI and Ombudsman, giving them bank documents, and for forfeiture sought information from the Land Transportation Office, Land Registration Authority and Registries of Deeds on ownership of property; it analysed covered transaction reports (cash or equivalent transactions exceeding PHP 500,000) and suspicious transaction reports on the accounts of Ms. N, her companies and employees, implementing agencies and lawmakers. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "covered transaction reports (for cash or equivalent transactions that exceed PHP 500,000)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: AMLC coordinated with U.S. FinCEN and acted on a Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty request from the U.S. Department of Justice for documents supporting seizure and forfeiture of properties of Ms. N and immediate family in the United States, acquired while the scam was ongoing. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "acted on the Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty request from the U.S. Department of Justice"</sub>

## Valuable Indicators of the Case

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: use of NGOs for money laundering; NGOs are generally viewed as at risk for terrorism financing, but this case shows NGOs formed for laudable objectives were misused for other serious crimes such as large-scale corruption. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NGOs are generally viewed as at-risk to terrorism financing"</sub>

## Evolution of the Case

- **descriptive / case example**: The scam was exposed in March 2013 when NBI agents rescued Mr. BL, a trusted aide and distant cousin of Ms. N who became key witness; she had illegally detained him after learning he planned to start his own business using a similar scheme. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NBI agents rescued Mr. BL, a trusted aide and distant cousin of Ms. N"</sub>

## Valuable Indicators of the Case

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators: ultimate beneficial ownership concealed because Ms. N and the lawmakers used dummies and intermediaries (employees and friends) who typically received funds in cash and transacted with banks on their behalf; large cash transactions, with lawmakers preferring cash kickbacks through dummies and intermediaries to obscure the audit trail. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "used dummies and intermediaries through employees and friends to provide a layer of anonymity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: use of MSBs to remit funds abroad; Ms. N avoided banks by using trusted MSBs to transfer funds to the United States to her daughter and brother. Indicator: purchase of high-value real estate and property abroad, e.g. a Ritz-Carlton condominium, a 63-room California hotel, a luxury car and stock shares. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ms. N avoided using banks by using her trusted MSBs"</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: On September 16, 2013 NBI filed complaints for plunder, malversation, direct bribery and graft against three incumbent senators, five former congressmen, their representatives, officials of three implementing agencies, Ms. N, NGO presidents and private individuals; these complaints were the main basis for AMLC's financial analysis. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The complaints filed by the NBI were used by the AMLC as the main basis for financial analysis and investigation."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Ms. N was convicted of plunder in December 2018, and in February 2021 she and one representative were convicted of graft and malversation; her assets remain frozen and subject to civil forfeiture cases; the Supreme Court ruled the PDAF unconstitutional for violating the prohibition on lump-sum allocations for projects. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ms. N was convicted of PLUNDER in December 2018."</sub>

## State Contracts for Construction Objects of Federal Importance Scheme (Russia, Rosfinmonitoring) 4

- **descriptive / case example**: Russia case 4: Rosfinmonitoring worked with Russian authorities on suspected organized criminal group members embezzling public funds for state construction and installation projects; using corrupt contacts in several ministries the group kept winning government tenders, and proceeds were spent on bribes and invested in the multisectoral holding structure the criminals owned. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "members of the criminal group kept winning govern- ment tenders and embezzling allocated public funds"</sub>

## Introduction

- **descriptive / case example**: A law enforcement agency approached Rosfinmonitoring over possible embezzlement of public funds under a contract to build a facility for ministry M1, saying company Alpha won the contract and senior Alpha employees colluded with ministry M1 officials. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "senior Alpha employees colluded with officials from ministry M1 to embezzle public funds"</sub>

## The Investigation

- **descriptive / case example**: Rosfinmonitoring checked all construction and installation contracts for ministry M1 and found most went to Alpha; much of Alpha's funds were advance payments under contracts for ministry M2; contracts were not concluded directly by the ministry but signed by state-run company Delta, set up by ministry M2 to carry out federal construction projects. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a significant amount of funds received by Alpha came in the form of advance payments"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Rosfinmonitoring received several STRs from credit institutions on suspicious activity in Alpha's accounts; analysis of STRs and statements made analysts suspect misuse of public funds. Countries: Russia, Latvia, Estonia, Cyprus, United Arab Emirates, Switzerland; sectors: construction, banking. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "received several suspicious transaction reports (STRs) from credit institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Analysis of how Alpha spent funds from the largest contracts for ministries M1 and M2 exposed many mutual transactions among Alpha, Beta and Gamma; bank statements and founders' relationships showed Beta and Gamma were unofficially part of the same holding structure and duplicated Alpha's activities. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "companies Beta and Gamma were integrated, albeit unofficially, into the same holding structure"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: About EUR 75 million of the criminal income was converted into cash by professional money launderers, and about EUR 50 million was transferred to credit institutions under contracts to purchase promissory notes. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Approximately EUR 75 million of the revealed criminal income was converted into cash"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Law enforcement information showed Alpha's beneficial owner was Ivan Ivanov, owner of a large construction holding company, connected to Delta's director Petr Petrov, who previously ran several entities of the holding; Delta's tendering committee was headed by a former employee of the holding that included shares in Alpha, Beta and Gamma. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the beneficial owner of company Alpha was Ivan Ivanov"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Bank information on the promissory notes showed the final link in the chain: notes bought with criminal income were ultimately redeemed by company Zeta, which managed the finances of Ivanov's holding. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the promissory notes purchased with criminal income were ultimately redeemed by company Zeta"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Rosfinmonitoring and law enforcement systematised information on the rate of fulfilment of contracts awarded to Alpha, Beta and Gamma, exposing embezzlement and corrupt intentions of Delta's management, which deliberately concluded government contracts with its affiliates. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had deliberately concluded government contracts with its affiliates"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: At least EUR 80 million was siphoned overseas through transnational money laundering centres; Latvian colleagues, informed of the offence, froze about EUR 1 million in the account of a company owned by an investigated individual. Account statements identified the first link: numerous shell entities that received more than EUR 200 million of public funds for fictitious works. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "numerous shell entities that had received more than EUR 200 million of public funds for carrying out fictitious works"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: FIU-Latvia statements on Latvian companies Sigma, Epsilon and Tau showed they were used as cash accumulators: funds sent to Sigma accumulated in Tau, beneficially owned by Ivan Ivanov. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the criminal group used these Latvian companies as cash accumulators"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Funds went to numerous entities in professional money laundering centres across Russia; with the Bank of Russia and law enforcement these centres were closed and the licences of operating credit institutions revoked for violating Russia's AML law. Further information identified the suspects' main financial assets and laundering of at least EUR 65 million through a Latvian bank, since liquidated by the European Central Bank. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the identified money laundering centres were subsequently closed and the licences of the credit institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: To complete laundering, funds were placed in a security deposit at a Latvian bank under a trust agreement; within days of each new deposit the bank lent the same amount to Russian company Omega (which, like Zeta, managed Ivanov's holding finances), about EUR 65 million in total, invested in Russia; finally EUR 70 million from the deposit was transferred under an assignment contract to settle Omega's loan. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the bank issued a loan in the amount of the deposited funds to the Russian company Omega"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Under Russian law, intermediate and final financial investigation results, with banking secrets, were provided to law enforcement as analytical references and diagrams; the findings were used to charge the suspects with creation of a criminal community. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "made available to law enforcement agencies in the form of analytical references and diagrams"</sub>

## FIU Action

- **descriptive / case example**: Rosfinmonitoring identified: the embezzlement scheme; the scheme for laundering proceeds in Russia through cash and promissory notes; the scheme for laundering proceeds sent overseas via a Latvian bank loan; the individuals and affiliated organizations; and the group's assets (movable and immovable, including foreign-based property and businesses). (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the scheme for laundering criminal proceeds siphoned off overseas by means of a loan issued by a Latvian bank"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Rosfinmonitoring hosted joint working meetings with Russian law enforcement agencies to coordinate proceedings and agree on the mechanism of the predicate offences and laundering; it maintained contact with 15 foreign FIUs (Austria, British Virgin Islands, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Israel, Italy, Latvia, Monaco, Switzerland, United Arab Emirates, United Kingdom); foreign intelligence identified foreign assets including a EUR 5 million country house and motor vehicles. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintained contact with a total of 15 foreign FIUs from the following countries"</sub>

## Evolution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The financial investigation identified an alleged scheme laundering about EUR 200 million, including at least EUR 65 million laundered abroad and returned to Russia, and helped safeguard Russian economic stability by preventing the holding's beneficiaries using dirty money and corrupt relationships to subdue competition. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an alleged scheme used for laun- dering of about EUR 200 million has been identified"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Investigations are complete and cases are being tried; evidence obtained with Rosfinmonitoring's help led perpetrators to admit guilt in a number of incidents and cooperate; convicted so far are a high-ranking ministry M1 official and a deputy head of Delta; trials of Ivan Ivanov, Petr Petrov and others are ongoing. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Among those already convicted are a high-ranking public official from ministry M1"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Egmont Group cooperation with FIU-Latvia uncovered a significant laundering scheme involving institutions in several jurisdictions; with the interaction system established, FIU-Latvia can provide information on all Russian organizations laundering through Latvian banks. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU-Latvia has been able to pro- vide information on all Russian organizations"</sub>

## Valuable Indicators of the Case

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicators of the Russia case: unusually high number of contracts awarded to the same company or group; use of foreign banks; use of advance payments for government work; relationships among officials of companies and ministries. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Unusually high number of contracts awarded to the same company or group of companies"</sub>

## Outcome/Contribution of the Case

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case, triggered by STRs on possible embezzlement of public funds for state construction works, led to detection and dismantling of an interregional corruption network whose beneficiaries used illegally obtained funds to further their own business interests. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the investigation led to the detection and dismantling of an interregional corrup- tion network"</sub>
