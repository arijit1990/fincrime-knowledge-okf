## SMUGGLING AND GAMBLING

- **descriptive / context**: The section examines how smuggling and gambling relate to money laundering; money launderers have devised an infinite number of schemes to hide large sums from illicit businesses. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This section analyzes how smuggling and gambling are related to money laundering."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Smuggling generates large sums needing laundering; in tobacco and cigarette smuggling there is a financial incentive to source product in a lower-priced market and transport, distribute and sell it in a higher-priced market, including within countries with intra-community price differentials. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is a financial incentive to source a product in a lower-priced market"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Illicit tobacco trade comprises various activities; smuggling is done for one or both of two reasons: to avoid excise taxes and to evade rules prohibiting the sale of such goods (citing FATF, Illicit Tobacco Trade, June 2012). (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to avoid excise taxes, and to evade rules prohibiting the sale of such goods."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Tobacco smuggling indicator: relative maturity of the individuals or groups involved in smuggling. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Relative maturity of the individuals or groups involved in smuggling"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Tobacco smuggling indicator: mitigation of the risk of detection at source, in transit or at point of sale, since this affects criminal profit margins. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mitigation of the risk of detection at source, in transit or at point of sale"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Casinos are by definition non-financial institutions, but the variety, frequency and volume of casino transactions makes the sector particularly vulnerable to money laundering; they are cash-intensive with most transactions cash based, and besides entertainment gambling they undertake financial activities similar to financial institutions, providing a means of laundering. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "makes this sector particularly vulnerable to money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Casino indicator: client produces seemingly false information or identification that appears counterfeited, altered or inaccurate. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Client produces seemingly false information or identification that appears to be counterfeited"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Casino indicator: pattern of name variations from one transaction to another, or use of aliases or counterfeit bank payment cards. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pattern of name variations from one transaction to another or use of aliases"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In the casino sector discussed in this section, knowledge of money laundering methods gives government decision-makers and operational experts a foundation for policies and strategies to combat financial crime. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Knowledge of money laundering methods in this sector provides government decision-makers and operational experts a foundation"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Measures that can help prevent money laundering in this sector include identifying red flags and using a risk-based approach, among others. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Measures that can help prevent money laundering in this sector include identifying red flags, using a risk-based approach"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: A further measure that can help prevent money laundering in this sector is reinforcing employees' duty to report to their officer any knowledge or suspicion of money laundering, whether by customers, guests or other employees, among others. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reinforcing the duty of employees to report to their officer any know- ledge or suspicion of money laundering"</sub>

## CASE

- **descriptive / case example**: In the Australian tobacco case (AUSTRAC), a freight forwarding company and a transport company, paid cash by the syndicate, checked container clearance status, warned the syndicate of law enforcement interest, and arranged container collection from the wharf; the forwarder also provided storage and helped unpack containers, and law enforcement identified several storage units holding tobacco and cover loads. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "checking the clearance status of containers, alerting the syndicate whether law enforcement had any interest"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: AUSTRAC contributed to a joint investigation of a syndicate importing illegal tobacco and cigarettes from the UAE and Indonesia, ending in arrest and sentencing of six offenders and seizure of 72 tonnes of tobacco and several million cigarettes with potential profit of AUD 35 million (USD 33.9 million) to AUD 45 million (USD 43.5 million); it also catalysed further Australian-Indonesian FIU collaboration. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the seizure of 72 tonnes of tobacco and several million ciga- rettes"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC provided financial analysis on syndicate members and associates, identifying transactions used to buy the tobacco and cigarettes, including funds flows to the UAE and Indonesia. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC provided financial analysis relating to the syndicate mem- bers and their associates."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The joint investigation identified an Australian syndicate illegally importing tobacco and cigarettes from the UAE, Indonesia, Singapore and Malaysia; it used a multi-agency task force of Australian law enforcement and AUSTRAC to detect, deter and disrupt serious and organised criminal group activity on Australia's waterfronts. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ILLEGAL IMPORTATION OF TOBACCO AND CIGARETTES from the UAE, Indonesia, Singapore and Malaysia"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC analysts primarily used internal holdings of financial transaction reports and suspicious matter reports (SMRs) from reporting entities including banks; the syndicate remitted funds by international funds transfer instructions (IFTIs) to companies in the UAE and Indonesia through personal and business accounts, many funded by large cash deposits by syndicate members. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC analysts primarily used internal data holdings consisting of financial transaction reports"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Case keywords: account and deposit-taking services, remittance services, illegal tobacco importation; countries Australia, Indonesia, UAE; sector banking (authorised deposit-taking institution). (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Keywords account and deposit-taking services, remittance services, illegal tobacco importation"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC analysed its own data holdings plus relevant SMRs giving visibility of the syndicate's suspicious activity, and disseminated its analysis to relevant law enforcement agencies. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC disseminated its analysis to relevant law enforcement agencies."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Multiple bank SMRs revealed the syndicate's activity, particularly unusual cash deposits (some used to fund IFTIs) and the IFTI destinations; AUSTRAC analysis of IFTIs and threshold transaction reports (TTRs) identified transactions associated with buying illicit tobacco from the UAE and Indonesia, and referred transaction reports to law enforcement. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple SMRs submitted by banks revealed the transaction activity of the syndicate"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Syndicate members deliberately disguised the origin of funds by making cash deposits and withdrawing the funds as bank cheques. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Syndicate members tried to deliberately disguise the origins of funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The freight forwarding business was associated with an export company linked to businesses and individuals in illegal cigarette importation; in one year the forwarder's director sent AUD 38,200 (USD 36,960) from personal and business accounts to the export company. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the director of the freight forwarding business sent AUD 38,200"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Customers made single cash deposits of AUD 100,000 (USD 96,750) to AUD 260,000 (USD 251,550), were reluctant to give information on origin and use of funds, and on the same day drew bank cheques payable to different banks for similar or equivalent amounts. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "were reluctant to provide information on the origins and use of the funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Over four months 17 IFTIs totalling about AUD 638,000 (USD 617,270) went to a UAE tobacco company, predominantly valued between AUD 23,000 (USD 22,250) and AUD 47,000 (USD 45,470). (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "17 IFTIs were sent to a tobacco company in the UAE totalling approximately AUD 638,000"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Large cash deposits totalled over AUD 300,000 (USD 290,250) in two weeks, with the cash carried into the bank in large garbage bags in AUD 50 and AUD 20 notes. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The cash was carried into the bank in large garbage bags"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Most of the 17 IFTIs to the UAE tobacco company stated building materials as the reason for transfer, and eight were remitted from the same bank branch by seven different ordering customers. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "stated “building materials” as the reason for transfer and eight were remitted from the same branch"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Three IFTIs of about AUD 30,000 (USD 29,030) each went to the same UAE tobacco company over three days, ordered by a syndicate member, his company and an associate. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Three IFTIs valued at approximately AUD 30,000"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: TTRs showed five of the IFTIs were cash-funded in amounts between AUD 29,000 (USD 28,060) and AUD 49,000 (USD 47,410). (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The TTRs showed five of the IFTIs were funded by cash"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: One IFTI was sent through the account of an Australian business run by a syndicate member, identified as a hairdressing company. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The business was identified as a hairdressing company."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: An employee of a syndicate member's company made aggregate cash deposits of AUD 100,000 (USD 96,750) over seven months and sent two IFTIs to UAE tobacco companies of AUD 24,780 (USD 23,970) and AUD 49,720 (USD 48,100). (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An employee of a syndicate member’s company made aggregate cash deposits"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Over four years eight IFTIs went to an Indonesian food export company and to the account of an individual listed as a manager in a tobacco business, totalling AUD 286,620 (USD 277,300), each valued between AUD 7,160 (USD 6,930) and AUD 62,700 (USD 60,660). (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "eight IFTIs were sent to a food export company in Indonesia"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: After search warrants at residential and business properties, law enforcement arrested and charged six syndicate members and associates. AUSTRAC gave Australian law enforcement financial intelligence analysis on members and associates, including assessments on persons of interest produced proactively and on request, identifying transactions used to buy the tobacco and cigarettes including funds flowing from Australia to source countries. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "law enforcement arrested and charged six syndicate members and associates:"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Four offenders were charged with possessing tobacco products knowing they were imported with intent to defraud the revenue (Customs Act 1901 s 233BABAD(2)), receiving prison sentences of 15 to 24 months. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "receiving prison sentences ranging from 15 to 24 months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Three of those four were also charged with attempts to possess such tobacco (Customs Act 1901 s 233BABAD(2) and Criminal Code 1995 s 11.1), receiving additional 24-month sentences. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "receiving additional prison sentences of 24 months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: AUSTRAC monitoring systems identified new targets whose transaction behaviour matched illicit tobacco methodologies noted by law enforcement, sending large cash transfers to a Dubai company tied to two earlier detections; they appeared to be third parties paid to send money overseas or investors in shipments, and the intelligence was proactively disseminated to law enforcement. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC monitoring systems identified new targets whose transaction behaviour matched illicit tobacco methodologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One offender was charged with three counts of importing tobacco products knowing they were imported with intent to defraud the revenue (s 233BABAD(2) Customs Act 1901) and received 18 months in prison. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "receiving a prison sentence of 18 months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One offender was charged with attempting to defraud the Commonwealth (s 135.1 Criminal Code 1995) and received six years and two months in prison. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "receiving a prison sentence of six years and two months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: At law enforcement request AUSTRAC prepared an intelligence report on a key person of interest who ran the freight forwarding company and was allegedly notifying importers of shipments before interception; AUSTRAC also referred relevant SMRs and gave advice on the transactions. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It was alleged this person was notifying importers of the tobacco shipments prior to law enforcement interception."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: multiple IFTIs in excess of AUD 20,000 (USD 19,350) sent to a South-East Asian or Middle Eastern country such as Indonesia or the UAE. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple IFTIs in excess of AUD 20,000"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: multiple ordering customers sending international wire transfers to the same beneficiary customer. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple ordering customers sending international wire transfers to the same beneficiary customer"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: a domestic company remitting funds to an overseas company not in the same industry, e.g., a hairdressing business remitting to an overseas tobacco supplier. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A domestic company remitting funds to an overseas company not in the same industry"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: the stated reason for an international wire transfer does not match the recipient company's business, e.g., IFTIs to a tobacco supplier citing building supplies. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The reason for transfer stated on the international wire transfer does not match the business services"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: large cash deposits followed by international wire transfers to companies associated with tobacco supply. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Large cash deposits followed by international wire transfers sent to companies associated with tobacco supply"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Although the investigation did not directly involve international FIU cooperation, identification of the syndicate and its Indonesian beneficiaries catalysed joint work between AUSTRAC and Indonesia's PPATK. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "became the catalyst for joint collaboration between AUSTRAC and its Indonesian counterpart"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The collaboration produced an intelligence report under a pilot AUSTRAC-PPATK analyst exchange program, letting analysts from both agencies work together to support Australian and Indonesian investigations into alleged illegal tobacco importation from Indonesia to Australia. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pilot analyst exchange program between PPATK and AUSTRAC"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the AUSTRAC tobacco-smuggling case, law enforcement seized nine residential properties as proceeds of crime, and found about 72 tonnes of tobacco and 64 million cigarette sticks through a container search and property searches. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approximately 72 tonnes of tobacco, and 64 million cigarette sticks found in a container and property searches"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report mainly supported Australian and Indonesian law enforcement efforts to disrupt entities in both countries and was disseminated widely across domestic agencies; AUSTRAC and PPATK analysts have since continued to work together on further investigations. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have since then, continued to work collaboratively on further investigations and operational matters"</sub>
