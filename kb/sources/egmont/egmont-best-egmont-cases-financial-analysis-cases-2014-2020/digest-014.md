## J-24 CASE PROCESSING CIRCUIT ML/TF — CENTIF CI >

- **descriptive / method**: The CENTIF CI case processing circuit diagram shows suspicious declarations transmitted to the President and General Secretary, recording, encoding and digitization by an encoder, archiving, and transmission of files to the members' committee; analysis is opened with heads of departments (analysis and national cooperation, legal affairs and international cooperation, intelligence and strategy, economic and financial survey), analysts, requests for additional information, survey data and a final investigation report, with outputs to subjects of the law, investigators, courts and other FIUs and partners. ## CASE

- **descriptive / case example**: Indonesia (PPATK) case 'The Use of Money Remittance Systems and Non-Profit Organizations to Finance Terrorism': after the Thamrin bombing attack in Jakarta, PPATK formed a special team to give quick response to terrorism and terrorism financing cases and support investigations by proactively exchanging information with investigators; it also maps terrorist financing networks in Indonesia from information exchange and PPATK databases. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK formed a special team to handle terrorism and terrorism financing cases"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Based on the Jakarta attack and risk assessment findings, PPATK began operational analysis, especially on any subject listed on the terrorist financing network map and on NPOs. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK began an operational analy- sis, especially on any subject listed on the terrorist financing network map"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Working with law enforcement, the team identified an NPO whose transactions were inconsistent with its mandate, which had links to members of a terrorist network and sent or received funds to or from high-risk countries; the arrests from PPATK's proactive analysis may have saved hundreds of lives. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had a number of transactions that were not consistent with its mandate"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Indonesia's 2015 National Risk Assessment on Terrorist Financing found that misuse of NPO funds had become a high-risk terrorism financing method. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "misuse of funds from NPOs has become a high-risk terrorism financing method"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: ABC Foundation was chosen as an example because its transactions were inconsistent with its purpose and it had links to terrorist network members; anomalies in its transaction remarks included terms such as 'love suriah', 'syuhada', 'mujahid' and references to the syuhada's widow, wife or children, and it was promoted on two provocative radical websites. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An anomaly was found in ABC Foundation’s transaction remarks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Case keywords include HF, Hendro Fernando, MIT, money remittance systems and terrorism financing; countries Philippines and Syria; sectors non-profit and financial service providers (banking, money remittance). (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Keywords HF, Hendro Fernando, MIT, money remittance systems, terrorism financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: PPATK noticed suspect HF through outgoing foundation transactions linked to his accounts at several banks, which received cash deposits tied to money from high-risk parties linked to terrorism financing, transfers to a mobile phone counter owner, purchases at a herbal shop and an electronic shop, for wives, and ATM cash withdrawals in areas known to be connected to terrorism. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK became aware of suspect HF due to several outgoing transactions from the foundation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Recipients of HF's funds were identified as foreign terrorist fighters, and the funds were suspected to buy communication devices to coordinate the January 2015 Thamrin bombing; the IFTI database showed HF received and sent funds from and to high-risk countries. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The parties that received funds from HF were identified as foreign terrorist fighters"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: HF matched PPATK's terrorist financing network map; open-source information said HF committed vandalism at Bekasi District Court during a 2014 demonstration for release of the ISIL commander for Indonesia, but no information on HF had been requested from PPATK. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "HF was also matched with the PPATK mapping of the terrorist financing network"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Analysis began when PPATK optimized its database to look for NPO links; once ABC Foundation was identified an analyst used varied tools including an on-site examination, open sources, and cooperation with domestic and foreign authorities; after setting up a task force to map the network, PPATK proactively gave intelligence to the Indonesian National Police. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK optimized its database to look for links with NPOs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: PPATK identified the beneficial owner of the funds as BN, on the designated individuals list and self-declared ISIL Southeast Asian leader; the transactions aimed to send IF (deceased) to join ISIL in a Middle East country; HF also sent money to a Southeast Asian country through a remittance service at the Bekasi post office. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK identified the beneficial owner of those funds as BN"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Database checks found HF and others making cash deposits to the NPO and receiving money purportedly for social activities that was actually used for terrorism financing, plus cash withdrawals in known terror red zones; PPATK partnered with domestic stakeholders. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "red zones for terror attacks"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: SIPESAT, the Integrated Customer Information System, is electronically administered and integrated with customer-specific information from financial service providers; it tracks funds of criminals hiding money or crime proceeds in financial services and can analyze more efficiently, quickly and cheaply. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Integrated Customer Information System (SIPESAT) is electronically administered"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: PPATK alerted the three countries involved in HF's international transactions to his affiliation to trigger monitoring, and exchanged information with related FIUs in the Financial Intelligence Consultative Group forum, which facilitates coordination and information exchange among FIUs in Southeast Asian countries, Australia and New Zealand on ML/TF. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial Intelligence Consultative Group forum"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: IFTI reports contain instructions for transfers to and from abroad reported to PPATK by banks and other international money transfer providers; the AML law mandate began in 2014 with no threshold; the IFTI database showed HF sent/received funds to/from high-risk jurisdictions for terrorism. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Indonesian anti-money laundering law gives the mandate to implement this regulation started in 2014 and there is no threshold"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Open-source Internet information matched HF's name in the citizenship database with media treating him as a radical martyr; the citizenship database holds identity numbers, birth date and place, addresses and spouses' and children's names, and confirmed the analyzed subject was the person named in media on terrorist acts. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The citizenship database contains identity numbers, date and place of birth, addresses"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: On-site examination analyzes or examines reports and information as intended; PPATK also analyzed relevant STRs from database exploration of HF and networks and worked with feedback from investigators. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "On-site examination analyzes or examines reports and information as intended."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Based on PPATK's reports the Indonesian National Police investigated terrorism and terrorist financing with HF as suspect, then submitted files to the Attorney General's Office; HF was charged with terrorism and terror financing and found guilty by the East Jakarta District Court in December 2016, sentenced to six years for illegal weapons possession. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "found guilty by the District Court of East Jakarta in December 2016"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The court seized evidence for the weapons charge: a black bag with seven firearms of V. Bernardelli Gardone, a 32-calibre Smith & Wesson revolver, a P-3A Kal. 7,65 mm gun, a magazine with 7 bullets, five magazines with 18 bullets, black gloves, a video recorder, sunglasses and a flash drive. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a 32-calibre Smith & Wesson revolver"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The investigation led to the arrest of a terrorist who planned several attacks in Indonesia; PPATK-police cooperation was essential, STR analysis prevented HF and related parties from carrying out attacks, and the case shows PPATK's ability to identify and link suspects, possibly saving hundreds of lives. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This investigation led to the arrest of a terrorist who had planned several terror attacks in Indonesia."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Further valuable indicators listed for the PPATK case: a nonprofit organization (NPO) transaction that is inconsistent with the NPO's purpose; NPO links to terrorist networks and websites; and multiple transactions in areas known to be connected to terrorism, or with high-risk countries. (p. 122)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NPO ties to terrorist networks and websites"</sub>

## ANALYSIS OF ML NETWORK

- **descriptive / data or feature**: The network diagram shows money remittance as the tool for cash movement in terrorist financing: donors donating to an NPO and ordering transactions, an NPO with board and treasury members, HF, HF's wife, foreign terrorist fighters, travel expenses to the Middle East, illegal firearms trade, and an NPO advertised on a radical website with employee salaries paid, linked to BN (ISIL). (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money remmitance as the tools for cash movement in terrorist ﬁnancing"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Senegal (CENTIF) case 'Following the Money in a Human Trafficking Case': national media coverage of police dismantling a procuring ring alerted a bank, which as part of its duty of vigilance found that a legal person's account was opened by Person B with Person A as proxy; after investigation police arrested Person A and sent results to the judicial authority. (p. 123)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As part of its duty of vigilance vis-à-vis its customers"</sub>

## CASE

- **descriptive / context**: Effective national coordination of Senegal's CENTIF with other AML/CFT stakeholders helped stop transnational organized crime; the human trafficking case extended beyond West Africa, and when CENTIF investigated possible proceeds it also uncovered a money laundering scheme. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective coordination at the national level of the National Financial Intelligence Processing Unit"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Because of the pending court case, bank officials theorized funds credited to the account could come from procuring and filed two STRs with CENTIF on their client and the company managers; CENTIF began its investigation on receiving them. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "deemed it necessary to file with CENTIF two suspicious transaction reports (STRs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A victim identified Person A, owner of Nightclub 1, to police; per the accuser, Person A recruited girls from the Maghreb and Eastern Europe via the Internet promising decent work and wages in Senegal, whose passports were confiscated on arrival and who were forced into prostitution with the nightclub's clientele. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recruits girls from the Maghreb and Eastern Europe via the Internet"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: To collect information CENTIF sent requests to national stakeholders, sent requests to international partners via the Egmont Secure Web, and used an environmental investigation to identify people and their relationships. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "used an environmental investigation to identify people and their relationships."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Case keywords: human trafficking, prostitution, corruption, money laundering; Senegal and three other countries; sectors banking, law enforcement, notaries, judicial. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Keywords human trafficking, prostitution, corruption, money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Information received showed Person A opened another account in Nightclub 1's name with similar operating mode, and is majority shareholder of Company H whose other shareholder is Person B, subject of another STR. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person A opened, in the name of Nightclub 1, another account whose operating mode is similar"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: A transfer from the Nightclub 1 account to a third business, Company C, showed prostitution money partly recycled into investments such as real estate, evidenced by a cheque of XOF 39 million to the order of a notary. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a cheque of XOF 39 million issued to the order of a notary"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The environmental investigation established that two nightclubs are in the same area. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Two nightclubs are located in the same area."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Person A was arrested and prosecuted after the police investigation; a judicial investigation was opened before an investigating judge of the Dakar Special Court and Person A was held in custody. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A judicial investigation was also opened before an investigating judge of the Dakar Special Court."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: CENTIF forwarded the case to the Public Prosecutor with a report on facts likely to constitute money laundering in real estate from illicit funds from procuring and human trafficking; bank funds were frozen and proceedings are ongoing. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "offence of MONEY LAUNDERING in real estate from illicit funds generated by PROCURING and HUMAN TRAFFICKING"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: massive cash deposit followed by massive cash withdrawal for a legal person. (p. 124)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Massive cash deposit followed by massive cash withdrawal, for a legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nightclub 2 is adjacent to Nightclub 1, where the prostitutes work; Person A owns the premises and his son Person B manages it. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Nightclub 2 is adjacent to Nightclub 1, where the prostitutes work"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: a significant gap between payments into the account and the income a nightclub of that size would generate from standard activities. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A significant difference between the volume of payments made into the account"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: systematic deposit of all funds in an account opened only for that purpose. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Systematic deposit of all funds in an account opened only for this purpose"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Valuable indicator: creation of a construction company, an unrelated business, to recycle funds from the nightclub. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Creation of a construction company to recycle funds from the nightclub, an unrelated business"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Close collaboration among FIUs and national stakeholders via information exchange led to assets and bank accounts being frozen; international exchange with foreign FIUs showed the suspects were not well known to intelligence services investigating terrorism and its financing. (p. 125)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "resulted in assets and bank accounts being frozen"</sub>
