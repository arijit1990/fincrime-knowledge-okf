## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: The Best Egmont Case Award (BECA) annual competition was developed in 2011 by the Training Working Group (now the Technical Assistance and Training Working Group, TATWG) to encourage Egmont members to contribute to the Egmont money laundering and terrorist financing case database and share knowledge, expertise and experiences within the global AML/CFT community. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Best Egmont Case Award (BECA) annual competition was developed in 2011"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: BECA case submissions were proposed by former BECA Champion Philip Hunkin as potential AML/CFT training case studies; the first BECA Book was published in 2015 with 22 of the best submissions from 2011 to 2013 and has become a useful reference for case studies and best practices. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the first BECA Book was published in 2015, which contained 22 of the best case submissions between 2011 and 2013"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Competition process: a call letter from the BECA Champion invites all Egmont member FIUs to submit cases; there is no limit on the number of cases per FIU; FIUs may submit cases on their own efforts or jointly, and joint submissions demonstrating close cooperation are welcomed. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no limit on how many cases an FIU member can submit"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: After the submission deadline, a panel of four to six judges scores all cases against a set of criteria and determines the two best cases; the finalist FIUs present to the plenary and the Heads of FIU vote for the best case, whose FIU receives the BECA trophy. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a panel of four to six judges scores all cases and determines the two best cases"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In June 2020 a subgroup of 12 FIUs was formed to review all submissions; from 117 case submissions it selected 26 best cases, balancing regional representation and diversity of predicate offences, and set seven categories: Bribery and Corruption; Cybercrime and Cryptocurrency; Drug Trafficking; Fraud and Embezzlement; Smuggling and Gambling; Trade-Based Money Laundering and Third-Party Money Laundering; Terrorism Financing, Organized Crime and Human Trafficking. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a subgroup composed of 12 FIUs was formed and started to review all case"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2020 BECA marked the 10th anniversary; despite COVID-19 preventing a face-to-face Plenary, there were 24 case submissions from 19 FIUs and the winner was selected by virtual voting; the second BECA publication, covering 2014-2020, was prioritised. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There were 24 case submissions from 19 FIUs and the winner was selected through virtual voting."</sub>

## The Role of Financial Analysis in the BECAs

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The overarching commonality of the selected cases is exceptional financial analysis by each FIU; analysts used limited data to trace and unravel multimillion-dollar frauds and other financial crimes spanning countries and continents, often starting from piecemeal information in a suspicious activity or transaction report. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The overarching commonality of the selected cases in this book is the exceptional financial analyses"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FIU analysts took information that may have been overlooked by criminal investigators to piece together schemes that often included many financial transactions and layered corporate and legal structures. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU analysts took information that may have been overlooked by criminal investigators"</sub>
