## B. ROLE OF FIU AND ANALYSIS

- **descriptive / method**: PPATK tracked and analyzed the flow of funds from TOBPI and Amin Kobaitri to beneficiaries in Indonesia who allegedly used the funds to support MIT, gathering information from both open and closed sources. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK used both open and closed sources to gather information."</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: IFTI reports contain instructions from banks and other financial services for transfers to and from abroad; the mechanism has been in place since 2014 under Indonesian law and the report has no transaction amount threshold. With the IFTI database PPATK immediately identified transfers from Australia by TOBPI and Amin Kobaitri to Indonesia with St Rugaya Umar as beneficiary, and found TOBPI also sent funds to two other beneficiaries: SBK (an NPO, TOBPI's Indonesian subsidiary) and NH (SBK's founder). (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no threshold of transaction amounts in this report."</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: A table of fund flows from Australia to Indonesia lists, with amounts in IDR: TOBPI (via ANZ Banking Ltd, Melbourne, and Commonwealth Bank of Australia) to St Rugaya Umar (a Bank Mandiri account), 133,213,091.15 from December 2019 to June 2021; Amin Kobaitri to St Rugaya Umar, 13,710,540.27 in May 2020; and two further TOBPI (ANZ Banking Ltd, Melbourne) transfers of 71,665,605.00 (July to August 2021) and 76,743,325.48 (June to July 2021) to the two other beneficiaries, SBK (a BNI account) and NH (a BCA account). The garbled table does not make clear which of those two amounts went to which of SBK and NH. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "133,213,091.15 Dec 2019 to Jun 2021"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The Integrated Customer Information System (SIPESAT) is an integrated database of individuals' and entities' ownership of financial facilities, making tracing more effective and efficient with shorter times and lower costs; PPATK used it to identify bank accounts and other financial facilities owned by St Rugaya Umar, SBK and NH. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Integrated Customer Information System (SIPESAT) is an integrated database"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The citizenship database (identity number, date and place of birth, other individual information) was used to verify that the parties under analysis and the recipients of TOBPI transfers were the same. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The citizenship database contains identity number, date and place of birth"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Open-source profiling of TOBPI via the Australian Charity and Not-for-profit Commission (ACNC) website showed TOBPI is a registered charity that has complied with annual reporting requirements. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TOBPI is a charity registered with ACNC and has complied with its annual reporting requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The legal entity and foundation registration database (registration, establishment, business type, structure) let PPATK identify an NPO founded by St Rugaya Umar, Ummaht Almal Alkhayrah, and SBK's organizational structure, in which NH is founder and President Director. ## C. DOMESTIC/INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION

- **descriptive / method**: Information exchange with AUSTRAC and the Philippine AMLC via ESW: on September 30, 2021 PPATK started a trilateral joint analysis with them. AMLC said TOBPI was allegedly connected to several NPOs of interest in the Philippines monitored on suspicion of terrorism financing, and AUSTRAC confirmed Australian authorities led by the New South Wales Police Force were investigating Amin Kobaitri for possible terrorism financing. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPATK initiated the implementation of a trilateral joint analysis with AUSTRAC and AMLC Philippines"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The three FIUs also asked FIU Uganda for information on donors in Uganda and possible indications of involvement with terrorist groups; FIU Uganda provided additional information on the NPO of interest and would keep monitoring its possible connection with terrorist groups in Uganda and neighbouring countries. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The three FIUs also submitted a request to the FIU Uganda to obtain information regarding donors in Uganda"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Domestic cooperation: PPATK worked with the DGCE exchanging Amin Kobaitri's travel record, used to confirm he visited Indonesia in 2019 and 2020 and forwarded to Densus 88 AT of the INP to support its investigation. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The travel record was utilized to confirm that Amin Kobaitri visited Indonesia in 2019 and 2020."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: As a DTTOT Task Force member PPATK worked with Densus 88 AT, the Indonesian State Intelligence Agency, the National Counter Terrorism Agency, DGCE and the Directorate General of Immigration to propose TOBPI and Amin Kobaitri for the Domestic Targeted Financial Sanctions List in accordance with UNSCR 1373, established by Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 05/Pen.Pid/2023/PN Jkt.Pst of April 5, 2023. As of January 2023 funds were still flowing from TOBPI to SBK, so financial disruption against TOBPI was said to be needed immediately. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in accordance with the UNSCR 1373"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: SIPENDAR has a Watchlist, updated every three months, of suspected individuals and entities involved in terrorism financing, published to reporting parties for information enrichment and usable by PPATK and law enforcement for monitoring, analysis and investigation. PPATK listed St Rugaya Umar, TOBPI, Amin Kobaitri, NH and SBK on it. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "SIPENDAR has a feature called Watchlist (updated every three months)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: PPATK and Densus 88 Anti-Terror of the Indonesian National Police coordinated continuously on initial detection, information exchange and investigation progress, exchanging information through SIPENDAR (Information System of Terrorist Financing Suspects). SIPENDAR is an integrated platform for exchanging terrorism-financing intelligence between PPATK, law enforcement agencies, other agencies related to terrorism and its financing, and reporting parties; it lets law enforcement and other agencies submit requests to PPATK for transaction analysis assistance and lets PPATK disseminate intelligence reports to them. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Exchange of information was carried out through SIPENDAR (Sistem Informasi Pendanaan Terorisme/Information System of Terrorist Financing Suspects)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Trilateral joint analysis between PPATK, AUSTRAC and AMLC ran from September 2021 to mid-2022, giving a full picture of the cases across the three countries; it used bi-weekly meetings, including sessions where each FIU came with investigators for updates. The three countries agreed to consider measures to disrupt TOBPI and its key personnel according to each country's approach to TF (such as prosecutions, designations and intercepting travel). (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Three countries agree to consider measures to disrupt TOBPI and its key personnels"</sub>

## Outcome/contribution

St Rugaya Umar, charged with terrorism financing and terrorism, was found guilty of terrorism financing by the West Jakarta District Court on August 18, 2022 and sentenced to three years' imprisonment and an IDR 50,000,000 fine with subsidiary three months' imprisonment (Decision No. 330/Pid.Sus/2022/PN.Jkt.Brt).
