## CASE 6

- **descriptive / case example**: Argentina's FIU (UIF) began from intelligence information tied to the detection of exports and unjustified banking movements, and supplemented it with information in FIU databases to analyze links between the individuals and their equity holdings, leading to a presumption of an economic group and funds flowing through bank accounts. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "received intelligence information associated with the detection of exports and unjustified banking movements"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The UIF later received a collaboration request about a court case that arose from spontaneous collaboration with the US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA), in which a large-scale criminal organization purportedly dedicated to drug trafficking/smuggling was being investigated. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "spontaneous collaboration with the US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA)"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Analysis of the gathered intelligence detected other natural and legal persons, besides the exporter, who took part in export activities and/or financial or exchange movements for amounts exceeding their economic capacity with no justification of source of funds; these activities included the purchase of airplanes in the border zone. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "amounts exceeding the economic capacity with no justification of the source of funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The UIF investigated foreign trade transactions for the sale and shipment of steel rolls to a company in Mexico, with final destination Spain and Canada. The export was unusual because it was carried out by someone who was neither in charge of manufacturing nor of commercializing the exported goods. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Of note was the unusual nature of the export"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Border-crossing information was obtained, which allowed verification of the connection with the boss(es) or organizers of the criminal organization. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information was also obtained regarding border crossing"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The judicial investigation proved that the intervening broker made his knowledge and resources available to transfer assets received in cash to Mexican citizens belonging to the criminal organization, and the link was established with companies recently created by Argentine citizens and used as shells by the group. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the link between the companies recently created by Argentine citizens"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The broker, an Argentine citizen operating virtual and fiat assets, had a bank account funded with cash and bitcoins that was liquidated in US dollars (in cash) in Argentina. In his court statement he indicated approximately eight transactions totalling approximately USD 400,000, with assets provided by the criminal organization in amounts exceeding his economic profile. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "his bank account was funded with cash and bitcoins, liquidated in US dollars (in cash) in Argentina"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Chart 2 (Result of the Operation) is a flowchart of steel-roll exports involving customs, an export company, buyer, broker, Canadian and Spanish import companies, a Canadian citizen, an Argentine company in Mendoza, a Mexican boss and partner, and a cryptocurrency broker. It shows two amounts, $12,636,363 and $613,542, and distinguishes merchandise movements, funds movements, wire, cash deposits and corporate relationships. (p. 31)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CHART 2: Result of the Operation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Following a request for information from a foreign counterpart (a citizen of that country was a lead in a drug-trafficking investigation in Argentina), the Argentine FIU identified the individuals investigated abroad as the owners of the Argentina-based companies receiving the adulterated steel coils. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Argentine FIU was able to identify the individuals under investigation by a foreign country"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Argentine FIU collaborated with the judiciary through several reports by its Analysis Division containing intelligence on economic-patrimonial-financial transactions and profiles; this work identified the group of companies, the criminal maneuvers and the assets of the organization. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collaborated with the judiciary through the submission of several reports"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The broker, acting as facilitator, was convicted of money laundering, described as a precedent in Argentina for a conviction for criminal activities associated with money laundering involving virtual assets. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a precedent in Argentina regarding a conviction for criminal activities associated with money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The foreign FIU provided intelligence verifying that a partner of the shell company under investigation in Argentina did business with the economic group under investigation, enabling the Argentine FIU to detect one of the main heads of the criminal organization and confirm his links to Argentine companies. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Argentina’s FIU was able to detect one of the main heads of the criminal organization"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Argentina's court convicted seven members of a transnational drug trafficking organization (three Mexican and four Argentine citizens) with sentences of 5 to 15 years in prison for aggravated illicit storage of drugs. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "convicted seven members of a transnational drug trafficking criminal organization"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Various entities with a duty to report to the Argentine FIU supplied information on the individuals under investigation, on the foreign trade transactions and on fund movements in their bank accounts. Information was also gathered on the involved companies, their shareholders and the economic patrimonial status of the organization's members. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information was requested by various entities with a duty to report to the Argentine FIU"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The broker was sentenced to five years in prison and ordered to pay a fine eight times the transaction amounts for money laundering through virtual assets; the amount laundered was estimated at at least USD 468,400. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sentenced to five years in prison and was ordered to pay a fine eight times the amounts of the transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A court-ordered search led to the seizure of almost two tons of cocaine valued at USD 60 million; cash and property such as cars, tools and machinery were also confiscated. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "almost two tons of cocaine were seized, valued at (USD) 60 million"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: At the same time, the Argentine FIU received a collaboration request from the local court; new information on some subjects was gathered and analyzed in depth, and transactions that could be deemed suspicious for money laundering were detected, including amounts credited to the bank account of the criminally charged assets broker. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transactions were detected that could be deemed as money-laundering suspicious transactions."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Case 6 indicators and lessons listed: reporting entities performed well in identifying suspicious activities; cooperation on domestic and international information exchanges; rapid detection and seizure of assets; and use of intelligence reports by the court. The FIU's conclusions and information on the group's assets were made available to the court. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Reporting entities performed well in identifying suspicious activities."</sub>

## CASE 7

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 7 (Hong Kong, China JFIU): a complex cross-border investigation led to a crackdown on a syndicate using virtual banks and cryptocurrency trading as a conduit to launder proceeds of fraud, with over USD 95.2 million laundered using money mules and the involvement of a hotel. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using virtual banks and cryptocurrency trading as conduit to launder crime proceeds derived from fraud"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Mutual Evaluation Report recognized Hong Kong, China (HKC) as having a comprehensive AML/CFT regime with a robust legal framework, effective law enforcement, rigorous preventive measures, international cooperation, public education and publicity. Its ML/TF risk assessment is updated periodically, and the latest assessment indicated fraud-related crime continues to be one of the most significant ML threats. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fraud-related crime continues to be one of the most significant ML threats to the jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: As an international finance, trade and transportation centre, HKC is exposed to external and internal ML threats, particularly transnational/cross-border ML syndicates. Accelerated use of technology in the financial system improves accessibility and efficiency but also gives criminals new and faster ML channels; the latest risk assessment identified exploitation of virtual banks and virtual assets (VAs) by criminals to launder proceeds as an emerging challenge. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it also provides new and faster ML channels for criminals"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In the first quarter of 2021 a virtual bank in HKC discovered 43 accounts intensively connected with an extremely high volume of transactions; some received crime proceeds, which triggered a real-time monitoring alert, and a U-turn pattern was also frequently observed. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a virtual bank in HKC discovered 43 accounts being intensively connected with an extremely high volume of transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The JFIU's initial analysis of STRs found a common set of IP addresses and device IDs shared among the suspicious accounts, with crime proceeds mixed with crypto trading circularly laundered among them. Enquiries with banks and crypto-trading platforms on those IP addresses surfaced over 80 local bank accounts that laundered HKD 740 million (USD 95.2 million) connected to at least 40 reported fraud cases. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "found a common set of IP addresses and device IDs shared among the suspicious accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Most of the bank accounts were short lived because the extremely high turnover triggered additional due diligence. The JFIU initially believed the accounts were opened by money mules controlled by an ML syndicate. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most of the bank accounts were short lived as the extremely high volume of turnover triggered additional due diligence."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The syndicate accessed the bank accounts and crypto-trading platforms through a VPN server that masked the original IP addresses. Although the VPN IP could not locate the syndicate, the JFIU evaluated all sharing device IDs, which identified every individual smartphone and a set of devices likely to be reused to operate new accounts. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "accessing the bank accounts and crypto-trading platforms via a Virtual Private Network (VPN) server"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The syndicate recruited many money mules and exploited the anonymity of crypto trading to avoid detection. To find those behind the mules, the JFIU looked beyond transactions to the digital footprint of each online access, applying advanced analysis to large, diverse data sets with semi-structured and unstructured raw data (IP addresses, tracked device IDs and associated digital footprint) to uncover hidden correlations. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "JFIU looked beyond transactions and examined every digital footprint left by each online access of the accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Traditional methods, for example reviewing photos captured during each account login, were also used alongside digital footprint tracking. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "traditional methods (e.g., reviewing photos captured during each account login) were also deployed by the JFIU"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: JFIU monitoring revealed the digital footprint of a router used by the syndicate, which located the syndicate's operating centre: a hotel in a neighboring jurisdiction (Jurisdiction A, a non-Egmont member) that was involved in a conspiracy to defraud and engage in money laundering. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "JFIU monitoring revealed the digital footprint of a router used by the syndicate"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The JFIU took the lead in requesting bank log records of suspicious accounts, and through analysis and mapping found common IP addresses and device IDs among them. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The JFIU took the lead, requesting bank log records of suspicious accounts for in-depth analysis."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Reviewing account-opening processes, the JFIU found a local male frequently behind the account holders in account-opening photos; most accounts were briefly accessed from his residence IP address or his smartphone. He was believed to be a core syndicate member recruiting local money mules and handing the accounts to the syndicate in Jurisdiction A. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a local male appeared frequently behind the account holders in their account-opening photos"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Beyond fund flow and digital footprint tracing, the JFIU reviewed photos captured during e-banking transactions on the mule accounts and found the background of the mules' account-opening photos matched the decor of some hotel rooms in Jurisdiction A, which led to identifying the target hotel. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "matched the decor of some hotel rooms in Jurisdiction A"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In September 2021 the HKPF and law enforcement in Jurisdiction A mounted a joint arrest operation. Fourteen individuals, including the account recruiter and over 10 money mule account holders, were arrested in HKC, and Jurisdiction A arrested eight core syndicate members inside the target hotel for conspiracy to defraud and money laundering. The case remains under active investigation by the Hong Kong Police Force. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A total of 14 individuals—including the account recruiter and over 10 money mule account holders—were arrested in HKC."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: With Jurisdiction A's law enforcement assistance, the eight core syndicate members were found renting rooms in the target hotel and had operated the Hong Kong money mule accounts for ML for over a year. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "eight core syndicate members were found renting rooms in the target hotel"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Observing the emerging risk of abusing virtual banks for ML, the JFIU is coordinating with the banking regulatory authority in HKC to encourage banks to provide digital footprints in their STR submissions, which was key to prompt, effective detection in this case. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "encourage banks provide digital footprints in their STR submis­ sion"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The virtual bank's prompt reporting reflected the close working relationship between the JFIU and reporting entities, and the case demonstrated the importance of public-private collaboration in detecting ML. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prompt reporting of the virtual bank reflected the close working relationship"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case illustrated the importance of intelligence exchange between FIU, regulatory authorities and reporting entities; the combined use of traditional intelligence gathering, digital footprint analysis and cross-border collaboration was described as indispensable. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "importance and use of intelligence exchange between FIU, regulatory authorities and reporting entities"</sub>

## CASE 8

- **descriptive / context**: Case 8 (Philippines AMLC) establishes common indicators of sexual exploitation of children. Online Sexual Exploitation of Children (OSEC) is described as an emerging, constantly evolving, significant threat in the Philippines and worldwide, and as the most disturbing aspect of cybercrime, extending across borders. The case says it can be fought through multisector collaboration among public and private sectors and the international community. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "successfully fighting against online sexual exploitation is achievable via multisector collaboration and cooperation"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The AMLC study identified common indicators that possibly associate a transaction with OSEC, the top locations where beneficiaries claim the funds, and the countries of the senders of funds. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The study identified some of the common indicators that possibly associate a transaction to OSEC."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In 2019 the AMLC conducted its first study on child pornography in the Philippines, using STR data from 2015 to 2018. It identified OSEC as a significant, emerging threat to which Filipino children were especially vulnerable, with OSEC operations ranging from local small-scale enterprises to large international organized networks operating inside and outside the Philippines. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Child Pornography in the Philippines: An Evaluation using STR data"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Using the 2019 study as comparison, the AMLC conducted in 2020 a global threat assessment titled "Online Sexual Exploitation of Children: A crime with a global impact and an evolving transnational threat", and a post-2019 study of STR data from 2019 to the first semester of 2020. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Post-2019 Study using STR Data (STR data from 2019 to the first semester of 2020)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Filipino government officials tasked with combating the problem describe the Philippine child pornography industry as among the largest in the world, worth more than USD 1 billion a year. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one of the biggest in the world, exceeding (USD) 1 billion a year."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The same account says foreign nationals from known offender countries operate in the Philippines with the assistance of Filipino nationals, who exploit Filipino children for child pornography. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Foreign nationals from known offender countries operate in the Philippines with the assistance of Filipino nationals"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The figure 'Volume of STRs associated with OSEC' covers yearly STR volumes and increases from 2015 to June 1, 2020 (as of run date June 4, 2020). Its extracted labels show counts 457, 1,344, 606, 10,633 and 20,448 and percentage changes 124%, 194%, -55%, 1655% and 92%; the excerpt's labels are garbled so the year-to-value mapping is not stated explicitly. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Yearly volume and increase of OSEC-related STRs received from 2015 to June 1, 2020"</sub>
