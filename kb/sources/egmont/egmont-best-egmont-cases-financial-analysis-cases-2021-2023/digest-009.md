## CASE 17

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 17 (Latvia FID): in early 2018 Country A published a notice of proposed rulemaking against Bank A on findings it was suspected of money laundering; later that year Bank A began voluntary liquidation and its license was soon revoked, with EUR 2.4 billion still to be paid to creditors. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Country A published a notice of proposed rulemaking against Bank A"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Latvijas Banka approved the liquidation, requiring a thorough assessment of each creditor and their source of funds. As the Latvian FIU assessed the bank's transactions and clients, it became evident Bank A systematically engaged in money laundering and laundered millions of euros through the Latvian financial system. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring a thorough assessment of each creditor and their source of funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case was a professional money laundering matter so complex it required unprecedented domestic and international financial intelligence exchange mechanisms involving FIUs from 25 jurisdictions, and it elevated the Latvian FIU into a leadership role among the project. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unprecedented domestic and international financial intelligence exchange mechanisms, involving FIUs from 25 jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Initial creditor analysis revealed typologies: large sums received in Bank A originated from foreign accounts of non-resident legal entities that were part of laundering schemes; transactions between the bank's non-resident customers showed signs of transit operations with no economic purpose; and a large number of customers were considered shell companies with declared non-resident Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBOs). (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Initial analysis of creditors revealed a variety of money laundering typologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Between 2013 and 2018 over EUR 300 billion was transferred through Bank A to dozens of jurisdictions linked to laundering schemes the bank facilitated. Latvia's FIU received all transactions of Bank A customers for the period, a list of IP addresses of transaction parties and KYC data, and processed the largest amount of financial intelligence data ever connected to a single case. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Between 2013 to 2018, over (EUR) 300 billion were transferred through Bank A."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FIU Latvia began disseminating and exchanging financial intelligence with the other involved FIUs to gather operational information and complete analysis of suspicious transactions and Bank A customers. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU Latvia began disseminating and exchanging"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The case created a significant public-private partnership, the Cooperation Coordination Group (CCG), a new framework for ad-hoc exchange of financial intelligence on operational and strategic matters with law enforcement, private partners and other stakeholders; over 100 meetings were held. (p. 71)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a significant public-private partnership: the Cooperation Coordination Group (CCG)"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FIU Latvia met the Latvian State Police and Prosecution Office to discuss draft analysis of the most complicated ML schemes, soon formalized with the formation of IFIT and the CCG. Regular meetings with Bank A's liquidators created a shared plan for analyzing clients and sharing large client data sets, reducing time and resources needed for analytical information exchange. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regular meetings were organized with liquidators of Bank A"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Experts from different backgrounds and institutions were involved in an early phase of the investigation, helping set goals for future actions. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and institutions in an early phase of the investigation, helping set goals for future actions."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The International Financial Intelligence Taskforce (IFIT), a novel multilateral forum, consisted of FIUs from 25 jurisdictions exchanging intelligence on Bank A. Foreign support was essential for FIU Latvia to assess and freeze funds and advance the case locally; IFIT built a shared understanding, served as a safe forum for sharing concerns and awareness of analysis, and was used to exchange priorities, typologies and methodologies. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The IFIT was a safe forum for FIUs to share concerns"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Legal entities were identified that Bank A employees used to process fees of a legalization scheme (disguised as payments for legal services or donations to charity); the profits were moved to other entities, obscuring connections between Bank A, its employees and its customers. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to process the fees related to a legalization scheme"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FIU Latvia requested and received access to Bank A's database of all transactions from January 2013 to June 2018, and created a data room with customized software (new tools to process large data sets in structured, searchable form) containing every historical customer transaction in the five-year period. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a data room was created using customized software"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: International cooperation at institutional and analytical levels involved exchanging information on large data sets and on individual cases through Electronic Single Window (ESW) channels, giving a wider perspective on Bank A clients and their role in the legalization schemes. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by using the Electronic Single Window (ESW) channels"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: FIU Latvia obtained consolidated KYC information on every customer (identity information, residence/legal address, account information, declared UBOs) plus the IP address list of every transaction in the five-year period. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The IP address list of every transaction made during this five-year period was also included."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: A new information exchange method for cooperating with Latvian law enforcement processed and compiled transaction information, allowing law enforcement to take faster procedural actions with documentation usable in court. FIU Latvia kept supporting police and the Prosecution Office with analysis after sending the case. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "documentation that permitted its use in court"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Two working groups were created, whose preliminary analysis indicated Bank A failed to comply with Latvian AML law by serving high-risk clients with signs of being shell companies, generally used to transfer funds from the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS) to credit institutions in other countries while disguising their source. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Two different working groups were created, whose preliminary analysis indicated:"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Bank A's services to clients allegedly included establishing shell companies, placing it under suspicion of being a willful participant in legalization schemes. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "thus placing Bank A under suspicion of being a willful participant in legalization schemes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Country A's announcement indicated Bank A's involvement in facilitating ML schemes, obstructing regulatory enforcement, and activity linked to North Korea. Within ten days the European Central Bank announced the bank and its subsidiaries were failing or likely to fail; the Latvian supervisor allowed self-liquidation and the ECB removed the license. FIU Latvia helped develop the liquidation methodology in line with AML rules. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducting activity linked to North Korea"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The liquidation methodology required each creditor to be assessed for source of funds; to enable this FIU Latvia requested and received full access to all historical Bank A customer transactions from 2013 to 2018, transaction party IP addresses and full KYC data. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The liquidation methodology required each creditor to be assessed for their source of funds."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: FIU Latvia traced almost 30 jurisdictions with significant transactions with Bank A in 2013-2018 and invited their FIUs to an ad hoc IFIT as a platform for intelligence sharing; 25 FIUs participated, led by FIU Latvia, to jointly analyze money flows, examine former customers to prevent pay-outs of illegitimate funds to creditors, and detect perpetrators and movement of criminal funds abroad. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU Latvia traced almost 30 jurisdictions that had significant transactions with Bank A from 2013 to 2018."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A criminal process against Bank A for suspicion of professional money laundering is ongoing. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is an ongoing criminal process against Bank A for suspicion of professional money laundering."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Funds received by Bank A came from foreign accounts of non-resident legal entities considered part of publicly known worldwide laundromat schemes (e.g., the "Russian Laundromat" or "Azerbaijani Laundromat"); non-resident customers' transactions showed transit operations with no economic purpose; many customers were shell companies with non-resident UBOs. These red flags quickly led to the conclusion that Bank A was involved in institutionalized ML. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These were but a few of the red flags that quickly led to the conclusion Bank A was"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Bank A typologies were significantly beyond the usual ones in such cases, and Bank A's role entailed long-term, systematic behaviour. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The role of Bank A in this case entailed long-term, systematic behaviour."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Through cooperation among 25 FIUs led by the Latvian FIU between 2018 and 2023: 1021 reports disseminated to law enforcement; at least EUR 1.18 billion frozen; over 581 criminal proceedings initiated in Latvia with 316 reports added to existing proceedings; at least EUR 179 million confiscated; and at least EUR 1.16 billion seized (excluding real estate, financial instruments or other assets). (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Freezing of at least (EUR) 1.18 billion in funds."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 17 indicators: many customers considered shell companies with declared non-resident UBOs; large funds from foreign accounts of non-resident legal entities in laundromat schemes; incoming and outgoing payments with generic references to contracts, invoices and goods acquisition often outside the entities' stated business purpose; transit operations without economic purpose between non-resident customers; money used to acquire assets (properties, yachts, jewellery, art) and for lifestyle payments (school fees, holidays, legal fees). (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both incoming and outgoing payments within Bank A contained generic references to contracts, invoices"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case triggered major reform of Latvia's financial sector, and Latvia became the first country to comply fully with all FATF standards. Across IFIT members changes included amended national AML/CFT legislation, more analytical staff and database access, improved guidelines and training for reporting entities, new IFIT and PPP cooperation frameworks, and closer international and local collaboration. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The country became the first to comply fully with all the standards set by the Financial Action Task Force (FATF)"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Scheme 1 (transactional data analysis of Bank A as a conduit, 2013-2018) shows Bank A with flows to and from countries and 'Other', labelled with amounts including EUR 30.92bn, 19.48bn, 14.46bn, 12.02bn, 10.79bn, 10.01bn, 8.62bn, 7.66bn, 7.51bn, 7.32bn, 7.17bn, 5.7bn, 5bn, 4.86bn, 4.71bn, 4.53bn, 4.3bn, 4.2bn, 3.8bn, 3.76bn, 3.7bn (twice), 3.5bn, 3.4bn, 3.37bn, 3.3bn, 3.22bn, 3.19bn, 2.95bn, 2.69bn and 2.39bn; the extracted labels do not tie amounts to specific countries. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Scheme 1: Transactional data analysis of Bank A as a conduit (2013–2018)."</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Scheme 2 (complex layering using Bank A accounts) shows outgoing flows of EUR 160.2 billion. Customers: 7,087 legal customers and 1,180 natural customers (EUR 0.3 billion); 6,575 customers registered in offshore locations (UK, Belize, Seychelles, Panama, Cypress, Marshall Islands, Hong Kong, Latvia, Domonica, Canada, Saint Kits and Nevis); amounts of EUR 5.0 billion (01/01/2013-19/02/2018) and EUR 4.7 billion also appear. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "## OUTGOING – (EUR) 160.2 BILLION"</sub>
