## CASE 15

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 15 (Bahrain FID): in 2022 Bahrain's Financial Intelligence Department discovered a case that launched a full parallel investigation alongside public prosecution and law enforcement. It involved Person A (victim), Person B (perpetrator 1) and Person C (perpetrator 2), showing the lengths perpetrators went to in committing fraud and laundering the profits. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would launch a full parallel investigation alongside public prosecution and law enforcement in that country"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2019 Persons A and B agreed to open a construction company; they registered it at the Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Tourism with A owning 50% and B 50% and capital of BD 250,000, and opened a company bank account. Person A deposited BD 200,000; Person B deposited BD 50,000 and borrowed BD 75,000 from Person A, which was deposited with his capital. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person A owned 50% of the shares and Person B owned the other 50% of the shares"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Person B proposed running the company while A received profits annually; A agreed verbally without signing documents. B ran the company until the whole capital was transferred into B's account and A became a victim of fraud. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person A would agree to this idea verbally without signing any documents"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Person B was charged with fraud, sentenced to five years in prison and ordered by the court to return the amount to Person A, but the amount was no longer in his account and had been laundered. B evaded authorities to avoid arrest while laundering and circulating the entire capital through many bank accounts to hide its source; these events led Bahrain's FID to launch a parallel investigation. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These events led to Bahrain’s FID to launch a parallel investigation with public prosecution and law enforcement."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FID began legal proceedings to obtain a warrant for access to all information on the perpetrators' assets and was permitted to access and freeze all assets and bank accounts. Analysis showed Person B had moved the whole stolen capital of BD 250,000 to his personal account and then to the account of a then-unknown individual. (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Permission was granted by authorities for the FID to access and place a freeze order"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Through additional intelligence, careful analysis and subpoenas to uncover identities, the unknown individual was revealed to be a friend of Person B and became suspect Person C (perpetrator 2), an accomplice; the FID also contacted Person C's clients, whom he had helped open new commercial registrations. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "filing subpoenas to uncover identities, the unknown individual in question was revealed to be a friend of Person B"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Clients reported that Person C knew they wanted to open new businesses and offered them the capital amount, taking it back once the Commercial Registration was completed and charging only BD 300 for his services (per statements). The gathered intelligence went to public prosecution to obtain arrest warrants for B and C. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only paying Person C (BD) 300 for his services (based on given statements)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Three days after the warrant was issued Person C was arrested first and said Person B was hiding at a farm; authorities found and arrested B, who was charged with fraud and is now suspected of money laundering. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Three days after the warrant was issued, Person C was the first person arrested."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The analysis report showed BD 250,000 in Person C's account, transferred from B's account. C opened a document-clearing office using it as capital, helping new investors open commercial registrations, and laundered the money by loaning people BD 50,000 as capital for their new companies so they could deposit it to ease the registration process. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person C opened up a document-clearing office and used this amount as the capital amount for his new company."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Once a registration was opened, the BD 50,000 was debited and returned to Person C's office account, then transferred back to Person B's account; C recirculated it many times, charging BD 300 per clearance service and sending amounts in segments to B until B recovered the whole amount in a seemingly legitimate way, with C taking his profit as accomplice. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person C would also charge (BD) 300 for every commercial registration clearance service"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The perpetrators were arrested and referred to public prosecution to complete legal procedures before referral to court. The investigation highlighted the need for a heightened awareness campaign on the dangers of fraud (subsequently implemented) and precautions against such incidents. (p. 65)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "heightened awareness campaign (subsequently implemented) on the dangers of fraud"</sub>

## CASE 16

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 16 (South Africa FIC): swift work between South Africa's FIU and domestic/foreign law enforcement helped track and trace public funds stolen from the Kingdom of Lesotho and repatriated to South Africa; most proceeds were returned, and nine accused, including seven officials, face fraud and money laundering charges. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "helped track and trace public funds stolen from the Kingdom of Lesotho and repatriated to South Africa"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIC South Africa, Lesotho's FIU, the Lesotho Mounted Police Service and its Directorate of Corruption and Economic Offences (DCEO), and South Africa's Asset Forfeiture Unit (AFU) cooperated closely. Collaboration produced a remarkable recovery rate of proceeds, and prompt action by law enforcement, the FIC, Lesotho's FIU and the banks resulted in successful recovery of funds. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all demonstrated exceptional cooperation, collaboration and respon"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The crime originated in Lesotho, where government officials diverted public funds intended for suppliers into bank accounts of shell companies registered in South Africa; officials siphoned about ZAR 50 million from state coffers, per the Lesotho Mounted Police Service and DCEO investigation. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "government officials diverted public funds intended for suppliers into bank accounts of shell companies registered in South Africa"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Fraudulent payment instructions had been approved by the Lesotho government and effected by the Lesotho Central Bank, which believed the instruction legitimate. More than ZAR 38 million of the ZAR 50 million had been transferred to South African bank accounts in the names of shell companies registered in Lesotho's neighbouring country. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fraudulent payment instructions had been approved by the Lesotho government"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The first tranche of funds was preserved less than a month after the FIC received a transnational information request from the Lesotho FIU. The FIC issued directives on bank accounts temporarily securing material positive balances, and the banks' responsiveness helped ensure funds were not dispersed while the AFU pursued legal proceedings to preserve them. (p. 66)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The first tranche of funds was preserved less than a month"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Lesotho authorities arrested and charged nine suspects, including seven government officials, for theft, fraud and money laundering. ZAR 23.7 million and a fixed property bought for ZAR 2.34 million were preserved, and eleven motor vehicles confiscated. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Amounts of (ZAR) 23.7 million and a fixed property purchased for (ZAR) 2.34 million were preserved."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC first searched its registration and reporting system for regulatory reports containing the bank account numbers (had any been found, it would have obtained bank statements under the FIC Act). None were found, so it queried the banks for account information, balances, bank statement copies and KYC documentation. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "no regulatory reports were found regard"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC compiled a financial flow analysis, in response to the Lesotho FIU request, showing suspects transferred more than ZAR 38 million from the Lesotho government to three South African bank accounts held by shell companies registered in the country. Analysts used open- and closed-source searches to profile suspects and examined international fund transfer data. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIC’s analysts used open- and closed-source searches to profile the suspects"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Bank queries and analysis found the shell companies were newly registered with new bank accounts that had been dormant before the large transfers via Lesotho, and that the banks had filed no regulatory reports on the accounts with the FIC; little financial activity occurred in the accounts before the proceeds were deposited. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The bank accounts related to the shell companies had been dormant before the large money transfers via Lesotho."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC issued 51 enquiries to the banks after receiving affidavits from the AFU in South Africa and the Lesotho government; all banks replied within one to two business days with balances, KYC documents, statements and contra account details. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIC issued 51 enquiries to the identified banks"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC ascertained that the positive balances were proceeds of the crime, giving due consideration to possible co-mingling of funds and to account balances before the proceeds were received. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "due consideration was given to possible co-mingling of funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC followed up with directives to freeze the accounts; under FIC regulations accounts may be frozen for ten business days, which gave time to prepare and submit an urgent ex-parte preservation application so funds were not dissipated. A financial flow chart helped visualize links between subjects and other bank accounts. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "According to FIC regulations, accounts may be frozen for ten business days."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Funds were traced further by analyzing contra account details and transaction descriptions (for example a description could include a vehicle's make and model), but a vehicle purchase was not assumed and was confirmed with contra account details. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "However, it was not assumed that a motor vehicle was purchased"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC started its analysis from the AFU and Lesotho FIU requests, focusing on the subjects and account numbers provided, and kept regular contact with the investigating officer (exchanging information on investigation progress and financial analysis), with banks (ensuring correct information and documents were obtained on time), and with conveyancers. (p. 67)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIC received requests for information from the AFU and Lesotho FIU"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Analysis showed subjects bought immovable properties. It established that the conveyancers were not subjects (not assisting with money laundering) and could be approached as accountable institutions. Funds went into conveyancers' trust accounts, then to third-party administration accounts opened to facilitate the purchases; registration of some properties was incomplete, so balances could be secured without affecting the conveyancers' businesses, and the FIC secured the interest of the proceeds in those accounts. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Funds were received into their trust accounts for the purchase of properties."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIC provided its analysis findings to the AFU and Lesotho FIU as they became available, and they acted to preserve or trace funds further. The FIC's contributions: identifying involved persons and entities, profiling subjects, tracing funds and assets, transactional analysis showing fund flow, identifying suspects benefiting from stolen funds, identifying entities and accounts used to launder funds, and identifying movable and immovable assets. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The findings of the FIC’s analysis were provided to the AFU and the Lesotho FIU as the information became available."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Directives to banks secured funds in accounts with material balances for ten business days, during which the AFU prepared and submitted an ex-parte preservation application. Time was of the essence to trace funds for inclusion while ensuring directives did not lapse before the preservation order; timely reports on secured funds and other implicated accounts went to the AFU and Lesotho FIU. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while ensuring the directives did not lapse before the preservation order was obtained"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIC filed affidavits supporting a successful AFU preservation order application under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act before the Free State High Court in South Africa, and the process will be repeated if further material positive balances are identified. FIC Act regulations allow the FIC to freeze proceeds of crime for ten business days. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "brought before the Free State High Court in South Africa"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: ZAR 23.7 million and a fixed property of ZAR 2.34 million were preserved and 11 vehicles confiscated; more than ZAR 38 million was laundered from Lesotho to South Africa; the AFU awaits conclusion of the forfeiture process. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The AFU is awaiting conclusion of the forfeiture process."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Lesotho government detected the fraudulent payment instructions during bank statement reconciliation; Lesotho treasury officials allegedly forged payment instructions to the Central Bank of Lesotho. The Lesotho FIU asked the FIC for information once it was discovered the funds had gone to accounts of front companies registered in South Africa. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "detected fraudulent payment instructions during their bank statement reconciliation processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIC's analysis of bank statements established the flow of proceeds from Lesotho to South African banks and traced other assets; its ability to secure funds for ten business days contributed extensively to preserving more than ZAR 23 million. (p. 68)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "contributed extensively to the successful preservation of more than (ZAR) 23 million in proceeds of crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Eight of the nine suspects were granted bail and one of them died after release; the main suspects remain under arrest and have not been convicted. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Eight of the nine suspects were granted bail."</sub>

## Trade-Based & Third-Party Money Laundering

- **descriptive / definition**: Trade-based and third-party money laundering involves disguising proceeds of criminal activity as payments for imports/exports or other transactions to facilitate illegal transfer of illicit funds; it is described as the most common type of money laundering globally and is often carried out by professional money launderers with specialized knowledge using varied risk-mitigating methods. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is the most common type of money laundering globally."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Because of their complicated, interconnected nature, global supply chains are used by organized crime, professional money launderers and TF networks to launder proceeds (e.g., from drug trafficking), finance terrorism and circumvent sanctions. Customs, law enforcement, FIUs, tax and other authorities find trade-related ML harder to detect than other activities, and even the most knowledgeable are hindered by its complexity and changing techniques. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "drug trafficking), financing terrorism and circumventing sanctions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FIUs are responsible for strategic analysis of their databases to detect ML/TF typologies and emerging trends, and for ongoing independent operative analyses to track suspicious targets; they notify authorities of illicit activities detected. The cases in this section illustrate techniques of individuals directly or indirectly involved in trade-based and third-party money laundering. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs are responsible for strategic analysis of their databases to detect ML/TF typology and emerging trends"</sub>
