## CASE 24

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 24 (Australia AUSTRAC, Third-Party Cash Deposit Project) concerns criminal use of Intelligent Deposit Machines (IDMs), ATMs offering high-speed, large-capacity cash deposits into a bank account, sometimes without identification or a bank card (for third parties). IDMs gave criminals anonymity, speed, efficiency and 24/7 access for laundering funds. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sometimes without needing identification or a bank card (for third-parties)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The investigation began in 2019 when the Western Australia Police Force (WAPF) partnered with AUSTRAC and industry partners to investigate a Perth-based organized crime syndicate using IDMs to launder proceeds, and evolved through the Third-Party Cash Deposit Project into identifying a laundering syndicate controlled from Europe that directed Australian proceeds of crime to a Southeast Asian country, leading to arrests in Australia and internationally. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a money laundering syndicate controlled from Europe"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: AUSTRAC focused on characteristics of depositors rather than the account holder, using innovative analysis techniques. Over six weeks depositors made 1,879 cash deposits via IDMs into 167 different bank accounts linked to the syndicate, totalling about AUD 5.4 million; this led WAPF to arrest five syndicate members, and while the arrests briefly disrupted laundering, AUSTRAC identified common laundering techniques used in other Australian cities. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "depositors made 1,879 cash deposits via IDMs into 167 differ"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC set up co-located operational hubs for timely, secure exchange of information and operational analysis, where security-vetted financial industry members worked with AUSTRAC analysts on classified matters to identify financial patterns and predict where and when syndicates would target IDMs. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC established co-located operational hubs to ensure the timely and secure exchange of information"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC expanded use of the indicators to target the broader money laundering risk through the cross-agency Third-Party Cash Deposit Project, aiming to respond to the risks the methodology identified and provide value-added intelligence to law enforcement. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC expanded the use of the indicators to target the broader money laundering risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Using its powers under Australia's AML/CTF legislation, AUSTRAC issued notices to private sector entities requesting 12 months of data equating to over 7.8 million IDM transactions, engaging industry to help draft the notices so the information requested was relevant and could be produced in a timely manner. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "over 7.8 million transactions conducted through IDMs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: AUSTRAC developed a new risk assessment of third-party cash deposits through IDMs and shared it with law enforcement and industry as a reference on typologies and behavioral patterns. In response, Australia's four largest banks reviewed their processes and tightened controls around third-party cash deposit rules for IDMs, mitigating vulnerabilities. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Australia’s four largest banks conducted a review of their processes and tightened controls"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC created a catalogue of IDM cash deposits made with a specific bank card or mobile phone number, which proved critical for physical law enforcement surveillance in establishing deposit patterns and identifying previously unknown accounts of interest. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC created a catalogue of IDM cash deposits using a specific bank card or mobile phone number."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Where AUSTRAC suspected a syndicate controlled multiple accounts, it analyzed internet banking login information to determine whether the same person controlled them. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC analyzed internet banking login information to determine whether the same person was controlling the accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The project also led to seizure of cash and illicit goods and disruption of one of Australia's largest money laundering syndicates. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "disruption of one of Australia’s largest money laundering syndicates"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC worked with industry to develop new analysis techniques, including clustering, and used trusted partnerships for a holistic picture of the risk. After identifying the IDM risk in 2019 it led the project through Fintel Alliance with law enforcement and industry partners, allowing indicators and findings to be developed further and transferred to a wider cohort of entities. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an analytical technique called clustering"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC developed a methodology identifying deposits with characteristics of a professional money laundering organization based on time between deposits, number of deposits and total value (clustered deposits or clusters). Analysis of about 7.8 million third-party IDM transactions valued at AUD 4.8 billion identified patterns matching the risk assessment's typologies. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "based on the time between deposits, number of deposits and total value"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AUSTRAC introduced a secure information sharing platform with video conferencing, instant messaging and document management, useful during COVID-19 lockdowns by letting analysts keep liaising with investigators. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AUSTRAC also introduced a secure information sharing platform with video conferencing"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the AUSTRAC Third-Party Cash Deposit Project, joint project members in the Fintel Alliance public-private partnership project shared information and resources to deepen their understanding of IDM vulnerabilities and to identify deposit patterns consistent with money laundering typologies. AUSTRAC shared these learnings with law enforcement partners to help investigators identify, analyze and handle financial information in support of prosecutions. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understanding of IDM vulnerabilities and identify deposit patterns consistent with money laundering typologies."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: A 'Deposit Clustering Algorithm' example shows four deposits: $2500 (Account A, 11:00am), $2500 (Account A, 11:03am), $1000 (Account B, 11:07am) and $2000 (Account B, 11:12am), a total deposited of more than $7000 across more than one beneficiary account. The criteria shown are at least four consecutive transactions with less than five minutes between each deposit. No currency is named in the example. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "11:00am 11:03am 11:07am 11:12am

At least 4 consecutive transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The project allowed easier dissemination of analyzed information and shared insights and feedback among partners, linking law enforcement with government and industry on operational matters, which enhanced suspicious matter reporting and prioritized responses to law enforcement requests. Joint work leveraged Fintel Alliance, the AUSTRAC-led public private partnership (PPP), and created a PPP project on the topic. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These opportunities enhanced suspicious matter reporting and prioritized responses to law enforcement requests for information."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The broader project outcome was hardening the environment to money laundering, with Australia's four largest banks reviewing and tightening controls on third-party cash deposit rules involving IDMs. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The broader project outcome was hardening the environment to money laundering."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Since resolution law enforcement seized: AUD 4 million in cash; 270kg of illicit tobacco; illicit drugs including cocaine, MDMA, methamphetamine and cannabis; firearms and ammunition; encrypted communication devices; and luxury vehicles. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "270kg of illicit tobacco."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Thirteen people were arrested and charged with multiple money laundering offences. Intelligence products produced: a risk assessment; a methodology report; two information reports for industry; spontaneous information disclosures to other FIUs; 14 notices to private sector entities; 28 tactical intelligence reports; 114 information disclosures; and triage of 568 Suspicious Matter Reports. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The project saw thirteen people arrested and charged with multiple money laundering offences."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 24 initial indicator: large amounts of cash deposited at IDMs in quick succession; deposits often occurred around midnight, when IDM or account deposit thresholds reset, allowing greater cash volumes in one session. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The initial indicator was large amounts of cash being deposited at IDMs in quick succession."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 24 indicator: anonymous third parties transferring funds in quick succession to accounts with which they had no clear relationship. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "anonymous third parties were transferring funds in quick suc"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 24 indicator: deposits at IDMs into accounts often made in brief windows of time with successive identical deposit amounts, which enabled an analytic technique focused on clustered deposits. (p. 102)[^original]

## WHAT MAKES A GOOD CASE

- **descriptive / context**: The Best Egmont Case Awards (BECA) competition is an annual Egmont Group event where members showcase exceptional work; a panel of judges evaluates cases against criteria defining what makes each interesting and educational, considering complexity and outcomes. The cases span the past four years, some still ongoing due to lengthy judicial procedures, and a few began before evaluation and recently concluded. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Best Egmont Case Awards (BECA) competition is a highly anticipated event"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Cases still before the courts are adjusted to avoid compromising ongoing investigations. Case complexity is assessed from intelligence-gathering methods, challenges faced, use of open-source criteria, and intelligence collected from domestic entities and foreign jurisdictions through the Egmont Secure Web. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Those cases still before the courts are adjusted to avoid compromising ongoing investigations."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FIU collaborates closely with law enforcement to identify financial and non-financial instruments involved, coordinating with police, prosecutors, judges, taxation and customs authorities and other entities to build a comprehensive picture of complex ML schemes. Selected cases showcase FIU analysts gathering information from public and private entities and untangling financial, legal and corporate obfuscation. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "coordinating activities with police, prosecutors, judges, taxation and customs authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FIU frequently advises and supports prosecutors and police in building cases that withstand judicial review. Because financial crimes are often international, the FIU gathers information and intelligence from other Egmont members, used for mutual legal assistance requests, ultimately to freeze criminal assets hidden abroad. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FIU frequently provides advice and support to prosecutors and police in building strong cases to withstand judicial review."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The cases illustrate how FIUs centralize effective domestic and international coordination, leading to arrest, conviction and sentencing of criminals and recovery of stolen assets; in each case the FIU plays a pivotal role in uncovering schemes, bringing perpetrators to justice and confiscating illicit assets through judicial procedures. Success is measured by effectively sanctioning criminals and recovering hidden stolen assets. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The success of these cases is measured by effectively sanctioning criminals and recovering their hidden stolen assets."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FIU analysts can collaborate with financial institutions, domestic law enforcement and foreign FIUs to discover novel schemes that would otherwise go unnoticed. Many cases show successful FIU-private sector collaboration through public-private partnerships, where banks and other institutions gather information (guided by the FIU) on suspicious clients and share information that may indicate criminal activity; these partnerships are said to enhance financial system integrity, eliminate illicit actors and ensure a level playing field. (p. 105)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Banks and other financial and non-financial institutions demonstrate a willingness to assist in these cases"</sub>

## Clear Demonstration of the Evolution of the Case

- **expected / expectation**: Each case should illustrate how it evolved and how feedback enhanced its development, ultimately leading to a positive outcome; challenges in identifying the ML scheme should be described, any new ML techniques or trends identified, and a successful outcome demonstrated.
