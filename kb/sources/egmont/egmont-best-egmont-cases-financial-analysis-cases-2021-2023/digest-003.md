## CASE 5

- **descriptive / case example**: France Tracfin case 'Financing terrorism with prepaid vouchers': between 2019 and 2023 Tracfin's investigations showed crypto assets can be an effective terrorism financing tool, with anonymously bought vouchers converted into crypto assets; analysis identified a complex new TF scheme mixing financial tools, contributing to several TF convictions and toughened domestic and European legislation. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "anonymously purchased vouchers are converted into crypto assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Tracfin opened the case on an STR on a possible TF scheme from an e-money company providing e-money accounts and prepaid vouchers; Company Y sold, through a France-wide retailer network (e.g., tobacconists, news agents), prepaid vouchers with a flash code or PIN convertible into bitcoin, which could only be primo-credited into a portfolio managed by Company Y. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company Y was selling prepaid vouchers bearing a flash code or a PIN"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Financial circuit: a European e-money institution issued e-money in Country A; an e-money distributor in another EU country (Country B) loaded it onto vouchers; a VASP in France (Company Y) distributed vouchers to retailers; retailers collected payments using cash register software from Company Z, which records the payment method but not the customer's identity; Company Z remitted funds to the Country B distributor, which then transferred funds to Company Y. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A European e-money institution would issue"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: First-level analysis: with direct access to law enforcement databases Tracfin cross-checked two suspects' names and established they had been sentenced in absentia to 10-year terms for conspiracy to commit terrorism and were at risk of returning to France to commit attacks. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sentenced in absentia to 10-year prison senten­ ces for conspiracy to commit terrorism"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The first scheme was short-lived because financial institutions blocked it at the KYC phase, but the suspects found new techniques to circumvent KYC using digital tools to avoid supervisory mechanisms of traditional cash transfer channels; in the new scheme vouchers bought in France funded crypto wallets held by jihadists in a war zone. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "vouchers were purchased on French territory to fund crypto wallets held by jihadists in a war zone"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because voucher e-money was used only to buy bitcoins held by Company Y, Company Y only met its due diligence obligations when it transferred the bitcoins to the customer's crypto-asset wallet or one it created. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company Y only fulfilled its due diligence obligations when it transferred those bitcoins"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Tracfin adapted by combining its counter-terrorism investigations team and crypto-asset analysis team, the latter using blockchain analysis to uncover financial information on several individuals. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The latter team used blockchain analysis to help uncover financial information"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Tracfin found two collectors affiliated with a jihadist group who opened two crypto-asset wallets collecting bitcoins converted from vouchers and, via intermediaries and exchange offices, sent money for a fee to jihadists in war zones. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Tracfin uncovered the central role played by two collectors"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Method of transfer: a customer bought a voucher in cash from a retailer and sent its details (flash code or PIN) by encrypted message to a combatant in a war zone, who presented them to a local exchange office; the office checked validity with help of a suspect's relative, the value was credited to a suspect's crypto wallet and routed through bitcoin address clusters to a VASP, which cleared the transaction with an exchange office in the war zone using the hawala system, and funds were paid out in cash minus a commission. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The exchange office would check the validity of"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Through this scheme more than EUR 250,000 was transferred to the terrorist organisation over 10 months. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "more than (EUR) 250,000 was transferred to the terrorist organisation over 10 months"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Tracfin relied on coordination with national administrative and judicial authorities, including direct access to law enforcement databases and exchanges with judicial authorities throughout the case. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "direct access to law enforcement databases and the engagement with judicial authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Cooperation with the private sector involved 100 requests for information to 15 reporting entities and calls for vigilance on the typology; the public-private partnership's quality played a decisive role. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "100 requests for information were sent to 15 reporting entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Cooperation with FIUs: more than 15 requests for information went to five foreign FIUs (Luxembourg, Belgium, UK, Malta and Germany), essential in identifying methods used to collect funds (voucher and timestamp information, e-money or bank accounts, VASP activity). (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sent to five foreign FIUs (Luxembourg, Belgium, UK, Malta and Germany)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Tracfin received its first crypto-asset STR in 2014 and such STRs grew to 518 in 2018, when it created a specialized unit for virtual asset cases responsible for raising stakeholder awareness and handling crypto-asset cases. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reaching 518 in 2018"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: Tracfin identified a new TF typology combining several assets enabling anonymity. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Tracfin identified a new typology of TF combining several assets enabling anonymity."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Using replies from reporting entities and exchanges with counterpart FIUs, Tracfin gave French judicial authorities and LEAs accurate, timely information enabling exploitation of video surveillance records and targeted police operations to identify anonymous voucher purchasers. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "set up targeted police operations aimed at identifying the anonymous purchasers of vouchers"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case confirmed the prepaid voucher and crypto asset sectors present heightened TF risk, considered in the September 2019 French National Risk Assessment, which names new financing methods such as prepaid cards as a high-risk TF vehicle. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The case confirmed that the prepaid voucher sector and the crypto asset sector present a heightened TF risk."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Combining traditional FIU investigative methods (requests to reporting entities and foreign FIUs, exchanges with domestic authorities) with newer techniques such as blockchain analysis can produce highly successful results. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The combination of traditional FIU investigative methods"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Tracfin contributed to identifying and arresting 29 donors in France in 2021 (63 later in total) and two suspected facilitators; crypto-asset wallets at VASPs were seized, the main suspect was added to the EU terrorist sanctions list freezing assets of relatives, and sentences (including suspended ones) totaled 130 months. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identification and arrests of 29 donors in France in 2021"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: After September 2020 French AML/CFT laws on VASP obligations changed; in 2021 legislation banned anonymous e-money to buy crypto assets and made it mandatory for VASPs to identify customers for all transactions, even occasional ones from the first euro; European legislation followed in April 2023. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "made it mandatory for VASPs to identify customers for all transactions"</sub>

(p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Checks vouchers validity and activates voucher"</sub>
