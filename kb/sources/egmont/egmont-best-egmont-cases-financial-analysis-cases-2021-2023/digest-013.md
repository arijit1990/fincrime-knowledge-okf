## CASE 22

- **descriptive / case example**: In 2017 JAFIC received notification from FIU-Nepal that a Nepalese bank's SWIFT interbank messaging service had been compromised, followed by fraudulent transfers of stolen money to multiple accounts outside Nepal. Eight transactions of about JPY 197 million (USD 1.7 million) were requested to Japanese bank accounts and two, about JPY 18 million (USD 155,000), were completed. The police, assisted via ICPO and central authority routes, identified the predicate offences as fraud. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had been compromised followed by fraudulent fund transfer of stolen money to multiple accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The case raised suspicions that suspects had refused to return their money by claiming the transfers were for legitimate dealings. JAFIC also learned that STRs on other declined cases showed suspicions of fraud involving substantial money transfers. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "claiming the transfers were for the purpose of legitimate dealings"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: JAFIC's STR analysis identified the account beneficiaries of the two fraudulent fund transfer destinations and learned the transferred money had been withdrawn immediately after the transactions. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transferred money had been withdrawn immediately after transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: JAFIC correlated the case with US fraud cases by reaching out to FBI Tokyo; with that information right after the investigation began, the prefectural police could form a complete picture of the laundering scheme and its correlation with the predicate offenses. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "JAFIC successfully correlated a case under analysis with fraud cases in the U.S."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the course of its money-flow analysis, JAFIC learned that FBI Tokyo had asked the Japanese National Police Agency for cooperation on other fraudulent money transfer cases involving one of the two beneficiaries of the fraudulent transfers, referred to as 'A'. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the course of JAFIC’s analysis of the money flows, JAFIC learned the FBI Tokyo requested Japanese National Police Agency"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Based on information shared by FBI Tokyo, JAFIC found that a Japanese company's bank account was the destination of a fraudulent money transfer from the United States. The money went from the company's account to A's account and was then retransferred from A's account to the bank account of another person, referred to as 'B'. JAFIC's analysis also showed the company's account was the destination of other fraudulent fund transfers, and these findings significantly contributed to the Japanese police investigation. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The money was transferred from the company’s bank account to A’s one"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: JAFIC is an administrative-type FIU established within the National Police Agency. To assist prefectural police (who have authority to arrest suspects) it analyzed STRs and information from overseas organizations, starting from FIU-Nepal input, then domestic STRs, and FBI Tokyo information. It told prefectural police that fraudulent transfers from Nepal and US accounts went to B's account via A's and the money was highly possibly concealed in Japan. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "JAFIC is an administrative-type FIU, which has been established within the National Police Agency."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 22 indicators: immediate withdrawal of received money; unsubstantiated source of funds; inconsistency with customer profile, namely a large amount transferred from multiple foreign jurisdictions to a bank account in a short time span. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inconsistency with a customer profile: a large amount of money was transferred from multiple foreign jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: With JAFIC's whole-picture intelligence, prefectural police identified the defrauded foreign-bank money piled up in B's account. Six suspects including A and B were arrested for "Concealment of Proceeds of Crime" (predicates: eight fraud cases), total JPY 200 million (USD 1.3 million), proven with evidence including some from international mutual investigation assistance. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Six suspects including A and B were arrested in charge of “Concealment of Proceeds of Crime,”"</sub>

## CASE 23

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 23 (Mexico FIU): a 2019 investigation into a transnational human trafficking network that recruited women, mainly from Venezuela, to Mexico under the pretense of work as models or teachers and then sexually exploited them; it involved laundering through five shell companies. Some individuals had been investigated before and had arrest warrants for human trafficking and sexual exploitation; the investigation led to the leader's arrest. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "brought to Mexico under the pretense of being hired as models or teachers but were later exploited sexually"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: SARs and information from an obligated subject (OS) among DNFBPs were detected through the FIU-MX risk model, identifying Subject A, leader and founder of a criminal organization (OC) dedicated to human trafficking and sexual exploitation. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "were detected through the FIU-MX risk model"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The OC leaders (Subjects A and B, spouses) contacted women abroad, mainly in South America, with a deceptive offer of work as models or teachers. On arrival passports were taken and returned only on settlement of a debt for travel, lodging, food and other expenses, to be repaid with sexual services. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their passports were taken and told they would be returned upon settlement of a debt"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Clients contracted services through a website, agreeing on profile, date, time, location and payment method; network members passed details to the victim, and payments were said to reduce the victim's debt and pay fees for advertising on the website. The website carried a catalog of victims with photographs modified to prevent family recognition; Company A owned it, with Subjects A and C (arrested for human trafficking and sexual exploitation) as shareholders. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The OC used a website with a catalog of the victims"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Proceeds were placed in the financial system through cash deposits and transfers (SPEI's) into shell companies created to hide origin and stratify funds among OC members via salary payments as if they were workers; Subject A finally integrated resources through real estate and virtual assets. Part of the payments covered operational expenses (e.g., salaries of drivers/escorts ensuring victims did not escape) and victims' expenses. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Finally, Subject A integrated the resources through the acquisition of real estate and virtual assets."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The DNFBP obligated subject complied with the LFPIORPI by correctly identifying its client and sending operation information to FIU-MX; with inter-institutional collaboration this allowed locating and arresting Subject A, a fugitive for two years, and developing a case that identified the OC's ML network, which became a starting point for other cases. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Compliance with the LFPIORPI by the DNFBP’s OS by correctly identifying its client"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FIU-MX identified partners, names, companies used to launder money and the main assets acquired. It signed multiple MOUs with other Mexican agencies to obtain additional information; tax information identified the companies that laundered the OC's money by placing cash in them and stratifying it through salary payments to Subject A. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it has signed multiple memorandums of understanding (MOU) with other Mexican agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A DNFBP in the real estate sector alerted on Subject A's acquisition of real estate in 2016-2018 worth USD 121,588.82 because, on identifying its client, open sources showed arrest warrants for human trafficking and sexual exploitation; this information and inter-institutional collaboration led to locating and arresting the leader. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for the acquisition of real estate in the period from 2016 to 2018 for a value of (USD) $121,588.82"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A police report was filed with the Attorney General's Office (FGR) for ML with human trafficking and sexual exploitation as predicate crime against 18 individuals and legal entities. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "against 18 individuals and legal entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The remainder of the proceeds of the human trafficking and sexual exploitation was laundered by the criminal organisation with its leaders (Subjects A and B) as final beneficiaries, through shell companies that simulated salary payments. Those payments were identified by collecting tax information, which made it possible to identify the amounts the companies paid to the leader and to see how this gave the proceeds a legal appearance.
