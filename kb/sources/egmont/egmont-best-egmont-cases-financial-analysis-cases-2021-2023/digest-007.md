## CASE 13

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 13 (Italy UIF and Hungary's FIU): repeated cash withdrawals from Italian ATMs used payment cards held by Hungarian shell companies with Italian business owners. The withdrawn money was sent abroad by Italian shell companies funded mostly by active Italian firms, which took fiscal advantage through false invoices that generated deductible expenses and input VAT to reduce their tax burden. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "generating deductible expenses and input VAT to reduce tax burden"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Collaboration between the Italian and Hungarian FIUs improved analysis of money laundering schemes involving funds linked to fiscal and other crimes that exploited misuse of foreign payment cards. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Collaboration between Italian and Hungarian FIUs improved the analysis of Money Laundering (ML) schemes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The case involved a sophisticated fraudulent tax scheme and confirmed the versatility of fiscal offences based on false invoicing, described as a particular social concern in Italy. The two FIUs' combined expertise led to discovery of a strategy aimed at tax avoidance and at repatriating to Italy funds sent abroad without adequate taxation. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confirmed the versatility of fiscal offences based on false invoicing"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In 2019-2020 UIF received STRs on anomalous use of foreign credit cards involving systematic withdrawals below the EUR 1,000 threshold from ATMs in Northern Italy. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "systematic withdrawals below the threshold of (EUR) 1,000 from ATMs in Northern Italy"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: UIF's initial analysis of ATM locations and withdrawal frequency identified concentrations of ATMs in an area and connections between different cards used at the same ATMs in sequence within a few minutes. The amounts and frequency of withdrawals with foreign cards issued by Hungarian financial intermediaries led to the assumption of a large-scale laundering operation exploiting the Italian and Hungarian financial systems. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in sequence within a few minutes"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The reporting entities (ATM owners) knew card numbers (PAN) and single-operation details (data, time, amount, location) but lacked access to cardholder identity; the recurrence of withdrawals with the same cards was considered a risk element, especially where significant cash amounts were withdrawn. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The recurrence of withdrawals made with the same cards was considered an element of risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Part of the funds were moved through a system of false invoices to Hungarian bank accounts held by the Italian fraudsters, then returned to Italy using Italian ATM payment cards linked to the Hungarian accounts. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Part of these funds were transferred through a system of false invoices to Hungarian bank accounts held by the Italian"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: UIF asked its Hungarian counterpart about the origin of the cash-withdrawn funds, cardholders and related accounts. Because many entities were involved, the analysis was focused on a cluster of six Italian enterprises that sent funds to four Hungarian firms. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a cluster of six Italian enterprises that sent funds to four Hungarian firms"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Using new operational and strategic analysis techniques, structured information exchanges and further bilateral initiatives, UIF and HFIU identified the illicit scheme. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "resorting to structured exchanges of information and further bilateral initiatives"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: During the same period FIU Hungary received a growing number of STRs reporting Hungarian companies for receiving considerable funds from Italian remitters; HFIU shared many spontaneous communications on the scheme with UIF and started a strategic analysis. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU Hungary received a growing number of STRs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: UIF identified Hungary as home country of the card-issuing intermediaries by examining the first six digits of each card's PAN, then sent requests to Hungarian counterparts to identify cardholders acting in Italy and linked accounts. Replies and card issuers' information showed the origin of the funds (Italian companies) and the withdrawal areas (the highly industrialized Northern regions). (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by examining the first six digits of each card’s PAN1"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Cardholders were Italian citizens using funds from newly established Hungarian companies held by Italian beneficial owners, with very simple corporate governance despite huge financial turnover. Their accounts showed similar patterns: credited exclusively by wire transfers from Italian firms (often connected to the Hungarian companies' beneficial owners), with no apparent legitimate business, appearing to be shell companies solely to monetize illicit Italian funds. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "very simple corporate governance structure, despite having huge financial turnover"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UIF's final analysis of the Italian companies ordering transfers to Hungary, using FIU archives, financial statements and banking operations, verified that the cash withdrawals served to repatriate funds linked to invoices for non-existent transactions. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "verify cash withdrawals were functional in repatriating funds related to invoices for non-existent transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: HFIU's spontaneous disseminations disclosed amounts of transactions, holders of cards and subsidy accounts and origin of funds but did not indicate card PANs, so they could not be connected with STRs from Italian reporting entities. UIF therefore requested the PAN codes of all cards linked to the accounts HFIU had shared; this extensive bilateral exchange was essential to exploit information held by both FIUs. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they did not indicate the PAN of the payment cards"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: UIF used network analysis techniques to detect other schemes misusing foreign payment cards, for example frauds in trading Energy Efficiency Certificates obtained through energy efficiency projects never actually carried out. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "UIF used network analysis techniques to detect other schemes characterized by the misuse of foreign payment cards"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 1: the primary account number (PAN) is the card identifier on payment cards; the card number prefix identifies the issuer, and the following digits are used by the issuer to identify the cardholder as a customer, which the issuer associates with the customer's designated bank accounts. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The primary account number (PAN) is the card identifier found on payment cards."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UIF estimated that in 2019-2020 Italian shell companies sent EUR 40 million to the Hungarian companies examined, with Italian ATM withdrawals of EUR 37 million. HFIU's strategic analysis for the same period showed wire transfers from Italian to Hungarian companies of approximately EUR 200 million and withdrawals exceeding EUR 230 million. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Italian shell companies sent (EUR) 40 million to the Hungarian companies examined in the investigation"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 13 indicator: repeated ATM cash withdrawals for large amounts with foreign payment cards are a trigger that assumes underlying illegal activity. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Repeated ATM cash withdrawals for large amounts involving foreign payment cards is a trigger"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UIF shared its analysis with law enforcement; in early March 2021 it received positive feedback about ongoing penal proceedings against some named subjects, and Italian law enforcement asked UIF to obtain further details from HFIU. An Italian court jailed a beneficial owner of certain Italian and Hungarian companies, and HFIU's analysis shared with Hungarian law enforcement triggered two criminal investigations on suspicion of ML. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In early March 2021, it resulted in positive feedback about ongoing penal proceedings"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The case uncovered vulnerabilities in the international AML regulatory framework, especially the potential role of companies managing payment networks, which hold complete information on cardholders' transactions. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "uncovered some of the vulnerabilities in the international anti-money laundering regulatory framework"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 13 indicator: national and foreign shell companies that are recently established, have simple corporate governance, lack updated financial statements, have many changes of offices, officers or shareholders, and have newly opened bank accounts with incoherent turnover and little accounting data, exploited exclusively to move funds unlawfully abroad. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lack of updated financial statements, many corporate changes to their offices, officers or shareholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Several criminal cases remain open in both countries on tax crimes based on false invoicing and frauds in the energy efficiency certificates market in Italy, and on money laundering in Hungary. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Several criminal cases remain open in both countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: These analyses prompted debate within both FIUs, leading them to encourage financial intermediaries to monitor potential misuse of foreign payment cards carefully, and to emphasize cooperation with bar associations responsible for AML and supervision of attorneys and other professionals (e.g., accountants, notaries, labour consultants) who could be involved in organizing illicit schemes. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leading them to encourage financial intermediaries to carefully monitor potential misuse of foreign payment cards"</sub>
- **expected / finding or result**: In the Italy/Hungary card-withdrawal case, the bankcard numbers linked to the foreign bank accounts are described as vital information, and the case states that mandatory cooperation is needed between the FIUs involved to identify the network of bank transactions and the active shell companies behind it. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mandatory cooperation is needed between the FIUs involved to identify the network of bank transactions"</sub>
- **permissive / finding or result**: The case states that the network analysis and pattern recognition techniques used can be exploited to identify suspicious money-laundering activity linked to other types of crime, because cash withdrawals, shell companies and false-invoicing systems are all ways of disguising and covering up funds from several illegal activities. (p. 59)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The aforementioned techniques of network analysis and pattern recognition can be exploited"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: A diagram titled Scheme 1 (cash withdrawal scheme from Hungarian shell companies for tax evasion) has an Italy side showing Italian companies involved in fiscal frauds, systems of false invoicing and Italian shell companies, and a Hungary side showing systems of false invoicing, cash withdrawals, Hungarian shell companies and corporate officers/shareholders, with credit wire transfers and withdrawals in cash as flows. Its labels give four estimates: EUR 83 million and EUR 86 million estimated by the Italian FIU, and EUR 230 million and EUR 200 million estimated by the Hungarian FIU (the EUR 200 million label sits beside credit wire transfers and the EUR 86 million label beside withdrawals in cash); the exact placement of the EUR 83 million and EUR 230 million labels is not clear from the extracted diagram text. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Scheme 1: Cash withdrawal scheme from Hungarian shell companies for the purpose of tax evasion."</sub>

## CASE 14

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 14 (Taiwan AMLD): since the end of 2019 Taiwan's FIU received STRs showing same or similar red flags on certain online banking accounts, linked them to crimes such as fraud and online gambling, shared a report with authorities, and with public-private cooperation its analysis assisted law enforcement investigations. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "received Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) indicating same or similar red flags related to certain online banking accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AMLD's initial detection analyzed STRs from January to December 2019. After studying the typology of dummy accounts it completed a strategic report sent to all relevant authorities, which led to opening two cases, in which subjects systematically collected dummy accounts and used them themselves or provided them to others for crime. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AMLD analyzed STRs from January to December of 2019"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Dummy account indicator: nearly all owners (over 99%) were Taiwanese nationals; 60% of owners were aged 21 to 30 (most of whom had just graduated or were new to the job market) and 25% were aged 31 to 40. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Nearly all owners (over 99%) of these dummy"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Dummy account indicator: owners set up the same username and password for online banking and designated the same transfer accounts; after setup a small amount was transferred in via ATM or online banking as a test that the online banking and ATM cards worked. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They also designated the same transfer accounts."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Dummy account indicator: the account owners were not related to each other; half had no criminal records and the other half had records related to fraud, gambling, narcotics and violent crime. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These account owners are not related."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Dummy account indicator: 68% of owners had no tax declaration record, and 31% had annual income under NTD 500,000 (approximately USD 33,000). (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "of tax declaration, and 31% of the owners’ annual income was under (NTD) 500,000 or approximately USD 33,000."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Usage indicator: 62% of the accounts stayed dormant and 38% were active; once activated an account had a very short life span (one to six months) with frequent transactions. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "38% were active. Once an account was activated, it had a very short life span (between one to six months)."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Usage indicator: all active accounts had frequent log-in and transaction activity with IP addresses indicating different geographic locations, and 80% of active dummy accounts transferred funds with third-party payment processors. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their IP address indicated these activities originated from different geographic locations."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: From account activity, the potential predicate offences linked to the active dummy accounts might include telecom fraud, online gambling, underground banking system and illegal fundraising. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "predicate offense related to these active dummy accounts might include telecom fraud, online gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Two key success elements: financial institutions filed STRs immediately when irregularities were detected, and there was domestic cooperation with relevant authorities including the financial institutions' supervisor and local law enforcement. The FIU acted as intermediary linking financial institutions' early warning and law enforcement investigation. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the financial institutes filed STRs to the FIU immediately when irregularities were detected"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: AMLD found connections among some reported dummy accounts, beneficiaries and criminal cases then being detected by law enforcement, such as illegal online gambling, cyber fraud and underground banking. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AMLD found connection among some of the reported dummy accounts, beneficiaries and criminal cases."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Online tele fraud: Mr. C, director of Company X, used social networking and communications apps to buy dummy accounts. Owners followed Company X's instructions to apply for online banking, set the same username and designate specific transfer accounts. Company X lured victims to transfer money into these accounts, then withdrew cash from ATMs or moved it to other Company X accounts via online banking. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "used social networking and communications apps to purchase dummy accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: AMLD reviewed all STRs showing similar suspicious activity (different accounts with the same online banking username and password); the analysis involved more than 1,000 dummy accounts and more than 50 designated transfer accounts. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There were more than 1,000 dummy accounts and more than 50 designated transfer accounts involved in this analysis."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account owners' statements indicated some saw online advertisements for business or recruiting opportunities and some sold their accounts to unknown criminal groups via social media or apps. They followed instructions from unspecific persons to activate online banking and reset username and password, then handed over the account book and ATM card, indicating that certain persons or groups collected dummy accounts intentionally. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some sold their bank accounts to unknown criminal groups or gangs via social media or other communication apps."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Online gambling: Mr. J set up Company W for online gambling, supported by a subsidiary providing hardware maintenance, client services and database management. Most gamblers transferred funds into dummy accounts held by Company W, which from 2016 to 2020 received approximately NTD 59.5 billion (approximately USD 20 billion in profits). (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "From 2016 to 2020, Company W received approximately (NTD) 59.5 billion"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: To launder proceeds Mr. J set up investment Company D to buy stocks of public companies worth NTD 700 million and invested in real estate in other countries. Seized cash in several accounts totalled approximately NTD 3 billion, and Mr. J and his companies were prosecuted for gambling and money laundering. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mr. J established an investment, Company D, to purchase the stocks of public"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: After tracing fund flows in the dummy accounts and combining background analysis (criminal history, career, tax records and CTR), AMLD found some accounts highly relevant to online gambling, fraud and underground banking under local law enforcement investigation; crime syndicates seemed to use them to conceal, convert and transfer illegal proceeds. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "combined with background analysis including criminal history, career, tax records and CTR"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Online banking offers low-cost convenience, privacy and fast responses but carries ML risks and uncertainty and can help criminals avoid detection. AMLD's shared early warning captured law enforcement's attention, detecting tele fraud and online gambling involving more than USD 20 billion, and is cited as an example of cooperation among banks, FIU and supervisors. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Online banking services provide low-cost convenience, privacy and fast responses."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Case 14 indicators: analyzing and detecting new trends and types of crime that lead to major cases from STRs; the FIU shared information and strategy reports with relevant authorities. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU shared information and strategy reports with relevant authorities."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Recognizing that dummy accounts could compromise Taiwan's financial stability, AMLD shared findings in December 2019 with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC), which forwarded them to all financial institutions immediately and demanded they pay attention to suspicious activities and take commensurate measures to mitigate ML/TF risks. Institutions reviewed customer data, reported suspicious activity to AMLD immediately, performed enhanced due diligence on high-ML-risk clients, and closed some accounts. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AMLD shared its findings in December 2019 with the country’s Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Case 14 evaluation: cooperation among the private sector (e.g., banks), law enforcement and supervisors led to an efficient criminal-investigation environment. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leading to an efficient criminal-investigation environment"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Starting from unusual accounts later revealed as dummy accounts, AMLD gathered STRs, analyzed the criminal pattern and uncovered a series of red flags, shared with authorities who detected several illegal transactions; after follow-up dissemination to law enforcement, with private and public sector help, law enforcement solved the case and seized the illegal proceeds. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AMLD uncovered a series of red flags."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The excerpt ends with the title of the next case, on criminals using shared construction companies to steal money from victims (Bahrain Financial Intelligence Department). (p. 64)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Criminals used shared construction companies to steal money from victim"</sub>
