## CASE 18

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 18 (Senegal CENTIF): OMEGA owned a car dealership specializing in importing used vehicles. An investigation by Senegal's FIU and law enforcement, initially prompted by newspaper reports, found he and other Senegalese nationals residing in Europe facilitated trafficking of stolen vehicles and motorcycles across Europe. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "were facilitating the trafficking of stolen vehicles and motorcycles across Europe"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2021 the FIU received information on OMEGA, who held two separate company accounts, with Bank 1 and Bank 2, which operated without incidents or anomalies. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The individual held two separate company accounts with Bank 1 and Bank 2."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Analysis of STRs, police information and other FIU data showed the transactions in the two accounts did not align with the legitimate activities of OMEGA's company: personal and professional expenses were mingled and the accounts received proceeds of stolen-vehicle trafficking, complicating cash flow management and tax accounting. Both accounts saw significant cash deposits from OMEGA, often followed by cheque withdrawals. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both bank accounts saw significant cash deposits from OMEGA, often followed by cheque withdrawals"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Newspapers alleged OMEGA's involvement in international trafficking of stolen vehicles and motorcycles; after his arrest, Bank 1 and Bank 2 submitted STRs to CENTIF. Given consistent indications of money laundering from this trafficking, the FIU forwarded information to the Public Prosecutor's Office for potential money laundering prosecution. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prompted both Bank 1 and 2 to submit Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) to Senegal’s CENTIF"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A police investigation provided substantial evidence implicating OMEGA in criminal association, international trafficking of stolen vehicles, money laundering through his shell company, and injection of illicit funds into the financial system, allegedly with help from other nationals residing in Europe. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "money laundering through his shell company, and the injection of illicit funds into the financial system"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The illicitly obtained vehicles were either sold in Senegal for cash via cheque transactions or exchanged for undeveloped land. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in Senegal for cash via cheque transactions or exchanged for undeveloped land."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIU conducted an asset investigation that identified all movable and immovable assets of OMEGA and associated parties and OMEGA's financial assets in local banks; international cooperation, including with France's Tracfin, gathered further information corroborating the allegations. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including identifying all movable and immovable assets owned by OMEGA and associated parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: At the end of the FIU and police investigations there were serious and consistent indications of money laundering via sale of stolen vehicles and motorcycles through OMEGA's company; the case went to the Senegalese Public Prosecutor, OMEGA was detained, and the judicial process is not complete. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The case was transferred to the Senegalese Public Prosecutor and OMEGA was detained."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The investigation spanned two years, collecting, compiling and analyzing information from banks, governmental organizations and foreign FIUs such as Tracfin. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The investigation into the case spanned two years."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Highlights: financial analysis was instrumental in establishing allegations of criminal conspiracy, international trafficking in stolen vehicles, receipt of stolen goods, use of shell companies to launder illicit funds and integration of illicit funds into the financial system; investigators identified luxury vehicles and motorcycles believed stolen overseas; OMEGA's accomplices in Europe were identified. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial analysis proved instrumental in establishing"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case 18 money laundering red flags: amalgamation of funds; strong manipulation of cash; suspect implication in international trafficking of stolen vehicles; criminal association; recycling funds of illicit origin in real estate. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recycling funds of illicit origin in real estate."</sub>

## CASE 19

- **descriptive / case example**: Case 19 (Burkina Faso CENTIF-BF): a politically-exposed person tried to hide USD four million by layering it through many bank accounts across several jurisdictions and then setting up shell companies with the complicity of a legal professional. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "attempted to hide (USD) four million in funds by layering it in many banks accounts across several jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The case began with a submission to CENTIF-BF in August 2018 by ECOBANK-BURKINA about a Saudi Arabian individual (Mr. X), his wife (Ms. X) and their companies, including one registered in the British Virgin Islands (as of April 2018) and Company Y, incorporated in Burkina Faso (as of August 2018). (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by local financial institution ECOBANK- BURKINA"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Company Y was wholly owned by Company Z, both owned by Mr. X (born in Riyadh, residing in the United States), who claimed he sought real estate activities in Burkina Faso such as land and property transactions, renovations, construction and sales. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company Y was wholly owned by Company Z."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Mr. X provided a property title in Arabic, for which the bank requested a translation that was never received; he instead supplied banking documents showing transfers from his wife's account at BANK ALJAZIRA in Riyadh to his accounts at Bank of America, Merrill Lynch, UBA in Ivory Coast and finally his ECOBANK account in Burkina Faso. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the bank requested a translated version. That was never received."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In May 2018 Mr. X began opening a corporate account for Company Y, still in setup, yet before incorporation or account opening was complete his Burkina Faso bank received two wire transfers of USD two million each on June 21, 2018. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "received two wire transfers of (USD) two million each on June 21, 2018"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: When the bank asked about the purpose of the transfers, Mr. X said the funds were contributions from Company Z to establish its affiliate Company Y in Burkina Faso. The transfers came from his personal account at UBA in Ivory Coast, and he explained that the funds came from the 2017 sale of a family property in Riyadh totalling USD seven million, wired from Bank of America, passed through his personal account in Ivory Coast, and deposited in his Burkina Faso account to finance Company Y's activities. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Upon inquiry by the bank regarding the purpose of these transfers, Mr. X stated the funds were"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In the Burkina Faso case, financial database research found four US filings (two SARs and two CTRs) totalling USD 21,700. One SAR identified Mr. X in suspicious wire transfers involving foreign high-risk jurisdictions; the other implicated both Mr. X and Ms. X in suspicious transactions across multiple locations and accounts, including transfers to Ouagadougou. The text says these transactions indicated potential layering tactics and structuring to evade reporting requirements. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One SAR identified Mr. X in suspicious wire transfers involving foreign high-risk jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Mr. X said the property sale was settled by a cheque in his wife's name, without proof of their marital relationship; she deposited it at BANK ALJAZIRA, then transferred it to Bank of America and on to his savings account, from where it moved among his accounts before reaching Company Y's account. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without proof of their marital relationship"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In September 2018 Mr. X asked for the entire sum to be transferred back to his Bank of America account, citing inability to keep investing in Burkina Faso real estate because of misinformation from local real estate agencies. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requested the transfer of the entire sum back to his Bank of America account"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FinCEN told CENTIF-BF that law enforcement queries on Mr. X, Ms. X and the two entities were negative, but database research found four filings (two SARs and two CTRs) totalling USD 21,700. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FinCEN informed CENTIF-BF that law enforcement queries on Mr. X, Ms. X and the two entities yielded negative results."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: CENTIF-BF analyzed ECOBANK-BURKINA's report in October 2018. The case was significant for the USD four million and the intricate transfers across banks and jurisdictions, and Mr. X, a politically-exposed person, posed an elevated risk of potential involvement in corruption. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mr. X—a politically-exposed person—posed an elevated risk of potential involvement in corruption"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The SAR also disclosed Ms. X's ties to the Royal Saudi Family and her involvement in the suspicious activity; subsequent cash withdrawals were structured to avoid reporting limits. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subsequent cash withdrawals, which were structured to avoid reporting limits"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: On receiving authorization CENTIF-BF forwarded the FinCEN information to the Public Prosecutor. A CENTIF-BF committee met on October 10, 2018, and issued a letter of objection to Mr. X's transfer request the same day to prevent the funds being returned to him. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issuing a letter of objection to Mr. X’s transfer request on the same date"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The committee made two information requests, to FinCEN (USA) and to the Saudi Arabia Financial Investigation Unit (SAFIU) on October 26, 2018, and CENTIF-BF filed the case with the Public Prosecutor at the Regional Court of Ouagadougou on October 12, 2018. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CENTIF-BF also filed the case with the Public Prosecutor at the Regional Court of Ouagadougou on October 12, 2018."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case remains unresolved in court. On October 12, 2018 the court issued an Order of Seizure following CENTIF-BF's October 10, 2018 directive to ECOBANK-BURKINA to refrain from executing the transaction, as stipulated by Article 68 of Burkina Faso's AML/CFT Act. On January 30, 2019 a mutual legal assistance request was sent to the judicial authority of Saudi Arabia. (p. 80)[^original]
