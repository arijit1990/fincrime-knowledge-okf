## CASE 1

- **descriptive / case example**: Italy's UIF, with law enforcement and the tax revenue agency, exposed a multi-billion euro fraud and money laundering scheme generating fictitious fiscal credits tied to legitimate pandemic-era government measures; foreign FIU collaboration was key to finding the cross-border network, assets abroad were frozen, and the case led to tax legislation reforms, enforcement controls and national/supranational regulation. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "brought to light a multi-billion euro fraud and money laundering scheme involving the generation of fictitious fiscal credits"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: To foster recovery from the pandemic Italy introduced fiscal incentives giving tax credits to homeowners renovating properties; the risks included possibly fictitious credits and tax crimes such as false invoices. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Italy’s government introduced a series of fiscal incen­ tives that provided tax credits to homeowners"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: To foster prompt detection, the UIF issued several communications drawing reporting entities' attention to risks of these measures, and then received many STRs on newly established building companies selling Italian financial intermediaries fiscal credits of disproportionate amounts relative to their structure. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the UIF issued several communications calling reporting entities’ attention to the risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Considerable money went to companies incorporated in different EU member states in sectors typically involved in fiscal frauds and was immediately re-transferred to companies in Asia already involved in other fiscal frauds; another part went into cryptocurrencies via VASPs in Italy and other EU states; a significant amount returned to Italy for repeated cash withdrawals at Italian ATMs near the legal offices of suspicious companies using foreign prepaid/credit cards linked to East European accounts, plus transfers to persons known to the UIF as crime-organization affiliates, wires for capital deposits of newly incorporated companies, and luxury goods purchases. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These funds were immediately re-trans­ ferred to companies located in Asia"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Through operational analysis with foreign FIUs and Italian authorities, the UIF uncovered a large-scale network of natural and legal persons that, using complex financial schemes, created and marketed large amounts of fictitious tax credits to unaware intermediaries, with proceeds laundered via cross-border transactions. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "create and market a large amount of fictitious tax credits to various unaware intermediaries"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The UIF's bank statement analysis, aided by information exchange with about ten foreign FIUs, was key to tracking use of funds from the tax credits; experimental data-mining software highlighted the scheme's main hubs (persons and flows) and was essential for identifying extended networks among complex transactions. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Experimental use of a data-mining software in this case highlighted the main hubs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Large suspicious flows were intermediated by the same EU-based e-money institution (EMI); the UIF's strategic analysis led to deeper analysis of it, finding its owners were two Italians living abroad under pre-trial ML investigations in three countries; information flows were set up among the UIF, the competent FIU and supervisors, and the EMI's licence was revoked for AML deficiencies. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The EMI’s license was revoked for anti-money laundering deficiencies."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The UIF shared outcomes immediately with national law enforcement, which opened investigations on several STRs; a large amount of credits sold was judicially seized and assets abroad were frozen thanks to FIU cooperation. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "successful freezing of assets abroad as a result of fruitful cooperation between FIUs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UIF results were immediately sent to Italy's finance authorities, its Anti-Mafia Investigation Department and the National Anti-Mafia Directorate because mafia-style organized crime was involved; UIF intelligence was key to launching and supporting formal investigations. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "due to the involvement of mafia-style organized crime in this illicit scheme"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The UIF played a strategic role in promoting regulatory amendments on trading tax credits to avoid abuses and raised international awareness of further anomalies: misuse of foreign credit cards, virtual asset financial activities and foreign EMIs. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The UIF played a strategic role in promoting regu­ latory amendments on trade of tax credits"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many criminal proceedings, mostly from STR analysis, led the Italian Finance Minister to call it one of the biggest frauds in the history of the Italian Republic; EUR 4.4 billion in credits was estimated to come from non-existent building refurbishments; the UIF focused on authenticity of tax credits and the ultimate destination of sales proceeds. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A total of (EUR) 4.4 billion in credits was estimated to come from non-existent building refurbishments."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Italian finance authorities further investigated most cases analyzed by the UIF, leading to several prosecutions and 35 arrests; law enforcement temporarily froze bank accounts credited with sales proceeds and judicial authorities seized EUR 2.3 billion in tax credits. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Judicial authorities also seized a total of (EUR) 2.3 billion in tax credits."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The UIF invited major acquiring financial institutions, as obliged entities, to check inconsistencies in documentation on works underlying tax credits; the most frequent anomalies were overpricing of works versus average market prices, incorrect or non-existing building addresses, and customers not matching building owners. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "overpricing of the alleged renovation works with respect to average market prices"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Italian Revenue Agency officials gave the UIF full details on tax credit transactions in its database; after the UIF analysis and the numbers of STRs, investigations and funds moved abroad, the Italian government updated its tax credit transfer legislation, involving the UIF in the definition. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Italian Revenue Agency officials provided the UIF with full details on tax credit transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: recurrent anomalies in new vendors of fiscal credits, including registered offices concentrated in limited geographical areas, capital contribution only in cash, use of frontmen, and bank statements showing no salary or materials charges but only credits from transfer price of fiscal incentives. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recurrent anomalies in new vendors of fiscal credits"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: possible misuse of a foreign EMI whose licence had been recently revoked for AML deficiencies and which was involved in this and further ML schemes, prompting extensive information exchange between the UIF, the foreign FIU and both countries' supervisors. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Possible misuse of a foreign EMI, whose license had been recently revoked for AML deficiencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: significant cash withdrawals from Italian ATMs with foreign prepaid credit cards. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Significant amount of cash withdrawals carried out from Italian ATMs with foreign prepaid credit cards."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: increasing recourse for ML purposes to investment in cryptocurrencies and decentralized finance applications. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Increasing recourse for money laundering purposes to investment in cryptocurrencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Chart 1 (FIU Italy, misuse of tax relief, reference period 2020-2022): detected fraud EUR 4.4 billion and seized tax credits EUR 2.3 billion; the flow runs from sale of state tax credits to banks and financial intermediaries through shell companies and banks, cash, cryptocurrencies and e-currencies, foreign outflows and triangulation, to integration via luxury goods and cash withdrawals. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Detected fraud: (EUR) 4.4. billion"</sub>

## CASE 2

- **descriptive / case example**: Bolivia (UIF) case: a recently created company, Tu Auto, was awarded a million-dollar contract by a Bolivian government entity for 41 ambulances despite lacking experience, track record or means to guarantee a contract of that size; complaints revealed it had earlier won two other vehicle contracts from the same entity. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "awarded a million-dollar contract for the acquisition of 41 ambulances"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The case gained notoriety due to those involved, including Governor J who signed the contracts, and officials signing a receipt for the ambulances without them being on site. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "government officials signing a receipt document for the ambulances without them being physically on site"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The Bolivian UIF did a financial and asset analysis of those involved, indicating Governor J was linked to corruption as the highest executive authority who signed the contracts. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "carried out a financial and asset analysis of those involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UIF identified Mrs. MT as the sister of Tu Auto's legal representative, who formed a separate company, Car United, with two other people in April 2021. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mrs. “MT” was the sister of the legal representative of Tu Auto"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Bolivian Customs reported 87 vehicles imported by HT, Tu Auto's owner and legal representative, but HT's personal accounts showed no significant movements to justify payment, and HT had opened bank accounts between November and December 2021, shortly before the events and contract signing. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "87 vehicles were imported by “HT,” the owner and legal representative of Tu Auto"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Over eight months from June 2021 to February 2022 Bolivian Customs registered 307 imported vehicles to Car United, with FOB value USD 7,813,294.42 and CIF value BOB 58,631,380, with unknown funding origin; the company was set up two months before imports began, and a 20-year-old partner with no operations in the financial system became the frontman. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "307 imported vehicles to Car United for an FOB value of (USD) 7,813,294.42"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The FIU requested information from public and private sector subjects, considering the funds were state resources; from its financial and patrimonial analysis it prepared four reports involving three companies and five individuals in a case of public resources management linked to corruption and the crime of Legitimation of Illicit Gains. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "four reports were prepared that involved three companies and five individuals"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The apprehension of the individuals and deepened analysis set an important precedent for authorities in charge of public resources. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "served as an important precedent for authorities in charge of public resources"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The investigation took about three years; it resulted in apprehension of the main actors, including the Bolivian government's executive authority, drew media attention, and the ambulance contract was annulled. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The investigation took approximately three years to complete."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: suspicious transactions with people who previously had no records in the Bolivian financial system. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Suspicious transactions and operations identified with people who previously had no records"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: young, inexperienced individuals managing to form companies later awarded million-dollar state contracts. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The age of the individuals who, despite being young and inexperienced"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: creation of companies shortly before signing contracts. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Creation of companies shortly before signing contracts."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: large numbers of vehicle imports soon after company formation without identifiable origin of the resources. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Large number of vehicle imports shortly after the formation of the companies"</sub>

## CASE 3

- **descriptive / case example**: Nigeria FIU case: former Petroleum Minister Diezani Alison-Madueke used her position to appoint associate Haruna Momoh as Managing Director of Pipelines and Products Marketing Company Limited (PPMC), an NNPC subsidiary; Momoh misappropriated funds through his wife via various companies, and Alison-Madueke directed NNPC subsidiaries to award USD 1.7 billion contracts to associates Aluko and Omokore. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leveraged her position to appoint her associate, Haruna Momoh, as Managing Director of Pipelines and Products Marketing Company Limited"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Offenses include bribery, corruption, fraud, forgery and tax evasion; funds were laundered across multiple jurisdictions through shell companies; FIU-law enforcement collaboration led to asset forfeiture and US civil suits, and efforts continue to extradite individuals and repatriate stolen funds. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Offenses include bribery, corruption, fraud, forgery and tax evasion."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: A financial investigation of Haruna Momoh and wife Ochuko Eileen Momoh found suspicious transactions exceeding known income, with repetitive cash deposits followed by large withdrawals often used for extravagant purchases globally. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "repetitive patterns of cash deposits followed by large withdrawals"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The investigation began with STRs from financial institutions on entities associated with the accused, highlighting cash withdrawals just below the reporting threshold totaling a significant amount in a short period. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unusual patterns of cash withdrawals: just below the reporting threshold"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Analysis found a network of shell companies in Nigeria, the UAE, the US and the UK used to funnel and launder proceeds of corrupt NNPC subsidiary contracts facilitated by Alison-Madueke. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a complex network of shell companies spanning multiple jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FIU's analysis of reported accounts found links between account holders and high-level officials including Alison-Madueke, and that she used her ministerial position to facilitate Momoh's appointment as PPMC Managing Director; predicate offences identified were bribery, corruption, contract fraud, forgery and tax evasion. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identification of predicate offences such as bribery, corruption, contract fraud, forgery and tax evasion"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The Nigerian FIU used the Crime Records Information Management System (CRIMS), open-source intelligence (OSINT) analysis and link analysis; access to the corporate registry database helped identify entities linked to NNPC contracts and connections to Mrs. Diezani. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Crime Records Information Management System (CRIMS) and Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) analysis"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Collaboration with domestic agencies such as the EFCC and ICPC proved crucial for evidence gathering and starting legal proceedings, leading to arrests, prosecutions and asset seizures, with the FIU giving timely information support. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collaboration between the Nigerian FIU and other domestic law enforce­ ment agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The US Department of Justice filed a civil suit against Alison-Madueke for benefiting from corrupt contracts awarded to entities of her associates. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The US Department of Justice filed a civil suit against Alison-Madueke"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The Nigerian FIU worked with a European FIU on the Momoh case to identify properties bought with laundered funds, leading to a Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty request, and exchanged intelligence with a Caribbean FIU on Kola Aluko revealing companies registered under his name. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Intelligence exchange with a Caribbean FIU concerning Kola Aluko"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Properties bought with illicit proceeds in Nigeria were identified and subjected to asset forfeiture; efforts began to trace and recover offshore assets; extradition proceedings and repatriation of looted funds with foreign jurisdictions are ongoing. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Properties purchased with illicit proceeds were identified and subject to asset forfeiture proceedings"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: use of cash below threshold, with multiple cash withdrawals below the reporting threshold indicating an attempt to avoid detection. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple cash withdrawals below the reporting threshold, indicating an attempt to avoid detection."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: unusual transaction patterns, including outflow rate from a bank account and cash deposits followed by withdrawals for extravagant purchases globally, suggesting potential ML. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Outflow rate from a bank account, patterns of cash deposits followed by withdrawals"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: high-value transactions exceeding the known profile of individuals as public servants, indicating possible illicit funds. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Those exceeding the known profile of individuals as public servants"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the Nigerian FIU case, access to Nigeria's corporate registry database helped identify entities linked to contracts awarded by the NNPC, exposing connections to Mrs. Diezani and, in the source's wording (which contains the editorial marker "[confirm?]"), suspicions of fund misuse during elections in Nigeria. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "[confirm?] suspicions of fund misuse during elections in Nigeria"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2016 the Nigerian FIU received an STR on Princess Jewelry with multiple cash withdrawals totaling NGN 113 million over two days just below the reporting threshold; the account owner Ochuko Eileen Momoh was the wife of former PPMC MD Haruna Momoh, whose accounts showed transactions exceeding their public servant profile with cash deposit/withdrawal patterns for lavish purchases; the pattern led to including PPMC and NNPC in the analysis alongside open-source information on Alison-Madueke's tenure. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "multiple cash withdrawals totaling (NGN) 113 million were conducted over two days"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: rapid asset acquisition of high-value assets such as luxury properties and yachts, potentially indicating laundering of illicit proceeds. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Rapid asset acquisition—Occurring with high-value assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Indicator: complex financial structures involving shell companies and offshore accounts used to obscure origin and destination of funds. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Complex financial structures—These involved shell companies and offshore accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case led to forfeiture of properties acquired with illicit proceeds in Nigeria and a US DOJ civil suit against Alison-Madueke; extradition and repatriation efforts continue; it highlights the importance of robust FIUs and cross-border collaboration against corruption and money laundering. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The case resulted in asset forfeiture for properties acquired with illicit proceeds within Nigeria"</sub>

(p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "N23,446,300,300 and $5 million (about N1.5 million)"</sub>
