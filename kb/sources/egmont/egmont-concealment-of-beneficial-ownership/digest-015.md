## ANNEX B. HORIZONTAL STUDY: ENFORCEMENT AND SUPERVISION OF BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP OBLIGATIONS

- **descriptive / method**: Annex B: two sets of questions were circulated to FATF and FATF-style regional body delegations seeking information on creation and maintenance of legal persons and arrangements and the supervision and enforcement of maintenance and beneficial ownership requirements. Question 1 asked which businesses or professions form and/or maintain legal persons or arrangements, to elicit the composition, size and importance of gatekeeper sectors and their roles. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Two sets of questions were circulated to FATF delegations and delegations from the FATF-style regional bodies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Generally, many of the same types of gatekeepers can be involved in forming both legal persons and arrangements, but the formation processes are in most cases quite different, so the annex addresses company formation under Question 1 and legal arrangements under Question 2. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the processes for forming legal persons and arrangements are, in most cases, quite different"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Company formation systems fall into four categories (hybrids possible): systems where gatekeepers are not necessarily required; systems where gatekeepers (other than notaries) are required; notarial systems; and systems where the Registrar tests the accuracy of filings or takes on a gatekeeper's CDD obligations. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "systems in which the Registrar tests the accuracy of filings or takes on the customer"</sub>

## Gatekeepers Optional

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In 29 of 64 responses, gatekeepers are available but not required for company formation; some jurisdictions let any member of the public form a company though gatekeepers often facilitate it (in the UK approximately 75% of companies are formed by gatekeepers), some make them optional in most but required in some circumstances, and six said gatekeeper services were available without indicating whether required or how often used. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approximately 75% of companies are formed by gatekeepers"</sub>

## Gatekeepers Required

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In sixteen jurisdictions a gatekeeper (other than a notary or government employee) must intervene to form a legal person in most if not all cases; four require it only for 'offshore' companies or corporate vehicles (companies or trusts) seen as higher risk, designed for international activities targeting non-residents, and two require it only for limited liability companies. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In sixteen jurisdictions, intervention by a gatekeeper (other than a notary or government employee) is required"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: In some instances there is no oversight to ensure companies restricted to certain activities on creation (e.g. international holding companies) do not later conduct other activities; the main incentive to register them correctly is preferential tax treatment. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no oversight to make sure that the companies that are restricted to certain activities upon creation"</sub>

## Box 1. Singapore’s Registered Filing Agents and Registered Qualified Individuals

- **descriptive / case example**: Box 1: since 2015 Singapore requires an individual forming a legal entity for another person in the course of business to be registered with ACRA as a Registered Qualified Individual (RQI), and firms providing such services to register as Registered Filing Agents (RFA) acting through at least one RQI; members of the public acting on their own behalf (generally lower risk) keep free access to registration while gatekeepers are registered and subject to AML/CFT obligations regardless of existing professional status. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be registered with Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) as a Registered Qualified Individual (RQI)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 1 (continued): legal persons are created online through ACRA's electronic transaction system, which only allows RQIs of RFAs and individuals to create legal persons and file documents; individuals use a SingPass (issued to Singaporeans and permanent residents), and foreigners without a SingPass must use RFAs, which prevents creation of legal persons by unauthorised people. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Foreigners who do not hold a SingPass have to engage the services of RFAs"</sub>

## Gatekeepers Required

- **permissive / context**: Annex B paragraph 8 says the concept of a risk-based approach to company formation mechanisms could be subject to closer consideration in future, and introduces Singapore's registered filing agent and registered qualified individual arrangements (Box 1) as one example. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The concept of risk-based approach to company formation mechanisms could be subject to closer consideration in future"</sub>

## Box 1. Singapore’s Registered Filing Agents and Registered Qualified Individuals

- **descriptive / case example**: Box 1 (continued): the registration requirement is additional to the usual application of AML/CFT obligations to DNFBP classes; lawyers and accountants performing FATF-defined activities remain supervised by their own regulators, while any CSP, law or accountancy firm must also register with ACRA. RFAs provide entity name, registered office address, nature of business and details of the RQIs they appoint; RQIs provide personal details and qualifications. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lawyers and accountants who perform FATF defined activities remain to be supervised by the respective regulators"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 1 (continued): a CSP firm cannot register as an RFA if any beneficial owner, director, partner or manager has been convicted of criminal offences or is an undischarged bankrupt; an individual cannot register as an RQI if convicted of a criminal offence (especially fraud and dishonesty) or an undischarged bankrupt; ACRA also checks background of legal owners, beneficial owners, directors, partners and managers of RFAs, and RQIs, including past conduct and compliance history. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A CSP firm will not be permitted to be registered as a RFA if any of its beneficial owners"</sub>

## Notarial Systems

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Thirteen responding jurisdictions use a notarial system for company formation, in which a notary vested with public office and directly responsible to a government ministry attests registry filings; such systems are almost exclusively in civil law jurisdictions, involve high formality and may not suit every jurisdiction, but FATF members have assessed them as some of the most effective systems for implementing beneficial ownership obligations. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF members have assessed these systems as some of the most effective systems for implementation of beneficial ownership"</sub>

## Box 2. Spain’s and Italy’s notarial system

- **descriptive / case example**: Box 2: in 4th Round mutual evaluations Spain and Italy were assessed as having substantially effective systems; notaries are public officials subject to AML/CFT obligations, duly executed notarial acts are presumed valid, self-authenticating, self-executing and probative, and a notary is required at company formation and later to validate register information and authenticate ownership changes. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both assessed as having systems that are substantially effective"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 2 (continued): Spain's Beneficial Ownership Database, operational since March 2014 and available to competent authorities in April 2014, builds on the Single Computerised Index by aggregating beneficial ownership and share transfer information, offering per company two levels of information: beneficial ownership obtained by the individual notary through normal CDD (a declaration which, if at least one risk indicator is met, includes a copy of the beneficial owner's ID); and, for private limited liability companies, ownership derived by aggregating successive share transfers. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Beneficial Ownership Database became operational in March 2014"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Box 2 (continued): Spanish private limited liability companies (Sociedades de Responsabilidad Limitada) represent some 92% of all legal persons and 96% of new incorporations; since notaries must be involved in share transfers, this information is always verified and updated twice monthly. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which represent some 92% of all legal persons and 96% of new incorporations in Spain"</sub>

## Registry with Oversight Functions

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Other FATF research suggests systems combining one or more approaches to ensuring availability and accuracy of basic and beneficial ownership information are often more effective than single-approach systems; in 21 jurisdictions one of the systems is complemented by a Registry with some oversight (verifying completeness or accuracy of filings, conducting CDD in certain cases, or cross-checking against other government databases): two with notarial systems (including Spain), six where gatekeepers (other than notaries) are required, and thirteen where gatekeepers are optional. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In 21 jurisdictions, one of the systems referred to above is complemented by a Registry"</sub>

## Box 3. Guernsey’s and Jersey’s Registrars

- **descriptive / case example**: Box 3: in Guernsey and Jersey, intervention by a fully regulated and supervised gatekeeper is mandatory for most company formations (optional for local residents), but the Registrar performs the gatekeeper's CDD functions when no gatekeeper is involved in formation or administration. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Registrar performs the CDD functions of gatekeepers when there is no gatekeeper involved"</sub>

## Box 4. UK’s Companies House

- **descriptive / case example**: Box 4: UK Companies House is part of the Government Agencies Intelligence Network; it does not conduct CDD or verify information but analyses data to identify suspicious activity and patterns of behaviour and shares them with law enforcement, identified through mechanisms including complaints from third parties that their details (name, date of birth and/or home address) were used without consent, and contact from law enforcement or government agencies about suspicions over a single company (list continues beyond the excerpt). (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Although Companies House does not conduct CDD or verify information"</sub>
