## Case Study 32 – Fiji

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 32 (Fiji): Mr. X, an accountant at a Fijian resort, altered the resort's cheques to creditors. A shell company was set up to conceal the converted funds; some altered cheques were deposited into the shell company's account and the rest were issued to family members and associates. Proceeds bought six motor vehicles, a private property and cash; vehicles were registered in Mr. X's and others' names, and the property in his mother's name and later transferred to an associate. (p. 129)[^original]

## Case Study 37 – Guernsey

A was under IRS investigation and TCSP client C under FBI investigation for operating a 'boiler room' fraud. ## Case Study 38 – Israel

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 38 (Israel): a scheme hid funds from social engineering fraud and other offences under a cover story of international trade (payments from merchants in Europe and the US to East Asian suppliers). The owner of a registered MSB also ran a second, unregistered MSB, and used contacts in East Asia who approached local TCSPs to set up international companies and bank accounts. Local straw men and shareholders registered from passports supplied by the contacts were used; registered addresses and bank accounts were in the same East Asian countries. (p. 132)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The suspect, the owner of a registered MSB, operated a second, unregistered MSB"</sub>

Funds came from companies the suspect set up, but the receiving bank did not know they were under his control. (p. 132)[^original]

## Case Study 39 – Israel

Afterwards the TCSPs were not involved in management; suspects either used the companies to launder money or sold them to third parties for profit. (p. 133)[^original]

## Case Study 40 – Israel

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 40 (Israel): a fraudulent tax scheme to evade tax on international trade income plus an ML infrastructure. Suspects used a TCSP to register and run two international shell companies (A and B) to make revenues look as though they did not belong to the local Israeli company they controlled. A and B traded only with each other and had no other income. Company A moved significant funds to local company C as a 'consulted fee'/'service commission'; only this commission, under half of the real income, was reported to Israel's tax authority, so tax was paid on only a small part of income. (p. 133)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The two companies traded with each other exclusively and did not have any other source of income"</sub>

## Case Study 41 – Israel

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 41 (Israel): underground banking in which suspects provided unregistered, concealed money services (check clearing, currency exchange, international transfers, loans). Customers gave illegally gained cash, and transfers were registered and declared as diamond export/import or local diamond trade. The 'bank's' managers laundered funds under the guise of diamond trade using false declarations and fictitious export/import documentation, and customers used diamond dealers' accounts to move money without reporting it. Total sums laundered were hundreds of millions of USD. (p. 134)[^original]

## Case Study 42 – Italy

Between 1996 and 2006 the subjects placed assets in Dutch and Luxembourg companies through complex corporate operations and transfers to different Channel Islands trusts, then legally repatriated the funds via a tax amnesty in December 2009.
