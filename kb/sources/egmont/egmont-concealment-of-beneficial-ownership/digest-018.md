## Case Study 4 – Australia

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 4 (Australia): a suspect declared minimal income to the tax office while living luxuriously and had disguised income from securities trading. He created several international companies owned on paper by a Netherlands stichting (a foundation whose beneficial owner identity is not yet publicly available), sold securities to them below market value to reduce Australian tax liability, then had the shares sold via those companies at market value. Proceeds returned to him in Australia disguised as loans from the international companies; over two years he arranged 15 international funds transfers from companies he controlled in Switzerland to his Australian company. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "created several international companies which, on paper, were owned by a stichting"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case Study 4 (Australia) indicators: activities and transactions inconsistent with customer profile; declared income inconsistent with assets, transactions or lifestyle; entity formed in a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/financial centre; no real business activities; exclusively facilitates transit transactions and does not appear to generate wealth or income; loans from private third parties without supporting loan agreements, collateral or regular interest repayments; funds unusual in the context of the client's profile; funds sent to or received from a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/finance centre. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Funds are unusual in the context of the client or customer’s profile"</sub>

## Case Study 5 – Australia

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 5 (Australia): the 'Round Robin' scheme made fund movements look like payments to other parties while the funds ultimately returned to the original beneficiary. Suspects moved funds from their companies' accounts to accounts of New Zealand companies controlled by a Vanuatu-based accountant who was a signatory. Payments were falsely recorded as 'management and consultancy fees' backed by false invoices matching the amounts, with no evidence of consulting work; they were claimed as deductible expenses in the tax returns of companies X, Y and Z, fraudulently reducing taxable income and tax owed. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The “Round Robin” scheme aimed to make funds movements appear as payments to other parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 5 (Australia), continued: the accountant sent the funds as international 'loans' through a series of round robin transactions via accounts of companies he owned and operated, into the suspects' personal accounts in Australia, using an overseas company he controlled that was separate from the New Zealand recipients. False documents purporting to be international loan agreements with a foreign lender were created to disguise the returning funds as loans, which are not assessed as income and carry no tax liability. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "false documents were created purporting to be international loan agreements with a foreign lender"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case Study 5 (Australia) indicators: entity formed in a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/financial centre; false invoices for services not carried out; no real business activities; exclusively facilitates transit transactions; legal person pays no taxes, superannuation, retirement fund contributions or social benefits; loans from private third parties without supporting agreements, collateral or regular interest repayments; transaction appears cyclical; professional intermediary involved without due cause; complicated routings without sufficient explanation or trade records; funds sent to or received from a foreign country with no apparent connection to the client; funds sent to or received from a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/finance centre. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Legal Person pays no taxes, superannuation, retirement fund contributions or social benefits"</sub>

## Case Study 6 – Australia

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 6 (Australia): suspect A ran an import business and took part in a tax evasion scheme run by an accountant. A and his wife were directors and shareholders of company 1, and A was also director and shareholder of company 2, co-directed by an associate. The accountant controlled company 3, registered in Hong Kong with a bank account in Australia, which over five and a half years issued false invoices to companies 1 and 2 for supposed 'brokering services'. A paid them (over AUD2 million in total), and the funds, less the accountant's 10% fee, were returned to A and associated individuals. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Over a five- and-a-half-year period company 3 issued false invoices to companies 1 and 2"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case Study 6 (Australia) indicators: entity formed in a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/financial centre; false invoices for services not carried out; falsified paper trail; family members with no role listed as beneficial owners; simple banking relationships established using professional intermediaries; no real business activities; exclusively facilitates transit transactions; client is both ordering and beneficiary customer for multiple international funds transfers; loans from private third parties without supporting agreements, collateral or regular interest repayments; transaction appears cyclical; two-way transfer of similar sums between a client and a professional intermediary; two legal persons with similar or identical directors, shareholders or beneficial owners; professional intermediary involved without due cause; complicated routings without sufficient explanation or trade records; funds sent to or received from a foreign country with no apparent connection to the client; funds sent to or received from a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/finance centre. (p. 116)[^original]

## Case Study 7 – Australia

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 7 (Australia): family members A and B owned and controlled Australian motor vehicle repair and automotive products companies. On an accountant's advice about international superannuation funds, A set up a superannuation fund in Samoa with a Samoa-based company as trustee. Company 1 (controlled by A and B) contributed AUD 200 000, which came back to Company 1 disguised as a loan while the contribution was claimed as a tax deduction. A and B also took a secondary loan on behalf of company 1 from the Samoa-based private bank, which stayed in place for over 10 years. (p. 116)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "contributed AUD 200 000 to the fund, which was then returned back to Company 1 disguised as a loan"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 7 (Australia), continued: the second loan was later transferred to other group companies, which made 'interest payments' by international transfer that were returned as further loans. To add complexity, an unrelated Australian organisation described as a charitable organisation was brought in and facilitated transfers between the bank's New Zealand subsidiary and the Australian group. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unrelated to the main group of companies and was described as a charitable organisation"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case Study 7 (Australia) indicators: entity formed in a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/financial centre; regularly sends money to such jurisdictions or centres; transacts with international companies without sufficient corporate or trade justification; focused on aggressive tax minimisation strategies; family members with no role listed as beneficial owners; client is both ordering and beneficiary customer for multiple international funds transfers; finance from a lender other than a known credit institution with no logical explanation or commercial justification; loans from private third parties without supporting agreements, collateral or regular interest repayments; transaction between connected parties without apparent business or trade rationale; transaction appears cyclical; complicated routings without sufficient explanation or trade records; funds sent to or received from a foreign country with no apparent connection to the client; funds sent to or received from a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/finance centre. (p. 117)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Focused on aggressive tax minimisation strategies"</sub>

## Case Study 11 – Bolivia

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 11 (Bolivia): multiple money orders from the same area of Spain, sent by individuals and corporations to often-related straw men nominees in one area of Bolivia, with the declared purpose of construction and purchase of properties through a local company. Funds also went to USD accounts at two financial institutions held by a money exchange house. The MSB's accounts also received international money orders from two companies with the same UK address. The nominees and MSB accounts moved money to another group including an MSB partner, who deposited cash into local currency accounts before sending electronic transfers to individuals in the Brazil-Bolivia border area. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple money orders originated from the same geographic area in Spain"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case Study 11 (Bolivia) indicators: registered at an address listed against numerous other companies or arrangements; large number of transactions with a small number of recipients; bank balance close to zero despite frequent incoming and outgoing transactions; family members with no role listed as beneficial owners; no real business activities; legal person pays no taxes, superannuation, retirement fund contributions or social benefits; connections between parties questionable or raise doubts the client cannot sufficiently explain; transaction between connected parties without apparent business or trade rationale; business-account transaction with an anomalous large cash deposit or withdrawal inconsistent with company profile; funds unusual in the context of the client's profile; funds sent to or received from a foreign country with no apparent connection to the client. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Bank balance of close to zero, despite frequent incoming and outgoing transactions"</sub>

## Case Study 12 - Canada

- **descriptive / case example**: Case Study 12 (Canada): a listed company's stock was part of a fraud on its market price involving stock promoters in Canada and elsewhere who manipulated the price with misleading representations and/or omissions; proceeds of up to USD 20 million were allegedly laundered through offshore banks. The US SEC showed shares flowing from Serbian nominees through intermediary international business companies; the shares were effectively bearer form, signed over by seed shareholders at issue. A US securities lawyer's opinion letter let the shares trade, and a reverse merger was completed right after the free-trading shares were anonymised and before a series of paid promotions. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These shares were effectively in bearer form having been signed over by the seed shareholders at the time of issue"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Case Study 12 (Canada) investigative challenges: Canadian investigators could not prove or confirm identities behind the real owners of the international business companies controlling the free-trading shares; they could not access documentation from offshore jurisdictions used to obscure beneficial ownership; and Serbian nominees repeatedly refused to co-operate or give witness statements. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Canadian investigators were unable to prove and confirm identities behind real owners"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Case Study 12 (Canada) indicators: entity formed in a low-tax jurisdiction or international trade/financial centre; nominee owners and directors; transaction involves transfer of bearer shares in an off-market sale. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transaction involves the transfer of bearer shares in an off-market sale"</sub>
