## Issue for Consideration 1

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paragraph 231 states that the majority of case studies analysed involved professional intermediaries, and approximately half of all cases were assessed as involving a complicit professional intermediary (complicit if, on the case summary, they appeared to have had a role in designing the scheme, knew of its illegal nature, or were charged with a crime); this is said to show complicity is not strictly necessary and that some professionals are unwitting or negligent. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approximately half of all cases were assessed as involving a complicit professional intermediary"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The FATF Horizontal Study of Supervision and Enforcement of Beneficial Ownership Obligations identified that a number of countries impose no AML/CFT obligations or supervision on any DNFBPs, despite this being a requirement of the FATF Standards. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a number of countries do not impose any AML/CFT obligations or supervision on any DNFBPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report describes professional intermediaries operating outside an AML/CFT regulatory regime as a "back-door" through which illicit wealth can enter the regulated banking and finance sector, putting financial institutions' AML/CFT programs at risk and detracting from the effectiveness of national and international regimes. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "represent a “back-door” through which illicit wealth can enter the regulated banking and finance sector"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The report states that the problem of professional intermediaries operating outside AML/CFT regimes should be addressed as a matter of priority through effective implementation of relevant FATF standards. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be addressed as a matter of priority through the effective implementation of relevant FATF standards"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Government authorities should work closely with private sector bodies to educate professionals about their vulnerabilities to ML/TF activity and the underlying threats seeking to exploit them, and allow professionals to share emerging risks drawing on their experience. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Government authorities should work closely with private sector bodies to educate professionals of their vulnerabilities"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Gateways exist in many countries for sharing information between law enforcement and regulated entities, and countries could consider how these avenues could be used to enhance risk awareness among professional intermediary sectors. (p. 89)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries could consider how these avenues of information exchange could be used to enhance risk awareness"</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 2

- **descriptive / recommendation**: Issue for Consideration 2: regulating professional intermediaries under AML/CFT law (per Recommendations 22, 23 and 28) and educating professionals about ML/TF threats and vulnerabilities (per Recommendation 34) will help mitigate vulnerabilities associated with concealment of beneficial ownership. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "will help mitigate the vulnerabilities associated with the concealment of beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Horizontal Study found inconsistent supervisory approaches when different intermediary types are supervised by different SRBs even for similar functions such as company formation; forums for co-operation exist but do not necessarily lead to a coherent supervisory approach. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a lack of consistency in the approach to supervision when different types of professional intermediaries are supervised by different bodies"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The TCSP sector is less clearly defined than legal and accounting sectors in many countries, making regulation and education difficult; some countries, particularly low-tax jurisdictions, have well-regulated TCSP sectors with integrity, competence and financial soundness tests, which the report calls a good means of professionalising the sector, and countries with less defined sectors should consider implementing similar measures. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries with TCSP sectors that are not as well-defined should consider implementing similar measures domestically"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Law enforcement and FIUs report LPP can be exploited by complicit legal professionals to hinder investigations (also reported in the FATF 2013 legal professionals report and 2014 guidance); privilege claims must be reviewed before being overturned, and its subjective nature will continue to challenge competent authorities through inconsistent application. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the subjective nature of LPP will continue to pose a challenge due to the potential for its inconsistent application"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Issue for Consideration 2 states that regulating professional intermediaries under AML/CFT law (per FATF Recommendations 22, 23 and 28) and efforts to educate professionals about their ML/TF threats and vulnerabilities (per Recommendation 34) will help mitigate the vulnerabilities associated with concealment of beneficial ownership. - **descriptive / finding or result**: TCSPs play an important role in establishing and managing legal persons, particularly where the beneficial owner lives in a foreign jurisdiction; the TCSP sector is less clearly defined or understood in many countries than the legal and accounting sectors, so authorities in many countries face challenges regulating and educating TCSPs on ML/TF risks. - **descriptive / finding or result**: Law enforcement and FIUs have reported that legal professional privilege (LPP) can be exploited by complicit legal professionals to frustrate and hinder investigations, as also reported in the 2013 FATF report on vulnerabilities of legal professionals and the 2014 guidance on Transparency and Beneficial Ownership. - **descriptive / limitation**: Privacy protections may limit the extent to which information exchanged under AEOI and EOIR can be used for law enforcement and financial intelligence purposes. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "privacy protections may limit the extent to which this information can be used for law enforcement"</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 6

- **descriptive / context**: Media attention on opaque ownership structures in tax evasion, ML and corruption (principally following the Mossack Fonseca 2015 and Appleby 2017 leaks) has prompted government responses including centralised beneficial ownership registers; corporate registries holding beneficial ownership information are also being implemented or enhanced, as among several mechanisms countries may consider under the FATF standards. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has prompted a range of responses from governments across the globe, including the consideration and development of centralised registers"</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 4

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Information exchanged through mechanisms such as Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI) and Exchange of Information on Request (EOIR) for Tax Purposes has the potential to significantly enhance law enforcement visibility of asset ownership in other jurisdictions, but privacy protections may limit how far it can be used for law enforcement and financial intelligence purposes. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "privacy protections may limit the extent to which this information can be used for law enforcement and financial intelligence purposes"</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 5

- **descriptive / recommendation**: Issue for Consideration 5: increased sharing of relevant information and transaction records would benefit global efforts to improve beneficial ownership transparency, and further consideration of possible ways to enhance this sharing is merited. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further consideration of possible ways to enhance this information sharing is merited."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because most schemes disguising beneficial ownership are transnational, it is often not possible for FIUs and competent authorities to have direct, independent access to the information needed to discover and prove beneficial ownership; mutual legal assistance has been identified as a key tool in most major investigations involving transnational corporate structures or international financial flows. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "mutual legal assistance has been identified as a key tool in most major investigations"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many law enforcement and intelligence practitioners reported that delays in mutual legal assistance requests are one of the issues that most significantly inhibits an investigation; the report acknowledges that a country's ability to respond depends on its resources and operational demands, but says it is evident that more can be done to improve the quality and timeliness of MLA responses. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "delays in mutual legal assistance requests are one of the issues that most significantly inhibits an investigation"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The report states that FATF Recommendations 36-40 require countries to implement formal and informal mechanisms for sharing information on ML/TF and predicate offences. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF Recommendations 36-40 require countries to implement formal and informal mechanisms for sharing information"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: The report says further study to understand what can be done to improve international co-operation, including MLA, is merited. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further study to understand what can be done to improve international co-operation, including MLA, is merited."</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 6

- **expected / recommendation**: Issue for Consideration 6: further research should be undertaken to understand what can be done to improve the quality and timeliness of cross-border information sharing, including through mutual legal assistance. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further research should be undertaken to understand what can be done to improve the quality and timeliness"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Competent authorities can use multiple information sources simultaneously, and the FATF Standards state it is very likely countries will need a combination of mechanisms to ensure law enforcement access to adequate, accurate and timely beneficial ownership information; if correctly monitored and supervised, beneficial ownership registers could support CDD by financial institutions and professionals. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is very likely that countries will need to utilise a combination of mechanisms"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In designing beneficial ownership repositories, governments should be conscious of ensuring information is accurate, up-to-date and readily available to competent authorities and the private sector; a register is only as valuable as the quality and accuracy of its information, and criminals' concealment techniques (including formal and informal nominees) are expected to be adapted to circumvent or diminish registers. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A register of information, whether it contains beneficial ownership or any other type of company information"</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 7

- **recommended / recommendation**: Issue for Consideration 7: countries using beneficial ownership registers should consider the resourcing and expertise needed for maintenance so recorded information is adequate, accurate, up-to-date and timely accessible; this also applies to maintaining and supervising company registries. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should consider the resourcing and expertise requirements associated with their maintenance"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Establishing companies, opening bank accounts and moving money without face-to-face contact is a growing vulnerability commonly exploited by criminals; the Horizontal Study confirms several jurisdictions permit direct on-line incorporation using digital identity; digital identification and information-sharing innovations will likely be part of future solutions, and the FATF and Egmont Group are increasingly engaged with the private sector. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "direct on-line incorporation of companies using various forms of digital identity is permitted by a number of jurisdictions77"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The private sector has identified emerging measures that may be highly valuable for CDD, and countries may wish to consider how these initiatives might be harnessed to improve transparency of business transactions. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries may wish to consider how these initiatives might be harnessed"</sub>

## Issue for Consideration 8

- **expected / expectation**: To meet the challenges of opaque ownership, governments, financial institutions and intermediaries need to understand vulnerabilities, threats and risks of legal persons and arrangements; it is essential for governments to maintain a robust, contemporary and publicly accessible ML/TF risk assessment, and national risk assessments should also analyse transnational threats and vulnerabilities, thereby informing assessments by financial institutions and professional service providers. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is therefore essential for governments to maintain a robust, contemporary, and publicly accessible assessment"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The report states the FATF Standards require countries to understand their risks, including having mechanisms to assess ML and TF risks associated with different types of legal person created in their country. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Standards require countries to understand the risks that they face"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Issue for Consideration 8: it would be beneficial for legal person risk assessments to carefully consider and articulate vulnerabilities and threats relating to domestic and foreign legal persons and arrangements, the domestic and foreign intermediaries involved in establishing them, and how criminals may exploit them for ML and other criminality. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It would be beneficial for these assessments to carefully consider and articulate the vulnerabilities and threats"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Concealment of beneficial ownership is a significant ML vulnerability in every country and will remain a major challenge; globalisation, digitisation and access to opaque legal vehicles are enduring challenges, and there is no single solution, only numerous iterative and interrelated solutions requiring continued will of governments, private organisations and the public. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is no one solution or panacea for this problem"</sub>
