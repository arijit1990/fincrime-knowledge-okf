## Trade-Based Money Laundering – Trends and Developments

- **descriptive / context**: The joint 2020 FATF-Egmont report Trade-Based Money Laundering – Trends and Developments helps public and private sectors detect TBML, uses case studies from the FATF Global Network to explain how criminals exploit trade transactions to move money rather than goods, and recommends using national risk assessments and risk-focused material to raise awareness among entities in international trade, improving sharing of financial and trade data, and cooperation between authorities and the private sector including through public-private partnerships. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it explains the ways in which criminals exploit trade transactions to move money, rather than goods"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Readers may also want to familiarise themselves with earlier TBML studies from the FATF Global Network: a 2006 report on TBML, a 2008 best-practices paper, and a 2012 typologies report by the Asia Pacific Group on Money Laundering. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a 2006 report on TBML,1 a 2008 best-practices paper,2 and a 2012 typologies report"</sub>

## Audience and how risk indicators can be used

- **descriptive / definition**: A risk indicator demonstrates or suggests the likelihood of unusual or suspicious activity. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A risk indicator demonstrates or suggests the likelihood of the occurrence of unusual or suspicious activity."</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: A single indicator for a customer or transaction may not alone warrant suspicion of TBML nor clearly indicate it, but could prompt further monitoring and examination, as appropriate; several indicators occurring together could also warrant closer examination. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may not alone warrant suspicion of TBML, nor will the indicator necessarily provide a clear indication"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Whether one or more indicators suggests TBML depends on the institution's business lines, products or services, how it interacts with customers, and its human and technological resources. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "how it interacts with its customers; and on the institution’s human and technological resources"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The indicators are relevant to both public and private sectors; in the private sector this covers financial institutions (including banks and money value transfer services), DNFBPs, and small and mid-size businesses and large conglomerates. They are intended for personnel in compliance, transaction monitoring, investigative analysis, client onboarding and relationship management, and other financial crime prevention areas. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "client onboarding and relationship management, and other areas that work to prevent financial crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Some indicators require cross-comparison of data elements (e.g. financial transactions, customs data, open market prices) often held externally, so the private sector will not observe all of them; for some it will need additional context from competent authorities, e.g. via law enforcement or FIUs. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the private sector will not observe all of the indicators identified below"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In using the indicators, private sector entities should take into consideration the totality of the customer profile, including information from the customer during due diligence, trade financing methods involved, and other relevant contextual risk factors. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "private sector entities should also take into consideration the totality of the customer profile"</sub>

## Structural risk indicators

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: the corporate structure of a trade entity appears unusually complex and illogical, such as involvement of shell companies or companies registered in high-risk jurisdictions. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The corporate structure of a trade entity appears unusually complex and illogical"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: a trade entity is registered or has offices in a jurisdiction with weak AML/CFT compliance. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A trade entity is registered or has offices in a jurisdiction with weak AML/CFT compliance."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: a trade entity is registered at an address likely to be a mass registration address (e.g. high-density residential buildings, post-box addresses, commercial buildings or industrial complexes), especially with no reference to a specific unit. Footnote 4 adds this may include the address of a trust and company service provider managing several shell companies for customers. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "registered at an address that is likely to be a mass registration address"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: the business activity does not appear appropriate for the stated address, e.g. the entity uses residential properties without commercial or industrial space and with no reasonable explanation. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not appear to be appropriate for the stated address"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: a trade entity lacks an online presence, or its online presence suggests activity inconsistent with its stated business, e.g. a website mainly of boilerplate material from other websites or showing lack of knowledge of the product or industry traded. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lacks an online presence or the online presence suggests business activity inconsistent with the stated line of business"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: a notable lack of typical business activities, e.g. no regular payroll transactions in line with stated employee numbers, no transactions for operating costs, no tax remittances. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "displays a notable lack of typical business activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: owners or senior managers appear to be nominees concealing the actual beneficial owners, e.g. lacking business management experience or knowledge of transaction details, or managing multiple companies. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appear to be nominees acting to conceal the actual beneficial owners"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: the trade entity, or its owners or senior managers, appear in negative news, e.g. past ML schemes, fraud, tax evasion, other criminal activities, or ongoing or past investigations or convictions. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appear in negative news, e.g. past money laundering schemes, fraud, tax evasion"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: a trade entity maintains a minimal number of working staff, inconsistent with its volume of traded commodities. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintains a minimal number of working staff, inconsistent with its volume of traded commodities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: the entity's name appears to copy, or be very similar to, the name of a well-known corporation, potentially to appear part of it though not actually connected. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appears to be a copy of the name of a well-known corporation or is very similar to it"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: a trade entity has unexplained periods of dormancy. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A trade entity has unexplained periods of dormancy."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Structural indicator: an entity is not compliant with regular business obligations, such as filing VAT returns. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An entity is not compliant with regular business obligations, such as filing VAT returns."</sub>

## Trade activity risk indicators

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: trade activity is inconsistent with the stated line of business of the entities involved, e.g. a car dealer exporting clothing or a precious metals dealer importing seafood. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a car dealer is exporting clothing or a precious metals dealer is importing seafood"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: complex trade deals involving numerous third-party intermediaries in incongruent lines of business. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "complex trade deals involving numerous third-party intermediaries in incongruent lines of business"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: transactions and shipping routes or methods inconsistent with standard business practices. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transactions and shipping routes or methods that are inconsistent with standard business practices"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: unconventional or overly complex use of financial products, e.g. letters of credit used for unusually long or frequently extended periods without apparent reason, or intermingling different trade finance products for different segments of trade transactions. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unconventional or overly complex use of financial products"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: consistently unreasonably low profit margins, e.g. importing wholesale commodities at or above retail value, or reselling at or below purchase price. Footnote 5 says determining margin may require estimating the "fair price", which can be difficult for commodities not traded on the open market. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consistently displays unreasonably low profit margins.5 in its trade transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: purchases of commodities, allegedly on own account, that clearly exceed the entity's economic capabilities, e.g. financed through sudden influxes of cash deposits or third-party transfers into its accounts. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the purchases clearly exceed the economic capabilities of the entity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trade activity indicator: a newly formed or recently re-activated trade entity engages in high-volume, high-value trade, e.g. an unknown entity suddenly appears in sectors with high barriers to market entry. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A newly formed or recently re-activated trade entity engages in high-volume and high- value trade activity"</sub>

## Trade document and commodity risk indicators

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: inconsistencies across contracts, invoices or other trade documents, e.g. contradiction between the exporter's name and the payment recipient's name, differing prices on invoices and underlying contracts, or discrepancies between actual commodity quantity, quality, volume or value and descriptions. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Inconsistencies across contracts, invoices or other trade documents"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: fees or prices that do not seem in line with commercial considerations, are inconsistent with market value, or fluctuate significantly from previous comparable transactions. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "display fees or prices that do not seem to be in line with commercial considerations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: vague descriptions of the traded commodities, e.g. described only generically or nonspecifically. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have vague descriptions of the traded commodities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: supporting trade or customs documents are missing, appear counterfeit, contain false or misleading information, are resubmissions of previously rejected documents, or are frequently modified or amended. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are a resubmission of previously rejected documents, or are frequently modified or amended"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: contracts for complex or regular trade appear unusually simple, e.g. following a "sample contract" structure available on the Internet. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appear to be unusually simple, e.g. they follow a “sample contract” structure available on the Internet"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: significant mismatch between the value of an entity's registered imports and its volume of foreign bank transfers for imports; conversely, registered exports significantly mismatched with incoming foreign bank transfers. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "significant mismatches to the entity’s volume of foreign bank transfers for imports"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: commodities imported under temporary importation and inward processing regimes are subsequently exported with falsified documents. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "temporary importation and inward processing regime are subsequently exported with falsified documents"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Document indicator: shipments of commodities are routed through a number of jurisdictions without economic or commercial justification. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "routed through a number of jurisdictions without economic or commercial justification"</sub>

## Account and transaction activity risk indicators

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: a trade entity makes very late changes to payment arrangements, for example redirecting payment to a previously unknown entity at the very last moment, or asking to change the scheduled payment date or amount. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A trade entity makes very late changes to payment arrangements for the transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: an account shows an unexpectedly high number or value of transactions that is inconsistent with the client's stated business activity. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unexpectedly high number or value of transactions that are inconsistent with the stated business activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: a trade entity's account looks like a "pay-through" or "transit" account, with rapid movement of high-volume transactions and a small end-of-day balance without clear business reasons. Two listed sub-patterns are (a) frequent cash deposits that are then transferred to persons or entities in free trade zones or offshore jurisdictions having no business relationship with the account holder, and (b) incoming wire transfers to a trade-related account that are split and forwarded to multiple unrelated accounts with little or no connection to commercial activity. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appears to be a “pay-through” or “transit” account with a rapid movement of high-volume transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: payment for imported commodities is made by an entity other than the consignee with no clear economic reasons, for example by a shell or front company not involved in the trade transaction. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payment for imported commodities is made by an entity other than the consignee of the commodities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: a trade entity's cash deposits or other transactions are consistently just below the relevant reporting thresholds. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consistently just below relevant reporting thresholds"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: transaction activity associated with a trade entity rises quickly and significantly in volume and then goes dormant after a short period. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "increases in volume quickly and significantly, and then goes dormant after a short period of time"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: payments are sent or received in large round amounts for trade in sectors where that is deemed unusual. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payments are sent or received in large round amounts for trade in sectors where this is deemed as unusual."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Account/transaction indicator: payments are routed in a circle, with funds sent out from one country and received back in the same country after passing through another country or countries. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "funds are sent out from one country and received back in the same country"</sub>
