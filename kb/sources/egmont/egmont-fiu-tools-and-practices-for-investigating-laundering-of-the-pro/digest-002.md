## Indicators of Corruption in Public Procurement

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 1: state-owned companies or public institutions receive services from shell companies, offshore companies or formations, companies in registration offices or P.O. companies. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Services provided to state-owned companies or public institutions by shell companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 2: state-owned companies or public institutions receive services from companies registered in high-risk jurisdictions. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "by companies registered in high-risk jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 3: long-term contracts repeatedly awarded to the same subcontractor, or a legal entity or arrangement consistently winning a majority of the largest contracting authority's tenders or public procurement bids. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Long-term contracts are repeatedly awarded to the same subcontractor"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 4: the procuring authority issues unreasonable contract specifications, including restrictive conditions on the contractor's location or on required materials, or particularly tight deadlines. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The issuance of unreasonable specifications for the performance of the contract"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 5: subcontractors share director(s) or beneficial owner(s) and/or are related to the contractor's management. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Subcontractors have common director(s), beneficial owner(s) and/or are related with the management"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 6: subcontractors or intermediaries are brought into business deals once a contract has already been agreed and for no obvious reason. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "brought in on business deals once a contract has already been agreed and for no obvious reason"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 7: contractors, subcontractors or their counterparties, within the timeframe for completing the state contract, are linked by address, telephone number, IP address and the like. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are linked by address, telephone number, IP-address, etc."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 8: procurement projects funded through loan agreements by governing bodies such as development institutions where the eventual tender price is significantly higher than the loan amount requested. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the eventual tender price put out is significantly higher than the loan amount requested"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 9: deposits in public officials' accounts of checks issued by construction companies, individuals or non-governmental entities that previously benefited from public works contracts. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Deposits in public officials’ accounts with checks issued by construction companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 10: legal entities with little or limited experience receiving highly complex and technical government contracts or projects not compatible with their size or experience, or contracts unrelated to their field of business. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Legal entities with little or limited experience receiving highly complex and technical government contracts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 11: a legal entity or arrangement that is a contractor to a state-owned company usually receives higher payments for goods or services than normal market prices for equivalent products or services. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "usually receives payments of higher amounts for goods or services which normally should cost less"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 12: funds received by a public procurement contractor are not spent within a reasonable timeframe to fulfil the contract needs. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not spent within a reasonable timeframe to fulfil the contract needs"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 13: checks issued in favour of public officials from accounts of persons who benefited from public procurement or funds, without an evident justification. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Checks issued in favor of public officials and come from accounts of persons that benefited"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 14: checks issued by a public entity are cashed out and subsequently deposited to accounts of public officials or entities related to them. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Checks issued by a public entity being cashed out and subsequently deposited"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 15: public officials, especially those with a role in government contract management or public procurement of high-value assets, receive funds transfer instructions from business and/or personal accounts where the funds appear excessive in value; according to in-built distribution methods or contractors or intermediaries; from distributors used at the request of the contracting party; according to rebate arrangements, particularly if agreed outside the contract; or under requirements to obtain licenses and other government permits as a pre-requisite of doing business. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "especially those having a role in government contract management or public procurement of high-value assets"</sub>

## EGMONT GROUP SET OF INDICATORS FOR CORRUPTION RELATED CASES FROM THE FIUS’ PERSPECTIVE

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Public procurement indicator 16 concerns the engagement of third parties (contractors, consultants, vendors, suppliers, advisors or intermediaries) to help carry out procurement contracts. Its warning signs about the third party are: it asks for compensation that its contract does not expressly provide for; it asks for payments to go to different third parties; it asks for charitable or political contributions; it works in a line of business different from the one it was engaged for; it has little or no experience of the relevant industry or activity; and it has no office in the country where the services are to be performed. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Use of third parties, such as contractors, consultants, vendors, suppliers and advisor/intermediaries"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Further warning signs under procurement indicator 16: the third party was only recently formed or incorporated; it has poor financial stability or credit record; it depends heavily on subcontractors or intermediaries (so-called 'fourth parties'); it joined the transaction because a public official expressly requested or insisted on it; it was recommended or referred by a public official; and its commissions are unreasonably large or rest on inaccurate or incomplete invoices. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The third party became part of a transaction at the express request or insistence of a public official"</sub>

## Indicators of Corruption in Public Procurement

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 17: the contracting party issues commercial cards to individuals who are not its employees, used to buy luxury goods, pay for high-cost services or make other transactions that are not normal business expenses. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Contracting party issues commercial cards to individuals that are not employees of contracting party"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 18: payments under a public procurement contract are made at a price higher than originally contracted. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payments based on a public procurement contract are conducted at a price higher than originally contracted"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 19: payments under a public procurement contract where there was only a single bid for the government tender, which signals lack of competition and closed access. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "only a single bid for a government procurement tender"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 20: receipt of commission or fees before signing the agreement for services or before carrying out a function or process relating to a public procurement contract. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Receipt of commission or fees before signing of agreement for services"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 21: commissions, interest or payments under the commercial terms of a public procurement contract are increased, reduced or restructured in a way that is not commercially viable. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in a manner that is not commercially viable"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 22: repeated or subsequent purchases of low-quality goods, works and services at market prices of higher-quality goods, or purchases at higher than market prices. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Repeated or subsequent purchases of low-quality goods, works and services at market prices"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 23: payments for goods under public procurement contracts without delivery of the goods to the customs territory of the country. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without delivery of such goods to customs territory of the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Procurement indicator 24: payments to accounts of providers of goods, works and services opened in countries different from where the goods, works and services originate or are provided. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payments are conducted to accounts of providers of goods, works and services"</sub>

## Indicators of Unexplained Wealth or Income

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 25: subjects in a transaction are domestic or foreign public officials who receive and/or send unusually large amounts of funds in different currencies. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "receive and/or send unusually large amounts of funds in different currencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 26: funds received in accounts of persons, legal entities or arrangements with no visible connection to public officials but known to be controlled by them or related persons (a frontman, strawman, or entity set up to conceal beneficial ownership), where funds were sent by a shell company and the accompanying information refers to loans, investment purposes or purchase of real estate, or otherwise reveals an irreconcilable conflict of interest between a private enterprise and a public official. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with no visible connection to public officials, but known to be controlled by such"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 27: a representative of a public official (e.g. lawyer, secretary, accountant) opens an account and buys expensive property or luxury goods with the express intent of bypassing CDD screening for public officials. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with the express intent of bypassing Customer Due Diligence (CDD) process screening for public officials"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 28: straw men (especially in the remittance sector) can obfuscate beneficial ownership by involving officials' employees such as a cleaner, gardener or driver; funds received on such straw men's accounts usually significantly exceed their legitimate employment income. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can be used to obfuscate the beneficial ownership of the assets by involving public officials"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 29: public officials receive or purchase shares (or share options) in a company in exchange for services; where the vendor finances the purchase; where the price is below the company's net asset value; where they receive a dividend disproportional to the purchase price; where the shares carry the right to sell at a price higher than current market value or the purchase price; or where they profit from a share transaction with purchase and sale dates within a short period. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public officials receive or purchase shares (or the option to purchase shares):"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 30: public officials receive loan guarantees from a public corporation or government body, or a loan under favourable conditions. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public officials receive loan guarantees from a public corporation or government body"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 31: public officials receive large sums for attending workshops or conferences or as project consultants, in order to disguise the origin of funds so they are not seen as payment of corruption. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "for their attendance in workshops, conferences or as consultants to projects"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicators 32 and 33: public officials receive debt forgiveness or have repayment requirements waived by the creditor (32); public officials perform transactions with sovereign wealth funds or government-linked companies (33). (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public officials receive debt forgiveness or repayment requirements are waived by the creditor."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 34: misrepresentation and/or inconsistency between the source of wealth declared by public officials in sworn asset declarations and that established during due diligence. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "between the declared source of wealth of public officials through their sworn asset declarations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 35: public officials have purchased virtual assets in a total amount higher than their legally declared income. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public officials have purchased virtual assets in a total amount higher than their legally declared income."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 36: purchase of goods or services, transfer of payments or receipt of other benefits (e.g. rental payments, school fees, chauffeur fees, private healthcare fees, funding of private jets, consultancy fees, high commissions) for or on behalf of a public official, from the contracting authority or a contractor during execution of the state contract. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from the contracting authority, or a contractor in the period of the execution of the state contract"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 37: transactions in public officials' accounts involving cash deposits or withdrawals of unusual frequency and amounts. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "involving cash deposits or withdrawals in unusual frequency and amounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 38: incoming transactions from foreign jurisdictions (specifically high-risk ones) on public officials' accounts, intended for real estate or high-value or luxury goods purchases, typically carrying no additional transaction information and vague remittance information (e.g. 'consultancy fees'), leaving a lack of transparency and difficulty determining the source of funds. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "typically contain no additional information about the transaction itself"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 39: purchases or leases of movable or immovable assets by public officials that do not coincide with the subject's income. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which do not coincide with the subject’s income"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 40: use of hawala type mechanisms (especially through the remittance sector) by public officials to move money abroad. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The use of hawala type mechanisms (especially through the remittance sector)"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 41: Fixed Term Deposit Certificates made by companies with the main purpose that the capital and interest should be transferred immediately to accounts of a political party. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be transferred immediately to accounts of a political party"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 42: cash deposits with no rationale, with two sub-points: credit card or home loan applications (even if declined) are useful to find out what the official earns versus what is deposited; and cash deposits made into the same official's account from different locations. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Credit card/ home loan applications (even if declined) are useful to find out"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 43: immediate transfer of funds from a private entity's account to a public official's personal account and onward movement to third-party accounts, eventually abroad, indicating the accounts are used as a temporary node; some persons in the chain may deduct a percentage before passing funds on, indicating they received a commission. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "indicates the use of the aforementioned accounts as a temporary node"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 44: incoming cash or electronic transfers from different external sources on public officials' accounts are later spent at online gambling sites, with credit from the same or different gambling sites then visible. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are later spent at online gambling sites"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 45: transfer of funds from public officials' accounts to high-risk vehicles abroad, such as corporate trusts. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to high-risk vehicles abroad, such as corporate trusts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 46: public officials establish legal entities or arrangements that have bought land and buildings of significant value (evident from accounting documents) despite no other commercial activity or without a justifiable source of funds. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "despite the absence of any other commercial activity, or without a justifiable source of funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 47: public officials make cash transactions involving large amounts (e.g. currency exchange, cash purchases of high value goods). (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public officials have made cash transactions involving large amounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 48: payments of unusual amounts or frequency from public officials to lawyers, accountants or other professional intermediaries. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transaction payments of unusual amounts or frequency from public officials to lawyers"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 49: payments in favour of public officials made to facilitate or expedite a government service. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payments in favor of public officials are made to facilitate or expedite a government service."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 50: use of state funds to buy shares in private companies, or private companies belonging to public officials, at prices above market value. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Use of state funds to purchase shares in private companies or private companies belonging to public officials"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 51: issuance of sovereign debt to public officials or entities known to be controlled by them at interest rates above the prevailing market rate. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Issuance of sovereign debt to public officials or entities known to be controlled by them"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 52: use of Joint Venture structures for government contracts in which public officials or their companies are silent partners, for example a third silent shareholder owned or controlled by an official inserted into a JV between a state-owned and a private company so the official takes a share of the profit. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in which public officials or a company belonging to them are silent partners"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 53: payments by entities to NPOs that public officials are known to be associated with. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payments by entities to NPOs that public officials are known to be associated with."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 54: a transaction or financial activity involving foreign nationals with no significant link (apart from the financial) to the country, known to be active consultants or employees of lobbying organizations and sometimes reluctant to explain the source of wealth or funds or giving unsatisfactory explanations. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These foreign nationals are known to be active consultants or employees of lobbying organizations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 55: financial flows revealing complex financial mechanisms and intervention by foreign legal entities or arrangements are received in an account in another jurisdiction that is related to a public official. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reveal complex financial mechanisms and intervention by foreign legal entities or arrangements"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 56: international transfer from the Treasury of a foreign country to shell companies, entities with no public profile or no physical or online presence, or individuals who are not known government employees. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International transfer from the Treasury of a foreign country to shell companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 57: the stated source of wealth of funds received in a public official's account may be inconsistent with the client's stated career history, expertise or age, including a mismatch between stated career history and total net worth. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be inconsistent with the client's stated career history, expertise, or age"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth indicator 59: a customer, especially a public official, transferring funds to or from other public officials, including law enforcement officers. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transferring funds to/from other public officials, including law enforcement officers"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unexplained wealth or income indicator 58: transaction activity typically marked by payments made by and to the customer's own accounts (the same name) or moving between offshore company and trust structures that are linked, or known to be linked, to public officials. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "first party payments to and from accounts in the same name"</sub>

## General Indicators

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 60: open source information that can relate specific financial activity to ongoing investigations into individuals, and concerns about corruption. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Open source information, which can relate specific financial activity to ongoing investigations"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 61: an entity that receives public contracts and its legal representative(s) appear in media reports linking them to corruption or other financial crimes. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appear in media reports, which link/s him/her/them to corruption or other financial crimes"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 62: payments by contractors for consultancy services, particularly in higher corruption-risk industries such as arms, mineral extraction, telecoms and public infrastructure, where the amount appears outside the normal price range for consultancy services. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly in industries with a higher risk to corruption, such as arms, mineral extraction, telecoms"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 63: a fiduciary service company that set up the structure for the applicant may be the subject of negative press reporting. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may be the subject of negative press reporting"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 64: close family members or associates of public officials are appointed as senior management officials in private companies without meeting the necessary requirements, or the hire's salary or compensation package is not commensurate with market conditions. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are appointed as senior management officials in private companies without meeting the necessary requirements"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 66: an applicant who is a public official expresses urgency on an application (e.g. completion of a mortgage or other time-critical transaction). (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Applicant (who is a public official) expresses urgency on an application"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 67: explanations for transactions may use words and phrases often used as euphemisms for bribes (for example commission, marketing fees, surcharge). (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "words and phrases often used as euphemisms for bribes"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 68: public officials increase their standard of living after their mandate expires without legally justifiable reasons; or are unable or refuse to give a credible account of how wealth was generated or to corroborate the source of wealth; or the corroborating documentation raises authenticity concerns or is inconsistent with the source of wealth statement. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "increase their standard of living after the expiration of the officials’ mandate"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 69: opaqueness of government business schemes used to encourage diversity, which should be overtly transparent. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Opaqueness of government business schemes used to encourage diversity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 70: companies that pay other firms to perform logistical roles they could perform themselves, in countries with a high degree of perceived corruption, in order to transfer the risk to the other firm. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in order to transfer the risk to the other firm"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 71: companies changing the terms of agreements and the definitions of intermediaries to avoid registration and regulatory oversight in other countries. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to avoid registration and regulatory oversight in other countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 65: an applicant seeks to open an account with a needlessly complicated structure of economic and beneficial ownership, which may involve eclectic wealth planning arrangements or bearer share companies, known to be linked to a public official. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unnecessarily complex structure of economic and beneficial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: General indicator 72: a company wins a public tender with a short submission period (the number of days between publication of the call for tenders and the bid deadline). (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company wins a public tender with short submission period"</sub>
