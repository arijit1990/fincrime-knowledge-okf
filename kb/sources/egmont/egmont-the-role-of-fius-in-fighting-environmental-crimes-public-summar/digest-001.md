## 1.0 Introduction

## 2.1.1 LACK OF RESOURCES (FINANCIAL, TECHNOLOGICAL, HUMAN)

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Lack of resources was the most commonly reported FIU challenge. Limited resources hinder collecting and analysing data and detecting related financial activity; FIUs face limits in staffing, training and technology, and lack sophisticated tools such as data analytics to analyse large data volumes. Environmental crimes may also be a lower priority than other crimes, so fewer resources are allocated. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The most commonly reported challenge by FIUs was lack of resources."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Environmental crimes without an immediate, direct financial trail are hard to identify with traditional methods; for example illegal logging or illegal wildlife trade may involve payments through informal networks, barter or cash that leave little trail. FIUs reported difficulty connecting these activities to larger financial crimes or the broader criminal operation. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may involve payments through informal networks, barter transactions, or cash payments that leave little financial trail"</sub>

## CRIMES

- **descriptive / limitation**: Lack of awareness among financial institutions, regulators and FIUs of how financial activity relates to environmental crimes, or how criminal networks operate in environment-related sectors, can impede strategies and protocols for detecting, monitoring and investigating such activity. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "how criminal networks operate within environmental related sectors can impede abilities to design strategies and protocols"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Financial institutions may not easily recognize transactions tied to environmental crimes without a clear pattern of unusual behaviour linked to an environmental offence, and may lack training to detect signs such as bribes, movement of funds linked to illegal resource extraction, or payments for illegal wildlife trade products. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as bribes, movement of funds linked to illegal resource extraction or payments for illegal wildlife trade products"</sub>

## 2.0 Key Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Key Finding 4 lists the strategies FIUs proposed to improve their ability to combat environmental crime: stronger cooperation across international, public, private and non-government parties; automating processes; ongoing training investment; sectoral risk assessments; more information sharing; and clear communication channels. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strengthening international, public, private and non-government cooperation, automating processes, investing in continuous training"</sub>

## 2.1.1 LACK OF RESOURCES (FINANCIAL, TECHNOLOGICAL, HUMAN)

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: As with other crimes, criminal organizations behind environmental crimes may use complex structures, for example shell companies, offshore accounts or trade-based money laundering, to hide illegal profits; FIUs reported that this makes tracing suspicious transactions back to the criminal activity hard. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as shell companies, offshore accounts, or trade-based money laundering, to disguise illegal profits"</sub>

## CRIMES

- **descriptive / limitation**: FIUs may lack expertise to identify environmental-crime financial activity. These crimes can involve industries with complex supply chains, and understanding the financial indicators can require nuanced understanding of sectors like forestry, fisheries, mining and agriculture; without training or expert consultation FIUs may struggle to recognize suspicious patterns in illegal logging or illegal wildlife trade. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sectors like forestry, fisheries, mining and agriculture"</sub>

## 2.1.4. REGULATORY CONSTRAINTS

- **descriptive / limitation**: Environmental crimes may be enforced under multiple laws that may not align with AML/CFT standards. Environmental laws may be weak, poorly enforced or hard to link to financial crime detection, making it difficult to establish legal links between illicit finance and environmental crime and hindering FIUs from acting on suspicious transactions. Some FIUs face regulatory limits on collecting or sharing certain data or conducting specific activities concerning environmental crimes. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these legal frameworks may not align with anti-money laundering or anti-terrorist financing standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Environmental crimes can cross borders with syndicates operating in multiple jurisdictions that have varying standards for detection, reporting and enforcement. In some jurisdictions environmental crime is not a local law enforcement priority, making international cooperation difficult, and FIUs reported difficulty tracking cross-border financial flows, especially when corruption or lack of resources impedes cooperation with counterparts. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In some jurisdictions, environmental crimes may not be a priority for local law enforcement"</sub>

## 2.2 Detailed Overview of Key Finding 2 - National Risk Assessments

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Of 54 FIU respondents, 31 (57%) said environmental crimes are considered in their ML and terrorist financing NRAs. Topics and ratings differed: one respondent rated pollution crimes high and illegal wildlife trade and illegal fishing medium, while another rated illegal logging and trade in timber low and pollution crimes medium-low. Differences may be explained by laws, role in legal and/or illegal supply chains, geography and natural resources. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "31 (57 %) indicated that environmental crimes are taken into consideration"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: A targeted risk-based approach in NRAs for environmental crimes and ML can facilitate identifying high-risk areas, sectors or actors, enabling efficient use of resources on the most pressing risks, such as industries prone to illicit financial flows from environmental crimes and a jurisdiction's level of risk as source, destination and/or transit. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can facilitate the identification of high-risk areas, sectors, or actors that are more likely to be involved"</sub>

## 2.3 Detailed Overview of Key Finding 3 - Resources to Support FIUs

- **descriptive / finding or result**: FIUs said they want six resources: a guide for identifying environmental-crime typologies related to AML/CFT; a database of environmental crime offenders; a catalogue of red flags to be shared widely with obligated entities; high quality, comprehensive training for holders of judicial functions, initially and continuing; sectoral risk assessments focused on ML risks of the various environmental crime types; and a case study repository showing how FIUs supported investigations/regulatory enquiries and sharing success stories. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A catalogue of red flags related to environmental crimes to be shared widely with obligated entities"</sub>

## 2.4 Detailed Overview of Key Finding 4 - Strategies Proposed By FIUs to Bolster their Ability to Combat Environmental Crime

- **descriptive / recommendation**: Strategies proposed by FIUs (first group): develop a PPP with reporting entities and national authorities to raise awareness of ML linked to environmental crimes; enhance collaboration with agencies with more information when developing typologies; continuous training to strengthen typology and/or advisory development and deepen knowledge; and high quality, comprehensive training for holders of judicial functions, initially and continuing. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Develop a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) with reporting entities and national authorities to raise awareness"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Strategies proposed by FIUs (second group): undertake sectoral risk assessments on ML risks of the various environmental crime types; improve STR quantity and quality from obliged entities; automate detection processes; use typologies to identify criminals' methods and aid investigations; analyse the value chain of environmental crimes and links with other transnational crimes. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Improvement of STR quantity and quality in terms of STR submission by obliged entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Strategies proposed by FIUs (third group): harmonize national authorities' activities with international standards (primarily FATF standards); a whole-government approach with regular assessment of environmental-crime ML risks; and strengthen ML investigation and punishment for all major threats, including environmental crimes. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Harmonization of activities of national authorities with international standards (primarily FATF standards)"</sub>

## 3.1 Recommendations

- **descriptive / context**: Based on the key findings, the Project Team identified 6 recommendations to improve FIU effectiveness in preventing, detecting and deterring environmental crimes. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Project Team has identified the following 6 recommendations to improve the effectiveness of FIUs"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 1 (Key findings 1 and 3): FIUs should leverage existing resources on financial activity associated with environmental crimes to give regulated entities guidance on trends, typologies and indicators of laundering proceeds of environmental crimes, to facilitate STR submission. Footnote 1 cites existing examples: an operational alert on laundering proceeds from illegal wildlife trade, an EWG report on financial flows associated with IWT in South Africa (FIC), and AUSTRAC's illegal wildlife trafficking financial crime guide. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should leverage existing resources on the financial activity associated to environmental crimes"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 2 (Key findings 1, 3, 4): FIUs should establish and promote channels in their jurisdiction for meaningful sharing of intelligence on environmental crimes among themselves and with relevant law enforcement authorities and other agencies, as appropriate. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should establish and promote channels within their jurisdiction that allow for meaningful sharing of intelligence"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 3 (Key finding 4): FIUs should proactively share financial intelligence on environmental crimes internationally via established FIU-to-FIU channels, in line with the Egmont Group Charter. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should proactively share financial intelligence on environmental crimes internationally via established FIU-to-FIU channels"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 4 (Key finding 1): FIUs should take steps within their jurisdiction to ensure that offences connected to environmental crimes are treated as predicate offences for money laundering. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ensure that offences connected to environmental crimes are treated as predicate offences for money laundering"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 5 (Key finding 2): FIUs should identify and assess ML risks relating to environmental crimes, particularly in the context of national risk assessments on money laundering. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should identify and assess the money laundering risks relating to environmental crimes"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Recommendation 6 (Key findings 1 and 2): FIUs should actively seek to establish, maintain and enhance AML-focused public-private partnerships facilitating sharing of relevant information as appropriate, at tactical and strategic level, on laundering of proceeds of environmental crimes. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should actively seek to establish, maintain, and enhance AML-focused public-private partnerships"</sub>

## 3.2 Next Steps

- **descriptive / recommendation**: To implement the recommendations, the Project Team proposes the Egmont Group consider three deliverables, in a second phase or gradually as resources allow: (1) targeted indicator/red flag bulletins on illicit finance/ML for illegal wildlife trade, illegal/unregulated fishing, illegal logging and trade in timber, illegal mining and other environmental crimes, to help FIUs guide regulated entities on STR submissions; (2) an FIU Toolkit for environmental crimes consolidating indicators/red flags, keywords, case studies and reputable publications to improve detection and analysis of related transaction reports; (3) subject matter expert briefings for Egmont FIUs on the financial aspects of environmental crimes. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Preparation of an FIU Toolkit for environmental crimes which will consolidate indicators/red flags, keywords, case studies"</sub>
