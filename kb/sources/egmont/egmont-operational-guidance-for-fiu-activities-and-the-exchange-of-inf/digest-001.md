## EGMONT GROUP OF FINANCIAL INTELLIGENCE UNITS OPERATIONAL GUIDANCE FOR FIU ACTIVITIES AND THE EXCHANGE OF INFORMATION

- **descriptive / context**: The Egmont Group Operational Guidance for FIU Activities and the Exchange of Information was approved by the Egmont Group Heads of FIUs in July 2013 and revised in May 2024. Its contents cover the introduction, general framework, guidance for international co-operation and information exchange, guidance for other FIU activities, a table of amendments, and Annexes A (sample query form), B (sample spontaneous disclosure cover letter) and C (promoting the role of FIUs in national counter-terrorism apparatus). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Approved by the Egmont Group Heads of Financial Intelligence Units July 2013, revised May 2024"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The document provides FIUs with guidance on international co-operation, information exchange and other FIU activities, as a framework the Egmont Group membership as a whole can aspire to, given the membership's diversity. FIUs are strongly encouraged to implement the guidelines to the greatest extent possible. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs are strongly encouraged to implement these guidelines to the greatest extent possible"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Point 1: the Egmont Group Charter and the Principles for Information Exchange between FIUs are obligatory documents whose elements are subject to the Support and Compliance Process. The Operational Guidance can help FIUs become more effective and efficient, but its elements are not subject to the Support and Compliance Process. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are obligatory documents and their elements are subject to the Support and Compliance Process"</sub>

## Guidance for FIUs receiving requests

- **expected / expectation**: Point 22: all incoming information requests should be answered as soon as possible, whether positive or negative. Ideally acknowledgement of receipt includes a case reference number, the requesting FIU's case number and contact details of the responsible analyst. Turnaround times may differ with the nature of the request. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All incoming requests for information should be answered as soon as possible, whether positive or negative"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Point 22 turnaround guide: responses as deemed appropriate and timely, consistent with the urgency of the request, or within one month if possible; additional time is reasonable if external databases or third parties must be queried; ideally negative responses are given as soon as possible. Feedback is as deemed appropriate and upon request (footnote 1 cites FATF Recommendation 40, Interpretive Note paragraph 8). (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or within one month if possible. Additional time is reasonable if there is need to query external databases or third parties"</sub>

## Analysis function

- **expected / expectation**: Point 44: based on information from STRs and other disclosures, FIUs should seek to identify potential cases of money laundering or terrorist financing. Suspicion is higher if additional information compounds issues such as complexity, strangeness or lack of legitimate rationale, and lower if it points to a legitimate rationale; additional information may come from the FIU's databases or external sources. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should seek to identify potential cases of money laundering or the financing of terrorism"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Point 44 continues that cases or reports can be graded as high priority (requiring full analysis and dissemination), medium priority (requiring ongoing analysis and more information) or low priority (requiring no immediate action). (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cases or reports can then be graded as high priority"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Point 45: depending on the nature and number of STRs received, FIUs may analyse individual STRs or perform bulk analysis using IT tools; the volume may create a need to identify high priority STRs to address rapidly and low priority STRs to address later. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs may perform analysis on individual STRs or bulk analysis using information technology (IT) tools"</sub>

## Types of analysis

- **descriptive / definition**: Point 46: operational analysis aims to identify specific targets (persons, assets, criminal networks etc.), possible proceeds of crime and the links between them (footnote 2 cites FATF Recommendation 29, Interpretive Note paragraph 3). FIUs are highly encouraged to use effective IT tools for maximum advantage. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Operational analysis aims to identify specific targets (persons, assets, criminal networks etc.)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Point 47: strategic analysis aims to decipher underlying patterns and trends enabling conclusions for the strategic prevention of ML and terrorist financing and input for policy formulation and operational priorities. In an FIU context it means developing strategic intelligence on ML, terrorist financing and other threats to the security of a country's financial systems. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Strategic analysis aims to decipher the underlying patterns and trends"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Point 46 lists components of operational analysis, the first two being matching against predefined lists and finding every report relating to the same entity using user-defined attributes such as first name, second name, date of birth, a public identification (for example a passport number) and address components. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identifying all reports that pertain to the same entity, on the basis of various user defined attributes"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further components of operational analysis (point 46): picking up every possible relationship across several entities by comparing attributes like surnames, phone numbers and addresses, and examining those relationships to build clusters of closely linked entities at different degrees of separation. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "capturing all possible relationships, across multiple entities by comparing attributes such as surnames, phone numbers, addresses"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Point 47 lists components of strategic analysis, the first being to examine data for patterns and similar concepts, to develop a working hypothesis covering the who, what, when, where, how and why of the activity, and to collect, evaluate and collate further information as required. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collecting, evaluating and collating further information as required"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Further components of strategic analysis (point 47): spotting connections or links between pieces of information, which can also support an inference; developing inferences, an inference being the best estimate of the truth that can be drawn from facts, opinions or other inferences; and constructing the argument, meaning the logical sequence of elements that leads to the inference. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "constructing the argument, that is the logical flow of elements leading to the inference"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The sophistication of strategic analysis may vary with an FIU's capacity and resources. Its product ranges may include: typologies and trends; patterns; synthesis; geographical/regional analysis; behavioural analysis; activity analysis; and advanced intelligence products. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The sophistication of strategic analysis may vary based on the capacity and resources of the FIU"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Typologies and trends: a systematic classification of schemes that appear constructed in a similar fashion, built on indicators showing how money was laundered, which increases understanding of a ML or terrorist financing method. When a typology occurs for an event over time it can be classed as a trend; changes in crime types or in how money is moved are examples of trends. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this is a systematic classification of a number of schemes that appear to be constructed in a similar fashion"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Patterns: a recurring characteristic or trait that helps identify a phenomenon/problem and serves as an indicator or model for predicting future behaviour. Synthesis: identifying connections that yield break-through ideas and opportunities in detecting ML or terrorist financing. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a recurring characteristic or trait that helps in the identification of a phenomenon/problem"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Geographical/regional analysis shows the area of influence or location of the phenomenon. Behavioural analysis shows the type of operations, institutions and products used by the group. Activity analysis focuses on identifying weaknesses of an economic sector or activity. Advanced intelligence products may include vulnerability assessments, risk assessments and significant event assessments. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Activity analysis - This focuses on identifying weaknesses of an economic sector or activity"</sub>

## Collection, evaluation, verification and collation of information and analysis

- **descriptive / definition**: Point 48: analysis is the conversion of collected, evaluated and collated information into intelligence through integration and interpretation of all data collected. Reliable, accurate and timely information, often from diverse sources, is useful for operational and strategic analysis. Starting from STRs and other disclosures, the analyst can search the FIU database and expand to other domestic sources and foreign partners, including closed and open sources. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Analysis can be considered to be the conversion of information that has been collected, evaluated and collated into intelligence"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Point 48 also says FIUs are encouraged to have a policy about accessing websites so that searches are not easily traceable. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs are encouraged to have a policy about accessing websites to ensure that searches are not easily traceable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Point 49: information collection should ideally be planned, orderly and systematic, reducing repetitive searching and ensuring conclusions rest on a sound foundation; it is important to analyze each source for reliability and validity. Data diagnostic evaluates data quality through credibility (source), relevance (to the analysis goal), accuracy (for reliable analysis) and the process of translating data into information. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The collection of information should ideally be planned, orderly and systematic"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Point 50: after collection, information should ideally be collated so important information is not misplaced, misdirected or lost. A good collation system helps the analyst identify relationships between individuals and/or companies, determine significant events, identify patterns of activity, uncover information gaps and clarify the significance of collected data. Sources can be coded for retrieval, IT tools can collate large data quantities, and sampling, data grouping and sorting can be used for smaller data sets, all possible with commercially available software. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A good collation system will assist the analyst to: identify relationships between individuals and/or companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Point 51: relevant data, whether collected or already in FIU databases, can be matched and linked to identify relationships; in operational analysis, where emphasis is on case formation, it is important to match and link all relevant information for as complete a view of the case as possible. Linking reports on the same entity or establishing relationships among entities may be challenging because attributes (first name, surname, address etc.) vary; once data is collected, evaluated and collated it may be reviewed to ensure all requisite information is available. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In operational analysis, where the emphasis is on case formation, it is important to match and link all relevant information"</sub>

## Power to obtain information from reporting entities

- **expected / expectation**: Point 52: FIUs should be able to obtain and use additional information from reporting entities as needed for analysis (footnote 3 cites FATF Recommendation 29, Interpretive Note section C(a) 5). The use and extent of this power, including the range of reporting entities, depends on analytical needs, with relevant elements being the features of the FIU's assigned analytical tasks, the information already available from disclosures received (in principle, the wider this initial information the less likely further information is needed) and information available from other sources. (p. 15)[^original]
