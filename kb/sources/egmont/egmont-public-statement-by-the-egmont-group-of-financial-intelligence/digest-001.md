## Public Statement by the Egmont Group of Financial Intelligence Units – Advancing the Future of Public-Private Partnerships in Combating Money Launderi

- **descriptive / context**: The Egmont Group of FIUs adopted this Public Statement at its 32nd Plenary Meeting in Baku, Republic of Azerbaijan, dated 9 July 2026. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "convening its 32 nd Plenary Meeting in Baku, Republic of Azerbaijan, adopts this Statement"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Egmont Group member FIUs state their shared belief that effective public-private partnerships (PPPs) have become essential, not merely beneficial, in countering money laundering, its associated predicate offences and terrorist financing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have become essential, not merely beneficial, in countering the evolving and complex challenges"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Using its trusted secure channels and its framework for FIU-to-FIU cooperation, the Egmont Group, where possible, agrees to act as an enabler among national PPPs by facilitating the exchange of relevant financial intelligence between FIUs, so that insights from one jurisdiction can inform action in another and cross-border cooperation is supported. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "acting as an enabler among national PPPs by facilitating the exchange of relevant financial intelligence between FIUs"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The FIUs commit to shifting away from limited, ad hoc information exchanges toward more structured, intelligence-driven collaboration. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we commit to shifting away from limited, ad hoc information exchanges toward more structured"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The Statement recognizes that legal uncertainty and practical challenges of data sharing remain among the most significant constraints on effective PPPs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legal uncertainty and the practical challenges of data sharing remain among the most significant constraints"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Addressing these constraints requires leadership, clear legal bases, appropriate safeguards, trust and, where feasible, safe-harbour mechanisms that give FIUs, reporting entities and other partners the confidence to participate meaningfully. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where feasible, safe-harbour mechanisms that give both FIUs, reporting entities and other partners"</sub>
- **permissive / method**: Where capacity permits, technology can be a powerful enabler. Tools named are privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs), federated learning approaches, secure multi-party computation and, where appropriate, artificial intelligence-driven analysis, which can support early identification of risks and emerging patterns while strictly adhering to data protection and confidentiality principles. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs), federated learning approaches, secure multi-party computation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FIUs emphasize that effective PPPs do not require advanced or costly infrastructure; well-designed, structured information-sharing arrangements can deliver meaningful results across a wide range of jurisdictions, including those with limited technological capacity. Technology should be understood as an enabler, not a barrier to initiating conversations on PPPs. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "effective PPPs do not require advanced or costly infrastructure"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The FIUs state they will uphold a clear differentiation of roles between processes that can be automated and those requiring ongoing human insight, monitoring and discretion, ensuring they operate in close collaboration. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clear differentiation of roles between processes that can be automated and those that require ongoing human insight"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: At global level, these approaches can be further promoted by sharing practical experiences and examples showing tangible gains in early threat detection across traditional banking, fintech and other relevant sectors. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "tangible gains in early threat detection across traditional banking, fintech, and other relevant sectors"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: National PPPs have demonstrated clear value in improving the quality of reports sent by obliged entities to FIUs and in increasing the number of comprehensive, better actionable operational intelligence products from FIUs, leading to increased use of intelligence by law enforcement agencies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "improving the quality of reports sent by obliged entities to FIUs"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: While strengthening national PPPs and using cross-border best practices, the focus could turn to fostering seamless international coordination. The FIUs favor risk-based approaches and thematic cross-border PPPs focused on specific, shared risks, rather than a single one-size-fits-all model. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we favor risk-based approaches and thematic cross-border PPPs focused on specific, shared risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Egmont Group will look to intensify its partnerships with the FATF, FATF-Style Regional Bodies (FSRBs) and other key global actors to establish harmonized PPP approaches for intelligence sharing and enhanced effectiveness. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "intensify our partnerships with the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), Financial Action Task Force-Style Regional Bodies (FSRBs)"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The Statement says targeted collaborative efforts are needed in high-priority areas including, but not limited to, environmental crime, fraud, virtual assets, trade-based money laundering and corruption. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "environmental crime, fraud, virtual assets, trade-based money laundering, and corruption"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Long-term PPP success depends on sustained investment from every participant. Stronger engagement among AML/CFT stakeholders, and exploring inclusion of non-traditional partners while respecting FIU operational independence and autonomy and national legal frameworks, can help create adequate organizational, financial, technological and human resources for PPPs. FIU leadership is central to driving these efforts. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Long-term success of PPPs depends on sustained investment from every participant."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The FIUs agree that, where established, PPPs should as a best practice be integrated within national AML/CFT strategies, while retaining flexibility through governance structures that ensure ongoing financial support, sustainable institutionalization and collaborative leadership. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPPs, as a best practice, should be integrated within national AML/CFT strategies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Statement concludes that, by adopting a risk-based approach, the FIUs will strengthen prevention, enhance asset recovery and enable more effective disruption of crime, focusing on the most serious crimes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we will strengthen prevention, enhance asset recovery, and enable more effective disruption of crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 1 defines a Public-Private Partnership as a standing mechanism enabling effective information sharing, collaboration, cooperation and/or coordination between government authorities with an AML/CFT/CPF role and the private sector, through which partners jointly work to detect, understand and/or disrupt money laundering, terrorist financing and associated predicate offences, by means going beyond the statutory, one-directional reporting obligation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public-Private Partnership is a standing mechanism that enables effective information sharing, collaboration, cooperation and/or coordination"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A Fraud Alert dated 23 March 2021 notes that the Egmont Group is a membership-based international organization that does not solicit public funds, and that it has been made aware of attempts to misuse its name for fraudulent or illegitimate purposes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "does not solicit public funds for its operations"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: The member FIUs say they are committed to supporting the development of PPPs into a more forward-looking model with these features: transnational reach where needed, appropriate technology, legal certainty, full respect for privacy and data protection principles, and lasting resources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "transnational in scope, where needed; enabled by appropriate technology; grounded in legal certainty"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Statement says that, even where technology such as AI-driven analysis is used, human-led intelligence must stay the decisive authority, providing oversight, contextual judgment and accountability across all analytical outcomes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "human-led intelligence must remain the decisive authority, ensuring oversight, contextual judgment, and accountability"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The Statement says FIUs should keep leading work on PPPs, governance frameworks, interoperability protocols and oversight mechanisms, so that data is consistent and comprehensive and is used only as the applicable law permits. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs should continue to lead the development of PPPs, governance frameworks, interoperability protocols"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: To show impact and keep investment flowing, the Statement says PPPs should use practical, outcome-based indicators and concrete disruption results, presented in a way senior decision-makers and policymakers can readily understand. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPPs should adopt practical, outcome-based indicators and tangible disruption outcomes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Egmont Group states that it will work closely with international and regional bodies, non-profit organisations, private sector partners and stakeholder coalitions to carry out these objectives, and calls on all involved parties to help build PPPs that protect personal data, act decisively through proactive intervention, and are broad enough to meet threats of global scale. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "collaborate closely with international and regional bodies, non-profit organizations, private sector partners and stakeholder coalitions"</sub>
