## I. Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 1: the Basel Committee issues these guidelines (January 2014, revised July 2020) to describe how banks should include money laundering (ML) and financing of terrorism (FT) risks within their overall risk management, given the risk of banks being used, intentionally or unintentionally, for criminal activities. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to describe how banks should include money laundering (ML) and financing of terrorism (FT) risks within their overall risk management"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 2: the Committee has a long-standing commitment to sound AML/CFT policies and procedures, which are critical to the safety and soundness of banks and the integrity of the international financial system; it published an initial statement in 1988 and, in September 2012, revised Core principles for effective banking supervision with a dedicated principle (BCP 29) on abuse of financial services. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a dedicated principle (BCP 29) deals with the abuse of financial services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 3: the Committee supports adoption of the FATF standards (revised February 2012, with Committee input) and considered the FATF Financial Inclusion Guidance (March 2013); the guidelines aim to support national implementation of the FATF standards by exploring complementary areas, and in no way should be interpreted as modifying the FATF standards, either by strengthening or weakening them. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in no way should they be interpreted as modifying the FATF standards, either by strengthening or weakening them"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 4: the Committee included some cross-references to FATF standards to assist banks in complying with national requirements based on those standards, but not as a matter of routine because it does not intend to duplicate the FATF standards. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cross-references are not included as a matter of routine"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 2 of the paper records that the FATF defines money laundering as processing criminal proceeds so as to disguise where they illegally came from. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF defines money laundering as the processing of criminal proceeds in order to disguise their illegal origin."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5 adds that sound ML/FT risk management also preserves the integrity of the international financial system and the work of governments in dealing with corruption and combating the financing of terrorism. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it preserves the integrity of the international financial system as well as the work of governments"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 3: Annex 3 excerpts the most relevant FATF Recommendations that banks and supervisors should comply with in implementing AML/CFT measures; it is not exhaustive and other FATF Recommendations, including Interpretive Notes, may be relevant. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is not exhaustive and other FATF Recommendations, including the Interpretive Notes, may be relevant."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paragraph 6: inadequate or absent sound ML/FT risk management exposes banks to serious reputational, operational, compliance and concentration risks, highlighted by robust enforcement actions by regulators and the direct and indirect costs to banks; the guidelines say these costs and damage could probably have been avoided had banks maintained effective risk-based AML/CFT policies and procedures. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exposes banks to serious risks, especially reputational, operational, compliance and concentration risks"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Paragraph 7: these risks are interrelated and, besides fines and sanctions, any one could cause significant financial costs to banks such as termination of wholesale funding and facilities, claims against the bank, investigation costs, asset seizures and freezes and loan losses, plus diversion of management time and operational resources. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "termination of wholesale funding and facilities, claims against the bank, investigation costs, asset seizures and freezes, and loan losses"</sub>

(p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this paper should be read in conjunction with a number of related Committee papers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 9: the document merges and supersedes Customer due diligence for banks (October 2001) and Consolidated KYC risk management (October 2004), with increased focus on risks of banks using third parties to introduce business (Annex 1) and providing correspondent banking services (Annex 2). (p. 6)[^original]

## II. Essential elements of sound ML/FT risk management

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 14: under the 2012 Core principles all banks should be required to have adequate policies and processes, including strict customer due diligence (CDD) rules, to promote high ethical and professional standards and prevent the bank being used, intentionally or unintentionally, for criminal activities; this is part of banks' general obligation to have sound risk management programmes covering ML and FT risks. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all banks should be required to"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 14 (continued): 'adequate policies and processes' requires implementing other measures besides effective CDD rules, and these measures should be proportional and risk-based, informed by the bank's own ML/FT risk assessment. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These measures should also be proportional and risk-based, informed by banks’ own risk assessment of ML/FT risks."</sub>

## 1(a) Assessment and understanding of risks

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 15: sound risk management requires identifying and analysing ML/FT risks within the bank and designing and effectively implementing policies and procedures commensurate with identified risks; in a comprehensive risk assessment a bank should consider all relevant inherent and residual risk factors at country, sectoral, bank and business relationship level, among others, to determine its risk profile and the appropriate level of mitigation. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a bank should consider all the relevant inherent and residual risk factors at the country,17 sectoral, bank"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 15 (continued): policies and procedures for CDD, customer acceptance, customer identification and monitoring of the business relationship and operations will have to take into account the risk assessment and resulting risk profile, and a bank should have appropriate mechanisms to document and provide risk assessment information to competent authorities such as supervisors; footnote 17 says AML/CFT risk assessments at supranational level should be taken into account where appropriate. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A bank should have appropriate mechanisms to document and provide risk assessment information to competent authorities such as supervisors."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 16: a bank should develop a thorough understanding of inherent ML/FT risks in its customer base, products, delivery channels and services (including products under development or to be launched) and the jurisdictions where it or its customers do business, based on specific operational and transaction data and internal information plus external sources such as national risk assessments and country reports from international organisations. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A bank should develop a thorough understanding of the inherent ML/FT risks present in its customer base, products, delivery channels"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 16 (continued): customer acceptance, due diligence and ongoing monitoring policies and procedures should be designed and implemented to adequately control identified inherent risks, residual risk should be managed in line with the risk profile, and the assessment and understanding should be able to be demonstrated to, and be acceptable to, the bank's supervisor. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Any resulting residual risk should be managed in line with the bank’s risk profile established through its risk assessment."</sub>

## II. Essential elements of sound ML/FT risk management

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 14 states that other Committee guidelines (referred to in paragraph 8) are applicable or supplementary where no specific AML/CFT guidance exists. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "other guidelines (see paragraph 8 above) are applicable or supplementary where no specific AML/CFT guidance exists"</sub>

## 1(b) Proper governance arrangements

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 17: effective ML/FT risk management requires proper governance arrangements; the requirement for the board of directors to approve and oversee policies for risk, risk management and compliance is fully relevant to ML/FT risk, and the board should have a clear understanding of ML/FT risks, with risk assessment information communicated to it in a timely, complete, understandable and accurate manner so it can make informed decisions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information about ML/FT risk assessment should be communicated to the board in a timely, complete, understandable and accurate manner"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 18: the board should allocate explicit responsibility, taking the governance structure into account, for ensuring the bank's policies and procedures are managed effectively, and the board and senior management should appoint an appropriately qualified chief AML/CFT officer with overall responsibility for the AML/CFT function and the stature and authority for issues he or she raises to receive attention from the board, senior management and business lines. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should appoint an appropriately qualified chief AML/CFT officer to have overall responsibility for the AML/CFT function"</sub>

## 1(c) The three lines of defence

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 19: as a general rule in AML/CFT, business units (front office, customer-facing activity) are the first line of defence in charge of identifying, assessing and controlling their risks, should know and carry out the policies and procedures and be allotted sufficient resources; the second line includes the chief AML/CFT officer, the compliance function and also human resources or technology; the third line is internal audit. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the business units (eg front office, customer- facing activity) are the first line of defence"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 20: as part of the first line of defence, policies and procedures should be clearly specified in writing and communicated to all personnel, with a clear description of employees' obligations and instructions and guidance on keeping the bank's activity compliant, and there should be internal procedures for detecting and reporting suspicious transactions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There should be internal procedures for detecting and reporting suspicious transactions."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 21: a bank should have adequate policies and processes for screening prospective and existing staff to ensure high ethical and professional standards, and all banks should implement ongoing employee training so staff are adequately trained to implement the bank's AML/CFT policies and procedures. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A bank should have adequate policies and processes for screening prospective and existing staff"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 21 (continued): timing and content of training should be adapted to staff sectors, functions, job responsibilities, length of service and the bank's risk profile, and tailored to an employee's specific responsibility; new employees should be required to attend training as soon as possible after hiring; refresher training should keep staff reminded of obligations and knowledge up to date, with scope and frequency tailored to risk factors from their responsibilities and the level and nature of risk in the bank. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "New employees should be required to attend training as soon as possible after being hired"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 22: as part of the second line of defence the chief AML/CFT officer should be responsible for ongoing monitoring of fulfilment of all AML/CFT duties, implying sample testing of compliance and review of exception reports to alert senior management or the board if management is believed to be failing to address AML/CFT procedures responsibly, and should be the contact point on AML/CFT issues for internal and external authorities including supervisors and FIUs. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This implies sample testing of compliance and review of exception reports"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 23: the bank's business interests should in no way oppose effective discharge of the chief AML/CFT officer's responsibilities and potential conflicts of interest should be avoided; for example the officer should not have business line responsibilities and should not be entrusted with data protection or internal audit functions; where conflicts arise, procedures should ensure AML/CFT concerns are objectively considered at the highest level. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "not have business line responsibilities and should not be entrusted with responsibilities in the context of data protection"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 24: the chief AML/CFT officer may also perform the function of the chief risk officer or chief compliance officer or equivalent and should have a direct reporting line to senior management or the board; where duties are separated, the relationship and roles of these chief officers must be clearly defined and understood. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "He/she should have a direct reporting line to senior management or the board."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 25: the chief AML/CFT officer should also be responsible for reporting suspicious transactions and be provided sufficient resources to play a central and proactive role in the AML/CFT regime; he or she must be fully conversant with the bank's AML/CFT regime, its statutory and regulatory requirements and the ML/FT risks from the business. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The chief AML/CFT officer should also have the responsibility for reporting suspicious transactions."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 26: internal audit, the third line of defence, independently evaluates risk management and controls through periodic evaluations of the effectiveness of compliance with AML/CFT policies, reporting to the audit committee or similar body; a bank should establish policies for auditing (i) adequacy of AML/CFT policies and procedures in addressing identified risks, (ii) effectiveness of staff in implementing them, (iii) effectiveness of compliance oversight and quality control including parameters of criteria for automatic alerts, and (iv) effectiveness of training of relevant personnel. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the effectiveness of compliance oversight and quality control including parameters of criteria for automatic alerts"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 26 (continued): senior management should ensure audit functions are allocated knowledgeable staff with appropriate expertise; management should ensure audit scope and methodology fit the risk profile and that frequency is risk-based; internal auditors should periodically conduct AML/CFT audits on a bank-wide basis and be proactive in following up findings and recommendations; as a general rule, audit processes should be consistent with internal audit's broader mandate, subject to any prescribed AML/CFT audit requirements. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Periodically, internal auditors should conduct AML/CFT audits on a bank-wide basis."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 27: where a bank uses external auditors to evaluate the effectiveness of AML/CFT policies and procedures, it should ensure the audit scope is adequate to address its risks and the auditors have requisite expertise and experience, and should exercise appropriate oversight of such engagements; the guidelines note external auditors in many countries evaluate internal controls and confirm AML/CFT compliance in financial audits. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should ensure that the scope of the audit is adequate to address the bank’s risks"</sub>

## 1(d) Adequate transaction monitoring system

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 28: a bank should have a monitoring system adequate to its size, activities, complexity and risks; for most banks, especially internationally active ones, effective monitoring is likely to necessitate automation; a bank that considers an IT monitoring system unnecessary should document its decision and be able to demonstrate to its supervisor or external auditors an effective alternative. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should document its decision and be able to demonstrate to its supervisor or external auditors"</sub>

## (d) Adequate transaction monitoring system

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 28: when a bank uses an IT monitoring system, the system should cover all accounts of the bank's customers and all transactions made for the benefit of, or on the order of, those customers. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it should cover all accounts of the bank’s customers and transactions for the benefit of, or by order of"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 28: an IT monitoring system must allow the bank to carry out trend analysis of transaction activity and to identify unusual business relationships and transactions in order to prevent ML or FT. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It must enable the bank to undergo trend analysis of transaction activity"</sub>

## 1(d) Adequate transaction monitoring system

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 29: the monitoring system should provide accurate information to senior management on key aspects including changes in customers' transactional profile; in compiling the profile the bank should incorporate updated, comprehensive and accurate CDD information; the IT system should allow the bank, and where appropriate the group, centralised knowledge of information (by customer, product, across group entities, transactions in a timeframe), and without requiring a unique customer file banks should be able to risk-rate customers and manage alerts with all relevant information. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Without being requested to have a unique customer file, banks should be able to risk-rate customers and manage alerts"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 29 (continued): an IT monitoring system must use adequate parameters based on national and international experience on the methods and prevention of ML or FT; a bank may use the standard parameters provided by the system's developer, but the parameters used must reflect and take into account the bank's own risk situation. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the parameters used must reflect and take into account the bank’s own risk situation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 30: the IT monitoring system should let a bank determine its own criteria for additional monitoring, filing a suspicious transaction report (STR) or other risk-minimising steps; the chief AML/CFT officer should have access to and benefit from the system as far as relevant to the function, even if operated by other business lines; parameters should allow alerts of unusual transactions for further assessment by the chief AML/CFT officer; risk criteria should be adequate with regard to the bank's risk assessment. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Parameters of the IT system should allow for generation of alerts of unusual transactions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 31: internal audit should also evaluate the IT system to ensure it is appropriate and used effectively by the first and second lines of defence. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Internal audit should also evaluate the IT system to ensure that it is appropriate"</sub>

## 2. Customer acceptance policy

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 32: a bank should develop and implement clear customer acceptance policies and procedures to identify the types of customer likely to pose a higher ML/FT risk, in line with the bank's risk assessment. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A bank should develop and implement clear customer acceptance policies and procedures to identify the types of customer"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 33: it is important that the customer acceptance policy is not so restrictive that it leads to the general public, especially financially or socially disadvantaged people, being denied access to banking services. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is important that the customer acceptance policy is not so restrictive that it results in a denial of access"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 33: the FATF Financial Inclusion Guidance gives useful guidelines on designing AML/CFT procedures that are not overly restrictive for the financially or socially disadvantaged. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Financial Inclusion Guidance21 provides useful guidelines on designing AML/CFT procedures that are not overly restrictive"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 34: where risks are higher, banks should take enhanced measures to mitigate and manage those risks. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where the risks are higher, banks should take enhanced measures to mitigate and manage those risks."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Paragraph 34: enhanced due diligence may be essential for an individual planning to keep a large account balance and make regular cross-border wire transfers, or for an individual who is a politically exposed person (PEP). (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Enhanced due diligence may be essential for an individual planning to maintain a large account balance"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 34 states that enhanced due diligence is required for foreign PEPs. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In particular, such enhanced due diligence is required for foreign PEPs."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 33: policies and procedures should require basic due diligence for all customers and commensurate due diligence as the level of customer risk varies; for proven lower risk situations simplified measures may be permitted if allowed by law (for example basic account-opening procedures for an individual expecting a small balance and routine retail transactions). (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For proven lower risk situations, simplified measures may be permitted, if this is allowed by law."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 34 (continued): decisions to enter into or continue business relationships with higher-risk customers should require enhanced due diligence measures such as approval by senior management, and the customer acceptance policy should also define circumstances in which the bank would not accept a new business relationship or would terminate an existing one. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should also define circumstances under which the bank would not accept a new business relationship"</sub>

## 3. Customer and beneficial owner identification, verification and risk profiling

- **descriptive / definition**: Paragraph 35: for these guidelines, in accordance with FATF Recommendation 10 a customer is any person who enters into a business relationship or carries out an occasional financial transaction with the bank. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a customer refers, in accordance with the FATF Recommendation 10, to any person22 who enters into a business relationship"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 35 (continued): customer due diligence should be applied not only to customers but also to persons acting on their behalf and beneficial owners, and in accordance with the FATF standards banks should identify customers and verify their identity. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The customer due diligence should be applied not only to customers but also to persons acting on their behalf"</sub>
