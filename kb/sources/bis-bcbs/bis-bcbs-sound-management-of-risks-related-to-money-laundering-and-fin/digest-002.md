## 4. Ongoing monitoring

- **descriptive / requirement**: Paragraph 45: ongoing monitoring is an essential aspect of effective and sound ML/FT risk management; a bank can only manage its risks if it understands normal and reasonable customer banking activity, without which it is likely to fail in its obligations to identify and report suspicious transactions. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ongoing monitoring is an essential aspect of effective and sound ML/FT risk management."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 45 (continued): ongoing monitoring should be conducted for all business relationships and transactions with extent based on risk identified in the bank risk assessment and CDD efforts; enhanced monitoring should be adopted for higher-risk customers or transactions; a bank should also carry out cross-sectional product/service monitoring to identify and mitigate emerging risk patterns. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Enhanced monitoring should be adopted for higher-risk customers or transactions."</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "All banks should have systems in place to detect unusual or suspicious transactions or patterns of activity."</sub>

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 72: regardless of location, each office should establish and maintain effective monitoring policies and procedures appropriate to the risks in the jurisdiction and bank, complemented by robust information-sharing with the head office and, if appropriate, other branches and subsidiaries about accounts and activity that may represent heightened risk. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each office should establish and maintain effective monitoring policies and procedures that are appropriate to the risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 73: to manage ML and FT risks from such accounts a bank should integrate information based on the customer and also on its knowledge of beneficial owners and the funds involved, and should monitor significant customer relationships, balances and activity on a consolidated basis whether accounts are on-balance sheet, off-balance sheet, assets under management or fiduciary, and wherever held. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A bank should monitor significant customer relationships, balances and activity on a consolidated basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 73 (continued): the FATF standards now set out more details on banks' head office oversight of group compliance, audit and/or AML/CFT functions (Recommendation 18), and although conceived primarily for banks the guidelines might be of interest for conglomerates (including banks). (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they might be of interest for conglomerates (including banks)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 74: where large banks centralise processing systems and databases, a bank should adequately document and integrate local and centralised transaction/account monitoring functions so it can monitor for patterns of potentially suspicious activity across the group and not just at local or centralised level. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adequately document and integrate the local and centralised transaction/account monitoring functions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 75: a bank performing business nationally and abroad should appoint a chief AML/CFT officer for the whole group (group AML/CFT officer), responsible as part of global risk management for creating, coordinating and group-wide assessment of implementing a single AML/CFT strategy, including mandatory policies and procedures and authorisation to give orders for all branches, subsidiaries and subordinated entities nationally and abroad. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should appoint a chief AML/CFT officer for the whole group (group AML/CFT officer)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 76: the group AML/CFT officer's function includes ongoing monitoring of fulfilment of all AML/CFT requirements group-wide, nationally and abroad; he or she should satisfy him/herself, including through regular on-site visits, that there is group-wide compliance, and if needed should be empowered to give orders or take necessary measures for the whole group. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including through on-site visits on a regular basis"</sub>

## 4. Group-wide information-sharing

- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 77: banks should oversee coordination of information-sharing; subsidiaries and branches should be required to proactively provide the head office with information on higher-risk customers and activities relevant to global AML/CFT standards and respond to head office or parent bank requests for account information in a timely manner; group-wide standards should describe the process in all locations for identifying, monitoring and investigating potential unusual circumstances and reporting suspicious activity. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Subsidiaries and branches should be required to proactively provide the head office with information concerning higher-risk customers"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 78: group-wide policies and procedures should take into account local data protection and privacy laws and obligations, the different types of information that may be shared within a group, and requirements for storage, retrieval, sharing/distribution and disposal of this information. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should take into account issues and obligations related to local data protection and privacy laws and regulations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 79: the group's overall ML/FT risk management function should evaluate potential risks posed by activity reported by branches and subsidiaries and, where appropriate, assess group-wide risks presented by a customer or category of customers; it should have policies and procedures to ascertain whether other branches or subsidiaries hold accounts for the same customer (including related or affiliated parties). (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It should have policies and procedures to ascertain if other branches or subsidiaries hold accounts for the same customer"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Paragraph 79 (continued): the bank should also have policies and procedures governing global account relationships deemed higher-risk or associated with potentially suspicious activity, including escalation procedures and guidance on restricting account activities, including closing accounts as appropriate. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including escalation procedures and guidance on restricting account activities, including the closing of accounts as appropriate"</sub>
