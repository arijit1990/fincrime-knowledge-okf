## C. Focus on specific types of customer

- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 31 (retirement benefit programmes): where an occupational pension programme, employee benefit trust or share option plan applies for an account, the trustee and any other person with control over the relationship (eg administrator, programme manager, account signatories) can be considered beneficial owners, and the bank should take steps to identify and verify them. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can be considered as beneficial owners and the bank should take steps to identify them and verify their identities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 32 (mutuals/friendly societies, cooperatives, provident societies): persons exercising control or significant influence over the organisation's assets should be considered beneficial owners and therefore identified and verified, often including board members, executives and account signatories. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those persons exercising control or significant influence over the organisation’s assets should be considered the beneficial owners"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Paragraph 33 (professional intermediaries): when an intermediary opens an account for a single customer, that customer must be identified; for pooled accounts where funds are not co-mingled and sub-accounts are attributable to each beneficial owner, all beneficial owners should be identified; where funds are co-mingled the bank should look through to the beneficial owners, though circumstances permitted by law and set out in supervisory guidance (eg intermediary subject to equivalent due diligence standards, such as broker-dealers) may mean the bank need not look beyond the intermediary. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When a professional intermediary opens a customer account on behalf of a single customer that customer must be identified"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 34: where such circumstances apply and an account is opened for an open or closed-end investment company, unit trust or limited partnership subject to equivalent customer due diligence requirements, the bank should treat the vehicle as its customer and identify: the fund itself; its directors or controlling board (company); its trustee (unit trust); its managing (general) partner (limited partnership); account signatories; and any other person with control over the relationship (eg fund administrator or manager). (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the bank should treat this investment vehicle as its customer and take steps to identify"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 35: for other investment vehicles the same steps as paragraph 34 should be taken where appropriate, and where no equivalent due diligence standards apply, all reasonable steps should be taken to verify the identity of the beneficial owners of the funds and those who control them. ## A. General provisions

- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 2: supervisors should ensure efficient and effective cooperation between the prudential supervisory function and the AML/CFT supervisory function, regardless of the jurisdictional institutional arrangement for those functions. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors should ensure efficient and effective cooperation between the prudential supervisory function and AML/CFT supervisory function"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Annex 5 paragraph 2: this cooperation must not negatively impact the independence of either supervisory function in fulfilling its mandate, and must not result in different supervisory functions unduly duplicating efforts. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This cooperation must not negatively impact the independence of either supervisory function in fulfilling its mandate"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex 5 paragraph 3 defines prudential supervision as aiming to ensure banks' compliance with prudential requirements, safety and soundness and system stability, including authorisation, assessment of qualifying holdings, fit and proper tests of major shareholders, board and senior management, and ongoing assessment of governance, organisational structure, risk management and internal controls; the performing function is the "prudential supervisor". (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the function that carries out prudential supervision over banks is referred to as the “prudential supervisor”"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Annex 5 paragraph 4 defines AML/CFT supervision in the banking sector as aiming to ensure compliance with ML/FT countering requirements, assess banks' ML/FT risks, processes and internal controls and take supervisory action on that basis; the performing function is the "AML/CFT supervisor". (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the function that carries out the AML/CFT supervision over banks is referred to as the “AML/CFT supervisor”"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Because AML/CFT supervisors have specialised expertise, assessment of banks' ML/FT risk should be carried out by them in their ongoing supervision and considered in prudential supervision; AML/CFT supervisors should conversely consider prudential supervisors' insights, in particular on overall risk management, internal controls and governance. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be carried out by AML/CFT supervisors in the context of their ongoing AML/CFT supervision"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 5: supervisors are expected to adopt practices from applicable FATF Recommendations and the Committee's principles and guidelines on supervisors' role and powers (including sanctions) and information exchange, to appropriately consider ML/FT risks. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors are expected to adopt practices, from applicable Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5(1): FATF Recommendation 27 (Powers of supervisors), Recommendation 35 (Sanctions) and Recommendation 40 and its Interpretative Note (Other forms of international cooperation) describe supervisors' roles, powers to enforce AML/CFT requirements including effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions, and principles of information exchange and international cooperation including with prudential supervisors; Recommendation 2 (National cooperation and coordination) is also pertinent for AML/CFT supervisors. (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF Recommendation 2 (National cooperation and coordination) is pertinent"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 5(2): particularly relevant Committee publications are Core principles for effective banking supervision (September 2012), Corporate governance principles for banks (July 2015) and Principles for effective supervisory colleges (June 2014). (p. 49)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Principles for effective supervisory colleges, June 2014"</sub>
- **expected / finding or result**: Annex 5 paragraph 6: deficiencies in banks' AML/CFT systems could have prudential consequences, such as significant regulatory actions or criminal penalties causing reputational damage (depositor outflows, loss of counterparties or market access including correspondent banking relationships), and in some jurisdictions licence revocation or termination of deposit insurance; supervisors should be aware such deficiencies could be caused by or indicate wider failures in governance, organisational structure, risk management and compliance. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AML/CFT deficiencies could result in significant regulatory actions or criminal penalties"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 7: information exchange should be created and maintained, to the extent permitted by applicable laws, so prudential supervisors have timely access to relevant AML/CFT supervisory information and prudential supervisors share relevant information with AML/CFT supervisors; exchange is limited to information the requesting supervisor needs but does not have and the requested supervisor has available. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "limited to information that the requesting supervisor needs but does not have"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 8: cooperation and information exchange should occur in activities including (1) authorisation procedures (licence applications, qualifying holding assessments, fit and proper tests at authorisation and continuing); (2) ongoing supervision (governance, risk management and internal controls, business model and profitability drivers, operational risks, AML compliance); and (3) enforcement actions and/or revocation of a banking licence (enforcement actions defined in paragraph 22, per footnote 73). (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "authorisation-related procedures of a bank, which include: licence applications for banks, qualifying holding assessments"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 9: when considering ML/FT risks for the paragraph 8 activities, prudential supervisors should carefully consider potential ML/FT risks identified by AML/CFT supervisors stemming from international banking group structure and cross-border activities, including the impact of a change of shareholders; a bank generally includes a banking group under consolidated supervision. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prudential supervisors should also carefully consider the potential ML/FT risks identified by the AML/CFT supervisors"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Annex 5 paragraph 10: the annex provides principles with examples that are not prescriptive and are illustrative; boxes highlight practices, including features specific to certain jurisdictions that may not be applicable to all. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The examples are not prescriptive and should be regarded as illustrative elements"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 11: all principles and examples should be considered according to a risk-based approach (guideline paragraphs 86 and 87); exchanges of information or other cooperation should be proportionate to the risks and supervisory needs in each circumstance. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Exchanges of information or other forms of cooperation should be proportionate to the risks and supervisory needs"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 12: under the risk-based approach, using the size of a bank's operations or profits/losses as the main or central ML/FT risk indicator would not be appropriate for concluding a bank is low-risk, because ML/FT risk could arise from relatively small parts or activities or from a bank of smaller asset size. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using the size of operations or profits/losses of a bank as the main or central ML/FT risk indicator"</sub>

## B. Cooperation and exchange of information in the authorisation process

- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 13: the prudential supervisor is generally responsible for authorisation processes (licence, qualifying holding assessments, fit and proper tests), where applicable with other regulators; it should involve the AML/CFT supervisor in its assessment and share appropriate application information with, and receive relevant information from, the AML/CFT supervisor. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should, as part of its assessment, involve the AML/CFT supervisor among other relevant parties"</sub>

## B.1 Licence of a bank

- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 14(1): the prudential supervisor assessing a licence application should obtain information from the AML/CFT supervisor and should consider exposure to ML/FT risks in assessing soundness of the business model, for example indications that the business model (target customers, sectors, products and services, distribution channels) presents higher ML/FT risk especially within a peer group. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The prudential supervisor should consider the exposure to ML/FT risks in its assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Paragraph 14(1)(b): relevant jurisdiction information where the bank is established or has significant business relationships includes nature and level of predicate offences to money laundering, effectiveness of the legal and judicial system, scale and level of risk of terrorist activities and groups, ML/FT typologies identified by the FIU and other public authorities and private entities, and overall effectiveness of the jurisdiction's AML/CFT regime. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the nature and level of predicate offences to money laundering, the effectiveness of a jurisdiction’s legal and judicial system"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Annex 5 paragraph 14(2): the prudential supervisor should consider the bank's envisaged risk mitigation system, internal control system and adequacy of governance and organisational structures to manage ML/FT risks, for example assessing adequacy of AML/CFT policies and procedures; organisation of the AML/CFT function (financial resources, staffing, training, IT supporting the AML/CFT unit); and governance and management oversight of ML/FT risk exposures and the risk management system. (p. 52)[^original]

(p. 52)[^original]
