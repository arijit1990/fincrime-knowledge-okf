## Executive summary

- **descriptive / context**: The Governors of the BIS Economic Consultative Committee (ECC) mandated the CPMI to produce a report on correspondent banking, and the CPMI Working Group on Correspondent Banking prepared this technical report describing current trends and analysing measures that might alleviate concerns and cost issues. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have mandated the CPMI to produce a report on this issue"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Some banks providing correspondent services are reducing the number of relationships they maintain and establishing few new ones; the impact is uneven across jurisdictions and banks, so some respondent banks are likely to maintain relationships while others might risk being cut off from international payment networks, implying a threat that cross-border payment networks might fragment and the range of options narrow. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some banks providing these services are reducing the number of relationships they maintain and are establishing few new ones"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: An analysis using SWIFT data shows there seems to be a trend towards concentration in correspondent banking activities. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there seems to be a trend towards concentration in correspondent banking activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Banks cite rising costs and uncertainty about how far customer due diligence should go to ensure compliance (to what extent banks need to know their customers' customers, "KYCC") among the main reasons for cutting back correspondent relationships. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Rising costs and uncertainty about how far customer due diligence should go in order to ensure regulatory compliance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report takes the regulatory framework as given, including AML/CFT requirements and implementing legislation in different jurisdictions, and acknowledges that these requirements, as agreed by competent authorities, along with strict implementation, are necessary to prevent and detect criminal activities and ensure a healthy financial system. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The regulatory framework is taken as given in this report"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The working group limited its analysis to measures that could help improve efficiency of procedures, reduce compliance costs and address perceived uncertainty, without altering applicable rules and the basic channels for correspondent banking services between correspondent and respondent banks; it translated some measures into five recommendations. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without altering the applicable rules and the basic channels for correspondent banking services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An earlier version of the report was issued for consultation in October 2015, and this final version was revised in light of public comments received and further stakeholder interactions. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An earlier version of this report was issued for consultation in October 2015"</sub>

## (i) Recommendation on the use of "know your customer" (KYC) utilities

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many consultation comments pointed out that there is currently no standardisation in the type and format of information in different KYC utilities, and that such inconsistencies in gathering information limit the value of KYC utilities. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is currently no standardisation in the type and format of information in different KYC utilities"</sub>

## Executive summary

- **descriptive / context**: The executive summary says correspondent banking relationships give banks reach to financial services in other jurisdictions and let them offer their own customers cross-border payment services, which supports international trade and financial inclusion. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Through correspondent banking relationships, banks can access financial services in different jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The executive summary states that, to avoid penalties and the reputational damage that goes with them, correspondent banks have grown more sensitive to the risks associated with correspondent banking. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To avoid penalties and related reputational damage, correspondent banks have developed an increased sensitivity"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As a result of that sensitivity, correspondent banks have reduced services to respondent banks that: (i) do not bring in enough volume to outweigh compliance costs; (ii) are in jurisdictions seen as too risky; (iii) offer payment services to customers about whom the information needed for a proper risk assessment is unavailable; or (iv) provide products or services, or serve customers, that carry higher AML/CFT risk and are harder to manage. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not generate sufficient volumes to overcome compliance costs"</sub>

## (i) Recommendation on the use of "know your customer" (KYC) utilities

- **recommended / recommendation**: The CPMI decided to enhance the previous recommendation by inviting relevant standard setters such as ISO to consider defining a standardised minimum set of information and data (including format) that all utilities should collect and that all banks have to be ready to provide to other banks requiring it. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inviting relevant standard setters such as the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) to consider defining a standardised minimum set"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many comments argued that for KYC utilities to be more effective, in addition to standardising data and formats, banks would need some assurances from relevant authorities (regulatory, supervisory or law-enforcement) on the appropriateness of and reliance upon any such utility for AML/CFT compliance. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks have some assurances from relevant authorities"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The authorities with responsibility for AML/CFT (the FATF and the Basel Committee AML/CFT Expert Group, AMLEG) are invited to consider developing a set of issues that financial institutions should consider when using KYC utilities, to support their appropriate use. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are invited to consider developing a set of issues that financial institutions should consider when using KYC utilities"</sub>

## (ii) Recommendation on the use of the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) in correspondent banking

- **recommended / recommendation**: All authorities and relevant stakeholders are invited to consider promoting BIC-to-LEI mapping facilities, which allow easy mapping of routing information in the payment message to the relevant LEI. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "invited to consider promoting BIC-to-LEI mapping facilities"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Relevant authorities (e.g. the LEI Regulatory Oversight Committee (LEI ROC) and AMLEG) are encouraged to elaborate further on the extent to which banks can rely on the LEI as a means of accessing reliable information to support customer due diligence in correspondent banking. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are encouraged to elaborate further as to what extent banks can rely on the LEI"</sub>

## (iii) Recommendation on information-sharing initiatives

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many comments sought clarification on data privacy concerns in correspondent banking and highlighted potential conflicts between sharing relevant information across jurisdictions and existing national data privacy regulations. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "potential conflicts between the sharing of relevant information across jurisdictions and existing national data privacy regulations"</sub>

## (iv) Recommendation on payment messages

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The majority of comments validated the argument that both the serial MT 103 method and the cover MT 202 COV method can be used in full compliance with AML/CFT and relevant regulatory requirements when all data fields are accurately populated in a payment message. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both methods (ie the serial MT 103 method and the cover MT 202 COV method) can be used in full"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: The CPMI decided to modify the initial recommendation and leave to individual banks the decision as to which payment message method should be used. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leave the decision to individual banks as to which method should be used"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Given the importance of transparency and accuracy of information in payment messages, the CPMI invites the Wolfsberg Group and the Payments Market Practice Group (PMPG) to review their principles governing use-cases for payment messages, what information should be included and which data fields should be used; AMLEG is invited to consider developing further guidance on supervisors' role in ensuring banks meet FATF Recommendations and guidance on the quality of payment message content. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "invite the relevant stakeholders (ie the Wolfsberg Group and the Payments Market Practice Group (PMPG)) to review their principles"</sub>

## (iii) Recommendation on information-sharing initiatives

- **recommended / recommendation**: In the changes made after consultation, the FATF and AMLEG are invited to look further at ways of overcoming obstacles to information-sharing, aiming to identify potential best practices within an enterprise-wide context, between financial institutions outside the same group, and between the public and private sectors. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are invited to further explore ways to tackle obstacles to information-sharing"</sub>

## (v) Recommendation on the use of the LEI as additional information in payment messages

- **expected / recommendation**: Relevant stakeholders (e.g. the PMPG) should work to define a common market practice for including the LEI in the current relevant payment messages without changing the current message structure. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should work to define a common market practice for how to include the LEI in the current relevant payment messages"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: As part of a potential future migration to ISO 20022-based message formats, relevant stakeholders (ISO and SWIFT) are encouraged to consider developing dedicated codes or data items for including the LEI in these payment messages. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are encouraged to consider developing dedicated codes or data items for the inclusion of the LEI"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The CPMI believes its recommendations might alleviate some costs and concerns of correspondent banking but stresses that in isolation they will not resolve all issues; withdrawal from correspondent banking is complex, AML/CFT compliance costs are only one element (business considerations and economies of scope and scale also matter), enhanced technical tools address only part of compliance costs and not uncertainty about how far CDD should go, and the measures will not immediately help banks without access to correspondent services gain access. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in isolation, these measures will not resolve all the issues"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: As a next step before any potential implementation, the measures should be further analysed by all relevant authorities and stakeholders to gauge the potential impact of each measure and avoid unintended consequences; the CPMI expects relevant stakeholders to initiate necessary reviews as soon as possible. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be further analysed by all relevant authorities and stakeholders in order to gauge the potential impact of each measure"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The CPMI will (i) encourage, mainly through its members' participation in the FSB Correspondent Banking Coordination Group, review of the recommendations by relevant stakeholders and (ii) from the technical perspective of payment systems, facilitate implementation by contributing to stakeholders' work or workstreams. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from the technical perspective of payment systems, facilitate the implementation by contributing to the work or workstreams"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Before LEI use becomes widespread or compulsory for banks and corporate customers, relevant stakeholders could begin to study how the LEI might be used, on an optional basis and in a more structured manner, within the current MT 103 and MT 202 COV messages. (p. 7)[^original]

## Recommendations:

- **permissive / recommendation**: Recommendation on KYC utilities: use of KYC utilities by respondent and correspondent banks, provided they store at least a minimum set of up-to-date and accurate information, could be supported in general as an effective means of reducing the CDD compliance burden for banks active in correspondent banking. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could be supported in general as an effective means of reducing the burden of compliance with customer due diligence requirements"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Standard setters such as ISO may wish to consider defining a standardised minimum set of information and data (including format) that all KYC utilities should collect and that all banks must be ready to provide to other banks that require it. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may wish to consider defining a standardised minimum set of information and data (including the format)"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: In addition to standardisation, the FATF and AMLEG are invited to consider developing a set of issues that financial institutions should consider when using KYC utilities, to support their appropriate use. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to support an appropriate use of these utilities"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Recommendation on the LEI in correspondent banking: relevant stakeholders may consider specifically promoting use of the LEI for all banks involved in correspondent banking as an identification that should be provided in KYC utilities and information-sharing arrangements; in a cross-border context, this should ideally be coordinated and applied simultaneously in a large number of jurisdictions. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may consider specifically promoting the use of the LEI for all banks involved in correspondent banking"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: All authorities and relevant stakeholders may wish to consider promoting BIC-to-LEI mapping facilities, and relevant authorities (e.g. LEI ROC and AMLEG) are encouraged to elaborate further on the extent to which banks can rely on the LEI to access reliable information supporting CDD in correspondent banking. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may wish to consider promoting BIC-to-LEI mapping facilities"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: To facilitate compliance with FATF CDD recommendations, use of information-sharing mechanisms (if they exist in a jurisdiction and data privacy laws allow) for knowing your customers' customers could be promoted as the first source of information by default, which could be complemented bilaterally with enhanced information should there be a need. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "could be promoted as the first source of information by default"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: In the Recommendations section, the CPMI recommends inviting the FATF and AMLEG to (i) clarify further the due diligence recommendations for upstream banks, in particular how far banks need to know their customers' customers (KYCC), and (ii) continue exploring how to overcome obstacles to information-sharing, with the aim of identifying potential best practices. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provide additional clarity on due diligence recommendations for upstream banks"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: To support information-sharing, the respondent bank may include provisions in its contractual framework with its customers (e.g. in terms and conditions or a supplementary agreement) allowing it to provide general information on request to other banks for AML/CFT compliance purposes. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the respondent bank may include provisions in its contractual framework with its customers"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation on payment messages: banks are recommended to decide individually which payment method best meets their own and their clients' needs and to agree with the other banks involved on the method to be used. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It is recommended that banks decide individually which payment method best meets their own and their clients’ needs"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The Wolfsberg Group and PMPG are invited to review their principles governing use-cases for payment messages (such as PMPG market practice guidelines and white papers), which should include the data that should be in payment messages and the data fields to be used to provide information for CDD; AMLEG is invited to consider further developing guidance on supervisors' role in ensuring banks meet FATF Recommendations and guidance on payment message content quality. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The documents should include information about the data that should be contained in payment messages"</sub>

## Recommendation on the use of the LEI as additional information in payment messages

- **expected / recommendation**: Use of the LEI as additional information in payment messages should be possible on an optional basis in the current MT 202 COV and MT 103 messages; to allow this, relevant stakeholders (e.g. PMPG) should work to define a common market practice for including the LEI without changing the current message structure. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be possible on an optional basis in the current relevant payment messages"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: As part of a potential future migration to ISO 20022-based message formats, ISO and SWIFT are encouraged to consider developing dedicated codes or data items for the inclusion of the LEI in payment messages. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "relevant stakeholders (ie ISO and SWIFT) are encouraged to consider developing dedicated codes or data items"</sub>
