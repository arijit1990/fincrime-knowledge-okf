## Section 5 - Conclusion

- **descriptive / context**: Paragraph 71(ii) (further research on AI for regulatory compliance or regtech) notes financial institutions use AI to support AML/CFT compliance and to calculate regulatory capital, and that use of AI for compliance, especially if models are similar or from the same vendors, leads to concern about concentration and herding behaviour. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "leads to concern about concentration and herding behaviour"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The paper states that in the two cited examples (AML/CFT compliance and regulatory capital calculation) an error in the models could have financial integrity and financial stability implications, and suggests further research on how AI is used for regtech and the risks this poses to regulatory objectives. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an error in the models could have financial integrity and financial stability implications"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Paragraph 71(iii) (supervisory approaches) states that upskilling, acquiring and retaining AI expertise within financial authorities is imperative to provide effective supervisory oversight of AI, and this expertise can also help authorities use the technology in their supervisory work (suptech); authorities may categorise AI systems and apply risk-based supervision differently, and further work describing these approaches would be helpful. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Upskilling, acquiring and retaining AI expertise within financial authorities is imperative"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Paragraph 72 states collaboration among financial authorities domestically and internationally is important to continue understanding and monitoring AI risks as the technology evolves, for example to better understand AI use cases in the financial sector and identify areas of heightened risk. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Collaboration among financial authorities both domestically and internationally is important in continuing to understand and monitor risks from AI"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The paper states data on AI use cases in finance are anecdotal at best, the presence of various AI definitions across jurisdictions is a significant impediment to acquiring such data, international alignment of the definition is an obvious first step (while recognising any agreed definition may need adjusting as the technology evolves), and an agreed definition would facilitate identification of risks and where they can be found. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At the moment, data on AI use cases in finance are anecdotal at best."</sub>
