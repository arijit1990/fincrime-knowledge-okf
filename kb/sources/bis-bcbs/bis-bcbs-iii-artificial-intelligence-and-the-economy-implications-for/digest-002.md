## Nowcasting with artificial intelligence

- **descriptive / definition**: The goal of nowcasting is to produce high-frequency assessments and forecasts, for example of GDP growth or inflation, that can be updated easily as soon as new data become available; most central banks support their economic analysis with nowcasting models. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The goal of nowcasting is to produce high-frequency assessments and forecasts"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Because no single indicator suffices to track economic activity in real time, nowcasting models often process large data sets containing dozens or hundreds of indicators, ranging from industrial production, purchasing manager sentiment surveys and credit card spending to web data scraped from online retailers or social media. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "nowcasting models often process large and complex data sets that contain dozens, if not hundreds, of indicators"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Recent advances in the econometrics of high-dimensional data, which let models synthesise much of the complexity of the aggregate economy into a few indicators, have substantially improved nowcasting; an example is the Federal Reserve Bank of New York's nowcasting model, which extracts latent factors from a wide range of data to forecast several economic series, in particular GDP. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recent advances in the econometrics of high-dimensional data, which enabled models to synthesise important parts of the complexity"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The limited availability of timely data makes nowcasting challenging; structured data on firms' hiring or consumer spending are available only with a lag, so nowcasting is only as good as the data lags. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "structured data on firms’ hiring or consumer spending are only available with a lag"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: LLMs can process vast amounts of text- or image-based data and create data series from news reports, social media postings, web searches or aerial images (such as car traffic in retailers' parking lots), and can extract sentiment or tone from text or speech. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they can create readily available data series from, for example, news reports, social media postings, web searches or aerial images"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Sentiment indices can substantially improve forecasts for key macroeconomic quantities such as unemployment, GDP growth and inflation, so nowcasting models could draw on a much richer set of real-time data. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such sentiment indices can substantially improve forecasts for key macroeconomic quantities"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Most time series forecasting models are highly specialised for specific settings (a GDP nowcasting model differs from one for nowcasting financial risk build-up), expert judgment is required to choose input variables and specify parameters, and adjusting models as conditions change takes time. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Currently, most models used for time series forecasting are highly specialised, designed for specific settings"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Few-shot learners display much greater versatility: LLMs trained to predict the next word, by converting time series data into tokens resembling textual sequences, can be directly used for time series prediction without additional training, unlike existing forecasting models that often need significant fine-tuning ex ante. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "due to their advanced capabilities for few-shot learning, they can be readily applied to a wide array of time series forecasting tasks"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Pre-trained foundation models have been shown to outperform state-of-the-art specialised forecasting models. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pre-trained foundation models have been shown to outperform state-of-the-art specialised forecasting models."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Such a real-time and detailed view of labour market developments can help central banks understand the extent of technology-induced job displacement, how quickly workers find new jobs and attendant wage dynamics. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks understand the extent of technology-induced job displacements, how quickly workers find new jobs and attendant wage dynamics"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Satellite data on aerial pollution or nighttime lights can be used to predict short-term economic activity, and data on electricity consumption can shed light on industrial production in different regions and industries. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "satellite data on aerial pollution or nighttime lights can be used to predict short-term economic activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Central banks can thereby obtain a more nuanced picture of firms' capital expenditure and production, and of how the supply of and demand for goods and services are changing. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Central banks can thereby obtain a more nuanced picture of firms’ capital expenditure and production"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Central banks can use AI, together with human expertise, to better understand factors that contribute to inflation. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Central banks can also use AI, together with human expertise, to better understand factors that contribute to inflation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Neural networks can handle more input variables than traditional econometric models, making it possible to work with detailed data sets rather than relying solely on aggregated data, and can reflect intricate non-linear relationships, offering valuable insights during periods of rapidly changing inflation dynamics. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Neural networks can handle more input variables compared with traditional econometric models"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: If AI's impact varies by industry but materialises rapidly, the advantages of neural networks are particularly beneficial for assessing inflationary dynamics. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If AI’s impact varies by industry but materialises rapidly, such advantages are particularly beneficial"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Recent work decomposes aggregate inflation into sub-components: economic theory is first used to pre-specify four factors shaping aggregate inflation (past inflation patterns, inflation expectations, the output gap and international prices). (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "economic theory is used to pre-specify four factors shaping aggregate inflation: past inflation patterns, inflation expectations"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: A neural network then uses aggregate series (for example the unemployment rate or total services inflation) and disaggregate series (for example two-digit industry output) to estimate the contribution of each of the four sub-components to overall inflation, accounting for possible non-linearities. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A neural network then uses aggregate series (eg the unemployment rate or total services inflation) and disaggregate series"</sub>
