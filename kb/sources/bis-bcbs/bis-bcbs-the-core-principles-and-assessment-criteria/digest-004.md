## Principle 18 - Problem exposures, provisions and reserves

- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 5: the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies, processes and organisational resources for reviewing and classifying exposures, early identification of deteriorating exposures, ongoing oversight of problem exposures and collecting past due obligations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the early identification of deteriorating exposures; ongoing oversight of problem exposures; and collecting past due obligations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 6: the supervisor obtains information regularly and in relevant detail (or has full access) on classification of exposures, collateral and other risk mitigants, provisions and write-offs, and requires banks to have adequate documentation supporting classification and provisioning. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor requires banks to have adequate documentation to support their classification and provisioning."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 7: the supervisor assesses whether classification and provisioning levels are appropriate and adequate for prudential purposes and evaluates treatment of exposures to identify material circumvention of classification and provisioning standards (for example forbearance); where policies, classifications or provisions are inadequate it has power to act, for example by requiring the bank to revise policies or methodologies, adjust classifications, increase provisioning, reserves or capital, or impose other remedial measures; external experts may conduct supporting assessments subject to supervisory review of their work. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identifying any material circumvention of the classification and provisioning standards (eg forbearance)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 8: the supervisor requires banks to have mechanisms to regularly assess the value of risk mitigants, including guarantees, credit derivatives and collateral, with collateral valuation reflecting net realisable value considering prevailing market conditions and time required for realisation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The valuation of collateral reflects the net realisable value, considering prevailing market conditions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 9: laws, regulations or the supervisor establish criteria for an exposure to be identified as a problem exposure; identified as non-performing (full repayment unlikely, or 90 days past due for a material amount, or defaulted under the Basel Framework or applicable prudential regulation, or credit-impaired under the applicable accounting framework); reclassified as performing (no material exposure more than 90 days past due, repayments made when due over a continuous repayment period, counterparty situation improved so full repayment per contractual terms is likely, and exposure no longer defaulted or impaired); and classified as forborne. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "or which are 90 days past due for a material amount"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 49 to Principle 18 defines a forborne exposure as one where the bank's counterparty is experiencing financial difficulty in meeting its financial commitments and the bank grants a concession it would not otherwise consider. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the bank grants a concession that it would not otherwise consider"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 10: the supervisor determines that the board obtains timely and appropriate information on the credit portfolio, including classification, provisions and reserves and major problem exposures, at a minimum summary results of the latest credit exposure review, comparative trends in problem exposure quality, and measures of existing or anticipated deterioration and expected losses. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "comparative trends in the overall quality of problem exposures"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 11: the supervisor requires that valuation, classification and provisioning, at least for significant exposures, are conducted on an individual item basis, and that banks set an appropriate threshold for identifying significant exposures and regularly review it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are conducted on an individual item basis"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 12: the supervisor regularly assesses trends and concentrations in risk and risk build-up across the banking sector relating to problem exposures, considers concentration in banks' risk mitigation strategies and its potential effect on mitigant efficacy, and considers adequacy of provisions and reserves at bank and system level. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "considers any observed concentration in the risk mitigation strategies adopted by banks"</sub>

## Principle 19 - Concentration risk and large exposure limits

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 19 states the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies and processes to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate risk concentrations on a timely basis; supervisors set prudential limits restricting exposures to single counterparties or groups of connected counterparties; and, at least for internationally active banks, large exposure requirements are not less stringent than the applicable Basel standard. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors set prudential limits to restrict bank exposures to single counterparties or groups of connected counterparties."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 52 to Principle 19: connected counterparties may include natural and legal persons; two or more persons are a group of connected counterparties if either (a) control relationship (one directly or indirectly controls the other(s)) or (b) economic interdependence (financial problems at one would likely cause financial difficulties for the other(s)) is satisfied. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "economic interdependence: if one of the counterparties were to experience financial problems"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 19 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to have policies and processes giving a comprehensive bank-wide view of significant concentration risk sources, capturing exposures (including counterparty credit risk) from off- and on-balance sheet items in the banking and trading books, with large exposure requirements for internationally active banks not less stringent than the Basel standard; footnote 53 says concentration risk is broader than large exposures and covers excessive exposure to asset classes, products, collateral, currencies or funding sources, and credit concentrations by counterparty, connected group, industry, sector, region or common activity or commodity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a comprehensive bank-wide view of significant sources of concentration risk."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 19 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines that a bank's information systems identify and aggregate on a timely basis exposures creating risk concentrations and large exposures to single or connected counterparties and facilitate their active management; footnote 54 says large exposure measures should reflect maximum possible loss from counterparty failure (actual and potential exposures and contingent liabilities) and should not use Basel risk weighting, which could significantly underestimate potential losses. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The risk weighting concept adopted in the Basel Framework should not be used in measuring credit exposure"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 19 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that risk management policies set thresholds for acceptable concentrations reflecting risk appetite, risk profile and capital strength, understood by and communicated to relevant staff, and require all material concentrations to be regularly reviewed and reported to the board. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establish thresholds for acceptable concentrations of risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 19 essential criterion 4: the supervisor regularly obtains information enabling review of concentrations within a bank's portfolio, including sectoral, geographical and currency exposures. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including sectoral, geographical and currency exposures, to be reviewed"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 19 essential criterion 5: for credit exposure to single or connected counterparties, laws or regulations explicitly define, or the supervisor has power to define, a group of connected counterparties to reflect actual risk exposure, and the supervisor may exercise discretion in applying the definition case by case. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a group of connected counterparties to reflect actual risk exposure"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 19 essential criterion 6: laws, regulations or the supervisor set prudent requirements to control and constrain large credit exposures to a single counterparty or connected group, where 'exposures' include all claims and transactions (including those giving rise to counterparty credit risk) on or off balance sheet; the supervisor determines that banks assess connectedness through control relationships and economic interdependence using objective and qualitative criteria, and that senior management monitors limits and they are not exceeded on a solo or consolidated basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are not exceeded on a solo or consolidated basis"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Principle 19 additional criterion: for credit exposure to single or connected counterparties, non-internationally active banks are required to adhere to these limits: an exposure of 10% or more of Tier 1 capital is a large exposure, and 25% of Tier 1 capital is the limit for an individual large exposure to a private sector non-bank counterparty or connected group; minor deviations may be acceptable, especially if explicitly temporary or related to very small or specialised banks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "25% of a bank's Tier 1 capital is the limit for an individual large exposure"</sub>

## Principle 20 - Transactions with related parties

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 20 states that to prevent abuses in related party transactions and address conflicts of interest, the supervisor requires banks to enter into related party transactions on an arm's length basis, monitor them, take appropriate steps to control or mitigate risks, and write off related party exposures in accordance with standard policies and processes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "write off exposures to related parties in accordance with standard policies and processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 56 to Principle 20 says related parties should include (a) the bank's subsidiaries and affiliates (including their subsidiaries, affiliates and special purpose entities) and any party the bank controls or that controls it; (b) major shareholders including beneficial owners; (c) board members, senior management and key staff, corresponding persons in affiliates, and parties able to exert significant influence on them; and (d) for the natural persons in (a) to (c), their direct and related interests and close family members. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their direct and related interests and their close family members"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 57 to Principle 20 says related party transactions include on- and off-balance sheet credit exposures; service contracts, asset purchases and sales, construction contracts and lease agreements; derivatives; borrowings; and write-offs, and 'transaction' should be interpreted broadly, including where an unrelated party with an existing exposure later becomes a related party. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "situations in which an unrelated party (with whom a bank has an existing exposure) subsequently becomes a related party"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor set out a comprehensive definition of 'related parties' that should at least consider all elements in footnote 56, and the supervisor may exercise discretion in applying it case by case. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should at least consider all of the elements detailed in footnote 56"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 2: laws, regulations or the supervisor require that related party transactions are not undertaken on more favourable terms (for example credit assessment, tenor, interest rates, fees, amortisation schedules, collateral requirements) than corresponding transactions with non-related counterparties; footnote 58 says exceptions may be appropriate for certain intragroup transactions consistent with sound group-wide risk management and for beneficial terms in overall remuneration packages. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not undertaken on more favourable terms"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 3: the supervisor requires that related party transactions and write-offs of related party exposures exceeding specified amounts or otherwise posing special risks need prior board approval, and that board members with conflicts of interest are excluded from the approval process for granting and managing such transactions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "board members with conflicts of interest are excluded from the approval process"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that banks have policies and processes to prevent persons benefiting from a related party transaction (and persons related to them) or otherwise conflicted from being part of the process of granting and managing it. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to prevent persons benefiting from the transaction (and/or persons related to such a person)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 5: laws, regulations or the supervisor set, generally or case by case, limits on exposures to related parties or require them to be collateralised or deducted from capital; where limits apply only to aggregate related party exposures, they are at least as strict as those for single or connected counterparties under Principle 19 (footnote 60: the supervisor may exclude exposures to certain group entities if consistent with sound group-wide risk management). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "those are at least as strict as those for single counterparties or groups of connected counterparties under Principle 19"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 6: the supervisor determines that banks have policies and processes to identify individual related party exposures and transactions and total exposures, and to monitor and report them through an independent credit review or audit process; exceptions to policies and limits are reported to appropriate senior management and, if necessary, the board for timely action, and senior management monitors these transactions with board oversight. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "monitor and report on them through an independent credit review or audit process"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 20 essential criterion 7: the supervisor obtains and regularly reviews information on aggregate related party exposures, and requires banks to report (or obtains through other means) individual material related party transactions, for example those exceeding a specified amount or percentage of Tier 1 capital. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "individual related party transactions that are material (eg those exceeding a specified amount or a percentage"</sub>

## Principle 21 - Country and transfer risks

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 21 states the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies and processes to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate country risk and transfer risk in their international lending and investment activities on a timely basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "country risk 62 and transfer risk 63 in their international lending and investment activities on a timely basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 62 and 63 to Principle 21 define country risk as the risk of exposure to loss caused by events in a foreign country, broader than sovereign risk as it covers all lending or investment involving individuals, corporates, banks or governments; and transfer risk as the risk that a borrower cannot convert local currency into foreign currency and so cannot make foreign-currency debt service payments, normally arising from government exchange restrictions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transfer risk is the risk that a borrower will not be able to convert local currency into a foreign currency"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 21 criterion 1: the supervisor determines that policies and processes adequately cover country and transfer risk, are consistent with risk profile, systemic importance and appetite, consider market and macroeconomic conditions, and give a bank-wide view; exposures (including intragroup where relevant) are identified, monitored and managed on a regional and individual country basis as well as end-borrower basis, and banks monitor developments and apply appropriate countermeasures. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "monitored and managed on a regional and an individual country basis"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 21 criterion 2: the supervisor determines that country and transfer risk strategies and policies are approved and regularly reviewed by the board, which oversees management so policies are implemented effectively and integrated into overall risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strategies and policies for the management of country and transfer risks have been approved and are regularly reviewed"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 21 criterion 3: the supervisor determines that banks have information, risk management and internal control systems that accurately aggregate, monitor and report country exposures on a timely basis and ensure adherence to established country exposure limits. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ensure adherence to established country exposure limits"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 21 criterion 4: there is supervisory oversight of setting appropriate provisions against country and transfer risk, which may take one of three forms: the supervisor (or relevant authority) sets minimum provisioning via regularly set fixed percentages per country and reviews them; the authority sets percentage ranges per country within which banks choose provisioning for individual exposures; or the bank sets percentages or guidelines or decides provisioning for individual exposures, with adequacy judged by the external auditor and/or supervisor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regularly sets percentage ranges for each country, considering prevailing conditions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 21 criterion 5: the supervisor regularly obtains and reviews sufficient and timely information on banks' country and transfer risk and has power to obtain additional information as needed (for example in crisis situations). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor has the power to obtain additional information, as needed"</sub>

## Principle 22 - Market risk

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 22 states the supervisor determines that banks have an adequate market risk management process considering risk appetite, risk profile, market and macroeconomic conditions and the risk of significant deterioration in market liquidity, including prudent policies to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate market risks on a timely basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the risk of a significant deterioration in market liquidity"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 22 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to have market risk processes giving a bank-wide view of market risk exposure, consistent with risk appetite, risk profile, systemic importance and capital strength, considering market and macroeconomic conditions and market liquidity deterioration, and clearly articulating roles and responsibilities for identifying, measuring, monitoring, reporting and controlling market risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clearly articulate the roles and responsibilities for identifying, measuring, monitoring, reporting and controlling market risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 22 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines that market risk strategies and policies are approved and regularly reviewed by the board, which oversees management to ensure effective implementation and integration into overall risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strategies and policies for the management of market risk have been approved and are regularly reviewed"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 22 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that policies establish a properly controlled market risk environment including comprehensive measurement systems for accurate, timely identification, aggregation, monitoring and reporting of exposures; market risk limits consistent with appetite, risk profile, capital strength and management's ability and communicated to staff; exception tracking and reporting; effective controls around models used to measure risk and set limits; and sound policies for allocating exposures to the trading book. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sound policies and processes for the allocation of exposures to the trading book"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 22 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that systems and controls ensure marked-to-market positions are revalued frequently, all transactions are captured on a timely basis, and valuation uses consistent, prudent practices and reliable market data verified independently of risk-taking units (or, absent market prices, internal or industry-accepted models); models used for valuation are validated regularly by an independent function, and banks maintain policies for valuation adjustments for positions that cannot be prudently valued, including concentrated, less liquid and stale positions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including concentrated, less liquid and stale positions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 22 essential criterion 5: the supervisor determines that banks hold appropriate capital against unexpected losses and make appropriate valuation adjustments for uncertainties in determining fair value of assets and liabilities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "make appropriate valuation adjustments for uncertainties in determining the fair value of assets and liabilities"</sub>

## Principle 23 - Interest rate risk in the banking book

- **expected / requirement**: Principle 23 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to have an appropriate interest rate risk strategy and management framework giving a bank-wide view, with policies to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control material sources; the supervisor determines these are consistent with risk appetite, risk profile and systemic importance, consider market conditions, and are regularly reviewed and adjusted. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an appropriate interest rate risk strategy and interest rate risk management framework"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 23 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines that interest rate risk strategies and policies are approved and regularly reviewed by the board, which oversees management for effective implementation and integration into overall risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strategies and policies for the management of interest rate risk have been approved and are regularly reviewed"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 23 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that policies establish a properly controlled interest rate risk environment including comprehensive measurement systems; regular review and independent (internal or external) validation of models, including key assumptions such as optional elements embedded in assets, liabilities or off-balance sheet items where bank or customer can alter cash flow level and timing; limits approved by the board and senior management reflecting appetite, risk profile and capital strength and communicated to staff; and effective exception tracking and reporting. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "independent (internal or external) validation of any models used by the functions tasked with managing interest rate risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 23 essential criteria 4 and 5: the supervisor obtains from banks the results of internal interest rate risk measurement systems, expressed as threat to both economic value and earnings using standardised interest rate shocks on the banking book, and assesses whether internal capital measurement systems adequately capture interest rate risk in the banking book. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using standardised interest rate shocks on the banking book"</sub>

## Principle 24 - Liquidity risk

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 24 states the supervisor sets prudent and appropriate liquidity requirements (quantitative, qualitative or both) reflecting banks' liquidity needs, determines that banks have a strategy enabling prudent liquidity risk management and compliance with requirements over appropriate time horizons, and, at least for internationally active banks, sets liquidity (including funding) requirements not lower than applicable Basel standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At least for internationally active banks, liquidity (including funding) requirements are not lower than the applicable Basel standards."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to consistently observe prescribed liquidity requirements, including thresholds that trigger supervisory action; for internationally active banks the requirements are not lower than Basel standards and the supervisor uses a range of liquidity monitoring tools no less extensive than those prescribed in Basel standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a range of liquidity monitoring tools no less extensive than those prescribed in the applicable Basel standards"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 2: prescribed liquidity requirements reflect the liquidity risk profile of banks, including on- and off-balance sheet risks, in the context of their markets and macroeconomic conditions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reflect the liquidity risk profile of banks (including on- and off-balance sheet risks)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that banks have a robust liquidity management framework requiring sufficient liquidity to withstand a range of stress events, including board-approved policies giving a bank-wide view of liquidity risk consistent with liquidity risk tolerance, risk profile and systemic importance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintain sufficient liquidity to withstand a range of stress events"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that the liquidity strategy establishes a properly controlled environment including a board-approved liquidity risk appetite, sound day-to-day and intraday practices, comprehensive measurement systems for liquidity exposures and funding needs (including active collateral management), adequate board oversight, and board review at least annually with adjustment for changing risk profile and external developments. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regular review by the bank's board (at least annually)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 5: the supervisor requires banks to establish and regularly review funding strategies and processes for measuring and monitoring funding requirements and managing funding risk, considering effects of other risks (credit, market, operational, reputational), including analysis of funding needs under alternative scenarios, a cushion of high-quality unencumbered liquid assets usable without impediment in stress, diversification of funding sources (counterparties, instruments, currencies, markets) and tenor with regular review of concentration limits, regular efforts to maintain relationships with liability holders, and regular assessment of capacity to monetise assets. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a cushion of high-quality, unencumbered, liquid assets that can be used, without impediment, to obtain funding in times of stress"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 6: the supervisor determines that banks have robust liquidity contingency funding plans that are formally articulated, documented and set out a strategy for liquidity shortfalls in a range of stress environments without reliance on lender of last resort support, with clear lines of responsibility, communication plans (including with the supervisor) and regular testing and updating; the supervisor assesses feasibility and requires deficiencies to be addressed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without placing reliance on lender of last resort support"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 7: the supervisor requires banks to include a variety of short-term and protracted bank-specific and market-wide liquidity stress scenarios (individually and in combination), with conservative, regularly reviewed assumptions, in stress testing, and determines that results are used to adjust liquidity strategies, policies and positions and to develop effective contingency funding plans. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "short-term and protracted bank-specific and market-wide liquidity stress scenarios"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 8: the supervisor identifies banks carrying out significant foreign currency liquidity transformation; where foreign currency business or exposure in a currency is significant, it requires separate analysis and monitoring for each significant currency, including stress testing mismatches and, where appropriate, limits on cash flow mismatches in aggregate and per significant currency, and monitors the bank's ability to transfer liquidity across currencies, jurisdictions and legal entities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "carrying out significant foreign currency liquidity transformation"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 24 essential criterion 9: the supervisor determines that a bank's level of encumbered balance sheet assets is managed within acceptable limits to mitigate effects on funding cost and long-term liquidity sustainability, and requires banks to commit to adequate disclosure and set appropriate limits to mitigate identified risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "level of encumbered balance sheet assets is managed within acceptable limits"</sub>

## Principle 23 - Interest rate risk in the banking book

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 23 adds that these systems consider the bank's risk appetite, risk profile and market and macroeconomic conditions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These systems consider the bank's risk appetite, risk profile and market and macroeconomic conditions."</sub>
