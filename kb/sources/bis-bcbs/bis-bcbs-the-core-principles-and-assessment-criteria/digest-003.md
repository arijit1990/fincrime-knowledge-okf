## Principle 14 - Corporate governance

- **expected / requirement**: Principle 14 essential criterion 6 (as written, except where laws or regulations require otherwise): the supervisor determines that the board has established fit and proper standards for selecting senior management and heads of control functions, developed effective processes to allocate authority, responsibility and accountability, maintains succession plans, and actively and critically oversees senior management's execution of board strategies, including monitoring senior management and control function heads against set standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has established fit and proper standards in selecting senior management and heads of the control functions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 14 essential criterion 7: the supervisor determines that the board actively oversees the design and operation of the compensation system, with appropriate incentives aligned with prudent risk-taking and effective in addressing misconduct that potentially results in losses; the system and related performance standards are non-discriminatory, consistent with long-term objectives and financial soundness, and rectified if deficient. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "aligned with prudent risk-taking and effective in addressing misconduct that potentially results in losses"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 14 essential criterion 8: the supervisor determines that the board and senior management know and understand the bank's operational structure and its risks, including those from structures that impede transparency (for example special purpose or related structures), and that these risks are effectively managed and mitigated where appropriate. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including those arising from the use of structures that impede transparency"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 14 essential criterion 9: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to notify the supervisor or publicly disclose, as soon as they become aware, any material and bona fide information that may negatively affect the fitness and propriety of a board member or senior management member. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "any material and bona fide information that may negatively affect the fitness and propriety"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 14 essential criterion 10: the supervisor has power to require changes in the composition of the bank's board if it believes individuals are not fulfilling their duties related to satisfaction of these criteria. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor has the power to require changes in the composition of the bank's board"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 28 and 29 to Principle 14 define 'independent director' as a non-executive board member without management responsibilities in the bank and not under other undue influence, internal or external, political or ownership, that would impede objective judgment; 'duty of care' as the duty to decide and act on an informed and prudent basis (as a prudent person would for their own affairs); and 'duty of loyalty' as the duty to act in good faith in the company's interest, preventing board members acting in their own or another's interest at the expense of the company and shareholders. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "“duty of loyalty” as the duty of board members to act in good faith in the interest of the company"</sub>

## Principle 15 - Risk management process

- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 33 to Principle 15 says that although the supervisor is required to determine that banks' risk management policies and processes are adhered to, responsibility for ensuring adherence remains with the bank's board and senior management; footnote 32 says precise requirements may vary from risk type to risk type (Principles 15 to 25). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the responsibility for ensuring adherence remains with a bank"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 1: the supervisor determines that banks have board-approved risk management strategies and that the board establishes an effective risk appetite statement and framework; the board ensures a sound risk culture, risk-taking policies consistent with strategy and appetite, recognition of uncertainties in risk measurement, appropriate limits consistent with appetite, risk profile, capital strength and liquidity needs that are understood by and regularly communicated to relevant staff, and that senior managers monitor and control all material risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the board establishes an effective risk appetite statement and framework to define the level of risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 2: the supervisor requires comprehensive risk management policies and processes for all material risks (including, per footnote 34, reputational, step-in and strategic risks where relevant) and determines they provide a bank-wide view across material risk types, fit the risk profile and systemic importance, assess macroeconomic risks in the bank's markets, and assess longer-horizon risks including digitalisation, climate-related financial and emerging risks; banks use scenario analysis where appropriate. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where appropriate, banks use scenario analysis as a tool."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that risk strategies, policies, processes and limits are documented, aligned with risk appetite, regularly reviewed and adjusted, and communicated within the bank, and that adequate procedures exist for breaches of limits and significant deviations from policy so they get prompt attention and authorisation from the appropriate management or board level and proportionate, timely remedial follow-up. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adequate procedures are in place for breaches of risk limits and significant deviations from established policies"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that the board and senior management obtain sufficient information on and understand the nature and level of risk taken and how it relates to adequate capital and liquidity, and regularly review and understand the implications and limitations (including measurement uncertainties) of risk management information received. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regularly review and understand the implications and limitations (including the risk measurement uncertainties)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 5: the supervisor determines that banks have an appropriate internal process for assessing overall capital and liquidity adequacy and business model sustainability relative to risk appetite, risk profile and forward-looking strategies (footnote 35: banks should include climate-related financial risks assessed as material over relevant horizons, including in stress testing where appropriate), and reviews and evaluates those assessments and strategies. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor reviews and evaluates banks' internal capital and liquidity adequacy assessments and strategies."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 6: where banks use models to measure components of risk, the supervisor determines that banks comply with supervisory standards on model use, boards and senior management understand model limitations and uncertainties and inherent risk, and banks perform regular independent validation and testing; the supervisor also assesses whether model outputs appear reasonable as a reflection of risks assumed. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks perform regular and independent validation and testing of the models"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criteria 7 and 8: the supervisor determines that banks have information systems adequate under normal and stressed conditions for measuring, assessing and reporting exposures bank-wide across all risk types, products and counterparties, with timely, suitable reports to the board and senior management, and that banks maintain risk data aggregation and reporting capabilities commensurate with risk profile and systemic importance, with the board and senior management reviewing and approving the framework and ensuring adequate resources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate risk data aggregation and reporting capabilities commensurate with the risk profile and systemic importance of the bank"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 9: the supervisor determines that banks have policies and processes ensuring boards and senior management understand risks in new products (including, per footnote 36, those developed by a third party and purchased or distributed by the bank), material modifications to existing products and major initiatives (such as changes in systems, processes, business models and major acquisitions), monitor and manage them, and require board or board committee approval of such major activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "understand the risks inherent in new products, 36 material modifications to existing products"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 10: the supervisor determines that banks have risk management functions covering all material risks with sufficient resources, independence, authority and board access, duties clearly segregated from risk-taking functions, direct reporting on exposures to the board and senior management, and regular review by internal audit. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "their duties are clearly segregated from risk-taking functions in the bank"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 11: the supervisor requires larger and more complex banks to have a dedicated risk management unit overseen by a chief risk officer (CRO) or equivalent; if the CRO is removed for any reason this should be done with prior board approval and generally disclosed publicly, and the bank should discuss the reasons with its supervisor. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor requires larger and more complex banks to have a dedicated risk management unit"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 13: the supervisor requires banks to have contingency arrangements as an integral part of risk management for stress conditions (including serious viability threats) and, if warranted, robust and credible recovery plans; working with resolution authorities as appropriate it assesses their adequacy and feasibility in stress and seeks improvements where deficient. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor requires banks to have appropriate contingency arrangements, as an integral part of their risk management process"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 14 also says the supervisor's assessment scope may include whether the stress testing programme promotes bank-wide risk identification and control, adopts suitably severe assumptions and addresses feedback effects and system-wide interaction between risks, benefits from active board and senior management involvement, and is appropriately documented and regularly maintained and updated. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adopts suitably severe assumptions and seeks to address feedback effects and system-wide interaction between risks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 15 essential criterion 15: the supervisor assesses whether banks appropriately account for risks (including liquidity impacts) in internal pricing, performance measurement and new product approval for all significant business activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in their internal pricing, performance measurement and new product approval process"</sub>

## Principle 16 - Capital adequacy

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 16 states the supervisor sets prudent and appropriate capital adequacy requirements reflecting the risks banks undertake and present in their market and macroeconomic context, defines capital components bearing in mind loss-absorbing ability, and, at least for internationally active banks, sets requirements not less stringent than the applicable Basel standards; footnote 38 says implementing the Basel Framework is not a prerequisite for compliance with the Core Principles and compliance with Basel capital regimes is only required of jurisdictions that have declared voluntary implementation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At least for internationally active banks, capital requirements are not less stringent than the applicable Basel standards."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 16 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to calculate and consistently observe prescribed capital requirements, including thresholds triggering supervisory action, and define qualifying capital components, emphasising elements permanently available to absorb losses on a going concern basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "permanently available to absorb losses on a going concern basis"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 16 essential criterion 3: the supervisor has power to impose a specific capital charge or limits on all material risk exposures if warranted, including risks not adequately transferred or mitigated through transactions such as securitisation; on- and off-balance sheet risks are included in prescribed capital requirements. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor has the power to impose a specific capital charge and/or limits on all material risk exposures"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 16 essential criterion 4: prescribed capital requirements reflect risk profile and systemic importance, constrain leverage build-up and reduce contagion risk; in assessing adequacy the supervisor focuses on loss absorbency of capital instruments, appropriateness of risk weights as a proxy for risk profile, adequacy of provisions and reserves for expected losses, and quality of risk management and controls; requirements may vary bank to bank and jurisdictions may set higher overall standards than Basel. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the appropriateness of risk weights as a proxy for the risk profile of its exposures"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 16 essential criterion 5: use of banks' internal risk assessments as inputs to regulatory capital is approved by the supervisor; if approved, assessments adhere to rigorous qualifying standards, cessation or material modification of processes and models requires supervisory approval, the supervisor can evaluate the assessment process and rely on it as a reasonable reflection of risks, may impose conditions on approvals, and can revoke approval if standards or conditions are no longer met. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the supervisor has the power to revoke its approval"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 16 essential criterion 6: the supervisor has power to require a forward-looking approach to capital management (including stress testing), requiring banks to set capital levels and manage available capital and planned capital expenditures in anticipation of business cycle effects, market conditions and bank-specific changes, and to have feasible contingency arrangements to maintain or strengthen capital in stress. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to have in place feasible contingency arrangements to maintain or strengthen capital positions in times of stress"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 16 essential criterion 7: laws or regulations require, or the supervisor has power to impose, a simple, transparent, non-risk-based measure capturing all on- and off-balance sheet exposures to supplement risk-based capital requirements and constrain leverage build-up. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a simple, transparent, non-risk-based measure that captures all on- and off-balance sheet exposures"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Principle 16 additional criteria: for non-internationally active banks, capital requirements (definition, risk coverage, calculation, scope of application, capital required) are broadly consistent with the principles of the applicable Basel standards; the supervisor requires adequate distribution of capital among group entities according to the allocation of risks; and laws permit authorities to require additional capital (which may include sectoral requirements) in a form that can be released when system-wide risk crystallises or dissipates. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are broadly consistent with the principles of the applicable Basel standards relevant to internationally active banks"</sub>

## Principle 17 - Credit risk

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 17 states the supervisor determines that banks have an adequate credit risk management process considering risk appetite, risk profile, market conditions, macroeconomic factors and forward-looking information, including prudent policies to identify, measure, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate credit risk (including counterparty credit risk) on a timely basis, covering the full credit life cycle from underwriting and evaluation to ongoing portfolio management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The full credit life cycle is covered, including credit underwriting, credit evaluation"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 42 and 43 to Principle 17 say credit risk may arise from on- and off-balance sheet exposures including loans, investments, interbank lending, derivatives, securities financing and trading activities, and counterparty credit risk arises from OTC and exchange-traded derivatives, long settlement transactions and bilaterally or centrally cleared securities financing transactions with banks, non-financial corporates and non-bank financial institutions (not limited to these). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Transactions that give rise to counterparty credit risk include: OTC derivatives, exchange-traded derivatives"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require sound credit risk management processes providing a comprehensive bank-wide view of credit exposures, including a robust methodology for early identification and appropriate measurement of credit losses, consistent with risk appetite, risk profile, systemic importance and capital strength, considering current and forward-looking market and macroeconomic factors and resulting in prudent underwriting, evaluation, administration, monitoring, measurement and control standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a robust methodology for the early identification and appropriate measurement of credit losses"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines that the board approves and regularly reviews the credit risk strategy and significant policies, consistent with board-set risk appetite, and oversees management to ensure they are implemented effectively and integrated into overall risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approves and regularly reviews the credit risk management strategy and significant policies"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criterion 3: the supervisor requires and regularly determines that policies establish an appropriate, controlled credit risk environment, including a documented strategy and policies for assuming credit risk without undue reliance on external credit assessments; defined criteria for approving new exposures (including prudent underwriting and understanding of borrower risk, and for securitisations all material transaction features), renewing and refinancing exposures, and identifying the appropriate approval authority for size and complexity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "without undue reliance on external credit assessments"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criterion 3 continues with credit administration and control elements: continued analysis of a borrower's ability and willingness to pay (including reviews of underlying asset performance, for example securitisation or project finance); monitoring of documentation, legal covenants, contractual requirements, collateral and other credit risk mitigation; an appropriate exposure grading or classification system; effective information systems for accurate, timely identification, aggregation and reporting of exposures; prudent credit limits understood by and communicated to staff; exception tracking and reporting; and effective controls on data quality and model validation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exception tracking and reporting processes that ensure prompt action at the appropriate level of the bank's senior management or board"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that banks have policies and processes to monitor total indebtedness of obligors and any risk factors that may result in default, including significant unhedged foreign exchange risk. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "monitor the total indebtedness of obligors to which they extend credit"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criteria 5 and 6: the supervisor requires banks to make credit decisions free of conflicts of interest and on an arm's length basis, and requires credit policy to prescribe that major credit exposures above a certain amount or percentage of capital, and exposures that are especially risky or not aligned with core business, be decided by the board or senior management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "make credit decisions free of conflicts of interest and on an arm's length basis"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 17 essential criterion 7: the supervisor has full access to information in credit and investment portfolios and to bank officers involved in assuming, managing, controlling and reporting credit risk; footnote 44 says securitisation includes traditional and synthetic structures and supervisors should provide guidance where appropriate on whether a transaction is a securitisation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor has full access to information in the credit and investment portfolios"</sub>

## Principle 18 - Problem exposures, provisions and reserves

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 18 states the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies and processes for early identification and management of problem exposures and maintenance of adequate provisions and reserves. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adequate policies and processes for the early identification and management of problem exposures"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 46 to 48 to Principle 18: for internal risk management a problem exposure is one where there is reason to believe all amounts due, including principal and interest, may not be collected per contractual terms; Principle 18 covers all provisioning approaches used for prudential purposes (incurred loss, expected credit loss, calendar provisioning), cumulative provisions being called loss allowances in some jurisdictions; reserves are 'below the line' non-distributable profit appropriations required by a supervisor in addition to 'above the line' provisions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a problem exposure is an exposure for which there is reason to believe that all amounts due"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to have policies, processes and methodologies for grading, classifying and monitoring all credit exposures (including off-balance sheet and forborne exposures) and identifying and managing problem exposures, with regular reviews of exposures individually or by homogeneous portfolio to ensure appropriate classification, detect deterioration and identify problem exposures in a timely manner. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "grading, classifying and monitoring all credit exposures"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that the board approves and regularly reviews significant policies for classifying exposures, determining provisions and managing problem exposures and write-offs, and oversees management so these are implemented effectively and integrated into overall risk management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "significant policies for classifying exposures, determining provisions and managing problem exposures and write-offs"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 18 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies, processes, methodologies and organisational resources for provisions and write-offs, ensuring they are timely and reflect realistic repayment and recovery expectations and, where relevant, reasonable and supportable expectations of future credit losses, and that provision and write-off methodologies and levels are reviewed and validated by a function independent of the relevant risk-taking function. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "subject to an effective review and validation process conducted by a function independent of the relevant risk-taking function"</sub>

## Principle 15 - Risk management process

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 15 further covers banks assessing the adequacy of their capital and liquidity and the sustainability of their business models in relation to their risk profile and market and macroeconomic conditions, and developing and reviewing contingency arrangements (including robust and credible recovery plans where warranted) that consider the bank's specific circumstances; the process is commensurate with the bank's risk profile and systemic importance. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The risk management process is commensurate with the risk profile and systemic importance of the bank."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: A Principle 15 essential criterion: the supervisor issues standards on, in particular, credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, interest rate risk in the banking book, operational risk and large exposures. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor issues standards related to, in particular, credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The supervisor regularly assesses a bank's stress testing programme and determines that it captures all material sources of risk and adopts plausible adverse scenarios; it also determines that the bank integrates results into decision-making, risk management processes (including contingency arrangements) and assessment of capital and liquidity levels. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor regularly assesses a bank's stress testing programme and determines that it captures all material sources of risk"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The supervisor requires corrective action if material deficiencies are identified in a bank's stress testing programme or if stress test results are not adequately considered in the bank's decision-making process. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor requires corrective action if material deficiencies are identified in a bank's stress testing programme"</sub>

## Principle 16 - Capital adequacy

- **expected / requirement**: A Principle 16 essential criterion: at least for internationally active banks, the definition of capital, the risk coverage, the calculation method and the thresholds for prescribed requirements are not lower than those in the applicable Basel standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "are not lower than those established in the applicable Basel standards."</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Footnote 39 says that, as an alternative to full sub-consolidation, applying the framework to the standalone bank (not consolidating subsidiaries' assets and liabilities) would achieve the same objective, provided the full book value of any investments in subsidiaries and significant minority-owned stakes is deducted from the bank's capital. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "providing the full book value of any investments in subsidiaries and significant minority-owned stakes is deducted"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Footnote 39 states that supervisors must also test that individual banks are adequately capitalised on a standalone basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Supervisors must also test that individual banks are adequately capitalised on a standalone basis."</sub>

## Principle 18 - Problem exposures, provisions and reserves

- **expected / requirement**: A Principle 18 essential criterion: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to formulate policies, processes and methodologies for consistently establishing provisions and ensuring appropriate and robust provisioning levels. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "policies, processes and methodologies for consistently establishing provisions and ensuring appropriate and robust provisioning levels"</sub>
