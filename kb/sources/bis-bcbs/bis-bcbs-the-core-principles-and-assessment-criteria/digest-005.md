## Principle 25 - Operational risk and operational resilience

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 25 states the supervisor determines that banks have an adequate operational risk management framework and operational resilience approach considering risk profile, risk appetite, business environment, tolerance for disruption to critical operations and emerging risks, with prudent policies to (i) identify, assess, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate operational risk on a timely basis and (ii) identify and protect against threats and potential failures, respond, adapt, recover and learn from disruptive events to minimise impact on delivering critical operations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an adequate operational risk 69 management framework and operational resilience 70 approach"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 69 to 71 to Principle 25 define operational risk as the risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems or external events, including legal risk but excluding strategic and reputational risk; operational resilience as the bank's ability to deliver critical operations through disruption, an outcome that benefits from effective operational risk management; and tolerance for disruption as the level of disruption from any operational risk type a bank is willing to accept given severe but plausible scenarios. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Operational risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 71 says 'critical operations' encompasses critical functions and includes activities, processes, services and their supporting assets whose disruption would be material to the bank's continued operation or its role in the financial system, and whether an operation is critical depends on the nature of the bank and its role in the financial system. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Whether a particular operation is critical depends on the nature of the bank and its role in the financial system."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to have operational risk management and operational resilience strategies, policies, procedures, systems, controls and processes to identify, assess, evaluate, monitor, report and control or mitigate operational risk, and to identify and protect against threats and failures, respond, adapt, recover and learn from disruption; these are consistent with risk profile, systemic importance, risk appetite, tolerance for disruption and capital strength, and consider market and macroeconomic conditions and emerging risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "respond and adapt to, as well as recover and learn from, disruptive events to minimise their impact"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines that the board approves and periodically reviews strategies and policies for operational risk management for all material products, activities, processes and systems (including risk appetite) and for the operational resilience approach (including tolerance for disruption), oversees senior management implementation and integration, and that banks have adequate functions (footnote 72: including control functions, risk management and internal audit) to identify external and internal threats and potential failures in people, processes and systems on an ongoing basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approves and periodically reviews the strategies and policies for its"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that the bank has identified its critical operations and mapped the people, technology, processes, data, facilities, third parties or intragroup entities and their interconnections and interdependencies necessary to deliver critical operations through disruption. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has identified its critical operations (consistent with its operational resilience approach) and mapped the people, technology"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that banks develop and implement response and recovery plans for incidents that could disrupt critical operations, in line with risk appetite and tolerance for disruption, and continuously improve them by incorporating lessons from previous incidents. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "develop and implement response and recovery plans to manage incidents that could disrupt the delivery of critical operations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 5: the supervisor requires banks to conduct business continuity exercises under a range of severe but plausible scenarios, reviews the quality and comprehensiveness of business continuity and disaster recovery plans, and determines that the bank can operate on an ongoing basis and minimise losses and service interruptions in a severe disruption (including at a service provider and disturbances in payment and settlement systems). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conduct business continuity exercises under a range of severe but plausible scenarios"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 6: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to implement a robust information and communication technology (ICT) framework, including cyber security, within their operational risk and resilience approach; the supervisor determines banks have policies to identify, assess, mitigate, monitor and manage ICT risks, with the board regularly overseeing and senior management routinely evaluating ICT risk management effectiveness, and resilient ICT subject to protection, detection, response and recovery processes that are regularly tested. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "robust information and communication technology (ICT) 73 framework (including cyber security)"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnotes 73 and 74 to Principle 25 say ICT refers to the underlying physical and logical design of information technology and communication systems, hardware and software components, data and operating environments, and ICT risk policies include cyber security, ICT response and recovery programmes, ICT change management, ICT incident management and timely transmission of relevant information to users. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Information and communication technology refers to the underlying physical and logical design of information technology"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 7: the supervisor assesses whether banks have processes and effective information systems to regularly monitor operational risk profiles and material exposures, compile and analyse operational risk event data (internal loss data and, when feasible, external loss event data), and facilitate reporting at board, senior management, independent risk function and business unit levels supporting proactive management. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "compile and analyse operational risk event data, which include internal loss data"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 8: the supervisor requires banks to have reporting mechanisms keeping the supervisor apprised of developments affecting operational risk, including incidents that disrupt critical operations and their severity. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including reporting of incidents that disrupt critical operations, and their severity"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 9: laws, regulations or the supervisor require the board and senior management to understand risks of activities performed by service providers and ensure effective risk management; third-party risk policies should cover determining whether and how activities can be provided by service providers and due diligence in selection; sound structuring of provision including data ownership, confidentiality and termination rights; managing and monitoring risks including the provider's financial condition; an effective control environment including a register of outsourced activities, metrics and reporting; managing dependencies for critical operations; and contingency planning and exit strategies (footnote 75: considering near-term and long-term disorderly and orderly exits). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "includes a register of outsourced activities, metrics and reporting to facilitate service provider oversight"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 9 also says the bank's business continuity plans should assess substitutability of service providers used for critical operations and other viable alternatives (such as bringing the activity back in-house), and that arrangements include comprehensive contracts or service level agreements clearly allocating responsibilities, the bank's right to inspect the provider's books and records and request reporting (for example audit reports), and permission for the bank's supervisor to access documentation, data and information on the activity directly or via the bank. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "permission for the bank's supervisor to access, directly or via the supervised bank, documentation, data"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 10: the supervisor determines that senior management has established a comprehensive, appropriately resourced change management process, adequately divided between risk management and control functions and able to assess potential effects on critical operations and their interconnections; footnote 76 says operational risk evolves with change such as new activities, products, unfamiliar markets, new processes or technology, or businesses geographically distant from head office, and change management should assess evolving risks across the full product or service life cycle. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "established a change management process 76 that is comprehensive, appropriately resourced"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Principle 25 additional criteria: the supervisor regularly identifies common points of exposure across banks to operational risk or vulnerability (for example reliance of many banks on a common service provider, disruption to payment and settlement service providers, losses from physical risks or geopolitical events), and assesses concentration risk-related arrangements and potential systemic risks from concentration of services provided by specific service providers to banks in its jurisdiction. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor regularly identifies any common points of exposure across banks to operational risk"</sub>

## Principle 26 - Internal control and audit

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 26 states the supervisor determines that banks have adequate internal control frameworks establishing an effectively controlled and tested operating environment, including clear delegation of authority and responsibility; separation of functions that commit the bank, pay away its funds and account for its assets and liabilities; reconciliation; safeguarding of assets; and appropriately independent internal audit (including outsourced or co-sourced), compliance and other control functions to test adherence to and effectiveness of controls and applicable laws and regulations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "separation of the functions that involve committing the bank, paying away its funds, and accounting for its assets and liabilities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 78 to Principle 26 says that in assessing independence, supervisors give due regard to control systems designed to avoid conflicts of interest in performance measurement of compliance, control and internal audit staff, for example remuneration of such staff being determined independently of the business lines they oversee. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the remuneration of such staff should be determined independently of the business lines that they oversee"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 26 essential criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to have internal control frameworks (forward-looking, per footnote 79) that are the responsibility of the board and/or senior management and deal with organisational structure, accounting policies and processes, checks and balances, and safeguarding assets and investments, including measures for prevention and early detection and reporting of misuse such as fraud, embezzlement, unauthorised trading and computer intrusion. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prevention and early detection and reporting of misuse, such as fraud, embezzlement, unauthorised trading and computer intrusion"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 26 essential criterion 1 specifies controls on organisational structure (defined duties, clear delegation such as loan approval limits, decision-making policies, separation of critical functions such as business origination, payments, reconciliation, risk management, accounting, audit and compliance), accounting policies and processes (reconciliation of accounts, control lists, management information), checks and balances (the 'four-eyes principle': segregation of duties, cross-checking, dual control of assets, double signatures), and safeguarding assets and investments including physical control and computer access. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "segregation of duties, cross-checking, dual control of assets, double signatures"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 26 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines there is an appropriate balance in skills and resources of the back office, control functions and operational management relative to business origination units, and that back office and control staff have sufficient expertise and authority (and, for control functions where appropriate, sufficient board access) to be an effective check and balance on business origination. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "appropriate balance in the skills and resources of the back office, control functions and operational management"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 26 essential criterion 3: the supervisor determines that banks have an adequately staffed, permanent and independent compliance function assisting senior management in managing compliance risks, with suitably trained and experienced staff and sufficient authority, and that the board oversees management of the compliance function. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an adequately staffed, permanent and independent compliance function"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 26 essential criterion 4: the supervisor determines that banks have an independent, permanent and effective internal audit function (including outsourced or co-sourced) charged with assessing whether policies, processes and internal controls (including risk management, compliance and corporate governance processes) are effective, appropriate and sufficient for the bank's business, and ensuring policies and processes are complied with. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an independent, permanent and effective internal audit function"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 26 essential criterion 5: the supervisor determines that internal audit has sufficient resources and suitably trained staff; appropriate independence and accountability to the board or an audit committee, with status ensuring senior management acts on recommendations; timely information on material changes to risk management strategy, policies or processes; ability to communicate with any staff member and full access to records, files and data of the bank and affiliates; a methodology identifying material risks; a regularly reviewed risk-based audit plan; and authority to assess any outsourced functions. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has the authority to assess any outsourced functions"</sub>

## Principle 27 - Financial reporting and external audit

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 27 states the supervisor determines that banks and banking groups maintain adequate and reliable records, prepare financial statements under widely accepted international accounting policies, annually publish information fairly reflecting financial condition and performance bearing an independent external auditor's opinion, and that banks and parent companies have adequate governance and oversight of the external audit function. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "annually publish information that fairly reflects their financial condition and performance"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 27 criteria 1 and 2: the supervisor holds the board and management responsible for ensuring financial statements are prepared under internationally accepted accounting policies, supported by recordkeeping systems producing adequate and reliable data, and that annual public financial statements bear an independent external auditor's opinion from an audit under internationally accepted auditing standards; footnote 81 says the responsible supervisor need not be limited to the banking supervisor and may include securities and market supervisors. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "holds the bank's board and management responsible for ensuring that financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting policies"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 27 criterion 3: the supervisor determines that banks use valuation practices consistent with widely accepted international accounting standards, that fair value estimation frameworks and processes are independently verified and validated, and that banks document significant differences between valuations used for financial reporting and for regulatory purposes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "document any significant differences between the valuations used for financial reporting purposes and for regulatory purposes"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 27 criterion 4: laws, regulations or the supervisor set the scope of external audits and the standards to be followed, aligned with internationally accepted standards and requiring a risk- and materiality-based approach in planning and performing the audit. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require the use of a risk- and materiality-based approach in planning and performing the external audit"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 27 criteria 6 to 8: the supervisor has power to reject and rescind the appointment of an external auditor deemed to have inadequate expertise or independence or who does not adhere to professional standards; determines that banks rotate external auditors (the firm or individuals) from time to time; and meets periodically with external audit firms to discuss issues of common interest relating to bank operations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor has the power to reject and rescind the appointment of an external auditor"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 27 criterion 9: the supervisor requires the external auditor, directly or through the bank, to report matters of material significance (for example failure to comply with licensing criteria or breaches of banking or other laws, significant deficiencies and control weaknesses in financial reporting, or other matters likely to be of material significance to safety and soundness), and laws or regulations provide that auditors reporting in good faith cannot be held liable for breach of confidentiality duty. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "auditors who make any such reports in good faith cannot be held liable for breach of the duty of confidentiality"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Principle 27 additional criterion: the supervisor has power to access external auditors' working papers, where necessary. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The supervisor has the power to access external auditors' working papers, where necessary."</sub>

## Principle 28 - Disclosure and transparency

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 28 states the supervisor determines that banks and banking groups regularly publish easily accessible information, on a consolidated and where appropriate solo basis, that fairly reflects financial condition, performance, risk exposures, risk management strategies and corporate governance policies and processes (including compensation); at least for internationally active banks, disclosure requirements are not less stringent than applicable Basel standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regularly publish information on a consolidated and, where appropriate, solo basis that is easily accessible"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 28 criterion 1: laws, regulations or the supervisor require periodic public disclosures on a consolidated and where appropriate solo basis that adequately reflect true financial condition and performance and adhere to standards promoting comparability, relevance, reliability and timeliness; footnote 83 says the requirement may be found in accounting, stock exchange listing or similar rules instead of or in addition to supervisory directives. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adhere to standards promoting comparability, relevance, reliability and timeliness of the information disclosed"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 28 criterion 2: the supervisor determines that required disclosures include qualitative and quantitative information on financial performance and position, risk management strategies and practices, risk exposures, aggregate exposures to related parties, transactions with related parties, accounting policies, business models, management, governance (including major share ownership and voting rights) and compensation practices, with scope and detail commensurate with risk profile and systemic importance and, for internationally active banks, not less stringent than Basel standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "aggregate exposures to related parties, transactions with related parties, accounting policies, business models, management, governance"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 28 criteria 3 and 4: laws, regulations or the supervisor require banks to disclose all material entities in the group structure, and the supervisor or another authority effectively reviews and enforces compliance with disclosure standards. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "require banks to disclose all material entities in the group structure"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 28 criterion 5: the supervisor or other relevant authorities regularly publish aggregate information on the banking system, including aggregate balance sheet indicators and statistical parameters reflecting principal aspects of banks' operations (balance sheet structure, capital ratios, income earning capacity and risk profiles), to facilitate public understanding and market discipline. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to facilitate public understanding of the banking system and the exercise of market discipline"</sub>

## Principle 29 - Abuse of financial services

- **expected / expectation**: Principle 29 states the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies and processes, including robust and risk-based customer due diligence (CDD) rules and effective compliance functions, to promote high ethical and professional standards in the financial sector and prevent the bank being used, intentionally or unintentionally, for criminal activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including robust and risk-based 85 customer due diligence (CDD) rules and effective compliance functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 85 to Principle 29 says a risk-based approach enables competent authorities and banks to ensure measures to prevent or mitigate money laundering and terrorist and proliferation financing are commensurate with identified risks; footnote 86 says that in some jurisdictions other authorities, such as a financial intelligence unit, may have primary responsibility for assessing compliance on criminal activities in banks, so 'the supervisor' might refer to them (particularly in essential criteria 7, 8 and 10), with the banking supervisor cooperating with them. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Committee is aware that, in some jurisdictions, other authorities, such as a financial intelligence unit"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 1: laws or regulations establish the duties, responsibilities and powers of the supervisor related to supervising banks' internal controls and enforcing compliance with relevant laws and regulations regarding criminal activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establish the duties, responsibilities and powers of the supervisor related to the supervision of banks' internal controls"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 2: the supervisor determines that banks have adequate policies and processes promoting high ethical and professional standards and preventing the bank being used intentionally or unintentionally for criminal activities, including monitoring, detection and prevention of criminal activity and reporting of suspected activities to the appropriate authorities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and reporting of such suspected activities to the appropriate authorities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 4: if the supervisor becomes aware of additional suspicious transactions it informs the financial intelligence unit and, if applicable, other designated authorities, and directly or indirectly shares information on suspected or actual criminal activities with relevant authorities in a timely manner. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it informs the financial intelligence unit and, if applicable, other designated authorities of such transactions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 5: the supervisor determines that banks have well-documented CDD policies and processes communicated to all relevant staff and integrated into overall risk management, with steps to identify, assess, monitor, manage and mitigate money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risks with respect to customers, countries and regions, and products, services, transactions and delivery channels, on an ongoing basis. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "well documented and communicated to all relevant staff"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 5 lists the essential elements of the group-wide CDD management programme: a customer acceptance policy identifying business relationships the bank will not accept (or will terminate) based on identified risks; an ongoing customer identification, verification and due diligence programme including verification of beneficial ownership, understanding the purpose and nature of the relationship and risk-based reviews keeping CDD information updated and relevant. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a customer acceptance policy that identifies business relationships that the bank will not accept"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 5 further lists: policies and processes to monitor transactions on an ongoing basis and identify unusual or potentially suspicious transactions and individuals or entities subject to United Nations sanctions related to terrorism and proliferation financing; enhanced due diligence on high-risk accounts (for example escalation to senior management of decisions to enter or maintain such relationships, including when an existing relationship becomes high-risk); and enhanced due diligence on politically exposed persons (including family members and close associates) including escalation to senior management of decisions to enter relationships. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enhanced due diligence on politically exposed persons (including their family members and close associates)"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 5 also lists clear rules on what records must be kept on CDD and individual transactions and their retention period, with a retention period of at least five years. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such records have at least a five-year retention period."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 6: the supervisor determines that banks have specific policies and processes on correspondent banking and similar relationships, in addition to normal due diligence, including gathering sufficient information about respondent banks (nature of business and customer base, reputation, how supervised, and whether subject to money laundering, terrorism financing or proliferation financing investigations or regulatory actions); prohibitions on establishing or continuing relationships with banks lacking adequate controls over criminal activity risk, not effectively supervised, or considered shell banks; and senior management approval for new correspondent relationships. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "prohibitions on establishing or continuing correspondent banking relationships with those banks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 8: the supervisor has adequate powers to take action against a bank that does not comply with relevant laws and regulations regarding criminal activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "adequate powers to take action against a bank that does not comply with relevant laws and regulations"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 9: the supervisor determines that banks have requirements for internal audit and/or external experts to independently evaluate risk management policies, processes and controls (with supervisory access to their reports); policies to designate a management-level compliance officer for the financial crimes compliance programme and a dedicated officer to whom potential abuses including suspicious transactions are reported; a compliance function with adequate powers, reporting independence, staff and resources. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designate a compliance officer at the bank's management level to manage the financial crimes compliance programme"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 9 also requires banks to have adequate screening policies to ensure high ethical and professional standards when hiring staff or entering agency or outsourcing relationships, ongoing staff training including on CDD and methods to monitor and detect criminal and suspicious activities, and policies to report criminal activities by staff to competent authorities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ongoing training programmes for their staff, including on CDD and methods to monitor and detect criminal and suspicious activities"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 10: the supervisor determines that banks have and follow clear policies for staff to report issues related to abuse of financial services to local management and/or the relevant dedicated officer, and have and use adequate management information systems giving boards, management and dedicated officers timely and appropriate information on such activities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "clear policies and processes for staff to report any issues related to the abuse of the banks' financial services"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 11: laws provide that a bank staff member who reports suspicious activity in good faith, internally or directly to the relevant authority, cannot be held liable. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who reports suspicious activity in good faith either internally or directly to the relevant authority"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criteria 12 and 13: the supervisor, directly or indirectly, cooperates with relevant domestic and foreign financial sector authorities or exchanges information on suspected or actual criminal activities in banks where for supervisory purposes; and unless another authority is responsible, the supervisor has in-house resources with specialist expertise to address criminal activities detected in banks and regularly provides banks with information on money laundering, terrorism financing and proliferation financing risks. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has in-house resources with specialist expertise for addressing criminal activities detected in banks"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 14: the supervisor determines that banks have group-wide programmes to address money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing, including policies and procedures for sharing information within the group for these purposes. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "group-wide programmes to address money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing"</sub>

## Background to the Basel Framework

- **descriptive / context**: The Basel Framework is a consolidated version of the full set of BCBS standards, which BCBS members have agreed to fully implement and apply to internationally active banks in their jurisdictions; it comprises 14 standards divided into chapters, many with multiple versions (for example one applicable now and one applicable after Basel III reforms are implemented). (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The framework comprises the 14 standards listed below."</sub>

## Principle 25 - Operational risk and operational resilience

- **expected / requirement**: Principle 25 essential criterion 6: the supervisor determines that banks have resilient ICT that is covered by protection, detection, response and recovery processes which are regularly tested, which build in appropriate situational awareness of vulnerabilities, and which pass on relevant, timely information for risk management and decision-making, so as to fully support delivery of the bank's critical operations. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "incorporate appropriate situational awareness of vulnerabilities and convey relevant timely information for risk management and decision-making processes"</sub>

## Principle 29 - Abuse of financial services

- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 3: besides reporting to the financial intelligence unit or other designated authorities, banks also report suspicious activities and fraud incidents to the banking supervisor where these are material to the bank's safety, soundness or reputation. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks report suspicious activities and incidents of fraud to the banking supervisor"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 87 to Principle 29 essential criterion 3 states that, in line with international standards, banks are to report suspicious activities involving potential money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing to the relevant national centre, which serves as the financial intelligence unit and is set up either as an independent governmental authority or as a department within an existing authority or authorities. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks are to report suspicious activities involving cases of potential money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Principle 29 essential criterion 7: the supervisor determines that banks have adequate controls and systems for preventing, identifying and reporting potential abuses of financial services, covering money laundering, terrorism financing and proliferation financing. (p. 1)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sufficient controls and systems to prevent, identify and report potential abuses of financial services"</sub>
