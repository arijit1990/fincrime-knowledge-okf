- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: As of 2018 onward, financers increasingly used social media, mobile applications and instant messaging services. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As of 2018 onward, this includes increased use of social media, mobile applications, and instant messaging services."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Mobile money gained significance for financers and groups, especially where SIM card registration is not controlled and where mobile money operators are not subject to AML/CFT regulation or are unaware of terrorism-financing risks. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mobile money also gained increased significance for financers and groups, especially in jurisdictions where registration of SIM cards is not controlled"</sub>

- **descriptive / case example**: Multiple pro-Da'esh groups using cryptocurrency, including to recruit fighters to join ISIL-K in Afghanistan, were identified in Tajikistan. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Multiple pro-Da’esh groups using cryptocurrency, including to recruit fighters to join ISIL-K in Afghanistan, were identified in Tajikistan."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: One fundraising campaign, active for over a year, controlled an address that received about 2 million USDT on Tron in 2022. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One such fundraising campaign, which has been active for more than a year, controlled an address that received approximately 2 million USDT on Tron in 2022."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: TRM Labs traced the campaign's funds on the blockchain and notified the exchange the group used to cash out; the exchange, using its KYC controls, identified the account operator and alerted local authorities, who disrupted the activities. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Aided by its knowyour-customer (KYC) controls, the exchange identified the person operating the account and alerted the local authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: On 22 June 2023 Turkish authorities arrested a senior Da'esh fundraiser allegedly behind the campaign. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "On 22 June 2023, Turkish authorities arrested a senior Da’esh fundraiser who was allegedly behind the fundraising campaign."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Expenditure and funding purpose shifted from travel, living, medical, weapons and combat equipment costs toward sustaining individuals moving between conflict zones or financing their return home. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "from covering travel costs, living expenses in conflict zones, medical expenses and purchasing weapons or other equipment used in combat, towards sustaining individuals"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: There is a noticeable trend of larger sums sent to returning FTFs, often used for handover to terrorist groups before departure or to pay traffickers and smugglers. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is also a noticeable trend of larger sums being sent to returning FTFs, often used for handover to terrorist groups before departure"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Research indicates funds are now frequently used to establish new networks in conflict zones, including by bribing local officials or community leaders and integrating into new cells. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Research indicates that funds are now frequently used to establish new networks in different conflict zones"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Investment in training and recruitment in new locations to attract members has become a priority, and there is enhanced focus on technology, including secure communications devices, software and cybersecurity. (p. 11)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "covering costs related to maintaining ideological connections (such as funding small-scale propaganda efforts or ideological materials;"</sub>

## ONLINE FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGNS TO IMPROVE THE DETAINEES' CONDITIONS IN CAMPS AND/OR TO SECURE THEIR RELEASE

- **descriptive / case example**: Online fundraising campaigns on Telegram, WhatsApp and Facebook were launched after the March 2019 defeat of Da'esh in north-east Syria to support family members in camps, stating funds improve detainees' conditions or secure release. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "online fundraising campaigns on messaging and social media platforms, including Telegram, WhatsApp and Facebook, were launched in support of family members"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Campaigns typically avoid overt support for Da'esh to evade detection and suspension, but their content reveals ideology through religious terminology, exclusive Da'esh images, or glorification of attacks. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while these campaigns typically avoid displaying overt support for Da’esh to avoid detection and suspension by social media platforms and messaging apps"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Campaigns are often promoted by Da'esh supporters online, mainly in Arabic and English, with some in Russian, French and German indicating a focus on foreign women who moved to Da'esh territory. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The campaigns are often promoted by Da’esh supporters online and are primarily in Arabic and English."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: French financial intelligence identified a typology using offline and online crowdfunding to finance escape of detained female Da'esh members in north-east Syria, with prepaid vouchers convertible into virtual assets. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A recent investigation by the French financial intelligence also identified a typology whereby offline and online crowdfunding mechanisms were used"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Campaigns tracked by TRM Labs operate on multiple blockchains and use shared hosted and unhosted addresses, preferred wallet providers, temporary addresses, privacy coins and cashout mechanisms. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These include the use of shared addresses (hosted and unhosted), preferred wallet providers, temporary addresses, privacy coins, and cashout mechanisms."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most campaign operators appear unsophisticated, but a few show security concern by using temporary addresses and soliciting privacy coins such as Monero. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While most campaign operators appear unsophisticated in their use of cryptocurrency, a few display concern for security when collecting donations."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Bitcoin remains the primary cryptocurrency used, with Ethereum, Monero and Tron also available; donors use unhosted wallets, non-compliant and compliant exchanges and crypto ATMs, some with lower KYC standards often linked to high-risk activity. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While bitcoin remains the primary cryptocurrency used, other blockchains were also available, including Ethereum, Monero, and Tron."</sub>

## Conclusion

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Overall, interlinked trends show a more complex, globally distributed and technologically advanced approach to financing FTF travel and activities, while traditional methods are also maintained. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these interlinked trends paint a picture of a more complex, globally distributed, and technologically advanced approach"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The conclusion states the need to continuously monitor the phenomenon and keep global counter-terrorism efforts up to date. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "confirms the need to continuously monitor the phenomenon and keep global counter-terrorism efforts up to date."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Decentralisation of terrorist groups is likely to lead to decentralised financing, possibly more self-financed cells and lone actors, making detection harder and complicating disruption of larger networks. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such decentralization may also give rise to more lone actors or small cells"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Armed conflict contexts raise challenges for impartial humanitarian action, as counter-terrorist-financing measures may have negative unintended consequences on vital humanitarian aid. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where measures to counter the financing of terrorism may result in negative unintended consequences on the provision of vital humanitarian aid."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Technological advancements will continue to play an important role, with anticipated increased exploitation of emerging financial technologies, and concerns about terrorist use of artificial intelligence and machine learning. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Additional concerns have been flagged regarding the potential of terrorist groups leveraging artificial intelligence and machine learning"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Convergence between terrorist financing and transnational organised crime networks is likely to continue, particularly for financing and FTF-associated travel facilitation purposes. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is likely to continue, particularly for financing and FTF-associated travel facilitation purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The increased convergence in mixed use of old and new fund-raising and moving methods, such as online virtual-asset fundraising with hawala-like systems or cash couriers, essentially combines the challenges of each method. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "essentially combines the challenges and complexities related to each method"</sub>
