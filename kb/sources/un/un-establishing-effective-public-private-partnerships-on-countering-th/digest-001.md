  <sub>anchor: "The present Analytical Brief is based on information collected through CTED's engagement with Member States"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: CTED is committed to better understanding the effectiveness and possible unintended consequences of CFT PPPs and to tracking new technologies that support AML/CFT implementation. (p. 4)[^original]

## Overview

- **descriptive / context**: The private sector, including financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, plays a vital role in CFT; reporting entities have a legal duty to send suspicious activity information to FIUs and/or law enforcement. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with a legal duty to transmit information on suspicious activities to financial intelligence units (FIUs) and/or law"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Information received from reporting entities is analysed by authorities to proactively prevent, detect and disrupt terrorism-financing offences and to understand current and emerging threats and trends. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This information is then analysed and used by authorities to proactively prevent, detect, and disrupt terrorism-financing offences."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: While large banks have traditionally been the main reporters, fintech, Internet and social networking services and sectors such as natural resources, mining, pharmaceuticals, real estate and construction have become an effective but often underestimated source for detecting terrorist fund movements; they may not be reporting entities in all Member States. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have also become an effective, but often underestimated, source for detecting the movements of funds belonging or destined to terrorists"</sub>

## Background and methodology

- **descriptive / method**: For the brief, CTED consulted experts from States with PPPs, including France's TRACFIN, Germany's Federal Police Office counter-terrorism division with the Max Planck Institute, a Dutch Terrorism Financing Taskforce under the Financial Expertise Centre, the Malaysia Financial Intelligence Network and Western Union (South Africa). - **recommended / expectation**: The Security Council and the Counter-Terrorism Committee encourage national authorities to establish partnerships with the private sector, including financial institutions, fintech and Internet and social media companies, covering reporting and disclosure requirements, use of private-sector financial information, and sharing of TF trends. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both the Security Council and the Counter-Terrorism Committee encourage national authorities to establish partnerships with the private sector"</sub>

## Overview

- **descriptive / context**: Several FATF recommendations and Immediate Outcomes treat it as crucial that information on financial activity with possible links to crime and terrorism is shared in a timely and effective manner between and with public and private sectors. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is crucial that information concerning financial activity with possible links to crime and terrorism be shared in a timely and effective manner"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FATF guidance indicates that an effective two-way public-private relationship can be achieved if there are appropriate mechanisms for law enforcement to share strategic, operational, tactical and targeted information with the private sector. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an effective two-way relationship between the public and private sectors can be achieved if there are appropriate mechanisms"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Fundraising platforms, social media companies and payment service providers hold information useful for CFT, such as community engagement, user knowledge and network knowledge. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some fundraising platform companies shared their insights on community engagement, which is critical for understanding clients and their networks"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Researchers and private sector partners call for coherent regulation of financial institutions, crowdfunding sites and social media; applying KYC and CDD requirements to fundraisers on crowdfunding sites and social media could be advisable. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to which it could be advisable to apply know-your-client (KYC) and client due diligence (CDD) requirements in relation to fundraisers"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Non-profit organizations, crowdfunding intermediaries, social media and messaging apps say they face challenges preventing and detecting terrorism-financing abuse of their services; investigators and prosecutors face similar challenges due to complexity and pseudonymity of transactions. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "face challenges in preventing and detecting the abuse of their services for terrorism financing"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Terrorism financing can involve low-value transactions below monitoring or reporting thresholds and means to avoid detection and maintain anonymity. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which can involve low-value transactions that fall below monitoring or reporting thresholds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The private sector needs more guidance from FIUs, law enforcement and other authorities on terrorism-financing techniques, trends and red flag indicators; PPPs can help it mitigate high-risk activities. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the private sector to receive more guidance from FIUs, law enforcement, and other authorities regarding terrorism-financing techniques"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Governments tend to expect the private sector to self-police, but adequate frameworks for this are often missing; the private sector may over-comply, causing de-risking, including de-platforming, with little public sector reaction. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While governments tend to expect the private sector to self-police, adequate frameworks for doing so are often missing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Security Council called for full use of new and emerging financial and regulatory technologies to support financial inclusion and effective AML/CFT implementation; technology used responsibly and proportionally can help manage risks closer to real time. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Security Council called for full use of new and emerging financial and regulatory technologies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A multi-stakeholder approach involving public authorities, private sector, civil society and academia is key at all stages of CFT measures, from risk assessment to assessing effectiveness and unintended impacts. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A multi-stakeholder approach, i.e., one that involves a range of relevant public authorities, the private sector, civil society and academia, is key"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: CTED's 2022 thematic summary assessment found that most evaluated States lacked robust PPPs to share information, understand trends, understand the terrorism-organized crime nexus, build expertise and strengthen financial sector integrity. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most of the States evaluated during the reporting period lacked robust PPPs to share information"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: De-risking and de-platforming bring potential for discrimination, marginalization and human rights breaches, and miss chances to collect information on suspects, potentially pushing them to less accessible platforms such as private chats. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "potential for marginalization and breaches of human rights, such practices represent missed opportunities"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The brief underscores that States should allocate sufficient resources to public and private stakeholders and strengthen collaboration frameworks among competent authorities (FIUs, law enforcement, customs) and the private sector, while fully respecting international law. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The current Analytical Brief underscores the need for States to allocate sufficient resources to both public and private sector stakeholders"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Collaboration frameworks should include clear provisions on what information can and cannot be shared, with which stakeholders and in which circumstances, plus oversight and accountability mechanisms to protect privacy and data protection rights. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such frameworks should include clear provisions on what information can and cannot be shared and under which circumstances"</sub>

## Frameworks for public-private partnerships

- **descriptive / context**: Some Member States see PPPs as a way for private institutions, which often cannot share essential information with each other, to warn their sector about how terrorist financers exploit products and services. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PPPs are a way for private sector institutions, which are often unable to share essential information with each other"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Properly established PPPs add structured, trusted cooperation with two-way exchange, including early warnings in real time, so that timeliness in CFT information-sharing is served. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "properly established PPPs bring an added layer of structured and trusted cooperation"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The private sector holds data and analytics technology while law enforcement holds threat expertise; responsible use of blockchain and artificial intelligence can advance analysis, map networks and track suspicious activity. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Responsible use of technology and data, including blockchain and artificial intelligence, can advance operational and tactical analysis"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: There is asymmetry and misalignment of goals: private entities focus on compliance and avoiding reputational risk, so they limit sharing to what the law strictly requires, while law enforcement is sensitive on terrorism matters and sometimes legally barred from sharing. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the private sector will tend to limit information-sharing with the public sector to what is strictly required by law"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: PPPs can be instruments for proactive sharing of relevant information enabling early identification of threats, and may also be mechanisms to address information exchange requiring immediate and urgent action. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They may also be mechanisms to address information exchange that requires immediate and urgent action"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because public sector information is classified, the private sector is often deprived of information that could help it make informed decisions on what to share proactively. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the private sector is often ‘’deprived’’ of information that could assist it in making informed decisions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: As noted by FATF, partnerships share information among law enforcement, FIUs, vetted private participants and sometimes international partners; private data-mining and analysis with reciprocal exchange fills intelligence gaps. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information is shared across law enforcement, FIUs, and vetted participants from the private sector"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Private sector initiatives may include intra-group exchange, interbank forums sharing crime trends and typologies with law enforcement and supervisors support; safe harbours can give legal protection for information-sharing between financial institutions for AML/CFT purposes. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In such cases, safe harbours can carve out specific legal protections to enable information-sharing between financial institutions"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: CTED assessments indicate Member States face challenges institutionalizing partnerships with the private sector on financial information sharing. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CTED assessments indicate that Member States face challenges in institutionalizing partnerships"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Multi-stakeholder PPPs with real-time information exchange let authorities design more effective investigation strategies and track and map terrorist networks more proactively, especially where sectors are subject to different regulations. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "multi-stakeholder PPPs allow for the design of more effective and comprehensive investigation strategies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: A crowdfunding or social media campaign can involve formal financial institutions, virtual asset service providers and non-profit beneficiaries, each under different regulation and controls, and only some are obliged to report; authorities may lack the full picture. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Each of these stakeholders operate under different regulatory frameworks, implement different checks and controls"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Establishing a clear legal basis for public-private information sharing is essential to allow exchanges and to define the criteria, purposes and entities for sharing. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Establishing a clear legal basis for the sharing of information between public and private sectors is essential"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: PPPs for financial information sharing are very diverse, most have a broader scope than CFT, and there is no single specific model underlying the term PPPs. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is no single specific model underlying the term “PPPs”"</sub>

## Strategic information sharing

- **descriptive / definition**: Some PPPs are strategic: they co-develop typologies and knowledge products on threats and risk indicators, typically without confidential or identifying information about suspects. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "primarily serve to co-develop typologies and knowledge products covering threats and relevant risk indicators"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In France, TRACFIN issues 'calls for vigilance' to reporting entities to support risk-based approaches; it issued eleven such calls relating to terrorist financing in 2019. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In 2019, TRACFIN issued eleven “calls for vigilance” relating to terrorist financing"</sub>

## Frameworks for public-private partnerships

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Frameworks and procedures for feedback mechanisms between law enforcement, FIUs and reporting entities can help improve report quality and financial intelligence products and support trends monitoring and strategic analysis. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "frameworks and procedures for feedback mechanisms between law enforcement, financial intelligence units and reporting entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Developed data-sharing frameworks and protocols facilitate information exchange and safeguard human rights including privacy and data protection, and defining oversight mechanisms ensures PPPs adhere to data protection obligations. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "developed data-sharing frameworks and protocols not only facilitate information exchange"</sub>

## Strategic information sharing

- **descriptive / case example**: Canada's FIU FINTRAC produces strategic intelligence on terrorism financing threats for sectors, suspicious transaction patterns linked to violent extremism and indicators of terrorist financing related to foreign terrorist fighters. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FINTRAC’s strategic intelligence products provide analytical perspectives on the nature"</sub>

## Operational partnerships

- **descriptive / definition**: Operational PPPs share more sensitive information on incidents, actors and financial operations for financial intelligence and investigations, and the private sector becomes a contributor to investigative efforts. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where the private sector not only ensures compliance with financial crime regulation but becomes a contributor to investigative efforts"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Operational PPPs also help the private sector improve customer due diligence effectiveness and identify higher-risk customers. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it assists the private sector in improving the effectiveness of customer due diligence"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Operational PPPs target more vulnerable and key institutions or sectors rather than many entities; examples include the UK Joint Money Laundering Intelligence Taskforce, which produced successful investigations, and the US FinCEN. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The most well-known examples are the Joint Moneylaundering Intelligence Taskforce of the United Kingdom"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Netherlands' Financial Intelligence Unit works with banks collaboratively on reports and STRs, and the Malaysia Financial Intelligence Network shares case information for further review by reporting institutions. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Kingdom of the Netherlands’ Financial Intelligence Unit has developed a rapprochement with banks"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Malaysia partnership with tactical sharing of specific cases led to the arrest, prosecution and deportation of 22 individuals suspected of terrorism and proliferation financing. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This has led to the arrest, prosecution and deportation of 22 individuals"</sub>

## Modality of partnerships

- **descriptive / case example**: Some PPPs use dedicated restricted workspaces for real-time collaboration on cases, as in the Latvia Cooperation Coordination Group; others prioritize communication platforms, as in the Singapore AML/CFT Industry Partnership. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some PPPs have established dedicated workspaces with access restricted to nominated personnel"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most PPPs are led by competent authorities, while some have oversight where government and industry representatives jointly oversee operations and set the agenda; the Australian Fintel Alliance gave intelligence to the Federal Police leading to a disrupted terrorist attack. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as observed in the Australian Fintel Alliance, which successfully provided intelligence to the Federal Police"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: TRACFIN has bilateral information exchange protocols with selected private entities and in 2019 launched a special committee for combating terrorism financing to formalize exchange with obligated entities. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the French FIU (TRACFIN) has established bilateral information exchange protocols with selected private sector entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Europol's Financial Intelligence Public-Private Partnership, established in 2017, is a supranational PPP with a permanent structure; other supranational projects are project-oriented. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial Intelligence Public-Private Partnership, established in 2017"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The BeCaNet initiative is a two-year effort led by the German Federal Criminal Police with France, Spain and the United States to build public-private networks against terrorism financing. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The BeCaNet initiative (best practice, capacity building and networking initiative among public and private actors"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Eurasian Group has held consultative forums since 2006 to improve public-private engagement, exchange guidance and feedback, and address emerging issues such as new payment methods. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a series of consultative forums held by the Eurasian Group on Combating Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism since 2006"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Alliance for Financial Inclusion under ESAAMLG ran a 2013 public and private sector survey on financial integrity and inclusion, assessing communication with the private sector and the impact of de-risking. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducted a Public and Private Sector Surveys Report on Financial Integrity and Financial Inclusion Frameworks"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2020 GIABA report on counter terrorist financing in West Africa identified gaps in private sector suspicious activity reporting and made a series of recommendations. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the 2020 report on the assessment of counter terrorist financing activities of the Intergovernmental Action Group"</sub>

## Addressing gaps and challenges identified in PPP frameworks

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A common challenge to effective AML/CFT PPPs is inadequate investment in material, financial and human resources in both private sector and government authorities, including FIUs. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inadequate investments in the material, financial and human resources both in private sector and governmental authorities"</sub>

## Lack of resources and capacity

- **expected / recommendation**: Governments should invest more in AML/CFT-related resources and promote effective coordination of targeted capacity-building to build a pool of national expertise. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Governments should invest more in AML/CFT-related resources and promote effective coordination of targeted capacitybuilding"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Good practice includes reorganizing relevant services to optimize human resources and track a maximum number of cases involving State interests. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the reorganization of relevant services to optimize human resources and track down a maximum number of cases"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: Public and private sectors could capitalize on the cost-benefit of PPPs by designating dedicated focal points within their specialized units to coordinate on specific areas, taking into account the effectiveness of information flow through PPPs. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designating dedicated focal points within their specialized units to coordinate on specific areas"</sub>
