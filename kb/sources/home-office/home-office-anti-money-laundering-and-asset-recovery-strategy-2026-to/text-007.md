##### Annex B – Geographic Scope

The UK’s response to money laundering is led jointly by the Home Office and HM Treasury, with HMT responsible for the prevention of money laundering and the Home Office response for the criminal justice response including asset recovery. These departments coordinate activity across Government and with operational partners. Key roles are also held by the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, and other departments and agencies.

The UK’s AML/CTF supervisory regime operates across the whole of the UK, with the geographic scope of individual supervisors dictated by their specific remit rather than devolved governance. The FCA and HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) both exercise UK-wide jurisdiction, with the FCA overseeing the financial services sector and HMRC acting as the default supervisor for various non-financial businesses and professions. In contrast, the Gambling Commission regulates operators providing services to customers across Great Britain, reflecting distinct regulatory frameworks in Northern Ireland.

[pp. 99-101]

The legal and accountancy sectors are currently supervised by Professional Body Supervisors (PBSs) under the oversight of The Office for Professional Body Anti-Money Laundering Supervision, with accountants who aren’t members of a professional bodies supervised by HMRC. While some PBSs operate UK-wide, others have jurisdictions limited to specific nations to align with the UK’s separate legal systems. However, as set out in this strategy, the UK is consolidating professional services supervision and, when complete, this will see the FCA assume the role of the sole statutory supervisor for the legal, accountancy and trust or company service provider sectors.

The National Economic Crime Centre, hosted within the NCA, leads the cross-system operational response to economic crime, including money laundering. It brings together law enforcement, prosecutors, government departments, regulators and the private sector to coordinate a whole-system response across the 4Ps: Pursue, Prevent, Protect and Prepare. The National Police Chiefs’ Council, based in the City of London Police, coordinates the policing response to money laundering and asset recovery.

This strategy applies across the whole of the UK. However, some aspects of the response fall within devolved competence, and there are differences in the powers and responsibilities of the UK Government and the devolved administrations. In particular, policing and criminal justice are devolved matters in Scotland and Northern Ireland.

In Scotland, the Scottish Government is responsible for criminal justice policy. The Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service, operating under the authority of the Lord Advocate, is the sole prosecuting authority and is responsible for the investigation and prosecution of crime. This includes both reserved matters, such as money laundering, and devolved offences, such as fraud and bribery. Police Scotland is the single police service and works closely with the NCA, HMRC, the FCA and other partners. The Scottish Crime Campus, established in 2015, provides a multi-agency centre for tackling serious and organised crime, including economic crime.

In Northern Ireland, the Department of Justice is responsible for devolved policing and justice functions, including criminal justice policy. The Police Service of Northern Ireland is the single police service and leads the operational response to serious and organised crime, working closely with the NCA, HMRC and other agencies. The Public Prosecution Service for Northern Ireland is the prosecuting authority.

In Wales, policing and justice are not devolved, and responsibility remains with the UK Government.
