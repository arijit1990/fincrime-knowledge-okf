## 4.2.3 Capacity and Resources—Registering Beneficial Ownership

- **expected / expectation**: Expanding registry information to include beneficial ownership is only workable if steps are taken to ensure that the information is accurate. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we therefore believe it will be possible to expand registry information to include information on benefi cial ownership only if steps are taken ensure that accuracy"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Current registries are archival and passive: information supplied by applicants is logged rather than verified. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In current practice, registries are archival and passive in nature. Information supplied by applicants is logged, not verifi ed."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The registry should verify beneficial ownership information, either for every application or on a risk-sensitive basis. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the registry should verify that information (either for every application or on a risk-sensitive basis)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 40 registries surveyed, assuming equal resource allocation, considered inadequate resources a major impediment to such an expansion. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Overall, they considered inade- quate resources to be a major impediment."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The authors do not believe most registries can currently verify information supplied by a malevolent legal entity or someone acting on its behalf. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we do not believe that most registries are in a position to be able to verify information supplied by a malevolent legal entity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Resource challenges vary by jurisdiction according to economy size, development level and each registry's regulatory functions. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "challenges to resource allo- cation vary among jurisdictions, depending on the size of the economy"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Agencies with broader functions than business registration, such as a securities regulator, may have sufficient resources to improve operations and meet additional mandates. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th is category might include agencies that have a broader range of functions than just business registration"</sub>

- **descriptive / context**: Apart from such exceptions, registries required to obtain beneficial ownership information will need more government funding to verify it. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "then they will need more government funding to be able to verify the information supplied to them"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Sanctions for supplying inaccurate information need to be routine, since sanction power is credible only if actually used. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the imposition of sanctions on those who have sup- plied inaccurate information needs to be routine"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Identifying the beneficial owner in a complicated corporate structure is demanding and requires good knowledge of corporate law, not a routine administrative task. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "discovering the identity of the benefi cial owner in a complicated corporate structure is by no means a routine administrative procedure"</sub>

## BOX 4.1 The Jersey Model

- **expected / expectation**: Condition 1: the registry should be active and alert, verifying supplied beneficial ownership information or checking it for accuracy, which can be risk-based. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The registry is active and alert, that is, it veriﬁ es the information supplied to it, or checks it for accuracy (can be based on risk)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Jersey, beneficial ownership information at application is checked against an external database and an internal regulatory database, and applicants are often asked for additional information. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is checked against an external database (see World-Check, http://www. world-check.com/) and an internal regulatory database"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Jersey publishes a list of 'sensitive' activities and requires more information at incorporation where a company intends to conduct them; this policy is under review and may be extended. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They make it clear that, in cases in which a company intends to be conducting any of these activities, more information must be provided"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Condition 2: the registry should enforce compliance with registration requirements and with updating requirements when information changes. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The registry enforces compliance with legal registration require- ments and with updating requirements when information changes"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Jersey, non-compliant trust companies are identified through dialogue between the Registry and the Trust Company Business division, and only regulated trust companies and Jersey-resident individuals may file incorporation applications. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Only trust companies regulated by the Jersey Financial Services Commission and Jersey-resident individuals are able to ﬁ le applications"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Condition 3: registry staff should be sufficiently expert on beneficial ownership to identify the natural person in a complex structure; if not, a simplified definition may be preferable. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is sufﬁ ciently expert and knowledgeable on the concept of beneﬁ cial ownership and knows how to iden- tify"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In Jersey, applications can be approved only at director level, and a new deputy director post was created to strengthen experience. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Applications for registration can be approved only at the director level, where there is sufﬁ cient experience"</sub>
