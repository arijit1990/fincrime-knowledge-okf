## Part 4. Finding the Beneficial Owner I 89

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A Dominica provider and a UK provider had purely web-based order forms, and nowhere in their ordering process was any need for supporting documentation mentioned, suggesting none was required. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Nowhere, throughout the ordering process on these two providers’ websites, is there any mention of the need to supply supporting documentation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Dominica provider offers only Dominican companies, while the UK provider sells companies from the Seychelles, British Virgin Islands, and England and Wales, and offers a proposed European Private Company redomiciliable to any EU member state. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Although the Dominica provider off ers only Dominican companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Dominica and UK providers were among the cheapest in the sample, offering anonymous shell companies for US$1,200 to US$1,500. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "off ering anonymous shell companies (that is, no benefi cial ownership information held on fi le, nominee shareholders and directors) for US$1,200–$1,500."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The US provider's fees were: Belize companies US$1,500, British Virgin Islands US$1,950, Nevis US$1,850, Panama US$1,950, Seychelles US$1,650, and a Bahamian trust US$1,000. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "customers can form companies domiciled in Belize ($1,500), the British Virgin Islands ($1,950), Nevis ($1,850), Panama ($1,950), and the Seychelles ($1,650), as well as set up a Bahamian trust ($1,000)."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The US provider, largely a one-man operation, testified before a US Senate subcommittee; the 2006 report 'Tax Haven Abuses' says it set up offshore structures for more than 900 clients over six years, mainly US clients, grossing several hundred thousand dollars in 2003 and 2004. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "over the preceding six-year period, this provider had set up off shore structures for more than 900 individual clients"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The provider depended on other parties in offshore jurisdictions to act as trustee, trust protector, and company director. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this provider depended on other parties in various off shore jurisdictions to perform the roles of trustee, trust protector, and company director."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A US-based provider with a secondary office in the Bahamas told a customer that no documentation was needed to form an offshore company or trust, while opening an offshore bank account required ID and a recent bill or statement. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th ere is no documentation needed to form an off shore company or trust."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The provider's website confirms no identity documentation is needed to form companies or trusts, but a standard suite of documentation is required to open a bank account; company prices included Belize ($1,500), BVI ($1,950), Nevis ($1,850), Panama ($1,950), and Seychelles ($1,650), and a Bahamian trust ($1,000). (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In this way, customers can form companies domiciled in Belize ($1,500), the British Virgin Islands ($1,950)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The provider reportedly set up offshore structures for more than 900 individual clients over six years, largely from the United States, and depended on other parties abroad to act as trustee, trust protector and director. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this provider had set up off shore structures for more than 900 individual clients, largely from the United States"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The provider confirmed in 2010 that its company formation due diligence had not changed since 2005: identity documents, a bank reference and proof of address were required for offshore banks but not for companies or trusts. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Although identity documentation, a bank reference, and proof of address were required for all off shore banks"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The provider offered services to anyone except those who volunteered that they were in the pornography business or came from Iran or Cuba. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "except those who volunteered the infor- mation that they were in the pornography business"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: TCSPs from sampled OECD countries conducted customer due diligence to a lesser extent than those in other countries: 47 of 53 providers in other countries conducted proper due diligence versus 12 of 47 in sampled OECD countries. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Whereas 47 out of 53 providers in this latter group conducted proper due diligence, only 12 out of 47 did so"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Positive identification findings were particularly high among TCSPs from jurisdictions the OECD identified as tax havens in 2000 under its Harmful Tax Competition initiative, contrary to the portrayal of these jurisdictions as offering secrecy. ## Part 4. Finding the Beneficial Owner I 91

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The United States was the worst performer: of 27 US-jurisdiction providers giving valid responses, only 3 asked for any identity documentation, while 24 would form companies without any due diligence. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Out of 27 ser- vice providers under U.S. jurisdiction returning a valid response, only 3 said they asked for any form of identity documentation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Although most US providers noted nonresidents would need an employer identification number, the associated forms did not ask for identification; some providers in Wyoming and Nevada offered to use employees' Social Security numbers to avoid the EIN requirement. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some providers in Wyoming and Nevada actually off ered to use their employ- ees' Social Security numbers"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Senator Carl Levin noted in November 2009 that US states form nearly 2 million companies each year, in virtually all cases without obtaining the names of those who control or benefit from them. (p. 110)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "our 50 states are forming nearly 2 million companies each year"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The US forms around 10 times more legal entities each year than all 41 tax haven jurisdictions combined; because little information is collected, it is impossible to tell how many are shells, though US law enforcement indicates the number is high. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "around 10 times more than in all 41 tax haven jurisdic- tions combined"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The authors caution that positive results for some countries may reflect 'the compliance dance', firms paying lip-service to regulation; such checks were beyond the project's scope, though non-compliant TCSPs would have no reason to pretend compliance. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If we had engaged those seemingly compliant TCSPs further, perhaps the window dressing would have quickly become apparent"</sub>
