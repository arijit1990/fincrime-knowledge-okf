## Separating the Beneficial Owner from Formal Control via a Legal Fiction

- **descriptive / context**: Multiple layers of entities and arrangements make the beneficial owner's link to money laundering less apparent to investigators and may let the owner plausibly deny ownership or control if assets are discovered. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th ese layers may also allow the owner to plausibly deny ownership or control of such assets if they are discovered."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Investigations are especially complicated when layers are placed strategically across several jurisdictions, because no single investigating authority has legal compulsory power to obtain evidence from all parties involved. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Investigations are particularly complicated when such layers are placed strategically in multiple jurisdictions, because no investigating authority will have the legal compulsory power to procure evidence from all parties involved."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report discusses two most commonly used strategies for separating the beneficial owner from formal control: legal fiction and the use of surrogates. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th is section dis- cusses the two most commonly used strategies: legal fi ction and the use of surrogates."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In a tiered corporate vehicle structure, chains of legal entities or arrangements are inserted between the individual beneficial owner or controller and the assets of the primary corporate vehicle. (p. 70)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In a tiered corporate vehicle structure, layers or “chains” of legal entities and/or arrange- ments are inserted between the individual benefi cial owner(s) or controller(s) and the assets of the primary corporate vehicle."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Tiered entities let a beneficial owner access financial institutions under different entity names serving the same end and maintain control over the primary corporate vehicle that holds, receives or transfers assets. (p. 70)[^original]
