- **descriptive / context**: People in positions within a company, such as board members, executives and financial officers, can exercise control. (p. 40)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "Creditors, for instance, can exercise control if they have been given the right to block or approve certain signifi cant transactions"</sub>

## A Formal Approach to Beneficial Ownership

## A Substantive Approach to Beneficial Ownership

- **descriptive / definition**: The Wolfsberg Group describes beneficial ownership as the level of ownership in funds that equates in practice with control or entitlement to funds, distinct from mere signature authority or legal title; the focus should be on the person with this ultimate control or entitlement.   <sub>anchor: "Th e term “benefi cial ownership” is conventionally used in anti-money laundering contexts"</sub>

## Banks

  <sub>anchor: "the bank is faced with three less-than-ideal options"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Costs of relying on another institution's due diligence can be reduced if quantitative standards become widespread and institutions use comparable CDD methods and criteria. (p. 44)[^original]

## The Problem of Close Associates

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Corrupt parties often maintain control of corporate vehicles through legally enforceable means that a formal approach would not reveal; this can allow an investigator to construct a path of control from asset to official. (p. 45)[^original]
