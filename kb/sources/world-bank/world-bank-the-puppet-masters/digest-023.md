## 5.3.1 Obstacles Frequently Encountered

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A chief obstacle cited was that beneficial ownership information was often not available because it is not required to be collected and maintained by the registry or service provider, or was required but not collected due to negligence or wilful blindness. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the lack of availability of benefi cial ownership information in the fi rst place because it is not required to be collected and maintained"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Bearer shares, where still permitted, were among the most challenging obstacles investigators found to overcome. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Where bearer shares are still permitted, investigators found them to be one of the most challenging obstacles to overcome"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Corporate directors were more challenging but not necessarily insurmountable; where located in a different jurisdiction, overcoming them depended on access to beneficial ownership information there and on foreign law enforcement assistance. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the ability to overcome this obstacle depended on the availability of and access to the benefi cial ownership information in that jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Intermediaries used to form corporate vehicles range from informal strawmen such as family members or associates to formal nominees, including professional intermediaries who may be innocent agents. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Use of intermediaries to form corporate vehicles can range from informal strawmen, such as family members or close associates"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Intermediaries sometimes hold and disclose beneficial ownership information, but a low-level intermediary may only provide unhelpful information, and even cooperative intermediaries may offer little help in reaching the beneficial owner. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an intermediary can be a low-level associate who can only provide nonuseful information such as a mobile number"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Layering of corporate vehicles and use of multiple jurisdictions were among the most challenging obstacles, with time and resources needed to peel away layers as main factors. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Time and resources needed to peel away the layers of concealment are two main factors"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nominee shareholders and nominee directors were obstacles investigators could overcome because they involve natural persons. (p. 173)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "nominee directors were obstacles that investigators could overcome as they involve natural persons."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Foreign counterpart cooperation may be unavailable on a timely basis or at all, due to lack of legal basis for cooperation or practical barriers such as staff shortages. (p. 174)[^original]

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Lack of harmonised international standards on covered entities under domestic AML regimes lets criminals exploit jurisdictions with lax CDD and recordkeeping or use uncovered professionals. (p. 174)[^original]

## Appendix C. Short Description of Selected Corporate Vehicles

- **descriptive / definition**: A sole proprietorship is the legal recognition of an individual conducting economic activity without formal entity structure; it has no formal registration or filing fees and files taxes as part of personal duties. (p. 177)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the individual has no formal registration requirements or fi ling fees, does not need to create an operating agreement"</sub>

## General Partnerships

- **descriptive / definition**: General partnerships form when more than one person agrees to pursue a business activity; the agreement, express or inferred from actions, determines whether a partnership exists. (p. 178)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th is agreement is usually the determin- ing feature that dictates whether a court will acknowledge the existence of partnership"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Global laws lack uniformity on whether a partnership has legal personality distinct from its partners; a partnership may be a legal person, a legal relationship, or a hybrid with incomplete legal personality. (p. 178)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Global laws lack uniformity dictating what independent legal personal- ity a partnership may have"</sub>

## Appendix C. Short Description of Selected Corporate Vehicles

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Common law countries do not require business partnerships to register or to commit the governing contract to writing, whereas civil law countries generally require both. (p. 178)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Common law countries do not require business partnerships to register with a government entity or court"</sub>

## General Partnerships

- **descriptive / context**: The original French Commercial Code of 1807 created a partnership form with general partners liable to creditors and limited partners liable only to the amount of their investment, but restricted limited partners' control rights. (p. 179)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the original French Commercial Code of 1807 created a new form of partnership that allowed for a general partner"</sub>

## Limited Partnerships

- **descriptive / definition**: Limited partnerships grant limited liability to some partners only, can only be formed through a formal written agreement process, and are mostly useful for silent investment partners. (p. 179)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Limited partnerships (LPs) are partnerships in which limited liability is granted to cer- tain partners and not others"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A limited partner's liability is conditional: taking too active a role can make the limited partner jointly and severally liable with general partners for creditor obligations. (p. 179)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "limited partners who take too active a role in the partnership business can be found to have breached their limited status"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In most jurisdictions limited liability partnerships grant limited liability to all partners; LPs and LLPs have independent legal personality but are subject to registration and supervisory regimes similar to companies. (p. 179)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In most jurisdictions, limited liability partnerships (LLPs) convey limited liability status on all partners."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the Grand Corruption Database Project, limited partnership forms were not found to have been used specifically to achieve opacity of beneficial ownership. (p. 179)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these partnership forms were not found to have been used specifi cally achieve opacity of benefi cial ownership"</sub>

## Companies

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because virtually no country registers trusts, their total number cannot be precisely determined, though they may be as prevalent as companies. (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "because virtually no country in the world regis- ters those, we do not have a precise way to determine their total number"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Companies, originally designed to protect investors and creditors, are now often sought for camouflage; they are the most significantly misused vehicle documented in the study. (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Companies are the most signifi cantly misused vehicle documented within this study"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The OECD's 2001 report dismissed LLCs briefly as a recent spreading phenomenon; the study says the significant presence of LLCs in the grand corruption database now confirms their misuse potential. (p. 180)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the signifi cant presence of LLCs in the database of grand corruption schemes confi rms this potential"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Company law generally separates ownership through shares from control through a board of directors, which sets policy and selects officers who run daily operations. (p. 181)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In general, basic company law separates ownership (through shares) and control (through a board of directors)."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Shares carry votes or not; voting shareholders elect directors for fixed periods typically of one to three years, and key decisions need majority shareholder approval. (p. 181)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Generally, voting shareholders elect directors to serve for a fi xed period, typically between one and three years."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In most jurisdictions companies must keep a share register; pure bearer shares are allowed in some cases, requiring shareholders to approach the company to exercise rights. (p. 181)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In some cases, pure bearer shares are allowed"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Civil law jurisdictions divide companies into public and private types, with public companies subject to securities laws and stock exchange rules including voluntary disclosure of control thresholds. (p. 181)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Civil law jurisdictions typically divide companies into two types"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Jurisdictions typically require a certain minimum number of directors for a company. (p. 181)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions typically require a certain minimum number of directors."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Share registers reveal the registered holder controlling votes but not the ultimate physical person controlling voting shares, since most shares are held by other legal persons or arrangements. (p. 182)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such examination, however, will not identify who the ultimate physical person is who controls the vote of voting shares"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In all jurisdictions company assets belong to the company as a separate legal person, and creditors have first claim, with dividend and winding-up rules varying. (p. 182)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In all jurisdictions, the ownership of company assets belongs to the company as a separate legal person."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Documentation showing that covenants or contracts cede control to a third party is not normally available to shareholders. (p. 182)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such documentation is not normally available to shareholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most company laws require that shares be freely transferable, so shareholders may not form agreements that would deny free transferability. (p. 182)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most company laws require that shares be freely transferable"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Laws also require that certain information, such as the names of board members and officers, be made available to shareholders. (p. 182)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Laws also require that certain information such as the names of board members"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Common law countries do not typically distinguish between public and private companies at the company level; they distinguish based on whether share offerings are made to the general public. (p. 182)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Common law countries do not typically distinguish between public or private at the company level"</sub>

## Limited Liability Company

- **descriptive / definition**: An LLC typically applies partnership-style rules where ownership and control are set by a contract called the operating agreement, with limited liability for members; LLCs must register and are separate legal persons. (p. 183)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Unlike typical common law partnerships, however, LLCs must be registered with a government agency"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An LLC may be member managed or manager managed, and its operating agreement can give members wide latitude to divide ownership and control. (p. 183)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An LLC can be organized as either member managed"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: LLCs have legitimate uses but are also used to extend limited liability to individuals, such as physicians in US tort-plaintiff-friendly jurisdictions shielding assets from judgments. (p. 183)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "physicians in tort-plaintiff -friendly jurisdictions in the United States oft en use LLCs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Delaware is rapidly becoming the most important US state for forming LLCs, with an initial fee of $90, an annual fee of $250, and online registration approved in under 24 hours. (p. 183)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th e initial fee is $90 with a $250 annual fee, and registration can be completed online with approval granted in less than 24 hours"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Delaware requires disclosure of an agent for service of process but not of member or manager names, which may optionally be included; agents need not keep beneficial owner information. (p. 183)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Although Delaware requires the disclosure of an agent for service of process, it does not require the disclosure of member or manager names"</sub>
