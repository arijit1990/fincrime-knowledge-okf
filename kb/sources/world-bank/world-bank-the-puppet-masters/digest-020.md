## 2.2 Methodology

- **descriptive / method**: A multifaceted questionnaire was devised to gain insight from practitioners and was revised through several rounds in consultation with anti-money laundering and regulatory enforcement experts. (p. 141)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It went through several rounds of revision in consultation with anti-money laundering (AML) and regulatory enforcement experts."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Invitations were sent to 11 nations after clearance from each jurisdiction's financial intelligence unit or central bank; two or three financial institutions per jurisdiction were approached, and all invited parties accepted, with 50 compliance personnel from 25 banks participating. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All invited parties accepted the invitation, and 50 compliance personnel from 25 individual banks participated in the exercise."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Interviews followed the questionnaires; face-to-face meetings occurred with participants from 8 of the 11 jurisdictions, and others were by telephone or videoconference, with participants guaranteed confidentiality. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the end, such meetings occurred with participants from 8 of the 11 jurisdictions."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The questionnaire was primarily intended to facilitate a later dialogue between banks' compliance practitioners and the project team rather than only to generate data. (p. 142)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it was primarily intended to facilitate a dialogue, to be held at a later time"</sub>

## 2.3 Summary of Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Participants said the laws of their nations, with one exception, require measures to ascertain the beneficial owner of corporate vehicles in all instances of entering a relationship; in the exceptional nation such measures are only deemed necessary in certain circumstances. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the laws of their nation, with the exception of one jurisdiction, require that measures be taken to ascertain the benefi cial owner"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: All compliance personnel reported that beneficial owner is considered to refer only to natural persons in their jurisdictions, but two participants from the same jurisdiction said their national industry standard allows corporate persons to be beneficial owners, contrary to the FATF definition. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "two participants (from the same jurisdiction) indicated that, contrary to the FATF defi nition, their national industry standard allows for corporate persons"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Beneficial ownership standards generally use a percentage threshold, most typically in the 20 to 25 percent range, of ownership or control rights; all personnel felt their institutions had sufficient guidance, though standards may vary widely among institutions within countries. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most typically in the 20 to 25 percent range"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: International banks are most likely to have broad, self-imposed beneficial ownership definitions, subject to adjustment through a risk-based customer assessment. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "International banks are the most likely to have broad, self-imposed benefi cial ownership defi nitions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Banks rely to a significant extent on client-provided evidence but stress they do not rely on it alone, seeking collaborative and corroborating evidence from multiple sources. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most stress, however, that they do not rely on that alone"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Compliance officers endorsed disclosure forms, provided to the bank or to a government registry; participants said forms must be comprehensible to clients and should be based on a percentage threshold definition (usually 25 percent), and many corporate vehicle customers therefore have no beneficial owner in that technical sense. (p. 149)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they must be comprehensible to the client and furthermore should be based on the percentage threshold defi nition of benefi cial ownership (usually 25 percent)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Company registries are much relied on to cross-check client data and are the primary source of non-client-provided ownership and control information; domestic registries are highly regarded, while foreign registries may be difficult to identify or access. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "company registries are, for all fi nancial institutions, the primary source of non-client-provided ownership and control information"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Multinational banks often have less difficulty with foreign corporate vehicles, relying on a sister bank abroad to forward registry information and on local banker knowledge. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they can rely on a sister bank in a foreign nation to forward the rel- evant information obtained from registry checks"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Trust identification standards are far more rigorous among most banks willing to deal with international or foreign trusts; smaller or domestic banks in civil law jurisdictions usually accept such trusts only as exceptions. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Smaller or primarily domestic banks from civil law jurisdictions indicated that accepting such trusts as clients is typically the exception"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many compliance personnel said beneficial owner is a poor concept for trust clients, since beneficially interested parties differ from trust controllers or power-holders; all banks needed to identify declared settlors, trustees, beneficiaries and power-holders and the source of funds or wealth of settlors, and none raised concerns about economic settlors. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "None mentioned concerns about “economic settlors”"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Requirements to verify documents independently were generally found to be underwhelming and rely mainly on government registries; when these are unavailable, sighted or copied documents of foreign legal persons are usually accepted if notarized, certified true copies. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Requirements to verify documents independently generally were found to be underwhelming and to rely primarily on government registries"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: For most banks, risk-based assessment is regarded as the most efficient way to thwart money laundering, and it also appears to be the key factor in going above and beyond nationally mandated customer identification standards. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it also appears to be the key factor in going above and beyond nationally mandated standards of cus- tomer identifi cation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Risk-based assessment typically involving politically exposed persons, high net-worth persons, at-risk jurisdictions and at-risk industries is widely used, and some larger institutions compare corporate vehicle activity with industry benchmarks or medians. (p. 150)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Atypical transactional activity is also used as a risk indicator, with some larger fi nancial institutions monitoring the business volume"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Many participants complained of a lack of materials to confirm client-provided evidence; banks seldom can be certain they hold conclusive proof of a corporate vehicle customer's beneficial ownership and instead trace unrelated information streams to check the client's account is coherent. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they seldom can be certain that they hold irrefutable, conclusive proof of a (corporate vehicle) customer’s benefi cial ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: When faced with a credentialed corporate vehicle representative, few banks ask whether that person acts for an undisclosed party, usually because credentials make standing apparent; some officers note that 'straw men' may misrepresent themselves. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "few banks inquire whether that person ultimately is acting on behalf of (in the benefi cial interest of) an undisclosed party"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In one jurisdiction national guidance allowed institutions to treat the individual before them as the beneficial owner of the account, provided legal authority is clearly established, regardless of who owns or controls the corporate vehicle. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "just as long as legal authority is clearly established"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Powers of attorney are generally scrutinized thoroughly, must be properly notarized and kept on file; several personnel said their institutions do not accept general, all-purpose or overbroad power-of-attorney holders as signatories on corporate vehicle accounts, except regulated trust and company service providers from acceptable jurisdictions. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "several compliance personnel state that their institutions do not accept general, all-purpose, or overbroad power-of-attorney holders"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Few institutions keep records of refusals to begin customer relationships, and most estimate such refusals as very low in number, attributed to front-end due diligence deterring bad actors and low inherent money laundering risk of their products. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Few institutions keep records of such data, and most contributors supply esti- mates that are in all cases very low in number"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: All financial institution personnel said their institutions are subject to onsite inspections and assessment by mandated supervisory authorities. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All fi nancial institution personnel indicate that their fi nancial institutions are sub- ject to onsite inspections"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Despite several compliance personnel representing banks implicated in major money laundering schemes, only one admits to having been subject to supervisory action; the excerpt cuts off at that point. (p. 151)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Despite the fact that several compliance personnel represent banks that were implicated in major money laundering schemes, only one admits to having"</sub>
