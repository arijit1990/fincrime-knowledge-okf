## The Elusive Beneficial Owner

- **descriptive / definition**: Beneficial ownership should be understood as a material, substantive concept referring to de facto control over a corporate vehicle, not a purely legal definition. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should be understood as a material, substantive concept—referring to the de facto control over a corporate vehicle"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The report argues that beneficial ownership must not be reduced to a legally defined position such as a company director, a shareholder above a set percentage, or a legal entitlement to a trust's benefit. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "benefi cial ownership must not be reduced to a legally defi ned position"</sub>
- **expected / method**: In identifying the beneficial owner the focus should be on two factors: the control exercised and the benefit derived; control depends on context and may be exercised through ownership, contract or informally. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the focus should be on two factors: the control exer- cised and the benefi t derived"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: A formal approach based on ownership percentage thresholds or designated beneficiaries may yield useful clues, but service providers should be aware of its limitations. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A formal approach to benefi cial ownership, based on percentage thresholds of ownership"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: In suspicious cases, service providers need to go beyond their basic obligations and find out whether others are really in control or derive benefit. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In suspi- cious cases, they need to go beyond their basic obligations"</sub>

## Issue 1

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The company registry is the first source of information mentioned by investigators and service providers when seeking information on an incorporated entity, excluding trusts or similar arrangements. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the company registry. Th e vast majority of registries"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Most registries hold useful information such as entity name, address, articles of incorporation and directors' details, which should be publicly available in all company registries. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th is information should be publicly avail- able in all company registries"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Where a director acts as nominee for another person, that fact should be noted in the registry along with the name of the shadow director. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In cases in which a director is acting as a nominee for another person, that fact should be noted in the registry"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: All registries should collect and maintain information on owners, shareholders and members whose stake is large enough to be a controlling interest, updated and accessible in a timely manner at least to law enforcement. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All registries should collect and maintain this information"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In some jurisdictions registries held by a securities supervisor, regulatory commission or similar agency are more inclined to enforce obligations and have sufficient expertise and resources. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "company registries in some jurisdictions—typically held by a securities supervisor"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Countries should strive to make registry data freely available, ideally via free online access without preregistration or subscription fees, with search functions and access to historical records. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ideally, this would mean providing free online access (with- out preregistration requirements or subscription fees)"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Company registries are almost invariably archival, rarely conduct independent verification, and are often stretched for resources, so they are not a panacea and need complementing with other sources. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Registries are almost invari- ably archival in nature; they rarely conduct independent verifi cation"</sub>

## Why Service Providers Should Be Obligated

- **mandatory / requirement**: The FATF 40 Recommendations against Money Laundering require the collection of information about beneficial ownership. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires the collection of information"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Review of TCSP practice and country evaluations in more than 159 countries show banks (to some extent) and TCSPs (more generally) still do not adequately identify beneficial owners when establishing relationships. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "country evaluations carried out in more than 159 countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: U.S. banks are generally not obligated to collect beneficial ownership information when establishing a business relationship. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "U.S. banks are not generally obligated to collect benefi cial ownership information"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: An official declaration by the customer as to beneficial ownership could be useful in improving the situation. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an offi cial declaration by the customer as to benefi cial ownership could be useful"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Imposing due diligence obligations matters because in most misuse cases the intermediary is negligent, willfully blind or actively complicit, and mandatory full due diligence prevents pleading ignorance. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in the majority of cases in which a corporate vehi- cle is misused, the intermediary is negligent"</sub>

## A Two-Track Approach

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Service providers and registries both have a vital role in enabling law enforcement to access beneficial ownership information, though this may differ by jurisdiction; the authors believe service providers are generally the more useful source. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we believe that the service pro- vider generally will be the more useful source"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: An investigator reported that international requests for beneficial ownership information are referred to the service provider, since the registry gives only a name while the provider holds much more detail. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the service provider will be able to provide so much more"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some countries may not be able to impose CDD rules on service providers because lobbies such as bar associations block such legislation. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pressure groups or other lobbies (for example, a bar association) prevent the passage"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Where intermediaries are not subject to CDD, the company registry is the obvious second-best institution to maintain beneficial ownership information. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the obvious institution to main- tain benefi cial ownership information is the company registry"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: For registration purposes a formal definition, such as a natural person holding more than 25 percent of shares or the most shares, may be more practicable where registry staff lack corporate legal expertise. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a natural person holding more than 25 percent of the shares, or a natural person holding the most shares"</sub>

## Issue 3

- **expected / requirement**: Countries should ensure a resident person maintains beneficial ownership information on any entity incorporated under their laws, for example a resident director, registered agent or service provider. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "countries should ensure that a resident person main- tains benefi cial ownership information"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Documentation on a legal entity may be deliberately dispersed across jurisdictions, requiring requests in both the incorporating country and the country of administration. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the relevant documentation may be deliberately dispersed across diff erent jurisdic- tions"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: The resident person should receive all financial documentation relating to the legal entity, and this does not affect the service provider's own obligation to maintain the same information. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Th at person should receive all fi nancial documentation relating to the legal entity"</sub>

## Issue 4

- **recommended / requirement**: The authors recommend that all countries immobilize or abolish bearer shares and similar bearer instruments, as many countries have done without disrupting legitimate business. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "We rec- ommend that all countries immobilize or abolish them"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Bearer shares make the physical holder the deemed owner, so the problem is knowing who owns the shares at any point in time; the authors see no legitimate rationale for them. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No legitimate rationale exists for perpetuating bearer shares"</sub>

## Why Due Diligence Is Not Enough

- **descriptive / context**: New laws and due diligence by service providers or registries are necessary but insufficient on their own, since registries often lack current information and providers can do only so much due diligence. (p. 27)[^original]

## Transnational Investigations

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Investigators are familiar with domestic legal entities but largely unfamiliar with foreign corporate bodies and their fiscal rationale; a concerted effort is needed to improve this. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they are largely unfamiliar with foreign corporate bodies and the rationale"</sub>

## Building a Transnational Case

- **descriptive / context**: Investigators need resources to travel and coordinate with local investigators to gather documentary, testimonial and forensic evidence for court, and legal and informal cooperation channels are needed. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legal mechanisms and more informal channels are needed"</sub>

## Conducting Risk Analysis and Typologies

- **expected / recommendation**: Countries should undertake risk analysis and typology studies of corporate vehicle misuse in their jurisdictions to identify which entities and arrangements are typically abused. (p. 28)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Countries should undertake a risk analysis and conduct typology studies"</sub>
