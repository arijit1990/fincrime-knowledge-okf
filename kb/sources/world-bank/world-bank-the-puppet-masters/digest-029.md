## Corporate Vehicle Misuse—Not a Bullet-Proof Shield

- **descriptive / case example**: Dugsbery, Inc., a California corporation with no Lazarenko name on its incorporation documents, was used to funnel funds into a US$6.745 million estate in Novato, California; it was formed in 1994, registered to an individual tied to Kiritchenko, and based in a building Kiritchenko owned. (p. 222)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Lazarenko's name was not attached to any of the incorporation documents."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The scheme was brought down by the change of heart of Lazarenko's advisor and co-conspirator Peter Kiritchenko, who became a state witness; his cooperation penetrated the anonymity of the Dugsbery structure, and the court traced Dugsbery funds to Lazarenko's Bahamas account and then to his Swiss account CARPO-53. (p. 222)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "What ultimately brought down the scheme was the change of heart by Lazarenko's advisor and coconspirator turned state-witness, Peter Kiritchenko."</sub>

## Correspondent Banking Accounts—European Federal Credit Bank

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A February 2001 US Senate minority staff report stated that correspondent accounts in US banks give owners and clients of poorly regulated or corrupt foreign banks with weak anti-money laundering controls direct access to the US financial system. (p. 223)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A February 2001 report by the U.S. Senate noted that"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The USA PATRIOT Act, enacted in October 2001, prohibited US banks from holding correspondent accounts with offshore shell banks such as European Federal Credit Bank (EuroFed). (p. 223)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which prohibited U.S. banks from having correspondent accounts with off shore shell banks like European Federal Credit Bank (EuroFed)."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In August 1997 Lazarenko and Kiritchenko bought a 67 percent majority share in the Antigua-domiciled EuroFed for US$1.1 million; EuroFed then opened correspondent accounts with US banks, investment firms and banks in Lithuania, Liechtenstein and Switzerland. (p. 223)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In August 1997, Lazarenko and Kiritchenko purchased a 67 percent majority share in the Antigua-domiciled bank for US$1.1 million."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: According to the 2005 forfeiture claim, about US$85.5 million was formerly on deposit for Lazarenko's benefit at EuroFed, and almost US$100 million was cycled through Lazarenko- and Kiritchenko-controlled EuroFed accounts to launder proceeds. (p. 223)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "approximately US$85.5 million is alleged to have been formerly on deposit in accounts held for Lazarenko’s benefi t at EuroFed"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Lazarenko is alleged to have controlled EuroFed accounts in names including Lady Lake Investments Corporation, Fairmont Group, Ltd., Guardian Investment Group, Ltd., Firstar Securities, Ltd., Nemuro Industrial Group, and Orby International, Ltd. (p. 223)[^original]

## Case Study 9: Piarco International Airport Scandal

- **descriptive / case example**: From 1996 to 2000 Trinidad and Tobago ran a process meant to be competitive for contracts in building Piarco International Airport, but Birk Hillman Consultants (BHC), co-owned by Eduardo Hillman-Waller, and others rigged bidding so overpriced bids from companies they controlled, such as Calmaquip Engineering Corp., would win. (p. 225)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "BHC and others, such as businessmen Raul Gutierrez and Armando Paz, were able to rig the bidding and selection process"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: According to the civil complaint, political appointees including Steve Ferguson, Brian Kuei Tung and Ishwar Galbaransingh influenced the process, allowing BHC and conspirators to guarantee government approval for the projects. (p. 225)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the infl uence of political appointees, which included chairman of the National Gas Company Steve Ferguson"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Contract CP-9 for building enclosure and interior construction was awarded to Northern Construction Limited, owned by Galbaransingh, although only it submitted a bid from Trinidad and Tobago among eight prequalified companies, and its bid was about US$10 million above the cost estimate. (p. 226)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Northern's bid was approximately US$10 million above the cost estimate, Northern was awarded the contract for CP-9."</sub>

## Misuse of CVs to Give Appearance of Legitimacy

- **descriptive / context**: Corporate vehicles are often used to protect PEP anonymity, but in the Piarco case, given its scale, government role and high-level PEPs, the PEPs could hardly have stayed anonymous, so the primary motive for CVs was probably to make the bidding look legitimate. (p. 226)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the primary motivation for using CVs was probably not the protection of the anonymity of the PEPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Although BHC had been pre-assured the project manager position before bidding began, it was still asked to present to the selection committee, giving the appearance of a legitimate competitive process. (p. 226)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "despite the fact that BHC had been pre-assured of the position of project manager before the bidding process even began"</sub>
