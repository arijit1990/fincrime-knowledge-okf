## INTRODUCTION

- **descriptive / context**: The World Bank Group's National Risk Assessment Tool was commenced by the Financial Markets Integrity team in 2007 and drew on experts' contributions and the Bank's experience assisting countries with national risk assessments. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The development of the World Bank Group’s National Risk Assessment Tool"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The tool defines ML/TF threats as the scale and characteristics of proceeds of criminal activity or financing of terrorism in a jurisdiction. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "“Threats” here refer to the scale and characteristics of the proceeds of criminal activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The tool defines ML/TF vulnerabilities as weaknesses or gaps in a jurisdiction's defences against money laundering and terrorist financing. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "“Vulnerabilities” refers to weaknesses or gaps in a jurisdiction’s defenses"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Threats or vulnerabilities may exist at national or sector level and together determine the ML/TF risk level in a jurisdiction. (p. 2)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Threats or vulnerabilities may exist at the national or sector level"</sub>

## Objectives of the Tool

- **descriptive / context**: The first objective is to guide jurisdictions in assessing ML/TF risks so they can design a more effective, risk-based AML/CFT regime. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To guide jurisdictions in assessing their ML/TF risks, with a view to helping"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A second objective is capacity building for assessing ML/TF risks and improving data and information collection frameworks and practices. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To contribute to capacity building in the country, not only for assessing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A third objective is to raise awareness and trigger interaction and cooperation among government and private sector stakeholders. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To raise awareness, and trigger interaction and cooperation among the stakeholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The tool is not aimed at giving a quantitative estimate of the size of the illicit economy or financial flows in a country. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A quantitative estimate of the size of the illicit economy or financial flows"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The tool is not aimed at assessing the risks of predicate offences such as drug trafficking, fraud or corruption. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assessing the risks of predicate offenses (drug trafficking risk, fraud risk, corruption risk, etc.)"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The tool is not aimed at making cross-country comparisons of ML/TF risks. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Making cross-country comparisons of ML/TF risks."</sub>

## The General Structure of the Tool

- **descriptive / context**: The tool consists of nine modules: seven focus on money laundering risk, one on terrorism financing, and one on financial inclusion product risk. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The tool consists of nine modules that make it possible to assess money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The National Vulnerability Module and sector vulnerability Modules 3 to 7 use weighted averages, built-in preconditions and formulas. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "utilizes weighted averages, built-in preconditions, and formulas"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The threat, terrorism financing and financial inclusion product modules rely on relatively simple matrix-based structures. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "rely on relatively simple matrix based structures"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: During the NRA process the country establishes a national Working Group, which divides into teams each focusing on one module. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the country establishes a national Working Group (WG) to undertake the risk assessment"</sub>

## PART 1. MONEY LAUNDERING RISK ASSESSMENT

- **descriptive / method**: Overall national money laundering risk is determined by assessing the national threat of money laundering and the national vulnerability to money laundering. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the overall national money laundering risk is determined by assessing"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "which ranges from “low” to “high” on a five-point scale"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In the Threat Assessment Module the Working Group identifies main predicate offences, origins and flows of criminal proceeds, and money laundering techniques and trends. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Working Group identifies the main predicate offences, origins and flows"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The Threat Assessment Module facilitates systematic collection of data on ML threats and analysis of cross-border ML threats. (p. 5)[^original]

- **descriptive / method**: The vulnerability score corresponds to one of five levels from low to high. (p. 5)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "High ratings that relate to the combating ability have a positive implication"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Sector vulnerability ratings (banking, securities, insurance, remittance, lawyers etc.) are also entered in the National Vulnerability Excel file; a higher sector vulnerability negatively affects national vulnerability. (p. 10)[^original]

## PART 2. TERRORISM FINANCING RISK ASSESSMENT MODULE

- **descriptive / method**: The terrorism financing module follows the same logic as the ML modules and has three areas of analysis. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Terrorism Financing Risk Assessment Module largely follows the same logic"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Area 1 assesses the level of terrorist threat through quantitative and qualitative information on terrorist acts, enforcement data, intelligence and research. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the level of the terrorist threat for the jurisdiction is assessed through the review"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The terrorist threat level is said to affect the level of terrorism financing, but threat assessment is explicitly not the focus of the module. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It should be emphasized that this is not the focus of the module."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Area 2 analyses the direction of terrorist funds, their sources and channels. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the direction of movement of terrorist financing funds, as well as their sources and channels"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Direction: the Working Group determines whether funds are generated at home but used abroad, or the reverse, or simply pass through the jurisdiction. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Another possibility is that funds simply pass through the jurisdiction."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Sources of terrorist funds may be legitimate (such as nonprofit organisations or import/export businesses) or criminal (such as natural resource theft or drug trafficking). (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financing may come from legitimate sources (such as nonprofit organizations, or import/export businesses)"</sub>
- **expected / method**: The Working Group should examine which channels are used to move terrorist funds. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Working Group should examine which channels are being used to move terrorist funds"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: An estimate of undetected terrorist funds, based on qualitative indicators, must be made in addition to using enforcement and intelligence data. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an estimate must be made of undetected terrorist funds, based on qualitative indicators"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Area 3 supports assessing the strength of the jurisdiction's defence mechanisms, meaning controls adopted to detect and counter terrorist financing. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The last part of the module supports the assessment of the strength of the jurisdiction’s defense mechanisms"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Based on the module's analysis, the Working Group must make an overall assessment of terrorist financing risk in the jurisdiction. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Working Group must then make an overall assessment of the risk of terrorist financing"</sub>

## PART 3. FINANCIAL INCLUSION PRODUCT RISK ASSESSMENT MODULE

- **descriptive / context**: Module 9 provides a framework for evaluating ML/TF risks arising from current, new or emerging financial inclusion products, to help countries facilitate inclusion while mitigating risks. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "aims to assist national authorities by providing a logical, user-friendly framework"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Regulators may use the module to design products or features with low ML/TF risk, or to assess whether an existing product can be classified as low-risk. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The module can also be used by regulators to design financial products"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Section 1 asks regulators about product features; Section 2 asks about the country's overall ML/TF risk environment and controls for inclusion products; Section 3 gives an initial risk level per feature and mitigation guidance questions. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the first section of the Tool, regulators answer key questions about the specific product features"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The product risk assessment is a dynamic process in which product features and mitigation measures can be fine-tuned based on results. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is a dynamic process by which product features and mitigation measures can be fine-tuned"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Module 9 uses findings of other modules rather than feeding into the National Risk Assessment, and inclusion products must also be assessed in sectoral modules to capture their impact on national vulnerability. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial inclusion products also need to be assessed separately in the relevant sectoral modules"</sub>
