## 5. Glossary

- **descriptive / definition**: A virtual asset centralized exchanger (CEX) is a platform run by a central entity through which users trade digital assets, acting as intermediaries that hold users' assets. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a platform, managed and operated by a central entity, through which users can trade digital assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A custodial hot wallet is a cryptocurrency wallet in which a third party holds and manages the user's private keys while being internet-connected, so users rely on the custodian. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a cryptocurrency wallet in which a third party, like a centralized exchange, holds and manages the private keys"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A decentralized exchanger (DEX) is a peer-to-peer marketplace where traders exchange assets directly without a central authority, using smart contracts on a blockchain. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a peer-to-peer marketplace in which cryptocurrency traders can exchange assets directly without the involvement of a central authority"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Decentralized finance (DeFi) refers to financial services provided through smart contracts on distributed ledgers, enabling peer-to-peer transactions without traditional intermediaries. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial services provided through arrangements that use smart contracts on distributed ledgers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: DeFi lending lets users lend or borrow cryptocurrencies directly from each other, bypassing intermediaries such as banks, using smart contracts on blockchain networks. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "users can lend or borrow cryptocurrencies directly from each other, bypassing traditional financial intermediaries like banks"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Distributed ledger technology (DLT) is a protocol enabling simultaneous access, validation and updating of an immutable ledger distributed across multiple computers or locations. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enables simultaneous access, validation, and updating of an immutable ledger"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under FATF Recommendation 16, the Travel Rule requires VASPs and other entities involved in VA activities to obtain, hold and transmit specific originator and beneficiary information immediately and securely for VA transfers. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requires virtual asset service providers (VASPs) and other entities involved in virtual asset (VA) activities to obtain"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The glossary entry headed Fiat-to-Fiat Exchange actually describes an exchange between one or more forms of virtual assets for or on behalf of another person, as written in the source. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is an exchange between one or more forms of virtual assets for or on behalf of another natural or legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Inherent risk is the ML and TF risk before applying any controls or mitigation measures, based on the combination of threats and vulnerabilities. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the level of money laundering (ML) and terrorist financing (TF) risk before applying any controls or mitigation measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: An initial coin offering (ICO) is the crypto industry's equivalent of an initial public offering, used by a company to raise funds for a new blockchain app or service. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the cryptocurrency industry’s equivalent of an initial public offering"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: An ICO provider is a natural or legal person not covered elsewhere in national legislation who, as a business, participates in or provides financial services related to an issuer's offer or sale of a virtual asset. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "participates in or provides financial services related to an issuer’s offer and/or sale of a virtual asset"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Known threat is ML/TF threat supported by concrete evidence such as investigations, prosecutions, convictions, supervisory findings or FIU disseminations. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "supported by concrete evidence such as investigations, prosecutions, convictions, supervisory findings, or financial intelligence unit (FIU) disseminations"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A noncustodial (cold) wallet gives users complete control over their private keys and assets, with keys stored offline, so users rather than a third party manage their funds. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "users, rather than a third party, manage their keys and funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF defines peer-to-peer transactions as VA transfers made without a VASP or other obliged entity, such as transfers between two unhosted wallets acting on their own behalf. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such as VA transfers between two unhosted wallets whose users are acting on their own behalf"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: P2P transactions are not explicitly subject to AML/CFT controls under the FATF Standards, because the standards generally place obligations on intermediaries rather than individuals. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "P2P transactions are not explicitly subject to anti-money laundering/countering the financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) controls under the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF standards have some exceptions to the intermediary focus, such as requirements related to implementing targeted financial sanctions. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the standards generally place obligations on intermediaries rather than on individuals themselves"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Miners validate transactions and maintain the ledger of a decentralized VA network by solving complex algorithms, and are rewarded with new virtual assets or transaction fees. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "validate transactions and maintain the ledger by solving complex algorithms using special software"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Miners can pose ML/TF risks, especially in anonymous mining pools or where rewards are distributed across opaque structures, which can obscure illicit fund origins or process ransomware or fraud proceeds. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they can pose ML/TF risks, especially when operating in anonymous mining pools"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Virtual asset mixers, also called tumblers, obfuscate the origin and destination of digital assets by pooling funds from multiple users and redistributing them to new addresses. (p. 74)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They achieve this by pooling funds from multiple users, mixing them, and then redistributing them to new addresses"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Foreign PEPs are individuals who are or have been entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign jurisdiction, such as heads of state, senior politicians and senior officials. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Domestic PEPs are individuals entrusted domestically with prominent public functions, with examples matching those given for foreign PEPs. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "entrusted domestically with prominent public functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: PEPs entrusted with a prominent function by an international organisation are members of senior management, meaning directors, deputy directors and board members or equivalent. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to members of senior management—that is, directors, deputy directors, and members of the board or equivalent functions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The PEP definition is not intended to cover middle-ranking or more junior individuals in the listed categories. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The definition of PEPs is not intended to cover middle ranking or more junior individuals"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Privacy coins are virtual assets designed to enhance user anonymity and transaction details, making them harder to trace than traditional cryptocurrencies with public ledgers. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "designed to enhance user anonymity and transaction details, making them difficult to trace"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Residual risk is the ML and TF risk after applying mitigation measures, adjusting inherent risk for legislative, regulatory measures and their implementation. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It adjusts the inherent risk score based on legislative and regulatory measures and their implementation"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A stablecoin is a virtual asset pegged to a stable asset, most often a fiat currency, which aims to minimise price volatility and make it a more reliable option for transactions and store of value. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This peg aims to minimize price volatility, making them a more reliable option than other virtual assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: VA payment providers are natural or legal persons not covered elsewhere in national legislation who, as a business, conduct transfers of virtual assets for others. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducts the transfer of virtual assets for or on behalf of another natural or legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: VA-to-fiat exchange is the exchange between virtual assets and fiat currencies for or on behalf of another person. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the exchange between virtual assets and fiat currencies for or on behalf of another natural or legal person"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Virtual asset ATMs, also called crypto or Bitcoin ATMs, are internet-connected kiosks exchanging fiat currency for virtual assets or vice versa, operated by third-party companies and typically requiring a wallet. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "internet-connected kiosks that facilitate the exchange of fiat currency"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Perceived threat is the level of ML/TF threat drawn from assessments, expert judgment or credible open-source information where no incidents have been confirmed; such threats are reasonably assumed but not yet substantiated by formal evidence. (p. 75)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Refers to the level of ML/TF threat based on assessments, expert judgment, or credible open-source information"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: A virtual asset casino is an online gambling platform where users wager on casino games using virtual assets, often offering quick deposits, withdrawals and sometimes anonymity. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an online gambling platform that allows users to wager on casino games using virtual assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Virtual asset OTC services facilitate large trades of virtual assets, often between institutional clients, outside traditional cryptocurrency exchangers, without disrupting market prices. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitates large trades of virtual assets, often between institutional clients, outside of traditional cryptocurrency exchangers"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Virtual asset staking is depositing virtual assets on a platform to earn rewards, usually paid in the same asset, in return for contributing to a blockchain's validation process. (p. 76)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "investors contribute to the validation process of a blockchain network"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Wallet providers are natural or legal persons not covered elsewhere in national legislation who, as a business, safekeep or administer virtual assets or instruments giving control over them. (p. 77)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "conducts safekeeps and/or administers virtual assets or instruments enabling control over virtual assets"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Monero generating a new address for every transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Sending dusting transactions places small traceable transfers in wallets to track wallet behaviour; DeFi web apps reportedly block users hit by a Tornado Cash dust attack. (p. 80)[^original]
