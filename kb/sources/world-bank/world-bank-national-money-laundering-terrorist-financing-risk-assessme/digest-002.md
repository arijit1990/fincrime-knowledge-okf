## 2.5. Selecting the Appropriate Module Tier

- **descriptive / method**: A short quiz in the tool helps the working group select basic, intermediate or advanced version, based on the size and complexity of the VA sector, the regulatory framework and experience with VA risk assessments. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The tool includes a short quiz to assist the working group in selecting the most suitable version"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The basic version targets jurisdictions with limited VA activities and covers core VA and VASP services, focusing on exchangers and wallet services. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The basic version is aimed at jurisdictions with limited VA activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The intermediate and advanced tiers include more in-depth assessment of additional ML/TF techniques and VA services, including ML/TF risks linked to decentralized finance (DeFi). (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the intermediate and advanced tiers include a more in-depth assessment of additional ML/TF techniques"</sub>

## Step 1.1. Module Selection Quiz

- **descriptive / method**: Jurisdictions answer the Module Selection tab quiz starting with three baseline questions; further questions depend on answers and a recommendation appears at the bottom. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The working group should begin by answering the three baseline questions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The tool recommends a module by considering VA materiality and authorities' capacity and experience in assessing risk; basic for limited VA activity and limited experience, higher tiers encouraged for higher materiality and experience. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For jurisdictions with limited VA activities and limited risk assessment experience, the quiz will recommend the basic module"</sub>
- **recommended / expectation**: Higher-tier modules are encouraged for jurisdictions with higher VA materiality and experience reviewing risk or regulating the sector. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the tool will encourage jurisdictions with higher VA materiality and experience"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: All module recommendations are optional; the final decision rests with the working group. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All recommendations on the proposed tier are optional; the final decision on which module to use rests with the working group"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The quiz rates known or perceived VA activity as High (material number of domestic VASPs or local VA activity, regulated or not), Medium (limited VASP registrations but regular legal or illegal VA use), Low (almost no VA use and no registered VASPs), or Unknown (no information on materiality). (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "“High”: There are a material number of domestic VASPs or VA activities occurring locally"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The working group may refer to the FATF's 2024 list of jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The working group may refer to the list of jurisdictions with materially important VASP activity published by the FATF in 2024"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The international cooperation question rates threats as Yes many (material number of requests or STRs/investigations, judged against sector size), Yes some, or No (no requests and no ML/TF VA investigations, very limited STRs). (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "“Yes, many”: The jurisdiction has received a material number of international cooperation requests"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Jurisdictions should answer yes to the prior-steps question if they have taken any ad hoc steps to identify sector risks, even outside a formal national risk assessment. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions should respond “yes” if they have taken any ad hoc steps to identify the risks of the sector"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: The regulatory framework question should consider whether legislation regulates the sector for AML/CFT purposes and/or appoints an AML/CFT supervisor. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The response to this question should consider whether legislation has been passed to regulate the sector"</sub>
- **expected / method**: If the working group wishes to retake the quiz, it should use the Reset All button at the top of the tab. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they should use the “Reset All” button at the top of the tab"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Follow-up questions appear depending on baseline answers; all visible questions should be answered to obtain a tier recommendation; answers are yes or no. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Depending on the answers to the baseline questions, a subset of follow-up questions will appear"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Complex VA services are judged by the breadth of services offered; one local VASP offering one service to a limited market may be answered no, whereas multiple local VA companies or locals accessing complex services warrant yes. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions with only one local VASP which provides one type of service, to a limited local market may select “no”"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: Jurisdictions may consult local VASPs, financial institutions and blockchain analytics open-source information to gauge local access to decentralised services. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions may wish to consult local VASPs, financial institutions, and/or open-source information from blockchain analytics"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF defines peer-to-peer transactions as VA transfers without a VASP or other obliged entity, such as between two unhosted wallets acting for their own behalf. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF defines peer-to-peer (P2P) transactions as VA transfers conducted without the use or involvement of a VASP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: P2P transactions are not explicitly subject to AML/CFT controls under the FATF Standards, because obligations generally fall on intermediaries, with exceptions such as targeted financial sanctions implementation. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "P2P transactions are not explicitly subject to AML/CFT controls under the FATF Standards"</sub>

## Step 1.2. Qualitative Scoping and Mapping

- **descriptive / method**: The working group fills in the Home tab with the jurisdiction's name and desired module tier, drawing on the quiz recommendation. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the working group should fill in the requested information in the “Home” tab"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: If no module is selected in the Home tab, the automatic default is the advanced version. (p. 24)[^original]

- **permissive / context**: Subnational jurisdictions may use the tool to assess risks within specific provinces where evidence suggests risks differ. (p. 24)[^original]

- **descriptive / method**: The Definitions tab asks whether national definitions of VAs and VASPs exist and whether they are consistent with FATF definitions, and whether national definitions exist for subsectors, services or products. (p. 24)[^original]

- **descriptive / method**: The General Mapping tab collects contextual information on sector structure, key actors, regulatory frameworks and case studies; the contextual information is qualitative and does not directly affect final risk ratings but underpins later indicators. (p. 25)[^original]
