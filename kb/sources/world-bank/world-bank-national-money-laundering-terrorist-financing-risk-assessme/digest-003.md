## Box 3.1. What is a threat in the context of VAs?

- **descriptive / definition**: A money laundering or terrorist financing threat in the VA context refers to individuals, groups, entities or their facilitators who seek to exploit VAs or related services to conceal illicit origin of funds or to finance terrorism. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a money laundering or terrorist financing threat refers to individuals, groups, entities, or their facilitators who seek to exploit"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Example: an organised crime group generating cash proceeds and converting them into VAs to hide their illicit origin. (p. 30)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An organized crime group generating cash proceeds and converting them into VAs to hide their illicit origin"</sub>

## 3.2. Step 2: ML and TF Threat Assessment

- **descriptive / context**: Step 2 of the World Bank VA risk assessment tool helps jurisdictions assess ML/TF threats facing the VA sector; threats are distinguished between ML and TF because a jurisdiction may face high exposure to one but not the other. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Given that jurisdictions may face a high exposure to ML, but not TF, or vice versa"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Internal ML/TF threats are criminal proceeds or terrorist funds generated in the jurisdiction and laundered or used to finance terrorism domestically via VA activities or VASPs there. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Internal money laundering/terrorist financing (ML/TF) threats are criminal proceeds or terrorist funds that are generated in the jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Incoming threats are criminal proceeds or terrorist funds generated overseas that enter the jurisdiction to be laundered or spent via VA activities or VASPs there. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Incoming threats are criminal proceeds or terrorist funds that are generated overseas and then enter the jurisdiction"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Outgoing threats are criminal proceeds or terrorist funds generated in the jurisdiction and transferred abroad to be laundered via VAs or VASP activity overseas. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Outgoing threats are criminal proceeds or terrorist funds that are generated in the jurisdiction and are then transferred abroad"</sub>

## ML and TF Techniques

- **descriptive / method**: In the threat assessment's typologies part, jurisdictions review a list of ML and TF techniques in the Excel tab and rate known and perceived prevalence; the list was identified by the World Bank team from case studies and global typologies reports. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The World Bank team has identified these techniques using an in-depth review of case studies and global typologies reports"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Intermediate and advanced modules assess progressively more techniques; the Excel tool also allows jurisdictions to add other relevant techniques. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For jurisdictions that select the intermediate and advanced modules, there are progressively more techniques to assess"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Known ML and TF techniques involving VAs are grouped into four categories: traditional obfuscation techniques adapted for VAs, new obfuscation techniques relying on VA and distributed ledger features, techniques for generating criminal proceeds in VAs, and TF-specific techniques. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Known ML and TF techniques involving VAs have been grouped into four categories"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Traditional obfuscation techniques adapted for the VA sector mirror similar techniques seen in traditional finance. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These techniques mirror similar techniques seen in traditional"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: New obfuscation techniques rely on unique characteristics of VAs and distributed ledger technology, with no direct equivalent in traditional finance. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "New obfuscation techniques that rely on unique characteristics of VA and distributed ledger technology"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The World Bank tool automatically generates a prioritisation of techniques to help authorities' outreach to the private sector (output 3 in chapter 4). (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the World Bank tool automatically generates a prioritization of techniques"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For each technique the working group should rate prevalence based on known data and expert opinion/perception, using very high, high, medium high, medium, medium-low, low, very low, or unknown. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For each technique the working group should rate the prevalence based on (a) known data and (b) expert opinion/"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: For expert perception, sources include suspicion that the technique may have occurred or is likely to occur in future, based on expert judgment. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Suspicion that the technique may have occurred or is likely to occur in the future"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: If the working group lacks sufficient information to assess a technique, it should select the unknown option. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If the working group does not have sufficient information to make an assessment, it should select the"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Jurisdictions should use the comments column to justify ratings and cite any specific cases or data sources that informed the judgment. (p. 36)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should use this column to justify their assigned rating"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The final row of the Excel table allows jurisdictions to add further techniques, reflecting that techniques regularly evolve. (p. 36)[^original]

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Examples of VA vulnerabilities include domestic VASPs serving large numbers of high-risk clients such as PEPs or foreign clients from high-risk jurisdictions, and systemic deficiencies in local identity infrastructure that challenge customer due diligence.
