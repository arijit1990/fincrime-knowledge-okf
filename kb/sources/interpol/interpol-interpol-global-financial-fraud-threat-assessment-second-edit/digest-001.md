## Key Findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Since 2024 the number of fraud-related Notices and Diffusions increased by 54 per cent, most issued by European member countries; INTERPOL supported member countries in more than 1,500 transnational fraud cases with lost assets valued at USD 1.1 billion. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the number of fraud-related Notices and Diffusions has increased by 54 per cent, the majority issued by European member countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Global losses from financial fraud in 2025 were estimated at USD 442 billion, citing the Global Anti-Scam Alliance 2025 estimate. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Global losses related to financial fraud in 2025 alone have been estimated at USD 442 billion."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Scam centres have evolved from a regional phenomenon into a global threat, with centres found across multiple regions; they engage hundreds of thousands of people, many forced to perpetrate online frauds, and victims from nearly 80 countries have been trafficked into them. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "victims from nearly 80 countries have been trafficked into online scam centres, with no continent left untouched"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Dark Web marketplaces offer applications that can clone voices and faces from seconds of genuine audio or video, and 'agentic AI' can autonomously plan and execute fraud campaigns; INTERPOL reports a global surge in AI-enhanced schemes, notably sextortion intertwined with investment scams, and impersonation fraud including fake kidnappings for ransom. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Dark Web marketplaces offer applications which can clone voices and faces from mere seconds of genuine audio or video samples"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Criminal networks adapt business models for efficiency, including collaboration with specialised money laundering networks; INTERPOL assesses these offenders as highly organised, skilled and agile. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including through collaboration with specialized money laundering networks. INTERPOL assesses these offenders as highly organized, skilled and agile."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Terrorist groups in the African region have been found to rely on fraud schemes for resource generation, especially cryptobased scams, indicating an increasing nexus between financial fraud and terrorist financing. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Terrorist groups operating in the African region have been found to rely on fraud schemes for resource generation, especially via cryptobased scams."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: INTERPOL rates the overall global risk from financial fraud as HIGH. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INTERPOL assesses the overall global risk related to financial fraud as HIGH"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: INTERPOL expects the scale of fraud offending to escalate significantly over the next three to five years, mainly because AI technology is more available and entry barriers are low. (p. 6)[^original]

## Financial Fraud Types and Trend Updates

- **descriptive / definition**: Business Email Compromise (also called CEO fraud) is a prevalent impersonation fraud where criminals compromise email accounts, impersonate executives and manipulate employees into transferring funds to suspect accounts so the funds can be quickly laundered. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "By compromising email accounts and impersonating executives, the fraudsters manipulate employees into transferring funds to suspect accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Employment, inheritance and lottery frauds, which require upfront fees, are increasing; INTERPOL data indicates the African region is a primary target for this trend. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INTERPOL data indicates that the African region is a primary target for this fraud trend."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: BEC was the most frequently reported fraud type to INTERPOL; most reports came from Asia and Pacific and European regions, which are highly impacted and most frequently targeted. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The majority of these reports originated from the Asia and Pacific and European regions"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The report's framework uses an operational law enforcement definition of financial fraud as illegal activities aiming at financial gain through deceptive actions against an individual or entity; the assessment excludes fraud against government or public administration. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illegal activities that have the aim of financial gain through deceptive actions against and to the detriment of an individual or entity."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Impersonation fraud occurs when an offender poses as a person or institution with whom the victim has or could have a pre-existing real relationship, typically relying on eliciting fear or worry. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Impersonation Fraud occurs when an offender poses as a person or an institution with whom the victim has, or could have"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: INTERPOL has observed rising fake kidnapping scams in which criminals use AI-generated images and videos of victims' loved ones to demand ransoms. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "where criminals use AI-generated images and videos of victims’ loved ones to demand ransoms"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: 'Quishing' uses tampered QR codes distributed by email, SMS, posters, ads or marketplaces, presented as links to trusted providers, redirecting victims to fake websites to steal data, download malware or approve transactions. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraudsters distribute tampered QR codes via email, SMS, public posters, digital ads, or online marketplaces"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: In advance-payment fraud, payment is requested before any product or service is received, and that product or service may be non-existent or significantly sub-standard. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Payment is requested up front and prior to the reception of any product or service"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Identity fraud is the unauthorised acquisition and use of personal information for illicit financial gain; it has two elements, the technique to obtain the data and the fraud tactic misusing it, and can occur without the victim's direct involvement. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identity fraud is therefore comprised of two elements"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: INTERPOL detected an increase in AI-facilitated synthetic identity fraud; child identity theft for financial fraud is a particularly concerning new trend that often goes undetected until the victim reaches adulthood. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the theft and exploitation of a minor’s identity to commit fraud often goes undetected until the victim reaches adulthood"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Insurance fraud is any intentional act designed to deceive an insurer during application or claims, or to mislead about denial of a legitimate claim, including false claims, misrepresentation, premium fraud and staged accidents. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It encompasses various forms, including, false claims, misrepresentation, premium fraud and staged accidents."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Insurance fraud, notably car or medical claims, is escalating globally; INTERPOL data suggests Western and Eastern Europe are the regions most targeted. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Western and Eastern Europe are the regions most targeted by insurance fraud."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The UK saw a 25 per cent spike in motor insurance fraud driven by false applications and identity theft in early 2025; in France fraud case detection rose significantly in 2024, accounting for nearly USD 1 billion; German insurers flagged 10 per cent of claims as suspicious with annual losses over USD 7 billion, mainly motor insurance. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the UK saw a 25 per cent spike in motor insurance fraud driven by false applications and identity theft in early 2025"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Phishing is a fraudulent attempt to steal sensitive data by disguising as a trustworthy entity via email or fake websites; smishing is phishing via deceptive SMS; vishing is voice-based phishing via phone calls. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A fraudulent attempt to steal sensitive data by disguising as a trustworthy entity via email or fake websites."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Spoofing is falsifying communication identifiers such as caller ID, email address or website URL to appear as a trusted source. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The act of falsifying communication identifiers (like caller ID, email address, or website URL) to appear as a trusted source."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Investment fraud manipulates people into investing in fake or misleading ventures, usually via online platforms, using high-return promises, misrepresentation and urgency; pyramid and Ponzi models are common. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraudsters use various deceptive tactics, including promising high returns, misrepresenting investments and creating a sense of urgency."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In many investment frauds victims initially incur no losses: fraudsters return initial capital with purported profits to build trust, then abscond with funds once the victim increases investment. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraudsters often return the initial capital along with purported profits to build credibility and trust."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Investment fraud causes some of the highest financial losses to individuals; current trends include phoney virtual-asset (crypto) investments and fictitious real estate, and victims are often re-contacted by fake recovery agents or law enforcement. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Investment fraud results in some of the highest financial losses to individual victims."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Romance baiting combines romance and crypto-investment fraud: after building trust via social media or dating apps, offenders persuade victims to invest, often via fraudulent apps showing inflated profits, and victims may face recovery fraud. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Romance baiting victims have also reported being subsequently targeted for “recovery fraud,”"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Romance baiting, initially linked to organised crime from Eastern Asia and emerging in Southeast Asia, has spread globally to Africa, Latin America and Europe, and poses particular challenges due to cryptocurrency use. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has since spread globally to Africa, Latin America and Europe."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Romance fraud is perpetrated by criminals who build a relationship of trust or intimacy, often via social media, dating apps and messaging, then persuade victims to give money for fabricated emergencies; victims suffer financial and emotional harm. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Victims are gradually persuaded, under the guise of love and commitment, to provide financial assistance to the offender"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Sextortion is blackmail extorting money by threatening to publish real or AI-generated explicit images or information to victims' contacts. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "refers to the practice of extorting money from someone by threatening to publish or distribute real or AI-generated explicit images"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: INTERPOL detects a global increase in sextortion integrated into complex fraud schemes; scam-centre victims report that when investment-style tactics fail they are told to pursue sextortion; notable increases target the African and MENA regions; AI imagery and deepfakes are used across all regions. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Member countries have reported notable increases of this phenomenon targeting victims in the African and MENA regions."</sub>

## Extremely High Levels of Pervasiveness, Financial Loss and Harm

- **descriptive / finding or result**: INTERPOL ranks financial fraud among the most threatening global crimes alongside drug trafficking and money laundering; WEF 2025 found 77 per cent of surveyed business leaders reported increased fraud and 73 per cent were personally affected or had someone in their network affected by cyber-enabled fraud. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "77 per cent of business leaders surveyed worldwide reported an increase in fraud over the past year"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Between 2024 and 2025 INTERPOL data shows a 54 per cent year-to-year increase in fraud-related Notices and Diffusions; European countries issued 38 per cent and Asia and Pacific 28 per cent of them. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "European member countries issued 38 per cent of these Notices and Diffusions, followed by 28 per cent from countries in the Asia and Pacific region."</sub>

## The Scale of Fraud

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Estimating fraud costs is challenging due to significant under-reporting, but data indicate costs are very high and growing; one estimate puts global 2025 losses at USD 442 billion. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Estimating the financial cost of fraud is challenging for a number of reasons, including significant levels of under-reporting."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In the UK the Office for National Statistics reported fraud accounted for 43 per cent of all estimated crime in 2025; City of London Police reported a 33 per cent year-on-year rise in financial fraud losses, about USD 3.5 billion. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fraud accounted for 43 per cent of all estimated crime in 2025"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: US fraud losses rose steadily from nearly USD 4 billion in 2020 to USD 16.6 billion in 2024 according to the FBI IC3 report; in Singapore, losses fell from 2024 but were about USD 721 million in 2025. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The United States has seen a steady increase of financial loss due to fraud, from nearly USD 4 billion in 2020 to USD 16.6"</sub>

## The Human Cost

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Fraud victims suffer profound emotional distress, psychological trauma and social isolation from eroded trust, which can last long after the scheme ends; in the most severe cases victims have resorted to suicide. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In the most severe cases, victims of fraud have resorted to suicide."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Research on romance scams finds victims commonly experience guilt and shame, which often deter reporting due to fear of blame, worsening underreporting and reducing agencies' ability to investigate and support victims. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which frequently deter them from reporting the crime due to fear of being blamed"</sub>

## The Continued Expansion of Scam Centres

- **descriptive / context**: INTERPOL has monitored since 2022 a trend of human trafficking for forced online fraud, based on a dual-victim model: trafficked people lured by fake job offers forced to commit fraud, and other victims defrauded in other jurisdictions. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This threat is based on a dual-victim model"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: By late 2025 trafficked victims spanned nearly 80 nationalities, up from 66 in the first quarter of 2025. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "By late 2025, trafficked victims spanned nearly 80 nationalities — up from 66 in the first quarter of 2025"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Scam centres were initially concentrated in Southeast Asia with mostly Chinese-speaking victims from Asia; since 2022 victims have also been trafficked to Southeast Asia from South America, Western Europe and Eastern Africa, and new centres are reported in MENA, Central America and West Africa. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "new scam centres have been reported in the MENA, Central American, and in West African regions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Frauds launched from scam centres include investment fraud (Ponzi, romance baiting, crypto and Forex), fake art or precious metals sales, romance scams, impersonation fraud and unauthorised online gambling. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fake art or precious metals sales, romance scams, impersonation fraud, and unauthorized online gambling."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Trafficked individuals in Southeast Asian compounds are increasingly engaging in sextortion using AI-manipulated images; if a fraud approach fails, criminals may obtain sexually explicit images to extort victims. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using AI-manipulated images to blackmail victims"</sub>
