## Introduction

- **descriptive / context**: Financial fraud is largely underreported because victims are reluctant to come forward, so its true extent and impact are almost certainly not fully appreciated. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "due to the reluctance of its victims to come forward, financial fraud is largely underreported to law enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Research has focused on modi operandi, while much less is known about fraud offender profiles and how fraud is organised; collaboration between co-offenders varies and crime convergence needs better understanding. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it often focuses on modi operandi whereas much less is known about the different profiles of fraud offenders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report presents an assessment of global financial fraud trends, their regional and global manifestations, and the threat actors, to support strategies for collective action. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the purpose of this report is to present an assessment of global financial fraud trends"</sub>

## Methodology

- **descriptive / method**: The assessment follows an all-sources methodology, drawing mainly on INTERPOL data holdings (Notices and Diffusions on financial fraud), member country contributions, operational messages, and responses to the 2022 and 2024 Global Crime Trend Questionnaires, complemented by open-source documentation where appropriate. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The INTERPOL Global Financial Fraud Assessment has been elaborated following an all-sources methodology"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Financial fraud is defined from a law enforcement perspective as illegal activities aiming at financial gain through deceptive actions against and to the detriment of an individual or entity. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this report refers to financial fraud as an umbrella term encompassing a wide array of"</sub>

## Analytical framework

- **descriptive / definition**: Financial fraud is an interplay of motive (obtaining financial gain) and means (deception in various forms); an effective combination yields an unjust advantage detrimental to another, and fraud types evolve with social, economic, technological and legal change. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "financial fraud is fundamentally an interplay of two elements: motive (obtaining a financial gain) and means"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The assessment covers fraud against individuals and private entities and excludes fraud committed against government or public administration. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Assessment focuses on the commission of financial fraud against individuals and private entities"</sub>

## Offence Methods for Financial Fraud (Modi Operandi)

- **descriptive / definition**: Investment fraud involves deceiving people into investing in fake or misleading ventures, causing significant losses; victims often experience the highest losses compared with other individual fraud types. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Investment fraud involves deceiving people into investing money in fake or misleading ventures"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Investment fraud tactics include promising high returns, misrepresenting investments and creating a sense of urgency; schemes often use pyramid or Ponzi models to widen the victim base. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraudsters use various deceptive tactics, including promising high returns, misrepresenting investments and creating a sense of urgency"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Investment fraudsters target victims via social media, fraudulent websites or apps, tele-marketing from call centres or boiler rooms, and in person, and often mimic legitimate businesses. (p. 8)[^original]

## INTERPOL Data and Trends in Financial Fraud

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Between 2022 and 2023, 85 per cent of Red Notices and Diffusions issued by member countries were related to fraud, mostly issued by European countries, followed by Asia and Americas. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Between 2022 and 2023, 85 per cent of Red Notices and Diffusions issued by INTERPOL member countries were related to fraud"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2023 INTERPOL Financial and Anti-Corruption Centre (IFCACC) supported over 700 financial fraud cases initiated by member countries, concerning approximately USD 1.2 billion. (p. 10)[^original]
