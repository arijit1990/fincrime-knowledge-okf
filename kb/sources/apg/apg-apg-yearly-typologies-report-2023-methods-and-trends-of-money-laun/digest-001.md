## 1.1 Overview

- **descriptive / context**: In October 2018 the FATF amended its Recommendations to explicitly capture financial activities involving virtual assets and to set standards for regulation and supervision of VASPs; the relevant requirements are in Recommendation 15. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In October 2018, the FATF amended its Recommendations to explicitly capture financial activities involving virtual assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: VAs and related services can spur financial innovation and efficiency, but their distinct features also create opportunities for money launderers, terrorist financiers and other criminals. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VAs and related services have the potential to spur financial innovation and efficiency"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Cross-border rapid transfer lets criminals move value digitally, often outside the regulated financial system, obfuscate origin or destination of funds, and make it harder for reporting entities to spot suspicious activity in a timely manner. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "make it harder for reporting entities to identify suspicious activity in a timely manner"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Recent reports raise serious concerns about DPRK theft and laundering of hundreds of millions of dollars of VA to finance proliferation of weapons of mass destruction. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recent reports raise serious concerns about the Democratic People Republic of Korea"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Ransomware payments are almost exclusively demanded in virtual assets and ransomware incidents have grown significantly in recent years. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ransomware payments are almost exclusively demanded in virtual assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Terrorist groups including ISIL, Al Qaeda and affiliates, and extreme-right wing terrorist entities are increasingly using virtual assets to raise and move funds globally. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Terrorist groups, including ISIL, Al Qaeda and their affiliates, as well as extreme-right wing terrorist entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF Glossary defines a virtual asset as a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded or transferred and used for payment or investment, excluding digital representations of fiat currencies, securities and other financial assets covered elsewhere. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A virtual asset is a digital representation of value that can be digitally traded, or transferred, and can be used for payment or investment purposes."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The FATF Glossary defines a VASP as a natural or legal person not covered elsewhere under the Recommendations who, as a business, conducts for or on behalf of another person exchange between VAs and fiat, exchange between VAs, transfer of VAs, safekeeping or administration of VAs or instruments giving control over them, or participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer or sale of a VA. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Virtual asset service provider means any natural or legal person who is not covered elsewhere under the Recommendations"</sub>

## 1.2 Threats and Trends

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Chainalysis Crypto Crime Report 2023 reported illicit cryptocurrency volumes reached an all-time high in 2022, estimated at USD20.6 billion, representing only 0.24% of total cryptocurrency transaction volume. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illicit transaction volume estimated at USD20.6 billion.3 Importantly, this represents only 0.24%"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: ML and predicate offences are the crime types most commonly connected with VAs, while the prevalence of TF and PF through VA is increasing. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ML and predicate offences are the crime types most commonly connected with VAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Fraud, theft, hacking, blackmail and ransomware are the most common illicit activities through VAs according to APG members and blockchain analytics firms. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fraud, theft, hacking, blackmail and ransomware are the most common illicit activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Underlying predicate offences most commonly relate to online gambling, computer crimes, fraud, tax evasion, sale of illicit or controlled substances, child exploitation and human trafficking. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "online gambling, computer crimes, fraud, tax evasion"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: A key area of concern is the use of VAs to evade UN and other sanctions. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A key area of concern is the use of VAs to evade UN and other sanctions."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Chainalysis identified two types of entities and services used in ML through VA: intermediary services and wallets (including unhosted wallets, mixers, darknet markets) used to hold funds, obfuscate movement or swap assets, and fiat off-ramps that convert VAs to fiat, after which funds can no longer be traced on the blockchain. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Intermediary services and wallets, including unhosted wallets, mixers, darknet markets and other services."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Centralised exchanges were the biggest recipient of illicit cryptocurrency, with almost half of funds sent from illicit addresses going directly to them, which is notable given VASP AML/CFT obligations. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "centralised exchanges were the biggest recipient of illicit cryptocurrency"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Illicit fund volumes sent to DeFi protocols increased, commonly used by hackers stealing VAs; darkweb market operators sent illicit funds mainly to other illicit services or high-risk exchanges, and ransomware and fraud criminals sent large shares to mixers. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there was an increase in the volume of illicit funds sent to DeFi protocols"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Macao, China reports approximately 100 law enforcement investigations between 2018 and 2022 related to VA/VASP activities, with the trend observed to be increasing. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Approximately 100 investigations by law enforcement agencies between 2018 and 2022"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: From February 2022 the Philippines required all Covered Persons to use VA-specific transaction codes for CTR/STR filing, whereas VASPs previously used general codes for remittances, deposits or foreign currency exchanges. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "required all Covered Persons to use VA-specific transaction codes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: For large-value VA-specific transactions reported as of mid-October 2022, transfers within the same platform comprised 58%, VA transfers to external platforms 17%, fiat-to-VA conversions 15% and VA-to-fiat conversions 10%. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the majority comprised VA transfers within the same platform (58%)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: By transaction amount, fiat-to-VA conversions through online bank transfers were highest at 43%, followed by VA transfers to external platforms at 39% and VA transfers within platform at 13%. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fiat-to-VA conversions through online bank transfers accounted for the highest percentage (43%)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most Philippine STRs concerned VA transfers to external platforms, representing 93% of total value and 75% of total volume. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most STRs filed pertain to VA transfers to external platforms (93% of total value and 75% of total volume)"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Top suspicion reasons in Philippine VA STRs were fraudulent practices and unregistered investment solicitation (68% of amount), lack of underlying purpose or economic justification (14%), and deviations from financial profile or capacity (11%); other predicates were swindling (5%), child exploitation (0.15%) and photo/video voyeurism (0.03%). (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fraudulent practices and other violations relating to unregistered investment solicitation emerged as the top reason for suspicion (68% of total transaction amount)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: An AMLC study in March 2020 found investment fraud-related reasons were the most prevalent triggers for VASPs submitting STRs to the AMLC. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "investment fraud-related reasons (e.g., participation in swindling and illegal investment schemes) were the most prevalent triggers"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Persons of interest in Philippine cases used VA as a front for operations or as an explanation for unusually large funds, and some claimed funds came from gains on well-known foreign VASPs. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some used VA as a front for operations"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The Philippine statistics represent only a small subset of VA-related transactions in the financial system, though they suggest criminals may move funds across VASPs under different regulatory frameworks to hamper detection. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the cited statistics only represent a small subset of VA-related transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Hong Kong, China recorded 1,397 VA-related crime cases in 2021, a rise of 182.8% from 2020, with financial loss of HK$824.1 million, a four-fold increase on 2020. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In 2021, the number of VA-related crime cases reached 1,397, which was a rise of 182.8% from 2020."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Japan recorded 12,209 arrests for cybercrime in 2021, the highest ever. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The number of arrests for cybercrime in 2021 was 12,209, the highest number ever."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Wallets controlled by persons or VASPs in jurisdictions not obliged to identify the principal make owners of transferred crypto-assets hard to identify; almost all exchange transactions are online, giving high anonymity. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it becomes difficult to identify the owner of the crypto-assets transferred in a transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Drug traffickers in cases outside Japan converted proceeds into bitcoin via crypto ATMs using forged identification documents. (p. 10)[^original]

## 1.2 Threats and Trends

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Japan assesses VA transactions as relatively higher ML risk than other products and services of specified business operators, such as deposit-taking institutions and funds transfer providers. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VA transactions are assessed as relatively higher ML risk than other products and services"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In Japan it is believed that appropriate internal control systems for risks such as cyber security did not keep up with operators newly entering the crypto asset service provider business. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "did not keep up with the operators newly entering the business of crypto asset service providers"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Of the crypto-assets used in Japan by VA exchange service providers, one is known not to disclose transfer records, making transactions difficult to trace and so possibly more likely to be used for ML/TF. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one is known to not disclose transfer records, making it difficult to trace transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Another crypto-asset used in Japan is known to be poor at maintaining and updating its transfer records. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Another is known to be poor at maintaining and updating its transfer records."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The FATF guidance notes that using blockchain analysis tools to capture transaction histories, cited in Japan as a mitigation measure, faces challenges including technical constraints. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FATF’s guidance also points out challenges, including technical constraints"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Japan reports that where wallets are controlled by persons or providers in jurisdictions without obligations to identify the principal, identifying the owner of transferred crypto-assets becomes difficult. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it becomes difficult to identify the owner of the crypto-assets transferred in a transaction"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Because almost all crypto-asset exchange transactions in Japan are conducted online rather than in person, they have high anonymity. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Since almost all transactions handled by crypto- assets exchange service providers are not conducted in person"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Outside Japan, drug traffickers have converted drug proceeds into bitcoins via crypto ATMs using forged identification documents. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "drug traffickers convert criminal proceeds derived from drug trafficking into bitcoins via crypto ATMs using forged identification documents"</sub>
- **descriptive / expectation**: Given DeFi services with no claimed central controller and jurisdictions that do not yet regulate VASPs, the source states there is reason to expect a continued increase in the inherent ML/TF risk of VAs compared to other financial services. (p. 10)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is reason to expect a continued increase the inherent risk of VA being used for ML/TF"</sub>

## 1.5 Assessing the risk posed by VA/VASPs

- **mandatory / requirement**: FATF standards require jurisdictions to identify and assess ML/TF risks from VA activities and VASP operations, and apply a risk-based approach commensurate with identified risks. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF standards require jurisdictions to identify and assess the ML/TF risks emerging from VA activities"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: VASPs are required to take appropriate steps to identify, assess, manage and mitigate their enterprise and product ML/TF risks. (p. 12)[^original]
