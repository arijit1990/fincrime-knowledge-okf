## 1.6 Elements of Effective Policies for Crypto Asset: IMF

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The IMF paper observes that prohibition frameworks making all VA activity illegal may stifle innovation and drive illicit activity underground. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prohibition frameworks which make all VA activities illegal may stifle innovation and drive illicit activities underground."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The IMF notes prohibition is costly to enforce and, given VAs' borderless nature, increases incentives for circumvention and financial integrity risks. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prohibition can be costly to enforce and increase the incentive for circumvention"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The IMF notes that where substitute assets are not widely available legally, users are more motivated to access illegal markets and pay higher prices. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "When substitute assets are not widely available in the legal market, users may be more motivated to access illegal markets"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The IMF observes that VAs escaping prohibitions may create negative externalities, such as links to the dark web. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "VAs that escape prohibitions may generate additional negative externalities (e.g. more virtual asset activity may become linked to the dark web)."</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: The IMF says targeted restrictions could be justified to manage specific VA risks. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Targeted restrictions could be justified to manage specific risks."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The IMF states restrictions or prohibitions should not substitute for robust macroeconomic policies and credible institutional frameworks, the first line of defence against VA macro-financial risks. (p. 16)[^original]
