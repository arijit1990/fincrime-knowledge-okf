## 3.3.8 Singapore

- **descriptive / context**: Singapore states that from 2023 it has enhanced its AML/CFT legislative levers to keep them effective against the evolving crime landscape. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "From 2023, Singapore has enhanced our AML/CFT legislative levers to ensure that they remain effective against the evolving crime landscape."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Amendments to the CDSA and Computer Misuse Act in 2023 gave law enforcement agencies stronger powers against money mules and against those assisting others to retain criminal proceeds from crime and drug offences, aiming to deter facilitation of crime. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These changes empower authorities to take action against individuals acting as money mules in ML schemes"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In September 2024, 40 persons were charged in court for new offences connected to scams-related money mule activities, introduced through the 2023 CDSA and CMA amendments. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In September 2024, 40 persons were charged in court for new offences connected to scams-related money mule activities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Sentencing Advisory Panel published Guidelines recommending significant imprisonment as the norm for scams-related offences involving handing over bank accounts or disclosing Singpass credentials. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Sentencing Advisory Panel has also published Guidelines that recommend significant imprisonment sentences as the norm"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The Online Criminal Harms Act 2023 lets the Singapore Police Force direct online service providers to prevent suspected scam accounts or content from reaching users in Singapore and to disrupt recruitment of money mules. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The SPF is empowered to direct online service providers to prevent suspected scam accounts or content from interacting with or reaching users in Singapore."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Offences introduced in 2023 amendments came into force in February 2024 and apply to people who give others control of their bank accounts or share their Singpass credentials, when those others use them for scams and laundering. (p. 91)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These offences came into force in February 2024 and target persons who hand over control of their bank accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The Anti-Money Laundering and Other Matters Act 2024 enhances cross-agency data sharing to detect ML, TF and PF, lets law enforcement investigate ML linked to foreign serious environmental crimes, and strengthens prosecution of ML offences. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Act further enhances cross-agency data sharing to better detect ML, TF and PF"</sub>
- **permissive / requirement**: Under the 2024 Act the Court may order the sale of seized or restrained properties linked to suspected criminal activity where one of three criteria is met: parties consent; the property value is likely to depreciate or maintenance costs are undue; or the sale is in the interest of justice. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Parties consent to the sale. • The value of the property is likely to depreciate, or undue costs are involved in maintaining the property."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The Protection from Scams Bill 2024 empowers the SPF to issue Restriction Orders to banks to temporarily restrict banking transactions of targets of ongoing scams who refuse to believe they are being scammed. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This Bill empowers the SPF to issue Restriction Orders (RO) to banks to temporarily restrict the banking transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Restriction Orders are issued for 28 days in the first instance, giving the SPF time to take further measures; at the end of that period, if the person is still at risk, the RO may be renewed for up to 28 days at a time. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ROs will be issued for a period of 28 days in the first instance."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: Under the Protection from Scams Bill, money transfers out of the victim's accounts to other accounts are suspended, and Singapore will introduce a mechanism for individuals to apply to the SPF for access to their money for legitimate purposes. (p. 92)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money transfers (including online banking, mobile banking, and in person over-the-counter) out of the victim’s bank accounts"</sub>

## 4 - PROLIFERATION FINANCING METHODS AND TRENDS

- **descriptive / context**: Section 4 gives an overview of UNICRI's PF work, including the 1540 Compass reports and a report on the Southeast Asian perspective on PF, plus information from APG members on their PF risk assessments, publications and guidance. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "including overviews of the 1540 Compass reports and its report on the Southeast Asian perspective on PF"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Financial measures are described as among the most effective tools jurisdictions can use against proliferation activity, since preventive measures hinder raising and moving of funds by proliferation networks. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial measures are one of the most effective tools jurisdictions can use to counter proliferation activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Financial intelligence can give advance warning of illegal transfers of sensitive goods; shipments can be interdicted based on suspicious transaction reports, and financial investigation can follow money trails to identify facilitators. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Shipments can be discovered and interdicted on the basis of suspicious transaction reports by financial institutions."</sub>
- **descriptive / requirement**: The FATF Recommendations on countering PF set specific requirements for jurisdictions to establish legal authority and identify competent authorities to implement and enforce targeted financial sanctions in a timely manner. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They require jurisdictions to establish the necessary legal authority and identify competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Targeted financial sanctions apply to persons and entities that act on behalf of or under the direction of designated persons, are owned or controlled by them, or assist them in evading sanctions or violating resolution provisions. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Act on behalf of or under the direction of designated persons or entities."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Evaders may conceal involvement by exploiting trade finance products, clean payment services and money remittance services to procure and pay for proliferation-sensitive goods. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This includes the use of trade finance products, clean payment services, and money remittance services"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The FATF Recommendations require jurisdictions to tailor their PF risk assessments and mitigation strategies to the specific threats they face, including threats from their own context and exposure to high-risk jurisdictions. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The FATF Recommendations require jurisdictions to customise their PF risk assessments and mitigation strategies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Criminals exploit weaknesses in legal and operational systems across jurisdictions to raise and move funds, and PF actors use the same means. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Criminals often exploit weaknesses in legal and operational systems across different jurisdictions"</sub>
