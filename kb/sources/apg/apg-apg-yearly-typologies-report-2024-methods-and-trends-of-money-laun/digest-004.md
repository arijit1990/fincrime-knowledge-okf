## Beneficial ownership registers

- **expected / requirement**: An effective AML/CFT/CPF system should prevent legal persons and arrangements from being used for criminal purposes and make them sufficiently transparent. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prevent legal persons and arrangements from being used for criminal purposes. • Make legal persons and arrangements sufficiently transparent."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: An effective system should ensure adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and beneficial ownership information is available on a timely basis, and available to competent authorities. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Ensure that adequate, accurate and up-to-date basic and beneficial ownership information is available on a timely basis."</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: An effective system should subject persons who breach beneficial ownership measures to effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Persons who breach these measures are subject to effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Effective implementation makes the misuse of legal persons less attractive for criminals. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effective implementation of these measures makes the misuse of legal persons less attractive for criminals."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Many jurisdictions have created, are creating, or are considering centralised beneficial ownership repositories, and jurisdictions have flexibility to shape registers to their institutional context and types of legal persons. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions have significant flexibility to adjust their set-up to match the institutional context"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Member data shows beneficial ownership information requires significant uplift; little information is available on beneficial ownership of legal persons in the Asia/Pacific region. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Generally, there is little information available on the beneficial ownership of legal persons in the Asia/Pacific region."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Where members have a central public register, it commonly holds only basic information without beneficial ownership as defined by FATF Standards. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "commonly only basic information is maintained and does not include beneficial ownership information as defined by the FATF Standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Globally, register information is often unverified and not kept up to date, and legal frameworks may not require legal persons to keep beneficial ownership beyond immediate shareholders. (p. 29)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the quality of the information held on existing registers is often unverified and not kept up to date"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some jurisdictions are leading reforms, offering models for others to adapt at an appropriate scale to their own risk and context. (p. 29)[^original]

## Singapore AML/CFT Industry Partnership - Legal Persons - Misuse Typologies and Best Practices (2018)

- **descriptive / context**: Singapore's AML/CFT Industry Partnership was set up in April 2017 as a public-private partnership to identify, assess and mitigate ML/TF risks. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In April 2017, Singapore set up the AML/CFT Industry Partnership, a public-private partnership"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In May 2018 the Partnership published a best practice paper on legal persons misuse typologies; such papers highlight red flag customer behaviours and recommend preventive measures for financial institutions. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The AML/CFT Industry Partnership best practice papers highlight red flag customer behaviours or transaction patterns"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Professional intermediaries are key to setting up legal persons and providing ongoing corporate secretarial services, forming the first line of interaction with them. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Many professional intermediaries are key to setting up legal persons, as well as the provision of ongoing corporate secretarial services."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Law firm typology: a client requests legal help over a claimed dispute, deposits funds into the solicitor's client account, then after a claimed settlement asks for the balance to be sent to a different account, with no real work done. (p. 32)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Client A meets with Solicitor A requesting legal advice and assistance with potential litigation"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the law firm case the client had invented the claim; AML/KYC checks found nothing adverse, and returning the balance would have facilitated sham-litigation ML, while delay could tip off the client. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If Solicitor A had returned the balance of the monies to Client A, it would have facilitated a sham- litigation ML."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Dual nationality typology in company service providers: a foreign passport holder dissolved an offshore company and then sought to incorporate a South-East Asian company with a similar name using a passport from another jurisdiction. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Client A, who is a foreign passport holder of Jurisdiction A, approached a company service provider to incorporate a company"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most CSP involvement with legal persons occurs at incorporation, ad-hoc structural changes and annual return filing, so onboarding presents the highest ML/TF risk to CSPs. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As such, the on-boarding stage presents highest ML/TF risks to company service providers."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: CSPs can mitigate risks by identifying and verifying controllers, beneficial owners, shareholders, directors and authorised signatories, with examples including accounts prepared or audited for companies with nominee directors. (p. 33)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identification and verifications of controllers, beneficial owners, shareholders, directors and/or authorised signatories"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Where originals are not sighted, CSPs can accept copies certified as true copies by a suitably qualified person such as a notary, lawyer or accountant. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they can accept a copy of the document that is certified to be a true copy by a suitably qualified person"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: CSPs can use trusted commercially available screening databases during onboarding to identify risk indicators such as adverse news and politically exposed persons. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company service providers can use trusted commercially available screening databases during the on-boarding process"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: CSPs can understand the customer's purpose and business through interviews and additional information such as customer locations, beneficial owner occupation and source of funds, and bank records for significant share allotments. (p. 34)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company service providers can conduct interviews to understand the proposed business operations"</sub>
