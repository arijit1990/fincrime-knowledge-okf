- **descriptive / context**: Crowdfunding is growing; one cited estimate values the global crowdfunding market at USD 17.2 billion in 2020, expected to reach USD 34.6 billion by 2026. Previous FATF research shows online donation solicitation is a known TF method. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some estimates have valued the global crowdfunding market at USD 17.2 billion in 2020"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF report examines methods and techniques used by individual terrorists, terrorist organisations and violent extremists to finance terrorist activity through crowdfunding and sets out the types of crowdfunding posing the highest TF risk. (p. 78)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "builds deeper knowledge of the methods and techniques used by individual terrorists, terrorist organisations and violent extremists"</sub>

## Key findings, recommendations

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The report identifies four main ways crowdfunding platforms can be misused for TF, and terrorists typically combine multiple methods. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report notes four main ways in which crowdfunding platforms can be misused for TF purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Donations-based crowdfunding is noted by Global Network jurisdictions as the most vulnerable to TF misuse and often falls outside AML/CFT regulation. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Donations-based crowdfunding, noted by jurisdictions across the Global Network to be the most vulnerable to TF misuse"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Many jurisdictions do not systematically assess crowdfunding risks, so comprehensive data on its misuse is generally lacking; the diversity of models and fast-changing payment technologies also explain differing regulatory approaches. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "therefore comprehensive data about its misuse is still generally lacking"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Authorities face challenges proving funds were used for terrorism, and crowdfunding complexity, lack of data and anonymising techniques complicate tracing; intermediary platforms have limited TF expertise and often lack reporting mechanisms. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Intermediary platforms that offer crowdfunding services can have difficulty identifying TF activity on their sites"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Jurisdictions reported best practices including improving understanding of the domestic crowdfunding industry, including crowdfunding in their NRA, and conducting sector outreach, which proved effective in improving identification and reporting of TF. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Conducting outreach in the crowdfunding sector has also proved effective in improving identification and reporting"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: The report recommends that jurisdictions and stakeholders identify and understand TF risks of crowdfunding and have proportionate risk-based mitigation measures, in line with UNSCR 2462. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have proportionate risk-based measures in place to mitigate potential misuses, in line with United Nations Security Council Resolution 2462"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should be aware of the cross-border nature of fundraising activity and guard against their jurisdiction being used to finance terrorism abroad. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions should be aware of the cross-border nature of fundraising activity"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should also take into account that the crowdfunding sector is evolving and that TF risks may change over time. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions should also take into account that the sector is evolving and therefore TF risks may change over time."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF report includes a list of risk indicators to help public and private entities and the general public identify suspicious crowdfunding activity. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The report also includes a list of risk indicators to help public and private sector entities"</sub>

## Key findings, recommendations

- **expected / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should prioritise public-private information sharing and continuous outreach to private sector stakeholders, which improves detection and helps ensure legitimate crowdfunding is not restricted. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public-private information sharing should also be a priority for jurisdictions"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should fully implement the FATF Standards relevant to VAs, NPOs and money or value transfer services, and avoid treating crowdfunding as a siloed sector. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "jurisdictions should fully implement the FATF Standards relevant to VA, NPOs and money or value transfer services"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Jurisdictions should ensure a multi-stakeholder approach including competent authorities, private sector, civil society and academia when developing crowdfunding risk mitigation strategies, to ensure human rights due diligence and avoid hindering legitimate fundraising. (p. 80)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Finally, jurisdictions should ensure a multi-stakeholder approach that includes competent authorities"</sub>
