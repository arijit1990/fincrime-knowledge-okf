## Key findings

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nominee services have uses ranging from routine legitimate business purposes, to legitimate but misuse-vulnerable purposes, to cases where the primary purpose is hiding the beneficial owner. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Nominee services offered by CSPs can have many different uses, extending from those that are routinely used for legitimate business purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nominee arrangements are one of the most common devices for hiding the identity of those controlling shell companies, especially among the most problematic parts of the company formation industry. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "nominee arrangements are one of the most common devices for hiding the identity of those controlling shell companies"</sub>
- **expected / finding or result**: Lack of attention to misuse of nominee arrangements is a major vulnerability; greater enforcement attention is necessary, particularly for beneficial ownership registries. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Greater attention to enforcement is necessary, and this point applies in particular to beneficial ownership registries."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: On the illicit end, nominee services are often explicitly marketed to shopping clients as a way to keep the beneficial owner off the public record. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "nominee services are often explicitly marketed to clients shopping for shell companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Networks of shell companies with nominees threaten corporate transparency mainly because of their multi-jurisdictional nature, while rules remain single-jurisdiction, creating a fundamental disconnect. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Yet there is a fundamental disconnect between the multi-jurisdictional threat and the single-jurisdiction rules"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Enforcing effective regulation of CSPs and of nominee arrangements is critical to increasing transparency of beneficial ownership. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Enforcing effective regulation of CSPs and regulation of nominee arrangements is critical to increasing transparency"</sub>

## Enablers

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: CSPs offering nominee services are often lawyers acting as nominee directors or shareholders for a fee, may draft powers of attorney letting the true owner control the company while hidden, and may offer shelf companies with pre-existing bank accounts and nominee signatories. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The CSPs offering these kinds of services are often lawyers, acting as nominee directors or shareholders"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Nominee services create a significant vulnerability in the fight against ML, allowing criminals to hide identities and assets and undermining beneficial ownership registers. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This undermines the effectiveness of beneficial ownership registers and other transparency measures."</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: The report emphasises stronger regulation of nominee services, including increased transparency by requiring nominees to disclose their nominators and purpose of the arrangement. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring nominees to disclose their nominators and the purpose of the nominee arrangement."</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Licensing and supervision of professional nominees, subjecting them to rigorous supervision. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "licensing professional nominees and subjecting them to rigorous supervision."</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Enhanced due diligence by requiring CSPs to conduct thorough due diligence on clients and the purpose of the nominee arrangement. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requiring CSPs to conduct thorough due diligence on their clients and the purpose of the nominee arrangement."</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Strengthened international cooperation to address the multi-jurisdictional nature of nominee services. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "strengthening international cooperation to address the multi- jurisdictional nature of nominee services."</sub>

## A key challenge with professional enablers

- **descriptive / context**: Recent transparency advances include central beneficial ownership registries, global automatic exchange of bank account information, and mandatory disclosure rules for intermediaries to report schemes that hide beneficial owners. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Examples of these new policies include the establishment of central registries of beneficial ownership information"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The EU DAC 2 and 6 amendments required automatic exchange of bank account information and mandatory disclosure of schemes by intermediaries. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "required the automatic exchange of bank account information and the mandatory disclosure of schemes by intermediaries."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Expanded information collection and exchange has caused opposition, particularly regarding privacy, data protection and legal professional privilege. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has caused opposition, particularly in relation to the rights to privacy and information/data protection"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The European Court of Justice in November 2022 invalidated public access to beneficial ownership information for fighting ML, mainly on privacy grounds. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which invalidated public access to beneficial ownership information in relation to the fight against ML"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The ECJ recognised legal professional privilege to invalidate certain DAC6 mandatory disclosure reporting on cross-border tax arrangements. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recognised legal professional privilege to invalidate certain reporting of information based on The EU Council Directive 2011/16"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FATF and the Global Forum have warned against these developments and argued for limits on legal professional privilege. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have been warning against these developments and arguing for limits on legal professional privilege"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: FATF 2019 legal professionals guidance notes criminals may seek legal professionals to perform Recommendation 22 services to conceal activities and identity through privilege or secrecy. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "criminals may also seek out legal professionals (over other non-legal professions) to perform the services listed in Recommendation 22"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: FATF 2013 report notes privilege claims could impede and delay investigations, often requiring court resolution, which may lead investigators to seek evidence from alternative sources. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "claims of legal professional privilege or professional secrecy could impede and delay the criminal investigation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2013 FATF report's Annex includes 45 cases of legal professionals obscuring ownership through companies, trusts, bearer shares or trustee roles; Case 59 involved complex foreign structures and client-account transfers claiming privilege. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the report’s Annex included 45 cases of legal professionals obscuring ownership"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: There is an argument, noting differing views, that legal professional privilege should be limited to communications generated by legal professionals acting as legal professionals. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "there is an argument that the protection provided to clients under legal professional privilege should be specific"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The OECD Global Forum handbook states privilege applies only when an attorney acts as a legal representative; acting as nominee shareholder, trustee, settlor, director or under power of attorney is not covered. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "he cannot claim the attorney-client privilege with respect to any information resulting from and relating to any such activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: According to the Global Forum, ownership information would in principle not be subject to confidentiality. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ownership information would in principle not be subject to confidentiality"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Model Tax Convention 2017, a legal basis for exchange of information, states protection does not cover documents delivered to lawyers to avoid legally required disclosure, and identity of directors or beneficial owners is typically not protected. (p. 27)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "information on the identity of a person such as a director or beneficial owner of a company is typically not protected"</sub>
