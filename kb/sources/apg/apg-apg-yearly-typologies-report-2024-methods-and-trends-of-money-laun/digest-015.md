## 4.6.1 Republic of the Marshall Islands

- **descriptive / context**: The Marshall Islands operates a large, well-regulated open shipping registry, with the third-largest fleet in the world (over 5,600 vessels as of 31 January 2024). (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "operates a large, well-regulated open shipping registry. It is the legal domicile"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Revenue from the ship registry and non-resident domestic entities (41,000 entities) is the Marshall Islands' third largest source of income. (p. 106)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Revenue from the ship registry and non- resident domestic entities (NRDEs) (41,000 entities) is the Marshall Islands"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Trust Company of the Marshall Islands Inc. (TCMI) is the Registrar for NRDEs and the Maritime Administrator, and has implemented measures in shipping registration to mitigate PF and targeted financial sanctions evasion risks. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TCMI implemented a range of measures in its shipping registration processes to respond to and mitigate PF"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The Maritime Administrator screens all parties to a vessel registration (owners, beneficial owners, operators), vessel identifiers and seafarers against UNSCR lists and other sanctions and criminal lists using a commercial database. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it screens all parties to a vessel registration (including all owners, beneficial owners, and operators)"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Known parties are checked automatically every 12-24 hours, false positives are cleared under a detailed methodology with escalating approvals, and compliance personnel are notified automatically if a party is later listed or subject to criminal proceedings. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It also automatically checks all known parties every 12-24 hours and clears any false positives"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: The Maritime Administrator monitors vessels for sanctions evasion with a location monitoring solution, issues guidance on illicit shipping practices, and all registered vessels are subject to ongoing inspections including an annual inspection by a dedicated nautical inspector. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All vessels registered in the Marshall Islands are subject to ongoing inspections requirements, including an annual inspection"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: TCMI could strike a ship from the registry, revoke navigation permission or annul a company if required to freeze or seize entities under a UN sanction; one Marshall Islands entity listed by the UN was detected and forcibly dissolved before UN listing. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One Marshall Islands entity that was listed on a UN sanctions list (Kingly Won International Co., Ltd.,) was detected and forcibly dissolved"</sub>

## 4.6.2 Cook Islands

- **descriptive / context**: The Marshall Islands Shipping Registry was a founding member of the Registry Information Sharing Compact (RISC) in 2019, which lets flag States notify each other when a vessel is denied registration or de-registered for suspected sanctions-related activity; RISC includes over 40% of world merchant shipping tonnage. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The RISC includes over 40% of the world’s merchant shipping tonnage."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Cook Islands 2015 NRA assessed its PF exposure as low, negligible links with DPRK and Iran, and no identified or frozen funds of designated persons. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In its 2015 NRA, the Cook Islands assessed its exposure to PF as low"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The Cook Islands financial sector is prohibited from conducting business with DPRK and must apply enhanced due diligence and monitoring, including filing STRs, to business with Iran. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is prohibited from conducting business with DPRK but must apply enhanced due diligence and monitoring"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Since 2010 competent authorities were alerted to some interactions with DPRK and Iran, all related to shipping activities; in 2015 a bank lodged an STR over funds received from Iran linked to ship registration. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Since 2010 competent authorities had been alerted to some interactions with DPRK and Iran"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2016 the FSC found an insurance license applicant had insured DPRK vessels in 2005; the applicant said the business had long since discontinued and the FSC was satisfied. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the applicant had previously provided insurance to DPRK vessels in 2005"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The 2017 Review of Risk identified potential PF exposure through the shipping registry, which the Ministry of Transport outsources to a private contractor, Maritime Cook Islands; the Ministry is unfamiliar with TFS obligations and sees its role as overseeing the contractor for IMO standards compliance. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The MoT is unfamiliar with TFS obligations and considers its role to be limited to overseeing"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: As of November 2017 there were 566 vessels on the Ships Register, about half privately owned yachts, with corporate owners from 65 jurisdictions, most commonly the Marshall Islands, Cook Islands, Singapore, British Virgin Islands, Russia and the United States. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "As of November 2017, there were 566 vessels registered on the Ships Register"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: From December 2016 MCI subscribed to a commercial sanctions screening and vessel tracking provider; it screens the controlling principal at registration or re-registration, screens associated entities daily, and examines vessel movement history for high-risk areas. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "MCI screens the “controlling principal” of a vessel"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: MCI does not appear to screen beneficial owners or controllers other than the controlling principal, and there is no evidence the shipping registry was screened for TFS under UNSCRs 1373 or 1267 before December 2016. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "MCI does not appear to screen entities with beneficial ownership or control of a vessel other than the controlling principal"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Financial Transaction Reporting (Maritime Cook Islands) Regulations 2017 designate MCI as a reporting institution with limited obligations, including CDD and sanctions screening of the controlling principal and STR duties, but do not require screening of persons other than the controlling principal; the FIU supervises MCI. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "However, the MCI Regulations do not require MCI to conduct sanctions screening on persons other than the controlling principal"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The National AML/CFT Strategy included strengthening the Cook Islands' TFS-against-PF regime, and the National Anti-Money Laundering Coordination Committee adopted a PF action plan in 2016; no sanctioned entities have been identified operating in or moving funds through the Cook Islands. (p. 109)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There have been no sanctioned entities identified as operating in or moving funds or assets through the Cook Islands."</sub>
