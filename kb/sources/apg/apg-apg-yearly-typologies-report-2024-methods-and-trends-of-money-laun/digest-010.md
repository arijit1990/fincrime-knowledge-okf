## 3 - MONEY LAUNDERING AND TERRORISM FINANCING TRENDS

- **descriptive / context**: Jurisdictions with weak or ineffective controls are especially attractive to money launderers and terrorist financiers, who exploit the complexity of the global financial system, differences between domestic laws and the speed of cross-border money movement. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions with weak or ineffective controls are especially attractive for money launderers and financiers of terrorism."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: ML/TF activity in one jurisdiction can have serious adverse effects across borders and globally, and combating TF remains central to many jurisdictions' counter-terrorism strategies. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ML/TF activity in one jurisdiction can have serious adverse effects across borders, and even globally."</sub>

## 3.1.1 Australia

- **descriptive / context**: Australia's Money Laundering National Risk Assessment assessed crimes generating illicit proceeds and laundering methods and channels, the drivers influencing the environment, and how Australia mitigates money laundering, including where improvements could be made. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It also examines the international and domestic drivers that influence the Australian environment"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The key theme of the Australian ML NRA is 'persistence': continued exploitation of historically used channels such as banks, remitters and casinos, of high-value assets such as luxury watches, vehicles and real estate, and of professional service providers building complex structures. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The key theme to emerge from the assessment is ‘persistence’"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A further theme is criminal exploitation of legitimate channels, assets and services such as cash, bank accounts, payments technology, business structures and trusts used to place, layer and integrate proceeds. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Core features of Australia’s domestic economy, such as cash, bank accounts, payments technology"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Opacity, anonymity and lack of transactional visibility underpin many laundering activities in Australia, with cash, trusts, identity crime, mule accounts and third-party transactions a mainstay of laundering. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Underpinning many money laundering activities in Australia is opacity, anonymity and a lack of transactional visibility."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Australia's economy is exploited by money launderers, and lawful domestic financial channels remain fundamentally important for placing, layering and integrating funds domestically and internationally. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Lawful domestic financial channels remain fundamentally important pathways for money launderers"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Australia is attractive for storing and integrating criminal proceeds because of its stable political system, open and free economy, independent legal system, well-developed financial services sector and strong real estate market. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Australia remains an attractive destination to store and integrate criminal proceeds"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Crimes generating the highest illicit proceeds needing laundering are assessed as drug offences (cultivation, manufacture and trafficking), tax and revenue crimes, and defrauding government-funded programs. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Crimes generating the highest value of illicit proceeds that require laundering are assessed to be drug offences"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Criminals continue to use cash, luxury goods, real estate, domestic banks, casinos and remitters, and criminal use of digital currency, digital currency exchanges, unregistered remitters and bullion dealers is increasing. (p. 72)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Criminal use of digital currency, digital currency exchanges, unregistered remitters and bullion dealers is increasing."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Faster financial transactions make it harder for reporting entities to identify and freeze suspicious transfers before funds leave an account, especially when individuals use multiple products across institutions. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The increased speed of financial transactions in recent years has made it harder for reporting entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Opaque legal structures created in Australia can obscure ultimate beneficial owners of corporate entities, assets and financial infrastructure, creating a significant money laundering vulnerability. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They create a significant money laundering vulnerability for Australian authorities and industry."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Professional service providers, witting or unwitting, that establish or operate corporate and financial infrastructure also reduce visibility of beneficial ownership and create vulnerabilities. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The use of professional service providers, either witting or unwitting, to establish, advise on or operate"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Terrorism Financing in Australia National Risk Assessment assessed risks of methods and channels used to finance and support terrorism, the drivers influencing the environment, and how Australia mitigates terrorism financing. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Terrorism Financing in Australia National Risk Assessment84 assessed the risk associated with the methods"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The number of violent extremists with both intention and capability to attack in Australia has decreased, though extremists across ideological spectrums continue to connect and radicalise; attacks most likely by a lone actor or small group. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The number of violent extremists who have both the intention and capability to undertake terrorist attacks in Australia has decreased"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Australia's terrorism financing environment is small scale and low value; lone actors often self-fund and frequently lack a detectable financial element. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Australia’s terrorism financing environment is small scale and low value."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Australia is primarily an exporter of small-scale terrorism financing flows to offshore groups, most likely Islamic State, al-Qa'ida and affiliates, and Hamas and Hezbollah to a lesser extent; funds for foreign fighter travel have decreased. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Australia is primarily an exporter of small-scale terrorism financing funds flows to offshore terrorist organisations"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Terrorists largely use established fundraising methods, including personal funds and individual donations often under charitable guise; social media, communication and crowdfunding platforms are integral to recruitment and fundraising; banking, remittance and cash are preferred over complex schemes, and digital currency use has risen but is not expected to overtake simple methods within three years. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An uptick in the use of digital currencies has been observed, but there is no evidence to suggest it will overtake more simple methods over the next three years."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: TF is difficult to detect because regulated channel transactions mirror legitimate activity; faster products hinder freezing transfers; funds may move through cash or unregistered remittance dealers. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TF can be difficult to detect. Transactions conducted through regulated financial channels often mirror legitimate financial activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Australia's CTF regime, operational capabilities and investigative outcomes have evolved substantially; CTF working groups share intelligence across agencies and borders, and public-private partnerships enable near-real-time information sharing with the largest FIs. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Public private partnerships have enabled national security agencies and members of Australia’s largest FIs to share classified information in near-real time."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Financial intelligence is evolving from a largely reactive role towards a proactive role in countering violent extremism, which is critical given sometimes rapid escalation to violence. (p. 73)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial intelligence efforts continue to evolve from a largely reactive role"</sub>
