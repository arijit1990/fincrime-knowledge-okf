## 2.13 Philippines / Case Study # 59: No OTP, no mercy

  <sub>anchor: "The bank noted that there were 1,122 EMI transfers from 988 various individuals totalling PHP 28,300,872"</sub>

## 2.13 Philippines / Case Study # 63: Foreign currency and false promises

  <sub>anchor: "the profits will be distributed by 60:40, i.e. 60% for the investors and 40% as management fees"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Based on SEC reports, from 2017 to 2020 Person S and Person A of Company X, doing business as Company Z, invited the public to invest in foreign currency trading under a fund management service called Investment P. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person S and Person A of Company X (doing business under the name Company Z) invited the public to invest in foreign currency trading"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Promotions in March, June and November 2019 cut the management fee to 30% for deposits made in those months, encouraging more and larger deposits. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the deposits received within the said months are only subject to a 30% management fee instead of 40%"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Company X was registered in July 2018 as a consultancy but was not authorised to take investments from the public; in February 2020 the scheme was restructured into Company Y, offering a re-investment option. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Company X would undergo corporate restructuring"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The SEC issued an advisory in August 2021 and a Cease and Desist Order in December 2021 against Company X, Company Y and named persons; eight complainants lost PHP 4,750,025.60, and further victims reportedly invested PHP 80 million. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the total investment of the eight complainants is PHP 4,750,025.60 (~ USD 81,850)"</sub>

## 2.13 Philippines / Case Study # 65: Storefront or smokescreen?

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Store X's account, owned on DTI records by Person J but fully controlled by Person Z, a resident of Jurisdiction C, was used as a remittance account for drug proceeds; Person Z also opened accounts with fictitious IDs. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A certain Person Z, who is a resident of Jurisdiction C, however, has full control of the said account"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Store X, established in 2017, had deposits of about PHP 109 million in one year, averaging PHP 9.12 million gross sales per month, which was considered highly unlikely for a new store. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This averages gross sales of PHP 9.12 million (~ USD 157,100) per month"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Observed modus operandi: foreigners direct Filipinos to open sole proprietorship retail businesses whose accounts are fully controlled by foreigners through a Special Power of Attorney. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using a Special Power of Attorney (SPA)"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Another method used an account with an automatic transfer facility; the owner gave Person Z an SPA, making Person Z the authorised signatory who declared the account's income as Store X sales, raising suspicion of beneficial ownership. (p. 42)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person Z declared that the source of income of the account is from Store X"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Person Z's links to drug trafficking included a PHP 120,000 transaction in 2018 linked to a drug suspect, referral by an informant in the Visayas, and appearance on a phone seized in ARMM. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In 2018, the said bank account had one transaction worth PHP 120,000 (~ USD 2,000) linked to the said drug suspect"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Over three months in 2017 Person Z's account received PHP 114.6 million in credits averaging PHP 548,000 per transaction, without apparent legal or economic justification; in 2018 it received PHP 4.12 million from Person S, a subject of the W Enterprise Case. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For a period of three months in 2017, Person Z’s account had deposits/credits, totalling PHP 114.6 million"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Both accounts (Store X's and Person Z's, at two universal banks) received drug proceeds; Person Z's account, valued at about PHP 2.61 million, was frozen in 2018. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The account of Person Z with an estimated value of PHP 2.61 million (~ USD 45,000) was frozen in 2018"</sub>

## 2.13 Philippines / Case Study # 66: Nailed it

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Persons W and X, residents of Jurisdiction C, opened Philippine accounts claiming Company J, a hardware company, as income source; DTI records showed Company J registered to Person Y, a Filipino national. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They declared a hardware company – Company J as their source of income"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A drug enforcement agency found Person A, imprisoned for drug trade, still receiving drug proceeds through associates across several universal bank accounts; the joint account of Persons W and Y received about PHP 6 million in under two months. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The said account received an approximate amount of PHP 6 million (~ USD 103,250) in less than two months"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Links to Agency H (case study 58) were established via deposit slips; Person W's account received PHP 295,000 cash from drug suspects and Agency H's account received about PHP 2.7 million. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Person W’s bank account in Universal Bank B received a PHP 295,000 (~ USD 5,000) cash deposit"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: DTI and SEC certified no businesses registered under Persons W and X; Persons W's registration documents were fictitious, and Company J's address differed from the bank form; SEC had no records for Person Y. (p. 43)[^original]

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Person X transacted about PHP 1.5 billion in one year through six accounts at six universal banks, with flows not commensurate with declared funds. (p. 43)[^original]

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Person B's accounts had cash deposits of about PHP 800 million in one year; cash inflows of PHP 184 million and outflows of PHP 50 million in seven months; 503 cash and cheque deposits totalling PHP 133 million and 89 cheque transactions totalling PHP 128 million in two months.
