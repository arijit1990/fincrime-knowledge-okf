## AI-enabled surveillance and evasion in criminal networks

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Figure 4 (AI-driven business email compromise workflow) describes stages: reconnaissance (AI crawlers gather data from social media, corporate websites and other public information and build detailed profiles of individuals, companies and communication habits); targeting and attack crafting (AI generates convincing emails mimicking the writing style of the targeted executive or colleague, tailored to the target's position); deepfake voice cloning for phishing (replicating the voice of senior officials such as the CEO in calls or voice messages to authorize wire transfers). (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI crawlers gather vast amounts of data from social media platforms, corporate websites"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Figure 4 continued: communication frequency (AI automates email communication and sends multiple reminders or follow-ups, keeping the target engaged); social engineering (AI analyzes email thread flow and escalates requests to seem urgent and legitimate, adjusts tone to reflect urgency or authority, or switches to a different employee if the target resists); adaptation (real-time analysis of email communications and organizational activity lets attackers adapt fraudulent requests to changing conditions). (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI can analyze the flow of email threads and escalate requests in a way that makes the fraud"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Figure 5 (chatbots and types; text scrambled) distinguishes rule-based chatbots, which use decision trees and scripted responses for straightforward, repetitive queries, from AI-powered chatbots, which use machine learning, natural language processing and AI to interpret and generate conversational responses; a chatbot is a software application designed to simulate conversation with human users, used on messaging platforms, mobile applications and websites as a virtual assistant. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Use of decision treesRule based and scripted responses,for straightforward andrepetitive queries"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Criminals use public online data for behavioural analysis and target profiling: perpetrators of trafficking in persons and smuggling of migrants may deploy AI models to scrape social media for individuals showing indicators of financial vulnerability or employment-related distress, traits commonly exploited in fraudulent recruitment and trafficking schemes. Traffickers already mine social media for recruitment, and AI amplifies their ability to identify high-risk individuals at scale. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may deploy AI models to scrape social media platforms in search of individuals who display indicators of financial vulnerability"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Criminals craft fake documents and synthetic identities at scale to bypass KYC and verification processes: AI can generate high-quality fake passports, driver's licences and bank statements that deceive human checkers and sometimes automated systems. One fraud report noted ID card forgeries accounted for 75 per cent of identity fraud, many now AI-assisted to change names, photos and details convincingly. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "ID card forgeries accounted for 75 per cent of identity fraud"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UNODC states that continued integration of AI into criminal operations suggests existing KYC and due diligence mechanisms risk becoming ineffective within the next two to three years, as synthetic identities and fabricated documents surpass current detection capacities. Financial institutions may unknowingly onboard non-existent people, creating vulnerabilities exploitable for money laundering, credit fraud and other illicit financial activity; rapid large-scale generation of synthetic identities could overwhelm KYC systems and reduce manual verification effectiveness, and identity fraud will accelerate technologically and geographically. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "existing KYC and due diligence mechanisms risk becoming ineffective within the next two to three years"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In the context of online child sexual exploitation and abuse (OCSEA), offenders could use AI to identify and track children's digital footprints; facial recognition or reverse image search tools may be repurposed to locate known victims, and age-progression algorithms built for missing child investigations could hypothetically be misused to generate updated depictions of victims in order to locate them. The brief notes the dual-use nature of such tools. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "age-progression algorithms, originally developed for missing child investigations, could hypothetically be misused"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Generative AI already enables highly convincing synthetic child sexual abuse material without involving an actual child at production. As tools become more sophisticated over the next two to three years, there is a real risk reporting mechanisms and law enforcement channels will be inundated with fabricated content, complicating identification of genuine victims and diverting limited resources. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A wider problem lies in the emerging possibility of _entirely generated victims_"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Advanced malware strains may use machine learning to determine optimal timings or traffic patterns for data exfiltration, blending into legitimate network behaviour and making detection by intrusion prevention systems significantly harder. By automating document forgery, criminals facilitate money laundering (opening mule accounts with fake IDs and account takeovers) and illegal immigration support (false documents). (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "determine optimal timings or traffic patterns for data exfiltration"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Criminal actors are exploring AI to hinder identification and obscure operations. In online gambling fraud in parts of Asia, syndicates reportedly set up fraudulent digital casinos run by people trafficked for forced criminality and are believed in some instances to have used face-swapping during video interactions to conceal key figures' identities, obstructing identification by victims or authorities. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "criminal syndicates reportedly established fraudulent digital casinos operated by individuals trafficked for forced criminality"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Methods for evading detection and automating illicit activities are frequently shared across dark web forums, speeding adoption by criminal networks; threat actors have often integrated emerging technologies faster than regulatory frameworks or enforcement capacities can keep pace, and AI exploitation is a reason the criminal ecosystem is 'rapidly evolving' and difficult to contain. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "frequently shared across dark web forums, accelerating their adoption among criminal networks"</sub>

## Case studies

- **descriptive / context**: A growing number of cases show automation and AI exploited to facilitate crime in Southeast Asia and the Pacific, including deepfake use in extortion and fraud and AI tools integrated into wider organized crime, often outpacing existing prevention and enforcement mechanisms. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "automation and AI are being exploited to facilitate criminal activity in Southeast Asia and the Pacific"</sub>

## Case Study 1. Thailand Cyber Police Disrupt AI-Driven Deepfake Scam Networks

- **descriptive / case example**: In late 2024 and early 2025 Thai and Cambodian authorities dismantled two major scam networks using AI-powered deepfakes to defraud victims across Southeast Asia and beyond, using low-cost AI tools to enhance romance scams, impersonation fraud and investment scams. Both cases were led by Thailand's Cyber Crime Investigation Bureau (CCIB) with Cambodian law enforcement support and information from regional and international partners. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dismantled two major scam networks leveraging AI-powered deepfake technologies to defraud victims across Southeast Asia"</sub>

## AI-enabled romance and investment fraud in Thailand

- **descriptive / case example**: First case: Thai nationals arrested in Chiang Mai managed a Chinese crime group-backed network combining romance and investment fraud, using AI-generated video calls with synthetic faces, real-time facial animation and lip-sync to build trust with victims in Thailand, Viet Nam and the United States. Victims were directed to a fake platform called 'Streaming' showing fabricated returns. The network operated like a corporate entity with separate teams for victim engagement, technical operations and money laundering. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The group used AI-generated video calls, featuring synthetic faces with real-time facial animation and lipsync"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The CCIB arrested 20 suspects across 11 provinces, seizing digital evidence including automated CRM-style dashboards, mobile devices preloaded with scam scripts and fake app interfaces. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "CCIB arrested 20 suspects across 11 provinces"</sub>

## Deepfake call scam centres and identity impersonation in Cambodia–Thailand

- **descriptive / case example**: Second case: Thai nationals running a scam centre in Poipet, Cambodia were arrested for using AI voice cloning and real-time deepfake video to impersonate law enforcement and financial authorities. Per the CCIB, all 119 repatriated suspects were part of fraud schemes that evolved into sextortion in numerous cases. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "all 119 repatriated suspects were part of fraud schemes that evolved into sextortion in numerous cases"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The scammers used a tool called 'AIFace' to overlay facial images of real police officers (scraped from public sources) over their own faces on live video calls; it featured real-time facial tracking, voice adjustment and bilingual capabilities, increasing authenticity. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "tool known as “AIFace” that allowed them to overlay facial images of real police officers"</sub>

## Implications and way forward

- **descriptive / case example**: UNODC had earlier identified AIFace and similar deepfake technologies through open-source intelligence (OSINT) monitoring of Telegram-based markets, where they were offered as a subscription service packaged with scam playbooks; the CCIB confirmed AIFace use by matching the user interface to visual evidence from earlier information sharing. A follow-up raid hit two key venues in Poipet, including a reported 12-story building run by Thai nationals with mule account management, cryptocurrency scams and biometric identity scams. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "available as a subscription service and packaged with scam playbooks"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A similar case emerged recently in Cambodia where comparable AI-enabled methods were used to defraud nationals of the Republic of Korea, suggesting widespread, potentially cross-regional expansion of such tools among transnational scam networks. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "used to defraud nationals of the Republic of Korea"</sub>

## Technical modus operandi

- **descriptive / method**: Technical analysis showed both networks combined deepfake overlays and AI-driven voice synthesis, with scripted dialogues in local languages, automated emotional manipulation and financial requests coordinated through CRM-like software, enabling rapid scaling while bypassing conventional fraud detection measures. In both cases AI tools reduced the technical skill needed, letting low-level operators impersonate officials or romantic partners convincingly. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "AI tools reduced the need for technical skill, enabling low-level operators to impersonate officials or romantic partners convincingly"</sub>

## Case Study 2. Deepfake child sexual abuse material found in a major seizure in Thailand

- **descriptive / case example**: In February 2025 Thailand's Department of Special Investigation (DSI) began proceedings against a Thai national charged with possession of child sexual abuse material (CSAM), using intelligence from the US FBI via the US Embassy in Bangkok. Forensic examination of the suspect's computer yielded over 20,000 CSAM files, one of Thailand's largest seizures, including at least one AI-generated deepfake image of a child in a sexually explicit context. No evidence of distribution was found; the case went to the Office of the Special Prosecutor on charges under relevant Criminal Code provisions. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "produced over 20,000 digital files of CSAM, making it one of the largest CSAM seizures in Thailand"</sub>

## Implications and way forward

- **expected / expectation**: UNODC says regional cooperation frameworks should be strengthened so that governments can respond jointly, share intelligence and harmonise legal approaches to AI misuse. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regional cooperation frameworks should be strengthened to promote joint responses, intelligence sharing, and harmonized legal approaches to AI misuse"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Digital platforms must be held accountable for detecting and removing synthetic content that is used for illicit purposes. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "digital platforms must be held accountable for detecting and removing synthetic content used for illicit purposes"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The brief warns that without a coordinated and proactive response, Southeast Asia risks becoming a key environment for testing, refining and exporting AI-enabled deception by transnational criminal groups. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Without a coordinated and proactive response, Southeast Asia risks becoming a key environment"</sub>

## Technical modus operandi

- **descriptive / case example**: The suspect did not distribute the material but stored data in file formats and structures resembling files used in encrypted CSAM distribution networks; metadata suggested anonymization practices typical of darkweb server data, and dissemination via Telegram and Line is suspected. Generative AI tools use prompts to create synthetic abuse imagery with little technical skill and are often bundled with prompt guides shared in illicit forums, heightening detection challenges. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Many of the tools are bundled with prompt guides shared in illicit online forums"</sub>

## Implications and way forward

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The case illustrates increasing abuse of generative AI to produce synthetic CSAM. Even without an identifiable harm to a real child, such material normalizes exploitation and demand within offender networks. Some jurisdictions such as Thailand have laws that do not directly address AI-generated abuse content, limiting investigations and prosecutions; low-skill tools combined with encrypted services and anonymization make detection and removal difficult, and cooperation from platforms such as Telegram and Line with law enforcement remains an area for improvement. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they do not directly address AI generated abuse content, which limits the amount of potential investigations and prosecution"</sub>

## Case Study 3: Deepfake videos used in investment scams in Malaysia

- **descriptive / case example**: In early 2024 Malaysian authorities became aware of deepfake videos originating outside the country featuring fake representations of public figures including the Prime Minister and all cabinet members, inserted in falsified news reports and spread via social media and messaging platforms to promote staged, purportedly government-endorsed investment schemes. Victims suffered financial loss, and at least five separate instances of deepfake scam videos were noted, prompting statements and investigations by the Royal Malaysia Police and the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "At least five separate instances of deepfake videos being used for scams were noted"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Fraudulent material passing as official promotional material or interviews was altered to include AI-generated likenesses and voices. Authorities recognized the perpetrators were likely operating from outside Malaysia, using digital infrastructure to advertise fake investment opportunities endorsed by a real police service, with promises of immediate financial returns. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "perpetrators of this scam were likely operating from outside of Malaysia to deceive a local audience"</sub>

## Implications and way forward

- **descriptive / recommendation**: Measures listed to counter the risks from synthetic child sexual abuse material include criminalising synthetic CSAM through legislative reform and building digital forensic capacity. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legislative reform to specifically criminalize synthetic CSAM, the development of digital forensic capacity"</sub>

## Technical modus operandi

- **descriptive / method**: The scams built realistic deepfake videos of public figures (imitating hand movements, speech and voice patterns), shared on social media, messaging apps and cloned websites, most often imitating a news story or press event. Victims were diverted to publicly available investment platforms or told to transfer funds to the perpetrator on the basis of fake endorsements. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "imitations of hand movements, speech, voice patterns that realistically resembled the voices of original politicians"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The scam operations were transnational, using anonymized digital infrastructure or foreign-hosted platforms deliberately to avoid detection. Many links referenced organizations to suggest genuine government endorsement from different jurisdictions, and were sponsored posts or coordinated efforts to impersonate government brands on social media; deceivers used synthetic media to scale and customize deception for specific audiences and used networks to amplify reach. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using anonymized digital infrastructure or foreignhosted platforms intentionally to avoid detection"</sub>

## strategic response

- **descriptive / context**: UNODC states the convergence of AI and organized crime is a present reality with Southeast Asia at the epicentre and the Pacific increasingly drawn in. Meaningful response needs coordinated action from governments, the private sector, AI developers and international institutions and a shift from reactive enforcement to proactive resilience, built on consistent capabilities, trusted partnerships and cross-border cooperation. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a fundamental shift from reactive enforcement to proactive resilience"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Legal and policy reform: legislative frameworks should be updated in line with the UN Convention against Cybercrime to address AI-enabled threats, criminalizing deepfake abuse, synthetic identity fraud and AI-generated exploitative content. The brief says the Convention supports reform by requiring States Parties to adopt legislative measures criminalizing intentional, unauthorized access to information and communications technology systems; clear statutory definitions, harmonized cross-border standards and enforceable safeguards will close legal loopholes. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Legislative frameworks updated in line with the UN Convention against Cybercrime to address AI-enabled threats"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Institutional capacity building: police officers, prosecutors and judges require specialized training and access to advanced digital forensic tools; strengthening digital forensics units, establishing regional information-sharing platforms and promoting cross-border exchange of court-admissible evidence and case information are described as essential to operational readiness and consistent responses. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Police officers, prosecutors, and judges require specialized training and access to advanced digital forensic tools"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Public education and digital resilience: sustained digital and AI literacy and awareness campaigns are needed to reduce victimization, as online scams, sextortion and synthetic identity fraud feed into trafficking, money laundering and other organized crime; societal resilience is as critical as law enforcement capacity. Companies and governments should invest in training on AI-enabled cybercrime so employees can detect and report AI-enabled scam attempts. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Companies and governments should also invest in providing training on AI-enabled cybercrime"</sub>

## Implications and way forward

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Deepfakes of political leaders promoting investment schemes erode public trust in government communications, and laws on deceptive synthetic media may prove unenforceable. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Statutes, existing laws or new laws for deceptive synthetic media may prove un-enforceable"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: To improve detection, reporting and removal of scam-related deepfakes, law enforcement agencies, financial regulators and social media platforms are expected to collaborate. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is essential that law enforcement agencies, financial regulators, and social media platforms collaborate"</sub>

## strategic response

- **expected / expectation**: Law enforcement and cybersecurity actors need access to cutting-edge AI-driven detection systems that can spot deepfakes, trace synthetic content and flag unusual behavioural patterns. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Law enforcement and cybersecurity actors need access to cutting-edge AI-driven detection systems"</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Cross-border cooperation: transnational AI-powered crimes demand coordinated responses; strengthening intelligence sharing, joint operations, extradition agreements and mutual legal assistance mechanisms will help dismantle syndicates exploiting digital and jurisdictional fragmentation, so cases with victims and offenders in different jurisdictions are investigated with the same efficiency and consistency under similar and compatible laws. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Strengthening intelligence sharing, joint operations, extradition agreements, and mutual legal assistance mechanisms"</sub>
- **permissive / recommendation**: A regional hub for timely, secure information sharing, backed by clear legal frameworks and procedural standards, could help build trusted channels between law enforcement and private entities. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A regional hub for timely and secure information sharing, supported by clear legal frameworks and procedural standards"</sub>
