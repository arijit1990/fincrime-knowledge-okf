## 6.3. Supporting diagonal co-operation

- **descriptive / context**: Diagonal foreign exchanges can occur directly or indirectly through existing bilateral or multi-lateral networks, and their value lies in combining cross-functional data to develop more actionable leads. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "diagonal foreign exchanges can happen both directly and indirectly"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Domestic co-ordination between competent authorities and international co-ordination between counterparts are described as crucial for exchanges between non-counterparts. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "domestic co-ordination between competent authorities and international co-ordination between counterparts are crucial"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: FIUs, LEAs or other competent authorities should inform their foreign counterpart if the same request is made through other channels concurrently, providing reference numbers where possible, to avoid circumvention and duplication. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should inform their foreign counterpart if the same request is being made through other channels concurrently"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Sharing and transmission of information between non-counterparts should be done in a secure manner. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sharing and transmission of information between non-counterparts should be done in a secure manner"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: FIUs engaging in diagonal co-operation should consider using the FIU-to-FIU channel for maximum protection and integrity; responses to non-counterpart requests remain discretionary and go to the counterpart FIU to forward; requests from non-counterparts are routed via the counterpart FIU for direction. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIUs that decide to engage in diagonal co-operation should consider using the FIU-to-FIU channel"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Egmont Group Operational Guidance recommends that, under national law, an FIU retains discretion on whether to engage in diagonal co-operation. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the FIU retains discretion on whether or not to engage in diagonal co-operation"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Where FIUs must or may follow a different international co-operation pattern under domestic law, it is recommended that the FIU of the country where the non-FIU counterpart is located be informed of the request or information provided. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is recommended that the FIU of the country where the non-FIU counterpart is located be informed"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Bulgaria's FIU sends requests to foreign FIUs on behalf of domestic LEAs for pre-investigation, investigation or prosecution of ML, predicate offences and TF, not necessarily linked to an FIU analysis case; the FIU makes the final decision after checking Egmont Principles requirements. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FIU Bulgaria sends requests to foreign FIUs on behalf of domestic LEAs"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In early 2025, PPATK (Indonesia's FIU) and the Dutch LEA FIOD exchanged information directly, supported by the Dutch Police Attaché in Indonesia, to support a Dutch MLA request in a scam case; this built trust and let FIOD target its MLA request to achieve substantial results rapidly. (p. 46)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In early 2025, PPATK (FIU Indonesia), and the Fiscal Information and Investigation Service"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Through INTERPOL's I-FIU Connect initiative, member FIUs gain instant direct access to INTERPOL criminal databases, including nominal data and records on fraudulent travel and identity documents, to target individuals and bank accounts linked to INTERPOL databases. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "through the I-FIU Connect initiative, member countries’ FIUs can gain instant, direct access"</sub>

## 7. Transiting into investigation and prosecution

- **descriptive / context**: As cases move from investigation to prosecution, obtaining court-admissible evidence through formal procedures, such as locating and securing witnesses and extraditing suspects, often requires formal channels like MLA requests. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "measures that often require formal channels of co-operation, such as MLA requests"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: In complex ML investigations, it is best practice to ensure prosecutorial input from a very early phase, through direct investigative involvement where prosecutors hold investigative functions or through advice otherwise. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is best practice to ensure that there is prosecutorial input from a very early phase"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Early prosecutor involvement is particularly important in large transnational cases, helping decide the ambit of investigations, suspects and potential charges, advising on evidence admissibility, and identifying early the need for international co-operation given overseas evidence timelines. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is particularly important in large, transnational cases"</sub>

## 7.1. How to use informal co-operation to support formal co-operation

- **descriptive / context**: Sending completely 'cold' MLA requests without prior consultation is not considered a best practice. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not considered a best practice for countries to be sending completely “cold” MLA requests"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Prosecutors may engage informally through longstanding bilateral channels, liaison prosecutors or magistrates deployed abroad, domestic and foreign FIUs/LEAs, and multi-lateral prosecutor networks; continuous communication across informal and formal channels helps manage expectations and builds trust. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Prosecutors may engage informally with one another through longstanding bilateral channels"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: It is important that informal co-operation is leveraged as early as possible, so that its faster, more agile and flexible channels can identify accounts, perpetrators and transactions and narrow the official MLA to more targeted evidence collection. (p. 47)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important that informal co-operation is leveraged as early as possible in this process"</sub>

## Best practice: Informal co-operation between competent authorities in charge of MLA

- **descriptive / context**: Liaison prosecutors or magistrates are deployed by their domestic prosecuting authority to jurisdictions with high bilateral casework, understand the host legal framework, and engage directly with foreign prosecutorial counterparts. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Liaison prosecutors/magistrates are generally deployed on behalf of their domestic prosecuting authority"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Liaison prosecutors can advise domestic prosecutors on request requirements, engage the host country before a formal request to check legal requirements, identify where informal channels may be used, and progress submitted requests with host authorities. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "advising domestic prosecutors of the requirements for making a request for assistance"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Due to domestic legal constraints, many MLA authorities cannot engage directly with foreign counterparts informally; co-operation occurs indirectly through LEAs or FIUs acting as conduits, with a feedback loop so intermediaries know how information was used. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "co-operation generally occurs indirectly through local LEAs or FIUs who act as a conduit"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The International Association of Prosecutors (IAP) is described as the only global network of prosecutors, with over 170 organisational members representing more than 250 000 prosecutors across 175 countries and regions, offering a digital database of prosecution contacts. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The IAP is the only global network of prosecutors, with over 170 organisational members"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The European Judicial Network (EJN) facilitates judicial co-operation among EU Member States against serious crime; its national contact points can access details of members, including non-EU counterparts. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The European Judicial Network (EJN) facilitates judicial co- operation in criminal matters"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: WACAP facilitates direct contacts between focal points in 16 West African countries and provides secure communication channels. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitates direct contacts between dedicated focal points in 16 West- African countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SEAJust provides an informal platform for direct communication between central authorities across 22 South East Asian jurisdictions to support MLA. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provides an informal platform for direct communication between central authorities across 22 jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The CASC network covers 8 countries across Central Asia and the Southern Caucasus and facilitates direct communication between central authorities and other national bodies to strengthen inter-regional judicial co-operation. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "encompasses 8 countries across Central Asia and the Southern Caucasus"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: This feedback strengthens trust and improves future co-operation. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This feedback strengthens trust and improves future co-operation."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: It is important that a feedback loop is in place so that domestic intermediaries know how the shared information has been used and any further requirements. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is important that a feedback loop is in place"</sub>
- **permissive / context**: Information collected through informal channels can then be used to guide and support the formal MLA process, so that the request is targeted and actionable. (p. 48)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The information collected can then be used to guide and support the formal MLA process"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SEEPAG brings together 11 Southeast European Member States; each designates a National Prosecutorial Focal Point to enable swift exchange of information and evidence via MLA or informal channels. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "brings together 11 Southeast European Member States"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: AIAMP comprises 22 public prosecutors' and attorney general's offices from Latin America, Spain, Portugal and Andorra, fostering direct prosecutorial communication through formal MLA and informal channels. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "comprising 22 Public Prosecutors’ and Attorney General’s Offices from Latin America"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: UNODC's SHERLOC platform supports efforts against organised crime, corruption, ML and terrorism, with searchable databases on legislation, case law and strategies, a treaties database with ratification status, and a Directory of Competent National Authorities. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "UNODC’s SHERLOC knowledge management platform"</sub>

## Obtaining information requiring coercive powers and formal co-operation processes

- **descriptive / context**: Banking and financial transaction records and tax data are essential for turning financial analysis into ML enforcement outcomes, but evidential access typically requires coercive powers, generally available only through formal channels. (p. 50)[^original]

## Best practice: Informal co-operation between competent authorities in charge of MLA

- **expected / expectation**: Informal information requests should be tailored to the specific jurisdiction and investigative needs, including the predicate offence, and should not be indiscriminately shared with all available counterparts. (p. 50)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "should not be indiscriminately shared with all available counterparts"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: It is recommended that the requesting authority remain concise and targeted, and not include the entire official trial report or irrelevant information in its request. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "do not include the entire official trial report"</sub>

## Obtaining information requiring coercive powers and formal co-operation processes

- **descriptive / context**: Informal co-operation can validate the foreign nexus, strengthen the case for starting the lengthy MLA process early, alert foreign counterparts, and reveal absence of relevant assets, avoiding futile MLA requests. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using informal co-operation may uncover the absence of any relevant assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Informal co-operation can help identify which foreign assets are relevant, allowing narrower MLA requests on specific periods or transaction ranges, which speeds the response. (p. 51)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Sending a targeted MLA request greatly facilitates and accelerates the rate of response"</sub>

## Locating persons

- **descriptive / context**: Beyond financial information, formal assistance can be requested for arrest and extradition of criminals to be prosecuted domestically, and for witness testimony or service of notices for use as evidence. (p. 51)[^original]

- **recommended / recommendation**: Voluntary informal interviews with witnesses can establish whether they hold relevant and sufficient evidence before formal requests are made to secure their evidence. (p. 51)[^original]

## Tool: INTERPOL's color-coded notices and diffusion systems

- **descriptive / context**: INTERPOL's notices and diffusions are international requests or alerts allowing police in member countries to share crime information; the Red Notice seeks location and arrest for extradition and the Blue Notice seeks to locate or obtain information on a person of interest; they are sent via I-24/7 and countries may act in accordance with national law. (p. 52)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "which can choose to act on it in accordance with their national laws"</sub>

- **permissive / context**: Informal co-operation can help ensure formal requests are drafted correctly, achievable within the time frame, and suited to the receiving agency or jurisdiction. (p. 52)[^original]

## 7.2. Obtaining evidence via informal channels

- **descriptive / limitation**: Some information can be exchanged directly as evidence through informal LEA and prosecutorial channels, but FIU-to-FIU exchanges are rarely used to collect evidence and exchanging evidence via FIU-centric networks is generally not permissible. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the exchange of evidence via FIU-centric networks is generally not permissible"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Admissibility depends on each jurisdiction's judicial processes, but direct transmission may be permitted where information is open-sourced, typically held by other government authorities or commercially, such as corporate and land ownership records. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "While admissibility of evidence is dependent on the respective jurisdictions’ judicial processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Information not requiring coercive powers and not restricted by privacy laws may be shared informally; otherwise it typically needs formal channels of co-operation. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This information does not require coercive powers for access, and is not restricted by privacy laws"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Direct voluntary co-operation with foreign service providers can be an alternate avenue to obtain digital evidence, as some providers allow authorities to approach them directly. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "some foreign service providers allow competent authorities to approach them"</sub>

## Best practice: How the United States leverages informal co-operation

- **descriptive / case example**: In the United States informal co-operation is possible where compulsory measures such as court orders or search warrants are not required, mainly exchanging publicly available information such as incorporation records, property records and certain court documents including conviction records. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "informal co-operation is possible in cases where compulsory measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The US FIU and LEAs access multilateral networks for informal co-operation including CARIN and regional ARINs, the International Anti-Corruption Coordination Centre (IACCC), the Egmont Group and INTERPOL. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Camden Asset Recovery Inter-Agency Network (CARIN) and regional affiliated"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: These networks let the US exchange information on MLA procedures before formal requests, follow up prior requests, identify counterparts, access open-source data, use urgent channels, request foreign investigations, locate suspects, and develop joint case strategies. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These networks enable the United States to exchange and receive information on MLA procedures"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: US LEAs engage bilaterally through U.S. embassy and consulate channels, grounded in multilateral frameworks such as Article 9 of the UN Convention against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances; federal LEA representatives in many embassies act as liaisons. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "U.S. LEAs also engage in bilateral co-operation through channels established via U.S. embassies and consulates"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Practitioners from other jurisdictions may contact these US liaison officials before formal requests to confirm what information can be obtained without a formal request. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Practitioners from other jurisdictions may reach out to these officials"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The US Securities and Exchange Commission primarily relies on two IOSCO multilateral MoUs, which are non-binding and give access to bank records, brokerage data and beneficial ownership information; the Enhanced MoU adds audit work papers, compelled testimony, asset freeze assistance and ISP and telephone records. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These non-binding agreements are key tools for the SEC to obtain information"</sub>

## 8. Joint analysis and investigations

- **descriptive / definition**: Joint analysis and joint investigations are collaborative efforts by competent authorities working together for a dedicated period on specific cases; joint analysis is an intelligence-sharing framework usually pursued by FIUs and LEAs, while joint investigations are conducted by LEAs (and judges with investigative powers where allowed) in a judicial setting. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Joint analysis and investigations both refer to a collaborative effort by competent authorities"</sub>
