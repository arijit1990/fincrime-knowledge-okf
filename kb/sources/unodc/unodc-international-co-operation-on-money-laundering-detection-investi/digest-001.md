- **descriptive / context**: The FATF Recommendations are recognised as the global AML and CFT standard, and the FATF is an inter-governmental body developing policies against money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing. (p. 5)[^original]

- **descriptive / context**: The Egmont Group provides a forum for financial intelligence units to improve co-operation against money laundering and terrorist financing and to foster implementation of domestic programmes in this field. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to provide a forum for financial intelligence units (FIUs) around the world to improve co-operation in the fight against money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INTERPOL enables police in its member countries to work together against transnational crime, maintains global police databases and supports four global programmes including financial crime and corruption. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INTERPOL maintains global databases containing police information on criminals and crime and provides operational and forensic support"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: UNODC works in 150 countries on drugs, crime, terrorism and corruption, and also trains judges, police, border officials, healthcare and social workers. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "UNODC works in 150 countries, building networks of cooperation across borders and providing reliable data and analysis."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The handbook was co-led by the Egmont Group (through the FIUs of Australia and the Netherlands), INTERPOL and UNODC, and drew on input from members of the FATF Global Network and the Egmont Group. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the project team—co-led by representatives from the Egmont Group (through the FIUs of Australia and the Netherlands), INTERPOL, and UNODC—collected input"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In total 106 jurisdictions responded to two questionnaires circulated in September and October 2024, with further insights from a session at the Joint Experts Meeting in January 2025. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In total, 106 jurisdictions responded to two questionnaires circulated in September and October 2024"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Challenges identified include growing demand for timely, high-quality and diverse information, the need to engage a broad range of counterparts, differences in legal frameworks and powers, and the need to foster trust and reciprocity. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the need to foster trust and reciprocity among authorities in charge of ML enforcement."</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: It is essential to leverage both informal and formal co-operation mechanisms in synergy, combining informal channels strategically and using both as needed. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is essential to leverage both informal and formal co-operation mechanisms in synergy."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Informal channels are faster, more flexible and targeted, complementing formal mechanisms (typically based on mutual legal assistance treaties) which are often slower and procedurally complex. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Informal channels provide faster, more flexible, and targeted communication, complementing formal mechanisms"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The handbook outlines four types of informal co-operation, each with advantages and limitations; its purpose is to give authorities options rather than favour one approach. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The handbook outlines four distinct types of informal co-operation, each with its own advantages and limitations."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Multi-lateral networks support information exchange across many jurisdictions through established frameworks, governance, contact points, standardised protocols and secure channels. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "multi-lateral networks support information and intelligence exchange across numerous jurisdictions through established frameworks"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Bilateral co-operation is valued for its agility and the trust it fosters; diagonal co-operation between different types of authorities combines powers and expertise but can present challenges, so jurisdictions often favour counterpart co-operation. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Bilateral co-operation is valued for its agility and the trust it fosters."</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Joint analysis and investigations leverage complementary strengths, integrate diverse information sources to generate deeper insights"</sub>

## 1.1. Objectives and structure

- **descriptive / context**: Globalised financial systems and technology enable funds to move rapidly across borders, while financial information is decentralised across jurisdictions, creating enforcement challenges and growing information needs in volume and speed. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Financial crimes are increasingly transnational, necessitating information sharing and operational co-operation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The handbook was jointly developed by the Egmont Group, FATF, INTERPOL and UNODC to address operational challenges to international co-operation, as ML investigations and prosecutions are one of the least effective areas of global AML/CFT regimes. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one of the least effective areas of global AML/CFT regimes."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Part I gives a common understanding of the operating landscape for international co-operation, exploring operational challenges, evolving needs and channels for effective co-operation. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Part I provides competent authorities with a common understanding of the operating landscape"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Part II identifies best practices, tools and mechanisms that jurisdictions may consider adopting for successful transnational ML detection, investigation and prosecution, including multi-lateral networks and joint and diagonal co-operation. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "that jurisdictions may consider adopting to achieve successful transnational ML detection, investigation, and prosecutions."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Annex contains three operational brochures addressing practical challenges of FIUs, LEAs and prosecutors when co-operating internationally. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "addressing the practical challenges faced by three specific sets of actors of the AML- community"</sub>

## 1.2. Focus and scope

- **descriptive / context**: The handbook is primarily developed for FIUs, LEAs, prosecutors and other operational competent authorities in charge of ML detection, investigation and prosecution, in line with FATF Recommendation 30. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The handbook is primarily developed for FIUs, LEAs, prosecutors, and other operational competent authorities"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The handbook relies on the foundations of the FATF Standards, including Recommendations 29, 30, 31, 37, 39 and 40. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "this handbook relies on the foundations laid by the FATF Standards, including Recommendations 29, 30, 31, 37, 39, and 40."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The handbook aims to help competent authorities effectively detect, investigate and prosecute ML, and to seek mutual legal assistance and extradition. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Effectively detect, investigate, and prosecute ML activity; and"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The handbook builds on FATF typologies on ML and transnational illicit financial flows, and on FATF reports on recovering international proceeds of crime through inter-agency networks and on financial investigations guidance. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "builds upon key best practices from the FATF reports on Recovering International Proceeds of Crime through Inter-Agency Networks"</sub>

## 1.3. Methodology

- **descriptive / method**: The findings are based on a request to the FATF Global Network (over 200 jurisdictions) and the Egmont Group (177 FIUs), a literature review of open-source material, and discussions at the January 2025 Joint Experts Meeting; inputs were received from over 106 delegations. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In total, the project team received inputs from over 106 delegations."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FIU Australia and FIU The Netherlands (on behalf of Egmont) together with INTERPOL and UNODC co-led the project. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Experts from FIU Australia and FIU The Netherlands (on behalf of the Egmont Group) as well as INTERPOL and UNODC co-led this project"</sub>
