## Best practice: Joint analysis and investigations

- **descriptive / case example**: In 2024 FIU Italy launched a joint analysis with FIU Spain and FIU Netherlands into a cross-border complex ML scheme channelling large transactions across Europe to Asian countries, initially detected through a high number of STRs on funds sent to a Spanish payment account of a Dutch payment agent. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In 2024, FIU Italy launched a joint analysis exercise together with FIU Spain and FIU Netherlands"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Dutch payment agent appeared on websites as a bank or payment institution but held no actual licences in Europe; Italian companies owned by Chinese citizens were implicated in fiscal and public aid frauds. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This agent appeared on websites as “bank or payment institution” but had no actual licenses in Europe."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Italian reporting entities detected over 33 million euros transferred to Spain from January 2022 to September 2023. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Additional data was collected from the main Italian reporting entities, who detected over 33 million euros transferred to Spain"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The payment agent, part of an international financial group with branches in Europe, America and Africa, was responsible for laundering 95 million euros from 2021 to 2024. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "responsible for having laundered 95 million euros from 2021 to 2024"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The extended geographical distribution, diversification of financial licences and lack of a global supranational view on cross-border activities seem to have facilitated the agent's activities. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The extended geographical distribution, the diversification of financial licenses, and the lack of a global supranational view"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The three FIUs also requested data from national supervisory authorities, reporting entities, customs agencies and foreign FIUs whose countries were involved in the fund flows; the joint analysis was conducted rapidly with direct and efficient co-operation and results were disseminated to relevant law enforcement and supervisory authorities of all participating jurisdictions. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The three EU FIUs also requested data from the national supervisory authorities and reporting entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Operation AVARUS-X, led by the Australian Federal Police from 2022, dismantled a money-laundering organisation that controlled and criminally exploited a nationwide chain of legitimate money service businesses transferring billions of AUD per year; the controllers were suspected of a footprint in at least four other countries.   <sub>anchor: "Through the informal collaborative structure of a Five Eyes Law Enforcement Group (FELEG) money laundering working group"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Members of U.S. HSI sat physically with the Australian investigation team, and intelligence analysts from partner agencies including AUSTRAC were embedded, enabling ongoing direct intelligence exchange. (p. 58)[^original]

- **descriptive / case example**: An information-sharing mechanism with five major Australian banks was initiated to improve the efficiency of domestic financial enquiries; the chain was dissolved after police intervention in late 2023 and prosecution of alleged principals is ongoing in Australia. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An information-sharing mechanism with five major Australian banks was initiated"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A US–India parallel investigation, requested in 2022 by the U.S. Department of Justice under the Treaty on Mutual Legal Assistance in Criminal Matters, targeted a drug trafficking organisation led by two brothers that laundered proceeds through virtual assets. (p. 58)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "the brothers controlled over 8 500 bitcoins, valued at approximately USD 150 million at the time"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: India's Enforcement Directorate obtained records showing transfers exceeding Rs. 5.54 crore through a digital payment service provider; in April and May 2024 searches seized 268.22 bitcoins worth approximately Rs. 130 crore, and assets worth Rs. 9.67 crore linked to Mr. B and his wife were provisionally attached. ## 8.1. Building trust

- **descriptive / context**: Trust is fundamental to co-operation, especially in joint work sharing sensitive information; institutional trust is often based on personal trust, which is more likely where there are commonalities of role, priority and purpose. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Institutional trust is often based on personal trust, and building personal trust is more likely"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Building trust is particularly challenging in diagonal co-operation, where non-direct counterparts have no experience working with each other. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is why building trust is particularly challenging in instances of diagonal co-operation"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: To begin building trust, joint collaboration can first work at a strategic level, where information is less sensitive than operational leads. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "joint collaboration can first consider working on a strategic level, where information tends to be less sensitive"</sub>

## 112.

- **descriptive / definition**: Key criteria for deciding whether to start a joint analysis or investigation include the complexity and sophistication of the criminal network, the scale and intricacy of measures needed across jurisdictions, and the degree of interconnection between investigations. (p. 60)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Key criteria for determining whether to initiate a joint analysis or investigation include the complexity and sophistication"</sub>

## 8.1. Building trust

- **recommended / recommendation**: Examples of strategic joint collaboration include analysing strategic insights and trends to develop a collective risk assessment, implementing joint training and exchanging operational experiences and best practices, and holding frequent inter-jurisdictional meetings, including in person. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Analysing and studying strategic insights, information and trends, to develop a collective risk assessment"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Trust built through continuous collaboration and successful outcomes can later be leveraged for joint analysis and investigation of operational cases. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trust that is built upon continuous collaboration and successful outcomes can thereafter be leveraged"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Financial Intelligence Consultative Group and the Pacific Financial Intelligence Community began with strategic intelligence sharing and regional risk assessments, later moving to more operationally centric and actionable intelligence outcomes. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some good examples that have leveraged this process of trust building are the Financial Intelligence Consultative Group"</sub>

## Best practice: Start building trust at a regional level with the Egmont Regional Groups

- **descriptive / context**: Egmont Group Regional Groups build trust among FIUs through joint initiatives, regional trainings, technical assistance, workshops, case studies and operational exercises, and by sharing region-specific ML/TF risk assessments, vulnerabilities and typologies. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Egmont Group Regional Groups play a crucial role in trust-building among FIUs"</sub>

## 8.2. Creating common terms of reference

- **expected / expectation**: A joint analysis or investigation should be based on a common objective, and it is imperative that common expectations are established at the start of collaboration. (p. 61)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The formation of a joint analysis or investigation should be based on a common objective."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Common expectations may include selecting a case, topic or theme recognised as a priority by all participating authorities. (p. 62)[^original]

- **recommended / recommendation**: Pre-established agreements should cover roles, responsibilities, techniques, locations, guarantees on proportionality and suspects' human rights, and other legal provisions to mitigate jurisdictional issues, particularly in diagonal co-operation. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Having clear, pre-established agreements on roles, responsibilities, techniques to be used, locations"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Partners should identify and streamline resources and expertise for the shared purpose, including administration, equipment and costs. (p. 62)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Identifying and streamlining the range of resources and expertise for the shared purpose"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: A clear operating framework should be established, ideally covering standardised formats for sharing data, which reduce misinterpretation and time spent reconciling data structures. (p. 62)[^original]

- **recommended / recommendation**: Partners should agree common methodologies and data analysis techniques so exchanged information is interpreted consistently across jurisdictions. (p. 62)[^original]

## Best practice: INTERPOL's global police operations against financial crime

- **descriptive / case example**: INTERPOL's Operation Jackal targets West African organised crime groups responsible for large volumes of global online fraud and related ML; such operations gather jurisdictions to identify cases, exchange intelligence and meet face to face, fostering trust and an informal network of specialised practitioners. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "One example is Operation Jackal, which targets the West African organised crime groups"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: INTERPOL can assist participating member countries with operational analysis, case co-ordination guidance and on-the-ground assistance through deploying Operational Support Teams. (p. 63)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "INTERPOL can further assist member countries participating in the operation"</sub>
