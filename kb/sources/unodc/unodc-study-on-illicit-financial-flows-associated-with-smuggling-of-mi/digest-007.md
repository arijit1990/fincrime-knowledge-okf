## 3.2. Trafficking Actors

- **descriptive / context**: There are four main categories of actors in trafficking in and from GLO.ACT partner countries; categories can overlap and one person may hold more than one role. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Tere are four main categories of actors involved in trafficking in and from GLO.ACT partner countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The recruiter is involved in the first stage of trafficking and may use agencies, family connections, deception, force, coercion, abduction or threats; online platforms are used but less common in GLO.ACT partner countries. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Recruiters may use employment and travel agencies, family and friendship connections, deception, force or coercion"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Families may contribute to girls and women being trafficked for pleasure marriages, to resolve dowry costs, or to exploit victims in domestic servitude; in domestic cases where all actors are in one country, no IFFs are generated. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In such domestic trafficking cases, where all actors have their center of economic interest in one country, no IFFs are generated"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The customer is the end consumer who pays the exploiter; IFFs arise when the customer's centre of economic interest is in a different country from the exploiter or intermediaries. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "IFFs are generated when the customer has their centre of economic interest in a different country"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The victim is the person exploited to generate income for the trafficker; if the income has a cross-border element an IFF is generated, and if the victim is sold this may also generate an IFF. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If the transactions related to this income have a cross-border element, then an IFF is generated"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: If a recruiter has their centre of economic interest in a different country from other network members, salaries and expenses may be transferred to the recruiter from that country, constituting an IFF. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "then salaries and expenses may be transferred to the recruiter from a different country, constituting an IFF"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: The exploiter is involved in the exploitation stage and directly receives income from victims; this income may have a cross-border element and may be passed to other network members, generating IFFs. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "may also be transferred by the exploiter to other members of the network"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Other actors such as transporters, those harbouring victims and corrupt officials may facilitate TIP, for example by not raiding exploitation sites or issuing work permits to victims who do not meet requirements. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as well as corrupt officials, who are often also involved in facilitating aspects of TIP"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Traffickers come from varied socioeconomic backgrounds and may receive logistical support from many actors including government officials; they do not follow a single business model. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "they do not follow a single business model"</sub>

## 3.3. International vs. Domestic Trafficking

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Domestic trafficking is less likely to generate IFFs unless perpetrators, victims or other actors have centres of economic interest abroad, or proceeds are laundered abroad. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Domestic trafficking is less likely to generate IFFs unless the perpetrator, victim or other actors involved"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In 2020, 60 per cent of all detected trafficking victims were identified within their own country of citizenship. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in 2020, 60 per cent of all detected victims of trafficking were identified within their own country of citizenship"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Between 2016 and 2018, 341 victims from Afghanistan, Iraq and Pakistan were detected in Europe, especially in the United Kingdom; actual numbers are likely much higher. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a total of 341 victims from Afghanistan, Iraq and Pakistan"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Cross-border trafficking cases generate IFFs to and from origin, destination and transit countries and other countries where actors have centres of economic interest. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Such cross-border trafficking cases generate IFFs both to and from the origin and destination countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Income from recruitment is generally considered minor compared with income from the exploitation phase. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "income generated during the recruitment phase is generally considered minor compared to the income generated during the exploitation phase"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: NGOs in Iraq reported Syrian, Iraqi Yazidi and other Iraqi women and girls sold under temporary or pleasure marriages, and Iraqi Kurdish women transferred to the UAE and Kuwait under the name of marriage. (p. 54)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Syrian, Iraqi Yazidi and other Iraqi women and girls were sold"</sub>

## Vulnerability of smuggled migrants to abuse and exploitation

- **descriptive / finding or result**: In 2020, 38 per cent of 665 Afghans surveyed by the Mixed Migration Centre said smugglers were the most likely perpetrators of violence or dangerous incidents on the journey. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "38 per cent of 665 Afghans surveyed in a study conducted by the Mixed Migration Centre"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Delayed payments incentivise smugglers to ensure clients' well-being and safe arrival, whereas advance payment reduces their incentive to refrain from exploitative behaviour. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If payments are made in advance, however, there is less incentive for smugglers"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In a Greek case, two smugglers transported, irregularly admitted and housed 48 Afghans, Iranians, Iraqis and others from Türkiye to Greece, locking them in, limiting food and using violence and death threats to extort money. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "two Greek smugglers facilitated the transportation, irregular entry, and provision of accommodation of 48 Afghans"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In a Europol case before 2015, Pakistanis paid €14,000 per person to be transported on the Western Mediterranean route into the EU, then were forced to work in restaurants to repay debts; profits were invested in restaurants or sent to Pakistan, generating IFFs. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pakistanis paid €14,000 per person for transportation across the Western Mediterranean route into the EU"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Pakistan case predates Pakistan's AML/CFT regime and completion of its action plans; Pakistan is Compliant or Largely Compliant with 38 of 40 FATF recommendations and has been removed from the FATF grey list. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pakistan, which is Compliant or Largely Compliant with 38 out of 40 Financial Action Task Force (FATF) recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The MSE-based victim estimates are described by the source as limited, because where country-specific MSE ranges were unavailable an average range of four to five times detected victims was used. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Therefore, these calculations should be considered limited"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In smuggling, large debts may be contracted, which significantly increases the risk of exploitation and often incentivises trafficking. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In smuggling, large debts may be contracted, significantly increasing the risk of exploitation"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: For the United Kingdom, the study used a multiplier of three to five times the detected victims, as applied by the Home Office in 2014; for Finland, where no MSE study exists, a range of four to five was used. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For the United Kingdom, the range of three to five times higher than actual victims is used"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Pakistan is Compliant or Largely Compliant with 38 out of 40 FATF recommendations, is in the top tier of technically compliant countries, and has been removed from the FATF grey list. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pakistan, which is Compliant or Largely Compliant with 38 out of 40 Financial Action Task Force (FATF) recommendations"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: UNODC MSE studies in the Netherlands, Ireland, Romania and Serbia found actual victims were four to five times (Netherlands), four times (Ireland and Romania) and four to nine times (Serbia) detected numbers. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in Serbia it was somewhere between four and nine times higher"</sub>
