## Key Findings and Recommendations

  <sub>anchor: "organizers of smuggling operations may be based in third countries"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "need to be undertaken proactively"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "joint or parallel financial investigations, needs to be fostered"</sub>

- **expected / requirement**: Efforts to address illicit financial flows should not compromise the human rights of refugees, asylum seekers and others forced to leave their countries by threats to fundamental rights. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Efforts to address illicit financial flows should not compromise the human rights of refugees, asylum seekers"</sub>

## Key Finding 2

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Smuggling of migrants and trafficking in persons generate considerable illicit financial flows affecting origin, transit and destination countries, and also third countries through related payments, costs, income and management of crime proceeds. (p. 8)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Te crimes of smuggling of migrants and trafficking in persons generate considerable IFFs"</sub>

## Key Finding 3

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Smuggling of migrants and trafficking in persons are distinct crimes but are closely linked in practice, often using the same routes and transport methods and sometimes the same perpetrators, so some IFFs may relate to both. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is a need to strengthen the legislative framework required to address illicit financial flows"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: There is a need to strengthen the legislative framework addressing illicit financial flows from trafficking in persons and smuggling of migrants. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is a need to strengthen the legislative framework required to address illicit financial flows"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Such legislation must impose tougher penalties for organized crime involvement in trafficking in persons and smuggling of migrants. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This legislation must also impose tougher penalties for organized crime involvement"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Trafficking in persons and migrant smuggling should be included as predicate offences under money laundering provisions. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Trafficking in persons and migrant smuggling should be included as predicate offences under money laundering"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Money laundering provisions must enable seizure of assets used to commit these offences and confiscation of proceeds of crime. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "such provisions must enable both seizure of assets used to commit these offences and confiscation of proceeds"</sub>
- **expected / requirement**: Where smuggling of migrants is not specifically criminalized, it is essential to criminalize it by ratifying and implementing the Smuggling of Migrants Protocol supplementing the Convention against Transnational Organized Crime. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is essential to do so, by ratifying and domestically implementing the Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants"</sub>

## Key Finding 4

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Use of information and communications technology in transnational crime, including trafficking and smuggling, has become more widespread; smugglers sell fraudulent travel documents online and traffickers recruit victims through online platforms. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "smugglers sell and send fraudulent travel documents to clients via the Internet"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Cyber-enabled transnational crimes facilitate cross-border value transfers and generate illicit financial flows, including through extra profits and laundering of crime proceeds. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cyber-enabled transnational crimes facilitate cross-border value transfers and generate IFFs"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Criminal justice actors need a better understanding of how digital tools and services are used to facilitate online criminal activity, including trafficking and smuggling. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There is a need to enhance the criminal justice actors’ understanding of how digital tools"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Digital tools used in these crimes include social network and instant messaging platforms, navigation, encryption and data-erasing apps, digital money transfer and laundering services, cryptocurrency transfer solutions and the darkweb. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "digital solutions for cryptocurrency transfers; and use of the darkweb"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Vigilant intelligence gathering, including open-source intelligence (OSINT), on technology trends in IFFs is an essential measure for effective investigation and prosecution. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is an essential measure for the effective investigation and prosecution of trafficking in persons"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: Specialized training of criminal justice actors on detecting online criminal activity and collecting and preserving electronic evidence is crucial. (p. 9)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Specialized training of relevant criminal justice actors, including on how to detect such online"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "IFFs associated with smuggling often depend on the scale at which the smugglers operate"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "it can be estimated that nearly 166,000 were smuggling incidents"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "investigations of trafficking cases generally do not include financial investigations in practice"</sub>
