## CHAPTER б GLOBAL OVERVIEW

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The inconsistency between share of detected victims and share of traffickers convicted is explained in part by forced labour involving on average more victims per trafficker than sexual exploitation: based on analysis of court cases reported by national authorities, one trafficker on average can exploit one to two victims for sexual exploitation, while in forced labour the number can rise to two to three victims depending on the sector. In other forms such as forced begging, forced criminality, illegal adoption or forced marriage there is often just one victim per trafficker. (p. 53)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can exploit one to two victims for sexual exploitation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In some forms of forced labour, such as construction or the fishing industry, traffickers appear to traffic more victims per case than in other forms such as domestic servitude. The higher number of victims per trafficker in forced labour can be attributed to a higher level of professionalization of the traffickers involved compared with sexual exploitation; sophisticated criminal enterprises have been shown to engage in forced labour trafficking.
