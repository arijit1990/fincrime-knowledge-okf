## Regional overview

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Four categories of people in cyber-enabled crime compounds: victims of trafficking; offending victims; scammers who willingly engage in fraud; and workers in other roles such as logistics, HR, cooks, cleaners and translators. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "1) victims of trafficking; 2) offending victims; 3) scammers who willingly engage in fraud; and 4) workers who perform various other jobs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some networks use forceful methods including kidnapping; the professionalisation of the industry is partly attributed to increased law enforcement operations and rescues, which disrupted some compounds, notably in the Philippines. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "causing considerable disruption in operations in some compounds (most prominently in the Philippines)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: A significant proportion of people enter the scam workforce willingly, attracted by stories of quick riches, often young with limited job prospects at home. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Stories of earning quick riches are used to draw in willing recruits"</sub>

- **expected / expectation**: It is crucial to be aware of the distinct, possibly overlapping, categories of people in order to screen effectively for victims of trafficking and other offences, and to plan responses for witnesses and offenders. (p. 55)[^original]
