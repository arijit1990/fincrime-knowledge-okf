## 24 Use of unlicensed money service businesses (MSBs)

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unlicensed money transfer businesses lending gambling patrons, often from East and Southeast Asia, funds to gamble have been identified in numerous criminal cases as playing a key facilitation role in money laundering; the origin of the funds is often unknown and may be linked to criminal activity. - **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The unlicensed MSB lends funds to the gambler, who repays within another jurisdiction through a domestic-only transaction, which helps circumvent strict currency controls and gives access to capital to gamble in different countries. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the gambler will repay the funds within another jurisdiction where only a domestic transaction will occur"</sub>

## 25 Growing use of cryptocurrency by casino, junket, and online gambling operators

- **descriptive / finding or result**: No casino regulator in Southeast Asia authorises casino operators to use cryptocurrencies, yet UNODC has observed widespread advertising of crypto exchange and payment services by both licensed and unlicensed operators across the region. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while use of cryptocurrencies by operators is not authorized by any casino regulators in Southeast Asia"</sub>

## 26 Prevalence of multiplier betting among 'high rollers'

- **descriptive / definition**: Multiplier betting is 'under-the-table' gambling in which the formally recorded casino table bet is only a fraction of the total private bet agreed between gamblers and junket operators, used to avoid gaming revenue levies. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a bet formally denominated at the casino gambling tables only represents a fraction of the total amount of a private bet"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Multiplier betting lets clients pre-negotiate payment method, betting currency and cash-out method, raises the commissions received by junket promoters, and can conceal the total money transmitted by a bettor and obscure the source and destination of funds. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It allows clients to pre-negotiate their preferred payment method, betting currency, and cash-out method"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Such arrangements have grown in popularity because most junket customers in Macau SAR, China come from mainland China, where capital controls and a nationwide gambling ban mean customers rely on credit from junket agents rather than bringing money. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "due to strict capital controls and a nation- wide gambling ban in mainland China"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Illustration: for an HK $1 million credit request, the junket agent may ask the casino for HK $100,000 in chips, with an understood ten-times multiplier between agent and customer. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the junket agent can request the casino to provide HK $100,000 worth of chips"</sub>

## 27 Prevalence of multi-party transactions and cash deposits

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Too many agents transacting on a junket's accounts may reduce effective control over transactors, leaving authorities with a limited view of account behaviour and banks unable to separate junket-related transactions from other casino transactions, which poses transaction monitoring challenges. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks unable to isolate junket-related transactions from other casino transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Loosely regulated gambling accounts can receive large cash deposits from tens to hundreds of individuals in one reporting period. Only some depositors are registered with the junket, and depositors who are neither operator nor agent breach the common casino practice of letting only junket operators or agents transact on junket accounts. (p. 79)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with only some depositors registered with the junket"</sub>
