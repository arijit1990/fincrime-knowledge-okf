## Targeting of goverment websites by blackhat SEO service providers

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Blackhat SEO service providers advertised on underground marketplaces and forums involve hackers placing hidden backlinks into the source code of compromised websites, sometimes called SEO webshell injections. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This so-called blackhat SEO technique is easily found advertised on underground marketplaces and forums"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Hidden backlinks are placed to typically unrelated websites in the source code of compromised sites, so that the authority of the compromised site boosts their ranking. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to typically unrelated websites into the source code of compromised websites"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Official government websites are often targeted by these illicit service providers because of their high-authority status and country-code top-level domains, which can significantly boost SEO. (p. 107)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Official government websites are often targeted by these illicit service providers due to their status as high-authority websites"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In January 2024 Viet Nam's National Cyber Security Center found hundreds of state agency websites targeted by hackers using blackhat SEO techniques. (p. 108)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "found hundreds of state agency websites had been targeted by hackers utilizing blackhat SEO techniques"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Viet Nam's Ministry of Information and Communications reported in April 2023 that more than 260 websites using .gov.vn and .edu.vn ccTLDs had been exploited this way. (p. 108)[^original]

## Recent incidents involving false base stations and unauthorized Starlink terminals in the Mekong

  <sub>anchor: "False base stations, sometimes referred to as international mobile subscriber identity (IMSI) catchers or stingrays"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In January 2024 Cambodian authorities seized 19 false base stations, three signal jammers and related devices used for SMS phishing in Sihanoukville, arresting one foreign national. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "authorities in Cambodia arrested one foreign national in connection to the seizure of 19 false base stations"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Cambodian devices were installed in vehicles driven around the city, sending SMS phishing links for online gambling and private loans. (p. 111)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the devices were installed in multiple vehicles and driven around the city"</sub>

## Cryptocurrency drainers

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Crypto drainers are widespread malware commonly targeting Southeast Asian victims, extensively advertised on underground markets and forums and designed to siphon funds from compromised wallets. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Crypto drainers are widespread malware types, commonly targeting victims in Southeast Asia"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Drainers are promoted via phishing and malvertising and spread through fraudulent Web3 projects and compromised social media, tricking victims into connecting wallets and approving malicious transactions, often through social engineering. (p. 113)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "victims to connect their crypto wallets to the drainer and approve transaction proposals"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Standalone drainers target specific cryptocurrency services, unlike infostealers that harvest broad login credentials, though infostealers increasingly add crypto-theft modules. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "drainers themselves, sometimes referred to as standalone drainers, are not programmed to obtain broad types of login credentials"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Drainers are offered as a service as scripts or embedded into phishing pages, and drainer logs are increasingly sent to criminals via Telegram. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "drainer integration with Telegram has also been increasingly observed"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In August 2023 a phishing page used with the Atomic Drainer mimicked the CryptoGPT or LayerAI landing page, falsely offering rewards to users who connect a wallet via WalletConnect. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the page mimics the authentic landing page of the CryptoGPT or LayerAI platform"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: After gaining access, drainer scripts verify assets, select compatible tokens including NFTs, and may rank assets by value to prioritise higher-value targets. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Some drainers can also assess the relative value of the assets to prioritize higher-value targets"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Tracking the total amounts stolen by drainers and the actors using them is difficult because many incidents go unreported. (p. 114)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Tracking both the total amounts stolen by drainers and the actors using them is difficult, as many incidents go unreported"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Criminals use malicious or drainer smart contracts, with FBI, Chinese government and Singapore authorities issuing warnings; variants include approval contracts, liquidity pool contracts that never permit withdrawals, and deposit contracts that transfer funds to attacker wallets. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Common variations include approval contracts that provide criminals access to a particular token held by the victim"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2022 a phishing attack on OpenSea users, timed to a smart contract upgrade, stole more than 250 NFTs worth about US $2 million; attackers used authentic company emails and fake validation emails to lure users into signing forwarding transactions. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "resulted in the theft of more than 250 NFTs worth approximately US $2 million"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: DeFi app kits are advertised on underground markets as services for Southeast Asian fraud groups, often copying legitimate front-end code. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A wide range of DeFi app kits can be easily found on underground markets and forums"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Drainer smart contracts are used in pig butchering schemes targeting investors with limited DeFi awareness through fake liquidity mining pools. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Drainer smart contracts have also increasingly been observed being utilized by criminals in pig butchering schemes"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Liquidity mining scams promise regular high returns for investing in a liquidity pool, using fake pools with smart contracts giving scammers wallet access, showing fake daily payouts, then 'rug pulling' the funds. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Eventually, the scammers ‘rug pull’—yanking everything out of the wallet with the permissions granted them by the contract"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Victims of liquidity mining scams are often told they must reach certain staking targets to recover their funds. (p. 115)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Targets will often be told that they need to reach certain staking “targets” to get all their funds back"</sub>
