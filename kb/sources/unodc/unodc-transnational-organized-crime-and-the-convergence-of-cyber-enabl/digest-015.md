  <sub>anchor: "prompted to pay a 20 per cent fee to regain access to their funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Fraudulent profits shown in the apps, some initially withdrawable, built trust so victims deposited more before being unable to withdraw. (p. 118)[^original]

  <sub>anchor: "the BitScan and Ace Pro cryptocurrency investment applications which were reported by multiple pig butchering victims"</sub>

## Cryptocurrency clippers

- **descriptive / definition**: Cryptocurrency clippers are malware that monitor the clipboard and replace copied wallet addresses with attacker-controlled addresses; they are often combined with banking trojans, keyloggers, ransomware, RATs or adware. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Clippers can be combined with other types of malware including banking trojans, keyloggers, ransomware, RATs, and adware"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because wallet addresses are often very long, users commonly do not notice the substituted recipient address, making clippers effective. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is common not to notice a change in recipient address, thereby contributing to the effectiveness of clipper malware"</sub>

## Remote access trojans

- **descriptive / definition**: Remote access trojans (RATs) are malware giving attackers unrestricted remote control of infected devices, disguised as harmless files or apps and acting as backdoors. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Remote access trojans (RATs) are a type of malware designed to provide attackers with unrestricted access and remote control of infected devices"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: RATs establish a command and control channel to the attacker server, may be generalist or tailored, are often modular, and may include built-in keyloggers or download them as needed. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "RATs are designed to provide criminals with remote access and control by establishing a command and control (C2) channel"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Remote access scams using malicious Android APKs disguised as commercial or government apps were reported across Southeast Asia; iOS variants appear less prevalent. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These attacks have largely targeted Android device users"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Some authorities and cybersecurity experts have observed a trend of criminal networks in scams and phishing increasingly turning to mobile trojans, with RATs representing among top fraud-related threats in many countries. (p. 119)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "with RATs representing among top fraud-related threats in many countries"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The syndicate distributed malicious apps disguised as special-price offers; support staff told victims to grant full permissions and place a test order requiring a shipping payment, stealing banking credentials. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Upon installation, members of the syndicate’s customer support team would instruct victims to grant full permissions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The RAT captured credentials through keylogging and screen capture, read SMS to get OTPs to bypass 2FA, tracked geolocation in real time and persisted through reboots; criminals then made unauthorised transfers and factory-reset devices to destroy evidence. (p. 120)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "monitor SMS to obtain one-time passwords (OTP) and bypass two-factor authentication (2FA) measures"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Two Malaysian nationals suspected to be the main culprits and controllers were arrested; they controlled more than 50 C2 servers in Hong Kong, China where at least 260 customised RAT variants were stored. (p. 120)[^original]

- **descriptive / case example**: In May 2024 Taiwan PoC took down a small syndicate running a fake customer service centre in Kaohsiung City; assets worth about US $1.33 million were seized. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Assets, including cryptocurrency and real estate amounting to a total value of approximately US $1.33 million were seized"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Hong Kong SAR arrested 14 individuals who acted as money mules for laundering proceeds of malware-enabled scams in exchange for payment. (p. 121)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "arrested 14 individuals in connection to the laundering of criminal proceeds"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Researchers attribute SpyMax's origin to a RAT first developed in 2019 by a threat actor claiming to be Middle Eastern; its source code leaked in 2020 and another actor used it to create Craxs Rat, whose source code that actor offered for sale for US $100,000 in August 2023.
