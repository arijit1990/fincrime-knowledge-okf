## Professionalization and consolidation of a criminal service economy

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Cyber-enabled fraud operations have become industrial in scale, with independent fraud gangs replaced by larger consolidated groups often operating under the guise of industrial, science and technology parks, casinos and hotels. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cyber-enabled fraud operations have taken on industrial proportions, with independent and scattered fraud gangs being replaced by larger, consolidated criminal groups"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Authorities indicate these groups have built stable networks of large physical and ICT infrastructure and use a new online service-based business model under which previously independent groups now operate. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "these groups have created stable networks consisting of vast physical and internet communication technology (ICT) infrastructure"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Owners of these compound sites typically rent space to criminal groups, and one location may house numerous tenants running illicit online activities targeting different jurisdictions and managing online gaming platforms. (p. 3)[^original]

## Expansion of regional underground banking and the rise of cryptocurrency misuse and high-risk VASPs

- **descriptive / context**: Underregulated casinos, junkets and illegal online gambling platforms remain critical infrastructure for transnational organised crime; they increasingly use cryptocurrency and have evolved into unauthorised, high-risk VASPs in vulnerable parts of the region. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have turned to or in many cases evolved into unauthorized and high-risk virtual asset service providers (VASPs) based in vulnerable parts of the region"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Authorities in East and Southeast Asia find it extremely difficult to enforce laws against illegal online gambling and to determine the source of funds used to place illegal bets; the industry operates in 'grey', 'black' or 'pre-regulated' markets. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It has proven extremely difficult for authorities in East and Southeast Asia to effectively enforce laws and regulations to contain the spread of illegal online gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Illegal operators act as legal, regulatory and fiscal cover that criminals use to mask the true nature of illicit financial flows. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illegal operators have proven their ability to serve as an effective legal, regulatory, and fiscal cover utilized by criminals to mask the true nature of illicit financial flows"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Demand for laundering-as-a-service providers has become unprecedented, and these are heavily used by powerful criminal networks engaged in far more than illegal online gambling. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This has generated unprecedented demand for laundering- as-a-service providers which have been heavily utilized by powerful criminal networks"</sub>

## Figure 1. Stages of gambling-based money laundering

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Placement: illicit funds enter the financial system by depositing money into gambling accounts via credit or debit cards, cryptocurrencies, prepaid cards and cheques. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Illicit funds are introduced into the financial system by depositing money into gambling accounts using methods such as credit/debit cards, cryptocurrencies, prepaid cards, and cheques"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Layering: source of funds is disguised through complex transactions involving multiple bets, transfers and withdrawals within the gambling platform, making the money's origin hard to trace. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Source of funds are disguised through complex transactions involving multiple bets, transfers, and withdrawals within the gambling platform"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Integration: laundered funds are withdrawn or used for legitimate transactions such as buying assets or transferring money, and may be further obfuscated by processing through multiple online gambling platforms. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Funds can also be further obfuscated by being processed through multiple online gambling platforms"</sub>

## Executive summary

- **descriptive / context**: High-risk exchanges, OTC services, large peer-to-peer traders and related businesses controlled by or facilitating transnational organized crime have reshaped the business environment for criminal groups, particularly in the Mekong. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the proliferation of high-risk exchanges, over-the- counter (OTC) services, large peer-to-peer (P2P) traders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Stablecoins, cryptocurrencies pegged to and backed by fiat currencies such as the U.S. dollar, have become popular in East and Southeast Asia compared with other regions and are especially used by criminal groups for cyber-enabled fraud. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly using stablecoins – or cryptocurrencies pegged to and backed by fiat currencies like the U.S. dollar"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Gaps in regional regulatory frameworks, awareness and enforcement capacity are exploited by high-risk VASPs that present themselves as legitimate. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "major gaps in regional regulatory frameworks, awareness, and enforcement capacity are clearly being exploited by high-risk"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Authorities in East and Southeast Asia report that Tether (USDT) on the TRON blockchain is the preferred stablecoin for Asian crime syndicates engaged in cyber-enabled fraud and money laundering. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "stablecoins, and particularly Tether (USDT) on the TRON (TRX) blockchain, represent the preferred choice for Asian crime syndicates"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: One high-risk Mekong-based VASP is found to have up to 4.5 times more counterparty exposure to higher-risk entities than its regional competitors, including online gambling platforms, large cyber-enabled fraud schemes and high-risk exchanges. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the entity has up to 4.5 times more counterparty exposure to transactions with higher- risk entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The same VASP has engaged in at least hundreds of millions of dollars in transactions with entities linked to large-scale drug trafficking, human trafficking, cybercrime and online child sexual abuse material, including OFAC-sanctioned entities and wallets linked to Lazarus Group hacking. ## Adoption of new technologies and growing sophistication of criminal networks

- **descriptive / context**: Criminal service economies have developed products and services sold to other criminal actors, lowering barriers to entry across cyber and other crime types and promoting specialisation. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This has led to a thriving criminal service economy and promoted specialization within it"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Mekong-based high-risk VASP, whose core business relies heavily on USDT, processed between US $49 billion and $64 billion in total cryptocurrency trading volume between 2021 and 2024 and is described as the largest service provider in its category in the Asia-Pacific region. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "processed between US $49 billion and $64 billion in total cryptocurrency trading volume between 2021 and 2024"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Blockchain analysis experts and companies have found it challenging to analyse and profile large, diversified grey-market providers, since their high volumes mix legitimate business funds with proceeds of crime. (p. 4)[^original]
