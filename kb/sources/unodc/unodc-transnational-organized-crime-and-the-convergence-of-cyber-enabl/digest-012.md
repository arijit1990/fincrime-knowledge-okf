## Evolution of high-risk VASPs and Telegram underground marketplaces in Southeast Asia

- **descriptive / case example**: The Fully Light Guarantee, a Telegram underground marketplace run by a conglomerate owned by the former Kokang Border Guard Force, served over 350,000 users and offered supply and demand channels for money laundering, smuggling and grey and black technological services, with the marketplace guarantee acting as a backstop between buyers and sellers. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fully Light facilitated these activities through hundreds of public groups and listings"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: After the Kokang BGF was brought down by rival militia forces in January 2024, many similar marketplaces emerged in the region and absorbed most of the disrupted business, which continues to grow. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many other similarly modelled marketplaces have emerged throughout the region"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: High-risk VASPs in Southeast Asia have strong links to major transnational organized crime groups and provide money laundering and underground banking channels, and may let powerful syndicates layer their proceeds by becoming de facto financial service providers. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "risks enabling some of the region's most powerful, well-capitalized, and resourced syndicates"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some such entities have expanded into developing and managing large underground P2P marketplaces on Telegram, which the report describes as a fundamental change in how organized crime conducts illicit activity at scale. (p. 90)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "representing a fundamental change in the way organized crime is now able to conduct illicit activity"</sub>

## Challenges of hosted wallet services

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: SP 1 offers a nested custodial hot wallet that multiple VASPs share, allowing users of Alliance exchanges zero transaction fees because transfers occur off-chain on private ledgers managed by SP 1 rather than on the blockchain. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Users of exchanges within the Alliance benefit from zero transaction fees between VASPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Service Provider 1 (SP 1) describes itself as a Trust and Company Service Provider offering wallets-as-a-service and custody; centralised VASPs traditionally hold hot wallets that collect user funds and serve as proof of reserve. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Service Provider 1 (SP 1) is a self-described Trust and Company Service Provider (TCSP)"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Blockchain analytics firms found high-risk VASPs including BG 1 use SP 1's nested services as an intermediary layer to obscure their on-chain presence, and law enforcement requests may reach the wrong party and be disregarded. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "enabling them to obscure their on-chain presence by using such third-party nested services"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The nested wallet model challenges the credibility of blockchain intelligence platforms and the integrity of virtual asset tracing reports. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This model also raises several challenges for the credibility of blockchain intelligence platforms"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: SP 1 conducts only Know Your Business processes and has no access to end-user data, and has established an Alliance; no system exists for law enforcement or compliance authorities to query VASP data held by SP 1. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the company only conducts Know Your Business (KYB) processes and does not have access to end users' data"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A July 2022 Wancang County Court announcement estimated BG 3's illicit gambling profits since 2016 exceeded 5 billion yuan (US $700 million). (p. 95)[^original]
