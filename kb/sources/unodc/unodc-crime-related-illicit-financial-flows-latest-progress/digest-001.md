## Definition of illicit financial flows

- **descriptive / definition**: As defined in the Conceptual Framework for the Statistical Measurement of Illicit Financial Flows, IFFs are financial flows that are illicit in origin, transfer or use, reflect an exchange of value and cross country borders. They may arise from criminal activities and from some tax and commercial practices, and are either directly generated by illicit income (including cross-border transactions in illicit trade of goods such as drugs) or from management of illicit income through investment in financial and non-financial assets. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illicit in origin, transfer or use, that reflect an exchange of value and that cross country borders"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: National IFF data remain limited worldwide, but significant progress has been made since 2017, when the UN General Assembly adopted indicator 16.4.1 ('Total value of inward and outward illicit financial flows') to monitor progress towards Sustainable Development Target 16.4, which aims to significantly reduce illicit financial and arms flows by 2030; UNODC and UNCTAD are custodians of the indicator. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "National data on IFFs remain limited worldwide, but significant progress has been made since 2017"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The International Classification of Crime for Statistical Purposes (ICCS) defines the illegal activities generating IFFs; since 2017 UNODC and UNCTAD have taken coordinated actions leading to a conceptual framework, pilot measurement exercises and methodological guidelines to measure IFFs from selected illegal market activities. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "provides definitions of illegal activities generating IFFs"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In October 2019 the 10th IAEG-SDGs session in Addis Ababa reviewed the methodology and reclassified the indicator from Tier III to Tier II (conceptually clear with internationally established standards, though countries do not yet regularly produce data); the document details crime-related IFF estimates from UNODC's work with countries implementing the UNODC-UNCTAD conceptual framework methodology. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reclassified the indicator from Tier III to Tier II"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Figure 1 is a map showing the availability of crime-related illicit financial flow estimates by country (the 'Countries with crime-related IFF estimates' map); no country names are legible in the extracted text. (p. 4)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Figure 1. Availability of crime-related illicit financial flow estimates, by country"</sub>

## 2. Conceptual Framework

- **descriptive / context**: IFFs are multi-dimensional, arising from activities such as trafficking of drugs and firearms, trafficking in persons, corruption, kidnapping and cross-border tax avoidance; not all stem from illegal activity, e.g. aggressive tax avoidance, considered detrimental to sustainable development in many countries though generally not illegal. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Illicit financial flows are multi-dimensional, arising from several different kinds of activities"</sub>

## IFF Conceptual Framework

- **descriptive / data or feature**: Figure 2 shows categories of activities that may generate IFFs (source: UNODC and UNCTAD); the figure content is not legible in the extracted text. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Figure 2. Categories of activities that may generate IFFs"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: When illicit goods or services are sold, value flows from buyer to seller, and extortion generates income for the perpetrator. If money is spent abroad the cross-border operation is an outward IFF; if income from illicit activity outside a jurisdiction is spent or invested domestically it is an inward IFF. For a drug shipment sold by a resident of a producing country to a resident of a country where drugs are consumed or trafficked, the flow is outward for the importing country and inward for the exporting country. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "an outward IFF from the perspective of the country of import and an inward IFF"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: When cross-border operations directly generate illicit income for an actor in producing illicit goods and services, or during a non-productive illicit activity, the resulting IFFs relate to income generation; when the illicit income from cross-border operations is used to invest in legal or illegal financial and non-financial assets or to consume goods and services, the IFFs relate to income management. IFFs are thus four types: inflows and outflows from illicit income generation and inflows and outflows from illicit income management. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the resulting IFFs relate to income generation"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: UNODC has given technical assistance to measure IFFs under these definitions in selected countries in Africa, the Asia-Pacific region and Latin America, with several producing the estimates summarized in the document; the expectation is that this data and ongoing work will inform methodological development, culminating in a statistical framework to be finalized by the custodian agencies. (p. 6)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "culminating in a statistical framework to be finalized by the custodian agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote to the framework: per the System of National Accounts, only productive activities generate value added but non-productive activities can also transfer value between actors; illegal markets and exploitation-type activities clearly include, respectively, productive and non-productive activities. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "while only productive activities generate value added, nonproductive activities can also transfer value from one actor to another"</sub>

## Latin America

- **descriptive / method**: Pilot IFF measurement studies were carried out in four Latin American countries (Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico and Peru), gathering data from illegal drug markets and exploitation-type activities such as smuggling of migrants; the concepts and methodology follow the Conceptual Framework definition and the estimates are valid for measuring SDG indicator 16.4.1. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pilot studies for the measurement of IFFs have been conducted in four different countries from the Latin American region"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The report says Colombia continues to account for the largest share of coca bush cultivation and cocaine manufacture worldwide. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Colombia continues to account for the largest share of coca bush cultivation and cocaine manufacture worldwide"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: In the Colombia pilot, inward IFFs are generated when the cocaine produced is exported, and inward IFFs also may arise when resident citizens derive income from their participation in the international cocaine trafficking chain. Outward IFFs are generated when the intermediate inputs required for production (mainly chemical substances) are imported from abroad. (p. 7)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inward IFFs are generated when the cocaine produced is exported, and also may arise when resident citizens derive income"</sub>
