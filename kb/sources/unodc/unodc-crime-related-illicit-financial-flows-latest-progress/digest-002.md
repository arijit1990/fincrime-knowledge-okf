## Drug Trafficking

- **descriptive / finding or result**: UNODC states that the size and relative importance of inward and outward illicit financial flows (IFFs) from drug trafficking are heavily influenced by where a country sits on the drug supply chain. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "heavily influenced by the location of the given country on the supply chain"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: For production countries, outward IFFs from drug trafficking mainly reflect the cost of importing goods needed for production and tend to be overshadowed by inward IFFs. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "outward IFFs reflect mainly the cost of importation of goods needed for the production process"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Example for Colombia: inward IFFs from cocaine trafficking out of Colombia were estimated at USD 1.2-8.6 billion on average per year in 2015-2019, while outward IFFs from cocaine trafficking were estimated at an average of USD 227 million annually (range USD 197-267 million) in the same period. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "outward IFFs resulting from cocaine trafficking were estimated at USD$227 million in average"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: For transit countries with a relatively limited domestic market, inward IFFs also tend to exceed outward IFFs by a significant margin, largely because of the difference between importation and exportation prices. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inward IFFs also tend to exceed outward IFFs by a significant margin"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Example for Mexico: inward IFFs related to cocaine trafficking were estimated at an annual average of USD 4.5 billion (written in the text as USD$3.26.3 billion, i.e. a range) in 2015-2018, versus outward IFFs from cocaine trafficking averaging USD 338 million per year (range USD 258-453 million) for the same period. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "inward IFFs related to cocaine trafficking in Mexico were estimated at an annual average of USD$4.5 billion"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Proximity to a destination market may also strongly drive high IFF levels in transit and production countries, because of the sharp price markups typical of the later stages of the supply chain. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proximity to a destination market may also be a strong driver for high levels of IFFs"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: For Mexico, trafficking of heroin, cocaine and methamphetamine combined generated inward IFFs averaging USD 12.1 billion (range USD 8-17 billion) annually during 2015-2018. This is roughly equal to the average annual total of Mexican agricultural exports in the same period (USD 12.6 billion) and higher than inward IFF estimates for production countries such as Afghanistan, Colombia and Myanmar. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "generated inward IFFs averaging USD$12.1 billion (USD$8–17 billion) annually during 2015–2018"</sub>

## Smuggling of migrants

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Smuggling of foreign irregular migrants by Mexican-based smugglers in Mexican territory and across the Northern and Southern land borders was estimated to generate an annual average of USD 1.1 billion (range USD 1.0-1.3 billion) in inward IFFs into Mexico during 2016-2018, comparable to the average value of air passenger transportation services exports (USD 1.1 billion) in the same period. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "estimated to have generated an annual average of USD$1.1 billion (range: USD$1.0-1.3 billion) in inward IFFs into Mexico"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Outward IFFs associated with migrant smuggling on the Mexico-United States route are also substantial: annual average estimates obtained from Ecuadorian and Mexican migrants correspond to more than USD 13 million in each country. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "corresponding to more than USD$13 million in each country"</sub>
