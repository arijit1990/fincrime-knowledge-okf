## Principal Recommendation 1: Apply Enhanced Due Diligence to all PEPs, Foreign and Domestic

- **expected / recommendation**: Laws and regulations should make no distinction between domestic and foreign PEPs; standards should require similar enhanced due diligence for both. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Laws and regulations should make no distinction between domestic and foreign PEPs."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most international banks already apply enhanced due diligence to domestic and foreign PEPs although not required by law, and little additional burden would fall on banks applying this standard. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most international banks already apply enhanced due diligence to both domestic and foreign PEPs"</sub>

## Principal Recommendation 2: Require a Declaration of Beneficial Ownership

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A signed declaration could serve as evidence of criminal intent, a basis for civil suit, a reason for closing the account, and evidence in non-conviction based freezing or forfeiture proceedings. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the signed declaration could be used as evidence of criminal intent in a money laundering or fraud prosecution"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: At account opening and as needed thereafter, banks should require customers to complete a written declaration identifying the natural person(s) who are the ultimate beneficial owner(s). (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks should require customers to complete a written declaration of the identity and details of the natural person(s)"</sub>

## Principal Recommendation 3: Request Asset and Income Disclosure Forms

- **expected / recommendation**: If a customer refuses, the bank should assess the reasons and decide on a risk-based approach whether to proceed with the relationship. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "If a customer refuses, the bank should assess the reasons and determine, using a risk-based approach"</sub>

## Principal Recommendation 4: Periodic Review of PEP Customers

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Periodic review is common among visited banks and helps avoid transaction-by-transaction decisions; the individual or committee then decides on termination or continuation of the relationship. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This review is a common practice among the banks visited"</sub>
- **expected / recommendation**: PEP customers should be reviewed by senior management or a committee including at least one senior manager, using a risk-based approach, at least yearly, with results documented. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PEP customers should be reviewed by senior management or a committee including at least one senior manager"</sub>

## Principal Recommendation 5: Avoid Setting Limits on the Time a PEP Remains a PEP

- **expected / recommendation**: Where a person has ceased to hold a prominent public function, countries should not introduce time limits on how long the person, family member or close associate is treated as a PEP. (p. 19)[^original]

- **descriptive / context**: Influence of officials and close associates often outlasts their term by years or decades, and corrupt monies do not become legitimate after a certain period. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "corrupt monies do not become legitimate after a certain time period"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Banks should be encouraged to consider the ongoing PEP status of customers case-by-case using a risk-based approach, and regulators should provide guidance on this. (p. 20)[^original]
