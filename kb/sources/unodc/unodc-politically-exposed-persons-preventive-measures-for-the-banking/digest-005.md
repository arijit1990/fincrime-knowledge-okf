## 7. Identiﬁ cation of PEPs: How to Check

- **descriptive / context**: Many banks asked for a definitive list of all PEPs compiled by governments, arguing it would give a uniform basis for EDD, be easier for governments to obtain, and reduce compliance costs; one bank suggested an international organization collect country lists. (p. 55)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Many banks suggested that there should be a deﬁ nitive list of all PEPs"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: National or international PEP lists have limited utility: they contain only certain prominent officials, not family members or close associates, and quickly become out of date. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The lists have limited utility because they only contain cer- tain prominent public ofﬁ cials, not family members or close associates, and are quickly out-dated."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Corrupt officials may use a PEP list to target opposition members and to remove their own names and those of supporters; an overly broad list such as all public servants would be unmanageable. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Corrupt ofﬁ cials may use the list to target members of the opposition, as well as remove their own names and those of supporters."</sub>

## Identiﬁ cation of the Beneﬁ cial Owner: Will the True Owner Please Stand Up?

- **descriptive / definition**: The beneficial owner is the natural person(s) who ultimately owns or controls a customer or transaction, beneficiaries, controllers or relevant third parties, including directors, trustees, guardians, attorneys and protectors, and settlors of trusts or founders of foundations. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the natural person(s) who ultimately own or control a customer or transactions, beneﬁ ciaries, controllers, or relevant third parties"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Without knowledge of the beneficial owner, it is impossible for a bank to determine whether it is servicing a PEP; identification is especially difficult when the contracting party is a legal entity, trust or intermediary. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Without knowledge of the beneﬁ cial owner, it is impossible for a bank to determine if it is servicing a PEP."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Both UNCAC and the FATF 40+9 Recommendations require banks to identify and verify beneficial owners as part of CDD. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both UNCAC and the FATF 40+9 Recommendations require that banks identify and verify beneﬁ cial owners as part of the CDD process."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under FATF Essential Criteria 6.1, banks are required to put in place appropriate risk-management systems to determine whether the beneficial owner is a PEP. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In FATF Essential Criteria 6.1, banks are required to put in place appropriate risk-management systems to determine if the beneﬁ cial owner is a PEP."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The G20 leaders called for strengthening FATF standards on beneficial ownership and transparency, which FATF will consider in preparing the fourth round of mutual evaluations. (p. 56)[^original]

## Identiﬁ cation of the Beneﬁ cial Owner: Will the True Owner Please Stand Up?

- **descriptive / context**: Beneficial owner identification is difficult when the contracting party is a legal entity, trust or intermediary, and without knowing the beneficial owner a bank cannot determine whether it is servicing a PEP. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Without knowledge of the beneﬁ cial owner, it is impossible for a bank to determine if it is servicing a PEP."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under UNCAC and the FATF 40+9 Recommendations, banks must identify and verify beneficial owners as part of the CDD process. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both UNCAC and the FATF 40+9 Recommendations require that banks identify and verify beneﬁ cial owners"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under FATF Essential Criteria 6.1, banks are required to put in place appropriate risk-management systems to determine whether the beneficial owner is a PEP. (p. 56)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In FATF Essential Criteria 6.1, banks are required to put in place appropriate risk-management systems"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Legislation on beneficial ownership varies: some jurisdictions require institutions to identify the natural person behind ownership structures, others set thresholds (for example 25 percent) below which identification is unnecessary, and a number have not yet adopted the necessary legislation. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Others impose thresholds below which it is unnecessary for banks to determine beneﬁ cial ownership (for example, 25 percent)."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The true beneficial owner and origin of proceeds are often concealed through misuse of corporate entities such as corporations, trusts, foundations and limited liability partnerships, and intermediaries can further obscure the process. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The identity of the true beneﬁ cial owner and origin of the proceeds is often concealed through the misuse of corporate entities"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Under a threshold approach (checking only owners with a share greater than 25 percent), a corrupt PEP may have a great opportunity to conceal involvement. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the corrupt PEP may have a great opportunity to conceal involvement."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: FATF conducted a thematic study on corporate vehicles, identifying areas of money laundering vulnerability and risk factors to help countries identify misuse. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "FATF has conducted a thematic study to identify, in respect of corporate vehicles, areas of vulnerability for money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Additional work is needed to bring jurisdictions into compliance with FATF standards requiring procedures to identify beneficial owners of legal entities formed within a jurisdiction. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Additional work is needed to bring jurisdictions into compliance with FATF standards"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: All interviewed banks have beneficial owner identification procedures; most require identification of the ultimate owner, who must be a natural person, while one bank admitted going only 'as far as possible'. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Most banks indicated that they require identiﬁ cation of the ultimate owner, who must be a natural person"</sub>
- **recommended / expectation**: Although each jurisdiction, regulator and institution should decide the most comprehensive means of identifying beneficial ownership, a uniform document recording the customer's declaration would prove helpful. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "having a uniform document that records the customer’s declaration of the beneﬁ cial owner would prove helpful."</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some jurisdictions set thresholds below which banks need not determine beneficial ownership, for example 25 percent. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Others impose thresholds below which it is unnecessary for banks to determine beneﬁ cial ownership"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Jurisdictions that have not adopted the necessary legislation must address this by requiring disclosure of the natural person who is the beneficial owner. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This must be addressed by requiring disclosure of the natural person who is the beneﬁ cial owner."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Jurisdictions must provide information on the identity of beneficial owners of legal entities to law enforcement on request and on a timely basis. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Jurisdictions must also provide information on the identity of the beneﬁ cial owners"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Beneficial ownership is often concealed through misuse of corporate entities, and in some jurisdictions legislation lets banks rely on intermediaries with little or no information from the underlying customer. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "legislation permits this by allowing banks to rely on intermediaries with little or no information required"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the jurisdiction using the form, prosecuting authorities have prosecuted cases of forgery. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the prosecuting authority has prosecuted cases for forgery"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The form approach has been adopted by banks in other jurisdictions even where not required by law or regulation. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The form approach has been adopted by banks in other jurisdictions, even when not required by law or regulation."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The written declaration of beneficial ownership helps focus identification at the outset, supports verification and PEP determination, and aids regulatory oversight. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It assists in focusing on the process of identiﬁ cation"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Signing under penalty of a criminal offence, and where appropriate the consequence of non-conviction based or criminal forfeiture, alerts the contracting party to the seriousness of the information and acts as a deterrent. (p. 57)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "and, therefore, acts as a deterrent"</sub>

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most banks and law enforcement agencies said a written declaration would be a valuable additional tool for identification, verification, audit trail and investigations; the authors consider it a critical first step, not a 'silver bullet', in the beneficial ownership toolbox. - **expected / expectation**: Principal Recommendation: at account opening and as needed thereafter, banks should require customers to complete a written declaration of the natural person(s) who are the ultimate beneficial owner(s) as a first step in beneficial ownership CDD. (p. 58)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "banks should require custom- ers to complete a written declaration of the identity and details of the natural person(s)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Some jurisdictions may not have defined a criminal offence for lying to a financial institution about beneficial ownership; even then, or where an offence applies, the signed declaration can serve as evidence of criminal intent, a basis for a civil suit, a reason for closing the account, and evidence in non-conviction based freezing or forfeiture.
