## Regional Overview

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 21 (points running): criminals in Southeast Asia often use points running syndicates to transfer stolen money between multiple bank accounts and online casinos to obfuscate source and destination of funds, regularly using personal accounts to collect and transfer money for others for commissions. The systems collect money for activities such as telecommunications scams, fraud and illegal gambling, and funds can be laundered by cashing in and out through the platform to justify the source as casino winnings. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "regularly using personal accounts to collect and transfer money on behalf of others to earn commissions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 22 (motorcades): the keyword appears on platforms where criminals advertise services to each other, including Facebook and Telegram groups. Motorcades extend points running syndicates by offering layering through multiple bank accounts for a percentage of total funds; those at the front of the car who bear most detection risk have advertised commission fees of 20 to 40 per cent online. UNODC observed large motorcade teams working with others on very large contracts to improve concealment, and smaller online casinos are used down the laundering chain by organized crime groups and illegal betting syndicates to further white-wash funds. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "bear who bear the most risk of detection have been seen advertising commission fees"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 23: among the most common modalities are fourth-party payments and running points platforms. Fourth-party payments evolved from third-party payments that exploited gaps in transaction reporting in popular payment apps; though both have enhanced reporting of suspected gambling transactions at Chinese authorities' request, fourth-party providers now typically install an additional intermediary between bookmaker, bettor and the third-party payment application to circumvent these measures and further obscure transactions. (p. 35)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fourth-party payments are an evolution of third-party payments which, until recently, exploited gaps in transaction reporting"</sub>

## Responses by governments in East and Southeast Asia

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Following surging criminality and Chinese requests for tougher stances on Chinese-facing operators targeting mainland bettors, in August 2019 the Bank of Cambodia announced a ban on all new online gambling operations and the Philippine regulator PAGCOR froze all new licences for Philippine offshore gambling operators (POGOs), sending tens of thousands of foreign industry workers home. Return notices from Chinese law enforcement to curtail cross-border gambling in Shan State, Myanmar (Kokang SR 1, Wa SR 2, Mongla SR 4, hosting land-based and online casinos, unregulated fiat and crypto exchanges and cyberfraud centres) accelerated the outflow. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Bank of Cambodia announced the decision to ban all new online gambling operations in the country"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Per China's Ministry of Public Security, from January to November 2023 authorities resolved 391,000 telecommunications and network fraud cases, arresting 79,000 suspects including 263 'backbone members or paymasters' of cyberfraud groups via task forces in Cambodia, Indonesia, Lao PDR, the Philippines, Thailand and Viet Nam. This included intercepting 2.75 billion fraud calls and 2.28 billion fraud messages, removing 8.36 million fraud-related domain names, and 328.8 billion yuan (US $46 billion) in funds related to fraud cases. (p. 37)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "successfully resolved 391,000 cases related to telecommunications and network fraud"</sub>

## Responses by governments in East and Southeast Asia

- **descriptive / data or feature**: In 2022 Chinese police solved more than 37,000 cross-border gambling cases, cracking down nationwide on 2,600 online gambling platforms, more than 1,100 casinos, over 2,500 illegal payment platforms and underground banks, 1,200 technical support teams and 1,600 platforms promoting illegal gambling. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "solved more than 37,000 cases involving cross-border gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In August 2023 police in China, Lao PDR, Myanmar and Thailand launched a 'special regional campaign' against organized crime groups involved in online gambling, telecommunications and cyberfraud, human trafficking, kidnapping and illegal confinement. Within a month more than 1,350 suspects were arrested in Wa State SR 2 and Mongla SR 4 of Myanmar and Bokeo, Lao PDR; by November 2023 China reported 31,000 suspects transferred from Myanmar to Chinese custody, including 63 alleged masterminds, organizers or key members, plus 1,531 fugitives. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the arrest of more than 1,350 suspected criminals in Wa State SR 2 and Mongla SR 4 of Myanmar"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In October 2023 under Operation 1027, following announcement of the Three Brotherhood Alliance of the MNDAA, TNLA and Arakan Army, the armed groups' offensives, besides aiming to eradicate the military dictatorship, also ostensibly targeted online fraud in Kokang SR 1. Several Kokang cyberfraud centres relocated to safer territory and the MNDAA released many Chinese trafficking victims to China. In December 2023 China's Criminal Investigation Bureau issued ten arrest warrants for senior Kokang BGF leaders for leading violent criminal groups engaged in telecommunications and network fraud against Chinese citizens. (p. 38)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issued ten arrest warrants for high-ranking members of the Kokang BGF leadership"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Across Southeast Asia the situation has diversified beyond illegal online gambling, with many land-based casinos and SEZs housing cyberfraud operations using casino-related businesses for cover. Together with tens of thousands of rescued victims, this is evidenced by many embassy warnings in the region, particularly the Mekong, detailing risks including kidnapping, extortion and human trafficking by criminal groups in the online casino and cyberfraud industry. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many land-based casinos and SEZs housing cyberfraud operations utilizing casino-related businesses for cover"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In May 2023 Philippine police began operations against licensed POGOs in the Clark Freeport Zone, Pampanga, rescuing 1,090 individuals (919 foreign nationals, 171 Filipinos) at the Colorful and Leap Group Company in the Clark Sun Valley Hub. The syndicate, concealing its activities behind a POGO licence, forced victims into cyberfraud and cryptocurrency investment fraud, and authorities cited a wide-scale intelligence failure that let the compound operate so long. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "rescuing 1,090 individuals including 919 foreign nationals and 171 Filipinos"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In June 2023 police raided the Hong Tai compound in Las Pinas, Manila, rescuing more than 2,800 reported forced labour victims (over 1,500 Philippine nationals and 1,300 foreign nationals from 17 other countries). The raids intensified calls by some officials to ban offshore online gambling after an October 2022 senate inquiry, and PAGCOR imposed a new licensing scheme to reduce unauthorized sub-licensing and reveal beneficial ownership of online operators; in 2023 it placed all existing POGOs on a probationary one-year licence and sued 33 POGOs for non-payment of fees amounting to US $39 million. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a new licensing scheme being imposed by PAGCOR to reduce unauthorized sub-licensing and reveal beneficial ownership of online operators"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In September 2022 Cambodian authorities raided multiple suspected illegal online gambling and cyberfraud compounds in Sihanoukville and Phnom Penh, confirming evidence of illegal gambling, money laundering, human trafficking, illegal confinement, torture and prostitution. Victims, including skilled IT workers, were lured via social media job ads for casinos and hotels and trafficked into guarded compounds to perpetrate cryptocurrency-based romance and investment scams; thousands were rescued from many nationalities, and authorities seized more than 8,000 mobile phones, 804 computers and numerous passports and weapons. (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities also seized more than 8,000 mobile phones, 804 computers, and confiscated numerous passports and weapons."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Sustained measures against online gambling and cyberfraud in Cambodia, the Philippines, Malaysia, Thailand and Viet Nam caused partial displacement into Lao PDR and parts of Myanmar (Karen State, north and south Shan, especially the SRs), as well as the August 2022 establishment of the Laos Offshore Gaming Authority (LOGA). (p. 39)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have caused a partial subsequent displacement into countries including Lao PDR and various parts of Myanmar"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: LOGA, modelled on the Philippine system then being reformed, is particularly concerning as Lao PDR has very low regulatory enforcement capacity in its casino sector, shown by zero suspicious transaction reports issued within the sector. Lao PDR proceeded with a 2022 pilot licensing an undisclosed number of foreign-owned offshore online operators; its casino sector has virtually no functioning supervisory system and shows clear connections to transnational organized crime, most famously the Kings Romans Casino in the Golden Triangle SEZ. The 2023 APG Mutual Evaluation Report designated the sector among the highest money laundering risks in the country. Pressure in one jurisdiction tends to displace operations to another. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This is demonstrated by its issuance of zero suspicious transaction reports within the sector."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 46: LOGA is a private company working with Lao PDR's Ministry of Public Security, presently tasked with regulating the emerging online casino industry and providing traditional security and cybersecurity services. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "LOGA is a private company working in cooperation with the Ministry of Public Security of Lao PDR"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Lao PDR's government has stated intent to attract as many as 750 new companies into its SEZs and smart city projects, particularly the new Siphandone SEZ and the largely developed revitalized Boten SEZ. Between 2016 and 2020, 743 companies invested over US $12 billion in registered capital into SEZs, with foreign investors from 18 jurisdictions, and a further US $2.5 billion was invested in 2021. Footnote 51: sources indicated the new high-speed railway station and customs checkpoint are in Boten SEZ, posing a possible trade security risk if the zone were operated by the Zhao Wei transnational organized crime network. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "743 companies invested over US $12 billion in registered capital into SEZs"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The Kings Romans casino complex, inside the Golden Triangle SEZ on the east bank of the Mekong in Bokeo, Lao PDR, adjacent to the porous Myanmar and Thailand border, has various connections to the drug trade in Shan State and elsewhere in Asia and has been identified as a key drug trafficking and money laundering hub connected to other criminally implicated casinos and junkets. Regional officials also confirmed use of affiliate gold traders and money exchange networks across the Thailand-Myanmar border area. (p. 40)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has been identified as a key drug trafficking and money laundering hub connected to other criminally implicated casinos"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In January 2018 US OFAC sanctioned Kings Romans and Zhao Wei, declaring his network a 'transnational criminal organization' and sanctioning him, three associates and three of his companies in Lao PDR, Thailand and Hong Kong SAR. The US Treasury alleges the network also engages in human trafficking, wildlife trafficking, money laundering and bribery, much facilitated through Kings Romans. In 2015 the Environmental Investigation Agency reported several Lao SEZs, particularly Kings Romans, as a growing hub for illegal wildlife trade including tiger skins and body parts, rhino horns and ivory. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In addition to drug trafficking, the U.S. Treasury also alleges that Zhao"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Despite sanctions the importance of Kings Romans as a storage, trafficking, deal-making and laundering hub is likely to expand now that Zhao Wei made Osiano Trading Sole Co. the major investor in a nearby port development, buying out other investors' shares in October 2020; Osiano, an affiliate of the Dok Ngiew Kham Group, reportedly invested some US $50 million. The port will handle cargo into the SEZ and include a hotel and office complexes. The group is also understood to control a new airport about five kilometres east of the Golden Triangle SEZ, a joint venture with the Government of Lao PDR. (p. 41)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "reported to have invested some US $50 million to purchase the"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Kings Romans and its surrounding area are a key transit hub for drugs smuggled across the Mekong River into Lao PDR from Shan State, and drugs and other contraband are also transported between the ports of Sop Lwe, Ban Mom and others. (p. 41)[^original]
