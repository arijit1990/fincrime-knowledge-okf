## Additional cases

- **descriptive / case example**: In April 2023 prosecutors in Kaohsiung, Taiwan PoC arrested 21 individuals for an international laundering operation that laundered about US $146.1 million over 10 months for criminal groups using online casinos in Viet Nam; authorities seized many mobile phones, Vietnamese SIM cards, computers and a telecommunication network system, saying the phones can receive calls from Viet Nam and people can call and enter a password to transfer gambling profits. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "arrested 21 individuals for their role in an international money laundering operation"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: In that case money passing through online casino platforms was meant to appear as remittances mainly from Viet Nam processed in Vietnamese Dong; the group routed it through foreign bank accounts and overseas shell companies, taking a 'handling fee' to 'white-wash' the money in Taiwan PoC and evade law enforcement so it could return to Viet Nam as apparently clean profit. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "taking a ‘handling fee’ to ‘white-wash’ the money in Taiwan PoC and evade law enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Viet Nam-facing online gambling operations are proven to have extremely high cross-table rollings and often use cryptocurrency; these revenues can help commingle clean money from legitimate recreational gambling with proceeds of crime processed through online casinos, obscuring the source of funds. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Viet Nam-facing online gambling operations are proven to have extremely high cross-table rollings"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Law enforcement sources confirm that Sam Gor network members, including senior members, previously visited Cambodian casinos including NagaWorld, and that Sam Gor has arranged underground cross-border payments for drug shipments from the Golden Triangle and East Asia (Macau SAR, Hong Kong SAR, Taiwan PoC) through Mekong-based casinos and junket operators and Vietnamese money launderers. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had previously made visits to casinos in Cambodia, including NagaWorld"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Sam Gor member Hsieh Tsung Lun of Taiwan PoC, who frequented casinos including NagaWorld, was arrested in 2018 and extradited from Cambodia to Myanmar, where he is serving a prison sentence; criminal intelligence officials say he organized shipments of large quantities of methamphetamine and heroin for the syndicate while working out of a Golden Triangle casino. (p. 97)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "who was arrested in 2018 and extradited from Cambodia to Myanmar where he is serving a prison sentence"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In August 2022 Taiwan PoC's Criminal Investigation Bureau reported a human trafficking investigation into associates of the Bamboo Union and Heavenly Way Alliance and other crime groups based in Hsinchu, New Taipei City, Taichung and Taoyuan; the network lured Taiwan PoC residents with high-paying jobs in Cambodia but handed them to gangs on arrival to be forced into cyberfraud, and members had previous narcotics and fraud convictions. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "lured unsuspecting Taiwan PoC residents with high paying jobs in Cambodia"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The investigation also revealed a professional travel agent recruited to help procure passports, order air tickets and arrange transport to Taiwan Taoyuan International Airport, who allegedly advised outbound travelers on how to answer customs questions on arrival in Cambodia. (p. 98)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a professional travel agent who was recruited to help procure passports, order air tickets"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "lured 82 Taiwanese jobseekers to Cambodia in a 3-month period"</sub>

(p. 99)[^original]

## Kokang, Special Region 1 of Myanmar

- **descriptive / context**: Myanmar's Special Regions (SRs) in Shan State host significant illicit economies including globally significant methamphetamine and heroin production and industrial-scale money laundering, underground banking, cyberfraud and unregulated gambling; armed conflict, socioeconomic insecurity, governance challenges in SRs under de-facto armed group control and greater regional integration have worsened the situation and deepened transnational organized crime links. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "significant illicit economies, including globally significant production of methamphetamine and heroin"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Drug production and armed group-organized crime links are well documented, but Myanmar's sprawling, high-tech, unregulated casino operations that launder illicit-economy proceeds have not been examined in detail. The Kokang Self-Administered Zone (SR 1), controlled by the Kokang Border Guard Force (BGF), is among Myanmar's most casino-dense areas; Mongla SR 4, Wa State SR 2, Tachileik and Myawaddy also host many unregulated physical and online casinos. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "is among the most casino-dense areas of Myanmar"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: SR 1 has had a casino industry since the 1989 ceasefire between the Government and the MNDAA (which split in 2009, one part allying with the Government as BGF 1006); the industry grew rapidly after the Kokang BGF's 2009 installation and displacement of online gambling from Macau SAR into Laukkai, SR 1's commercial and administrative centre on the China border. Growth was enabled by the BGF's relations with Nay Pyi Taw, which permitted formal business access and an autonomous regulatory environment, and Kokang has close cultural and business ties with Yunnan. (p. 99)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "since the 1989 ceasefire agreement between the Government and Myanmar National Democratic Alliance Army"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Kokang BGF has a documented history in drug production and trafficking; officials report large-scale drug production sites near Laukkai, and Chinese court records show over 1,300 cases of cross-border crimes from SR 1 in 2010-2021, about 800 on illicit drugs, 181 on illegal cross-border gambling and money laundering, and 40 on kidnapping, including incidents involving companies controlled by BGF leadership overseeing SR 1's Gambling and Entertainment Management Committee. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "over 1,300 cases were registered involving crossborder crimes emanating from SR 1 between 2010 and 2021"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 3: the Kokang Gambling and Entertainment Management Committee approves all casino and entertainment operations in SR 1, notably large online casino operations that use underground banking and laundering networks and have been implicated in major money laundering cases in mainland China. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "oversees the approval of all casino and entertainment operations of SR 1"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In December 2023 China's Ministry of Public Security Criminal Investigation Bureau issued ten arrest warrants for high-ranking Kokang BGF leaders for leading violent criminal groups engaged in telecommunications and network fraud against Chinese citizens; most are members of SR 1's Gambling and Entertainment Management Committee, heavily involved in Kokang's online gambling and underground banking industry, including Werner International, Yuxiang International and GOBO East online casinos. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "issued ten arrest warrants for high-ranking members of the Kokang BGF leadership"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In December 2019 China's Ministry of Public Security opened a special investigation into two interconnected Kokang-based online gambling platforms, Warner International and Yuxiang International (names as written), which use live-dealer streaming within large land-based Laukkai casinos; authorities said both were conduits for underground banking and laundering, with over 40,000 agents, 300,000 active members and annual betting volume of at least US $3.6 billion as of 2021. (p. 100)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "developing a business consisting of over 40,000 agents, 300,000"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The investigation found two affiliated gambling software companies, Dayun Technology (renamed Chengdu Jinan Tiancheng Technology) and Sichuan Qiante Technology, posing as research and development companies while servicing both platforms (the latter reportedly set up by Yuxiang International representatives), and led to arrests in Guangxi, Hainan, Sichuan, Fujian and Guizhou and seizure of nearly 15,000 bank cards used for underground banking under cover of online gambling via 'points running' syndicates that deposit money and cryptocurrency to obtain virtual chips later 'withdrawn' and cashed out. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seizure of nearly 15,000 bank cards used to facilitate underground banking using the cover of online gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Both casinos continue to operate across multiple mirror websites despite notifications confirming their use in laundering; users registering through the Werner International agent network can top up in-game credit by various underground payment methods and currencies and cash out through 'settlement methods' in other jurisdictions via 'fund disbursement' options. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both online casinos continue to operate across multiple ‘mirror websites’8 at the time of writing"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 8: mirror sites replicate other websites under different URLs with identical or near-identical content; illegal online gambling and cyberfraud syndicates in East and Southeast Asia regularly use them to overwhelm authorities, circumvent enforcement and ensure business continuity if a URL is taken down. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Mirror sites or ‘mirrors’ are replicas of other websites"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: GOBO East Entertainment (also Dongfanghui Casino), a Cambodian subsidiary of the Fully Light International conglomerate held by BGF leadership members, was implicated by the People's Procuratorate in Guizhou and Shaanxi in the 2019 convictions of local triad members who had outsourced online gambling operations by partnering with it. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "held by members of the BGF leadership, was implicated by the People’s Procuratorate in Guizhou and Shaanxi"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Authorities found GOBO East colluded with the syndicate by providing end-to-end online live-dealer and telephone gambling operations through its platform, offshore servers, call centres, brand and marketing resources, underground credit network and multi-currency payment and settlement solutions from Kokang SR 1, resulting in 121 convictions, China's largest number in a single case at the time; GOBO continues to operate, claiming licensing by the Myanmar Gaming Inspection and Coordination Bureau, CEZA and FCLRC in the Philippines. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The investigation resulted in the conviction of 121 individuals affiliated with the syndicate"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The director of GOBO East Cambodia is closely affiliated with Warner International and referenced as a senior member of its leadership on official social media, and both platforms were built on almost identical source code; both also appear connected to formal banking institutions and licensed cryptocurrency exchanges. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both online platforms have been developed using almost identical source code"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Videos of Kokang casino operators including Warner and Fully Light International show casino cages filled with very large volumes of cash packaged in Wa State Bank bags; the bank is an unregulated SR 2 bank run by the sanctioned UWSA, historically among the region's largest drug producers and connected to the sanctioned Zhao Wei transnational organized crime group in Lao PDR. (p. 101)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casino cages filled with extremely large volumes of cash packaged in Wa State Bank bags"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Kokang casinos and associated companies have a strong presence in 'grey and black business' Telegram channels facilitating cross-border 'blockchain' gambling, underground banking, laundering and recruitment in Myanmar, Cambodia, China and elsewhere, including underground crypto exchanges and 'motorcade' teams offering 'black USDT to cash bleaching services' via affiliated online casinos and peer-to-peer transfers through groups linked to the official Fully Light and GOBO East channels. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "black USDT to cash bleaching services"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The companies' Telegram community has hundreds of thousands of members and agents, with groups for underground banking and exchange pass-through activity, cybercrime as a service, recruitment of migrant smugglers and building underground banking and laundering teams, plus 'supply and demand' and 'VIP' groups advertising real-time cross-border value transfer requests; calls are issued exclusively by Fully Light administrator accounts, some backed by an official 'Fully Light guarantee'. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "consists of hundreds of thousands of members and agents"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In one 'supply and demand' post, administrators sought a large amount of Singapore dollars for USDT at SGD $1.32 (one transaction, one return, large quantity) in exchange for Singaporean 'white capital'; beneath it, another user claiming to represent 'Second Sister's Pawnshop' advertised swapping USDT for 'pure white capital' at a high rate citing strong business reputation. (p. 102)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "requesting a large amount of Singapore dollars in exchange for USDT at the rate of SGD $1.32"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In an affiliated Fully Light 'VIP communication channel', a user 'Mr. Big' cited difficulty moving his company's multi-billion-dollar cyberfraud proceeds into China and posted a paid ad hiring a team for the 'final conversion operation process' and to smuggle gold and precious stones into China for a 10 per cent fee; another user for an overseas labour service, apparently a Fully Light affiliate, told brokers recruited migrant workers would be illegally smuggled into Laukkai in Kokang SR 1 through Wa State SR 2 and detailed the intermediary's per-labourer bonus. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "help the company complete the final conversion operation process"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Several other groups linked to the channel advertise large amounts of capital, robust money mule networks, online casino operations and other resources in countries including Malaysia, Taiwan PoC, the Philippines, Viet Nam, the UAE, and elsewhere in South Asia, the Middle East and Africa. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "robust money mule networks, online casino operations, and other related resources"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: The report notes each individual incident in the Telegram channels requires verification, but finds their open advertising more concerning because Fully Light has developed large construction projects and an 'entertainment city' along the Thai border in Myawaddy, Myanmar, a major hub for USDT-based cyberfraud and online casino operations. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "each individual incident described above requires verification"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Recent law enforcement-confirmed reports describe foreign nationals seeking rescue after being lured into these venues under fraudulent employment schemes and held for ransom by human traffickers; the conglomerate also appears to have engaged in similar activities in the Philippines using a company with the same name as the Myawaddy entertainment city. Footnote 16: Myawaddy is a BGF-controlled township housing one of Southeast Asia's largest concentrations of cyberfraud compounds. (p. 103)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "foreign nationals seeking rescue after being lured into one of these venues under fraudulent employment schemes"</sub>
