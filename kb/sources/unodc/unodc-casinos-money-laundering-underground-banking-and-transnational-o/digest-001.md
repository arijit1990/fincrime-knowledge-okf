## Southeast Asia and the Pacific

- **descriptive / context**: The report says transnational organized crime in Southeast Asia has changed fast in recent years. The shift was first marked by growth in cross-border trafficking of synthetic drugs and other commodities, but the report states that the landscape has since moved on, with major transnational organized crime groups adopting technology and transforming the region's crime environment. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Major transnational organized crime groups have embraced technology and revolutionized the crime environment in the region."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: According to the report, this transformation came after years of progressive enforcement and regulatory efforts aimed at cross-border cash movement, the casino industry and related money laundering. It has been driven in part by casino and junket operators, who have in effect become the bankers of organized crime. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casino and junket operators who have effectively become bankers for organized crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many casinos and connected businesses such as junkets have physically moved into autonomous areas and Special Economic Zones (SEZs) across the region; the report says some of these places have in some instances turned into safe havens and breeding grounds for criminal networks. (p. 3)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "many casinos and connected businesses like junkets have physically relocated into autonomous areas and Special Economic Zones"</sub>

## Foreword

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Online casinos and cyberfraud have mushroomed across Southeast Asia, particularly in the Mekong since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, with organized crime groups running many operations with growing sophistication through data mining and processing, blockchain technology and increasingly generative artificial intelligence. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly in the Mekong since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Technology-aided acceleration of globalized crime networks centred in the Mekong has dramatically expanded criminal revenue streams, necessitating a revolution in regional underground banking architecture and the development of systems and infrastructure capable of moving and laundering massive volumes of state-backed fiat currencies and cryptocurrencies. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "capable of moving and laundering massive volumes of state-backed fiat and cryptocurrencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report is based on analysis of lengthy law enforcement investigations and prosecutions, using selected excerpts of prior UNODC analysis of criminal indictments, case records and court filings, intelligence documents and corporate records, together with consultation with international and regional law enforcement and criminal intelligence partners. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "criminal indictments, case records and court filings, intelligence documents, and corporate records"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The Foreword states that failure to address this landscape will have consequences for Southeast Asia and other regions as organized crime reinvests to further innovate, professionalize and consolidate operations, and that the report provides recommendations to improve knowledge, awareness, policy, capacity and coordination. (p. 5)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to address this landscape will have consequences for Southeast Asia and other regions"</sub>

(p. 5)[^original]

## Executive Summary

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Southeast Asia has become a testing ground for new technologies, first seen in the shift to synthetic drug production, particularly methamphetamine, with supply expanding to record levels year after year in the Golden Triangle and particularly Shan State, Myanmar. A less visible, parallel shift was taking place in the regional underground banking and money laundering business. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a less visible, parallel shift was taking place in the regional underground banking"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 1 defines underground banking as banking activities running parallel to and outside the formal banking system, commonly as informal value transfer. Informal value transfer systems involve dealers who facilitate transfer of value to a third party in another jurisdiction without physically moving the items; in East and Southeast Asia this has increasingly involved unregulated or illegal casino operations. Final settlement between brokers occurs through exchange of cash, cryptocurrency, real estate, trade or other means. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Underground banking refers to banking activities that run parallel to, and operate outside of, the formal banking system"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 2 defines a junket as an arrangement between a hosting casino and a junket operator to facilitate gambling by an individual or group of high-wealth players for a period through VIP programmes or tours, with operators offering incentives and perks to VIP club members. Recent law enforcement action shows some junket operators serve as international bank-like entities providing credit issuance, currency exchange, multi-currency payment and settlement, remittances and extra-legal debt collection, which organized crime has exploited. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A junket is an arrangement between a hosting casino and a junket operator to facilitate gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The expansion of the illicit economy necessitated a revolution in regional underground banking. The booming casino industry, followed by junkets, online casinos, e-junkets and increasingly illegal and under-regulated cryptocurrency exchanges, became foundational pieces of the banking architecture used by organized crime. Casinos and related businesses moved and laundered massive volumes of state-backed fiat and cryptocurrencies undetected, creating channels to integrate billions in criminal proceeds into the formal financial system. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "creating channels for effectively integrating billions in criminal proceeds into the formal financial system"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Scalable and digitized solutions have supercharged the criminal business environment across Southeast Asia, particularly the Mekong, attracting criminal networks, innovators and service providers and driving the need for underground banking. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the development of scalable and digitized solutions has supercharged the criminal business environment across Southeast Asia"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As law enforcement and regulators stepped up efforts against casinos, illegal online gambling and cyberfraud, organized crime began migrating operations into inaccessible, autonomous armed group territories and other pockets of criminality in and around the Golden Triangle; ongoing pressure is likely to motivate groups to innovate and move further into the shadows. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "organized crime have started migrating operations into inaccessible and autonomous armed group territories"</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The report rests on extensive examination of criminal indictments, case records, financial intelligence, court filings and related public disclosure, plus consultation with international and regional law enforcement and criminal intelligence partners over more than a year, as an attempt to understand the mechanics, intricacies and drivers of underground banking in the region. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "case records, financial intelligence, court filings, and related public disclosure, as well as consultation with both international and regional"</sub>

## Evolution of cross-border gambling and displacement into Southeast Asia

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The casino industry in Southeast Asia, particularly the Mekong, grew exponentially to 340+ licensed and unlicensed land-based casinos by early 2022. This followed enforcement and regulatory developments in Macau SAR driven partly by efforts to address illegal capital outflows, corruption and money laundering. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "totaling 340+ licensed and unlicensed land-based casinos by early 2022"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Between 2019 and 2023 the Macau measures culminated in arrests and convictions of junket tycoons Alvin Chau of Suncity and Levo Chan of Tak Chun, two of the world's largest junket operators, sentenced to 18 years and 14 years respectively on hundreds of charges relating to organized crime and illegal betting. The industry is far larger than these two, and many operators and players have been relocating to areas in and around loosely regulated SEZs in Southeast Asia. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Alvin Chau of Suncity and Levo Chan of Tak Chun, two of the world"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Licensed junkets in Macau SAR fell from a high of 235 in 2014 to just 36 in 2023, with approximately 12 currently in operation (source: Centre for Gaming and Tourism Studies, Macao Polytechnic University, 2023). (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "dropped off from a high of 235 in 2014 to just 36 in 2023"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Before the decline, Macau SAR's biggest junket operators made enormous profits compared to licensed casinos. Junkets, historically closely linked with organized crime, served as credit providers for customers seeking to evade capital controls and quotas, while organized crime traditionally provided debt collection; these profits supported other criminal enterprises needing money laundering or transfer services and seemingly legitimate investments. In the mid-2000s forward-thinking junket operators diversified into online gambling, expanding the market and increasing revenues and profits. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "served as credit providers for customers seeking to evade capital controls and quotas"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Displacement into and growth of the casino industry is particularly noticeable in the Mekong region, where attractive environmental factors converge: autonomous Special Regions (SRs) in Myanmar with an absence of government control and rule of law, many under-regulated SEZs, remote and porous borders allowing easy cross-border movement of people and commodities, rapid digitization and large illicit economies. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "characterized by an absence of government control and rule of law"</sub>

## Proliferation of online casinos and infiltration of organized crime

- **descriptive / finding or result**: According to latest projections the formal online gambling market will grow to more than US $205 billion by 2030 (Polaris Market Research), with Asia Pacific representing the largest share of market growth between 2022 and 2026 at a projected 37 per cent (Technavio). The rise of the offshore online casino industry (including online sports betting) in high-risk Southeast Asian jurisdictions, particularly the Mekong, is a major and growing challenge, and Macau SAR junket operators and close criminal associates have been key drivers. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "projected to grow to more than US $205 billion by 2030"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Unregulated and under-regulated online casino platforms run by junket operators are profitable and also serve as a channel of credit settlement between junkets and clients, and have been misused extensively to commingle and disguise proceeds of crime as legitimate online gambling profits. Most if not all of the largest junket operators have established such operations, with smaller junkets and online platforms acting as customer referral agents where further laundering and layering can occur. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have been observed to be misused extensively to comingle and disguise proceeds of crime as legitimate online gambling profits"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Many illegal online casinos in Southeast Asia have diversified into cyberfraud operations, with extensive evidence of organized crime infiltrating casinos and SEZs to conceal various illicit activities. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have diversified their business lines into cyberfraud operations"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Because of limited access to SRs, SEZs and casino and cyberfraud compounds, it is not possible to determine the full extent of these operations, but cases involving dismantled illegal online gambling and cyberfraud operations, rescued trafficking victims, seizures of bulk cash and virtual assets and arrests of known organized crime figures show the scale is massive. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it is not possible to determine the full extent of these operations"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Mirror websites, cryptocurrency and third-party betting software from white-label providers mean it has never been easier to set up an online casino with limited technical expertise and capital, irrespective of a jurisdiction's gambling laws. Advances in internet payment technology and growth in third-party payment providers, e-wallets and other solutions support the market. The scale has drawn in unprecedented numbers of young people seeking work in the sector, with opportunities for some and risks for others linked to recruitment fraud and trafficking for forced criminality. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "it has never been easier to set up an online casino operation with limited technical expertise and overhead capital"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 7: the vast majority of online gambling platforms in Southeast Asia exploit mirror websites, exact replicas of primary betting websites under different URLs, often algorithmically generated and used to evade regulatory attention, with hundreds of mirror websites run on the same server so services continue if a URL is shut down. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exact replicas of primary betting websites housed under different URLs"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 8: white-labels are similar to franchises; operators can outsource every component of the business (betting technology, offshore licensing schemes, website design, customer data and management, branding and marketing, operating license) to third parties. A white label may be a product, such as a live-dealer casino platform, leased or sold to others who rebrand it, and providers may facilitate sub-licenses; for example, Asian-facing operators wishing to advertise on UK sports club jerseys need a local licence, commonly facilitated through white-label companies in offshore jurisdictions such as the British Virgin Islands or Isle of Man. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "betting operators being able to outsource every component of the business"</sub>

## Casino and junket-based underground banking and money laundering

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Cases analysed show casinos and junkets are a critical piece of underground banking and money laundering infrastructure, serving transnational organized crime groups in the region and globally. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casinos and junkets represent a critical piece of the underground banking and money laundering infrastructure"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Money laundering is typically understood to occur in three phases: placement, when funds are integrated into the financial system or a legal business; layering, the process of distancing money from its illegal source to make the money trail hard for investigators to follow; and integration, when money enters the legal economy and appears clean, so investigators can no longer tell its origin. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Money laundering is typically understood to occur in three phases:"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: All three laundering phases are at play in land-based and online casino and junket methods, offering an end-to-end solution that lets organized crime move massive volumes without exposure to the formal financial system. Methods include cash-in cash-out, collusion between gamblers, junket financing, so-called offsetting arrangements, and misuse of casino VIP cash accounts, among others. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All three phases of the money laundering process are at play in land-based and online casino and junket based methods"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 9, cash-in cash-out: the simplest, most typical casino laundering method; a criminal exchanges money for playing chips and converts them back to cash so dirty money can be mistaken for casino winnings. Some players divide money across several betting accounts to appear less suspicious. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A criminal simply exchanges their money for playing chips and then converts them back into cash."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 10, collusion between players (intentional gambling losses): proceeds of crime are brought into physical or online casinos and deliberately lost, for example in poker, in a way that benefits an accomplice playing in the same game. This lets launderers dodge AML detection policies triggered only by successful bets against the casino itself, not against other players. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allows launderers to dodge any AML detection policies that are only triggered by successful bets against the casino itself"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 11, junket financing: a gambler deposits money into a junket account in one country or stakes other assets, then accesses this credit in another jurisdiction to gamble; a system of debits and credits offsets wins and losses against the original deposit, letting the operator move money or value quickly and informally below the radar of tax and law enforcement. Junkets may offer high interest to individuals willing to store money with the junket for offsetting. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "allowing the operator to move money/ value quickly and informally below the radar of tax and law enforcement agencies"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Casinos and junket operators in East and Southeast Asia and the Pacific have been found to provide 'safekeeping' transactions in which players, including those with clear links to organized crime, may deposit casino chips with casino treasury divisions and cash out later. This evolved into 'investment' arrangements with major junket operators in which investors can earn between 5 to 7 per cent per month on funds deposited with the junket. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can earn between 5 to 7 per cent per month on the funds deposited with the junket"</sub>

## Attractiveness of online gambling for underground banking and money laundering

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Because of the various anonymous payment methods and authorities' very limited view of what goes on in an online gambling account, it is difficult to verify source of funds and whether an account is used for legitimate gambling or for underground banking and money laundering. The online gambling sector is characterized by a non-face-to-face element, minimal if any compliance staff, and huge, complex, often international transaction volumes and financial flows. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "minimal, if any, compliance staff, and huge and complex volumes of transactions and financial flows"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The involvement of various jurisdictions and limited legislative harmonization complicate investigations and create enforcement gaps and grey zones that organized crime exploits. As designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs), online casinos' compliance regimes lag far behind banks', making them attractive targets for criminals and enabling what continues to be a 'heads-down' approach by operators. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the sophistication of the compliance regimes instituted by online casinos lag far behind those of banks"</sub>
