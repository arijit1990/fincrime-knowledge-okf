## Taiwan PoC, Money Laundering Networks, and Organized Crime in the Mekong

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Taiwan PoC-based organized crime groups have a longstanding Southeast Asian presence, particularly the Mekong, from the heroin trade in north Shan State to industrial-scale synthetic drug production and trafficking, money laundering, underground banking and increasingly online casinos, cyberfraud and human trafficking; authorities link several cases to the Bamboo Union and Heavenly Way Alliance (Celestial Way). (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Taiwan PoC-based organized crime groups have a longstanding history in Southeast Asia"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Cambodia is increasingly targeted by Taiwanese networks for illicit manufacture and trafficking of synthetic drugs, particularly ketamine, and for money laundering; ketamine seizures there rose to nearly 2.8 tons in 2021 and over 13.5 tons in 2022. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "amounting to nearly 2.8 tons in 2021 and over 13.5 tons of the drug in 2022"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In the week of 4 July 2022 Cambodian authorities arrested 11 suspects from China and Taiwan PoC and seized a sophisticated clandestine ketamine laboratory and chemical storage facilities in Svey Rieng province about 10 km from the Viet Nam border. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "arrested 11 suspects from China and Taiwan PoC"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In July 2023 Cambodian authorities with US and Taiwan PoC officials seized 789 kg of ketamine in Sihanoukville and arrested six traffickers including a believed high-level Bamboo Union member; the drugs originated in Myanmar, transited Lao PDR to a Sihanoukville storage facility and were destined for Taiwan PoC. Taiwan's Criminal Investigation Bureau said ringleader Chen Qixiang was a high-ranking Heavenly Way Alliance triad member. (p. 93)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "seizing 789 kg of ketamine in Sihanoukville and arresting six traffickers"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2019 Taiwan PoC authorities convicted trafficker Lin Hsiao-tao, reported by police as controlling about one-third of the drug supply in Taiwan PoC, for arranging large maritime shipments of methamphetamine, ketamine and heroin from the Golden Triangle through Cambodia and Thailand using a fishing fleet and high-seas exchanges. Regional law enforcement report Taiwan PoC-based traffickers receiving payments partly through the Suncity junket network. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "controlling approximately one-third of the drug supply in Taiwan PoC"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Vast profits from the regional drug trade have required industrial-scale laundering and underground banking to disburse payments and enable informal cross-border value transfers, and both land-based and online casinos have been a preferred method; Taiwan PoC's 2021 AML risk assessment rated online casinos 'very high' overall risk. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "both land-based and online casinos have been one of the preferred methods used to do so"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Quoting Taiwan PoC's 2021 risk assessment: criminal groups put illicit gains from fraud and drug trafficking into gambling websites or third-party payment services as stored value or exchange tokens, swap cash for chips, then require the site to remit the money to a designated account; proceeds are mixed with legitimate funds to confuse cash flow and create multiple breakpoints. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Criminal groups usually put illicit gains such as fraud and drug trafficking into gambling websites"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: The assessment also says gambling companies require employees to open bank payroll accounts but turn over the deposit book, card and account use rights, making them the company's nominee accounts, with employees carefully monitored; illegal gambling companies need large numbers of nominee accounts to convert gambling money. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "must be turned over, which in fact become the company’s nominee account"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The assessment describes the division of labor: mainland Chinese are capital owners, IP addresses are set up in the Philippines, Taiwan is the gambling 'original equipment manufacturing' base, offshore internet data centers are set up in Hong Kong SAR, Macau SAR, Southeast Asia or the United States, and VPNs avoid detection; inflows and outflows of proceeds span Asia, mainly Mainland China, Hong Kong SAR, the Philippines, the UAE, Viet Nam and Cambodia. (p. 94)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "people from mainland China are the capital owners, the IP address is set up in the Philippines"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In July 2023, at Taiwan PoC police request, Thai authorities arrested Associate 2 for his alleged role in a regional telecommunication fraud and laundering network that included a large-scale online casino operation, underground money and cryptocurrency exchanges and forced labour; he left Taiwan PoC in October 2022 after a tip-off, travelled on a Cambodian passport issued December 2021, and was charged over laundering an estimated US $86.62 million plus violations of banking, organized crime, gambling and fraud laws; authorities later said he laundered as much as 20 billion Thai baht (about US $562 million). (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Associate 2 (referenced in above case study chapter on Alvin Chau and Suncity)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Authorities seized luxury real estate and cryptocurrency wallets with US $6.4 million of USDT; Associate 2 was found hiding in a luxury Bangkok condominium and extradited in August 2023, following New Taipei City prosecutors' November 2022 charges that led to the arrest of his partner and mentor Associate 1, an alleged Heavenly Way Alliance member and former business partner of Chau. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Associate 2 was found hiding in a luxury condominium in Bangkok, Thailand, and extradited in August 2023"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Authorities in Taiwan PoC and Thailand confirm Associate 2 headed several online gambling companies in Asia and Europe supporting laundering, leading a syndicate controlling a conglomerate of international companies used to launder proceeds of Cambodia-based online gambling and cyberfraud groups. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "controlled a conglomerate consisting of several international companies used for laundering the proceeds of online gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: With Associate 1, Associate 2 is understood to have serviced his online gambling operations through Software Company 1 (registered in Taiwan PoC) and companies in offshore gambling hubs including the British Virgin Islands, Curaçao, Cyprus and Vanuatu; his largest ventures included the i-gaming white-label Entertainment Group 1, with major operations in Bavet, active online across Europe, Asia and the Americas 'legally or illegally'. Other case details remain undisclosed due to the ongoing trial. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Among Associate 2’s largest ventures was high-profile i-gaming white-label, Entertainment Group 1"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Entertainment Group 1 claimed to have established Casino 1 and a tech-park in Bavet and the Junket Group 2 VIP club, which until recently operated in Macau SAR, the Philippines and Cambodia's Xi-Hu Hotel; Junket Group 2 had an extensive partnership with Suncity and Chau, opening a joint entertainment company in Taiwan PoC and joint VIP rooms in the Philippines and Macau from 2014, with Associates 1 and 2 appearing alongside Chau at regional opening events. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Junket Group 2 also held an extensive business partnership with Suncity and Alvin Chau"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: According to prosecutors, Associates 1 and 2 created several international shell companies and used money mule accounts to transfer and commingle clean funds and criminal proceeds via online gambling platforms and cryptocurrency, and also developed online shopping and instant messaging platforms providing third-party payment processing that further obscured the source and destination of proceeds; prosecutors also found evidence linking both to kidnapping and extortion rings that advertised jobs overseas. (p. 95)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had created several international shell companies and used money mule accounts to transfer and commingle clean funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Casino 1 and some Entertainment Group 1 online operations in its tech-park are in the Moc Bai commune of Bavet, a major hub for online gambling, cyberfraud and trafficking for forced criminality, largely staffed by workers recruited from China, Taiwan PoC and Viet Nam; some areas are blocked by high walls, razor wire and guarded gates, and law enforcement sources and victim testimonies name Casino 1 and its gated compound as sites of human trafficking, forced labour and extortion. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has emerged as a major hub for online gambling, cyberfraud, and trafficking for forced criminality"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Vietnamese and other Southeast Asian reports name Casino 1 and the broader Moc Bai area as places where workers have been sold and held against their will, including an interview with a man who helped rescue three teenagers from Casino 1 in mid-2022, paying a ransom of US $6,000 per person; recruiters regularly post jobs for the Casino 1 tech-park on Facebook and TikTok with basic job descriptions like those of other online gambling and cyberfraud operators. (p. 96)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "paying a ransom of US $6,000 per person"</sub>
