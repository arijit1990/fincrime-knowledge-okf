## Rise of 'points running' syndicates, 'motorcades', and cryptocurrency

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A key characteristic of online casino operations is operators' ever-evolving efforts to hide illicit fund flows from authorities. Together with capital control enforcement in some East and Southeast Asian countries, this was accelerated by COVID-19 restrictions on cross-border movement of goods and people, which limited cash smuggling across borders and into casinos and drove organized crime to innovate its fund transfer methods. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A key characteristic of online casino operations are the ever-evolving efforts of operators to hide flows of illicit funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 12, offsetting arrangements (also called mirror transactions): similar to traditional Hawala networks, they transfer value between jurisdictions via credit and debit relationships between entities in different countries; money debited from an entity in one jurisdiction is credited to (sometimes the same) entity in a second, requiring the facilitator to have fund access in both. Offsetting through casinos and junkets and trade-based arrangements has been reported as increasingly used by Asia Pacific money laundering organizations connected to drug production and trafficking, precursor chemical shipments and cybercrime, among other crime types. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Offsetting arrangements (also known as mirror transactions): Similar to traditional Hawala networks"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 13, misuse of gambling accounts for illegal transactions between players: buyers and sellers of illegal items could use their gambling accounts like bank accounts to make and receive payments; once the seller's account is credited, the money can be cashed out claiming it was a successful gamble, or used for hiding purposes on holding accounts or for wagering at casinos. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "buyers and sellers of illegal items could use their respective gambling accounts as traditional bank accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 14, 'dummy' room transactions: international VIP customers use a credit or debit card at the resort hotel to authorize a transfer of funds to be made available at the casino and/or junket; the hotel issues a room charge bill falsely asserting services were provided, the patron pays and receives a voucher acknowledging receipt of funds, is escorted to the casino cage and exchanges it for cash or chips. 'Incidental' charges: a patron who has not stayed at the hotel arranges to pay an incidental charge by credit or debit card, and the money for it is then made available to the patron. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The hotel issues a room charge bill to the patron, falsely asserting that the hotel had provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Authorities in the region report a growing number of methods used by organized crime to move illicit funds through and into gambling platforms. Common modalities include online casinos using underground money laundering and banking networks, including 'points running' or 'score running' syndicates, money mule 'motorcade' teams, and third- and fourth-party payment providers, sometimes called running points platforms and syndicates. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Authorities in the region have reported a growing number of methods being used by organized crime"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Footnote 15: UNODC has observed regional underground banking and money laundering networks in casino- and junket-based operations, increasingly online and cryptocurrency integrated, targeting diaspora populations in countries including Australia, Canada, the United States and several European jurisdictions. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "targeting various diaspora populations based in countries including Australia, Canada, the United States"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 16: DNFBPs are reporting entities including casinos, real estate agents, dealers in precious metals and stones, lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants, and trust company service providers; they are an attractive channel for money laundering and financial crime. DNFBPs are acknowledged as a global vulnerability with historically weaker implementation and enforcement of national AML measures, particularly acute in Southeast Asia and especially the five lower Mekong countries (Cambodia, Lao PDR, Myanmar, Thailand and Viet Nam). (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "DNFBPs are acknowledged as a global vulnerability and have historically enjoyed weaker levels of implementation and enforcement"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 17, points running syndicates (sometimes 'moving ants'): used to transfer stolen money between multiple bank or cryptocurrency exchange accounts and online casinos to obfuscate source and destination of funds. The informal, often cross-border modality can involve hundreds or thousands of individuals, popular among underemployed youth who provide their bank accounts and set up front companies for account and company pass-through activity (collecting and transferring funds of unknown origin) for a commission. Used to collect money for crimes including drug trafficking, cyberfraud and illegal gambling. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to transfer stolen money between multiple bank or cryptocurrency exchange accounts, as well as online casinos"</sub>

## Rise of ‘points running’ syndicates, ‘motorcades’, and cryptocurrency

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 17 says running points has mostly been used to support illegal online gambling. It adds a further laundering layer: funds pass through online gambling platforms and are then "white-washed" by cashing in and cashing out, so that the source of funds appears to be casino winnings. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "funds are routed through online gambling platforms and subsequently ‘white-washed’ by cashing in and cashing out"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 17 says this approach is usually combined with transaction miscoding to hide that the casino platform is being used, and it relies on third- and fourth-party payment providers to help disguise what the transactions really are. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This strategy is commonly mixed with transaction miscoding to obfuscate use of the casino platform"</sub>

## Rise of 'points running' syndicates, 'motorcades', and cryptocurrency

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 18, motorcades: the term is found on platforms where criminals advertise services to one another, including Facebook and Telegram groups. Motorcades extend points running syndicates by offering layering schemes routing money through multiple bank accounts for a percentage of total funds; those at the 'front of the car' who bear most detection risk have advertised commission fees of 20 to 40 per cent online. UNODC has observed large motorcade teams cooperating on very large contracts to improve concealment and effectiveness, and per authorities smaller online casinos are used further down the laundering chain by organized crime groups and illegal betting syndicates to 'white-wash' funds. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have been seen advertising commission fees of between 20 to 40 per cent online"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 19: among the most common modalities are fourth-party payments and running points platforms. Fourth-party payments evolved from third-party payments, which exploited gaps in transaction reporting in popular third-party payment apps; although both platforms have since enhanced reporting of suspected gambling transactions at the request of Chinese authorities, fourth-party providers now typically install an additional intermediary between bookmaker, bettor and third-party payment application to circumvent these measures and obscure transactions. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "fourth-party payment providers now typically install an additional intermediary between bookmaker, bettor, and third-party payment application"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: As third- and fourth-party payments became better understood by authorities following 'Operation Chain Break' and other measures in China, organized crime accelerated integrating cryptocurrencies into illegal betting, creating significant investigative challenges. Authorities report growing use of high-speed laundering 'motorcade' teams specializing in underground USDT-fiat exchanges across East and Southeast Asia, and mass recruitment of mule bank accounts across virtually all Asia Pacific jurisdictions that can be purchased for as little as US $30. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can be purchased for as little as US $30"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Footnote 21: in response to large-scale underground banking using third- and fourth-party payment platforms and the casino industry, the Chinese government initiated 'Operation Chain Break' in 2019 and intensified it in later years; efforts to disrupt money flows from mainland China and Macau SAR to Southeast Asia included an August 2020 'blacklist' of casino destinations. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In response to large-scale underground banking utilizing thirdand fourth-party payment platforms"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Due to cryptocurrency-integrated motorcades, points running syndicates and other challenges, in 2021 the Government of China banned cryptocurrency transactions, trading and mining. The industry migrated to other jurisdictions, driving up already rising cryptocurrency adoption in several Southeast Asian countries together with the establishment of high-risk and underground cryptocurrency exchanges. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in 2021 the Government of China banned cryptocurrency transactions, trading, and mining"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Cryptocurrency flows connected to organized crime have been cited as vastly underestimated by industry experts and by law enforcement and regulators in the region. Experts point to shortcomings in existing analyses: massive gaps in crime attribution on the blockchain, fabricated reporting by crypto exchanges, and prevalence of wash trading, which inflates crypto transaction volumes and shrinks the share of illicit transactions identified. Footnote 24 defines wash trading as market manipulation in which an entity simultaneously sells and buys the same instruments, creating a false impression of activity without market risk or change in position. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "massive gaps in crime attribution on the blockchain, fabricated reporting by crypto exchanges"</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Authorities in the region report major challenges in blockchain investigation capacity alongside rapidly growing use of high-risk and underground cryptocurrency exchanges by organized crime. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "major challenges in blockchain investigation capacity alongside rapidly growing use of high risk and underground cryptocurrency exchanges"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Money laundering using cryptocurrencies follows the general placement-layering-integration pattern with specific features: (1) cryptocurrencies are anonymous at creation, so the placement stage is often absent; (2) creating an account (address) takes seconds and is free; (3) a large laundering scheme with thousands of transfers can be created at low cost and run by computer script; (4) rapid exchange-rate increases make it very easy to justify unexpected wealth, or alternatively losses, through cryptocurrencies. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Cryptocurrencies are anonymous at their point of creation therefore the placement stage of the money laundering process is often absent."</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Online gambling platforms, especially illegal ones, are among the most popular vehicles for cryptocurrency-based launderers, particularly those using Tether (USDT) on the TRON blockchain. Funds are paid into an online gambling platform or an affiliate agent, possibly part of a laundering network, who arranges transfer of in-game points through identifiable or anonymous accounts; points are cashed out or bet, often in collusion with affiliates. Once the gambling account is paid out in a desired currency and jurisdiction, the money can effectively be given legal status and integrated into the formal financial system. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Once the money in the gambling account is paid out in a desired currency and jurisdiction"</sub>

## Convergence between online gambling, cyberfraud, and human trafficking

- **descriptive / finding or result**: A growing number of organized crime groups diversified extensively into cyberfraud in several Southeast Asian countries. Following the COVID-19 outbreak, with enhanced pressure on the online casino industry, groups innovated, digitized and diversified. Casino operators moved deeper into loosely regulated, vulnerable jurisdictions including Cambodia, Lao PDR and the Philippines and border areas controlled by armed groups in Myanmar, adding cyberfraud; others moved outside Asia (UAE, Africa, Eastern Europe, the Pacific) and tried to legitimize operations by investing in licensed but under-regulated offshore gambling companies and technology-related businesses. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casino operators have moved bases of operation deeper into loosely regulated and highly vulnerable jurisdictions"</sub>

## Convergence between online gambling, cyberfraud, and human trafficking

- **descriptive / finding or result**: These groups exploited casino and SEZ infrastructure, information technology advances and rising youth unemployment, recruiting hundreds of thousands of workers with promises of lucrative employment through professionalized schemes, often via Telegram, WeChat, TikTok and Facebook. Cyberfraud victims were targeted using data bought and sold on online data markets and lured to invest in professionally designed fraudulent investment platforms. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They have recruited hundreds of thousands of workers using the promise of lucrative employment"</sub>

## Growing indication of generative AI use, deepfake fraud, and other malicious technology

- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Advances in LLM-based chatbots, deepfake technology and automation have produced more sophisticated cyber fraud posing a major threat to individuals and the formal banking industry. Using AI to create computer-generated images and voices virtually indistinguishable from real ones, scammers execute social engineering scams with alarming success, including sophisticated investment fraud and financial grooming such as 'pig butchering' and increasingly task scams, sextortion and schemes impersonating law enforcement officers and other officials. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "recent advances in large language model-based chatbots, deepfake technology, and automation, have given rise to more sophisticated"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Criminals exploit data obtained from sources including the dark web and 'grey and black market' Telegram groups to profile targets, and use profile photos harvested from social media to create fake profiles and 'masks' that can bypass digital face verification systems and know-your-customer (KYC) measures, adding to challenges around money-muling, money laundering and underground banking. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "profile photos harvested from social media platforms to create fake profiles and"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Authorities report Southeast Asia-based cyberfraud operations rapidly diversifying by expanding into development of malicious mobile and web applications or malware, the broader blockchain gaming industry, online bank fraud schemes, high-risk and underground cryptocurrency exchange and payment services, and offering a range of cybercrimes as a service. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "offering a range of cybercrimes as a service"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 29: pig butchering is investment fraud in which criminals lure victims into digital relationships to build trust before convincing them to invest in cryptocurrency via fraudulent platforms. Footnote 30: task scams involve online recruitment for what appears to be a remote work scheme; victims carry out online tasks such as liking posts or leaving automated reviews for commission, but 'earning' more requires layers of 'investment' into the tiered operation via cryptocurrency which is then stolen. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pig butchering is a type of investment fraud in which criminals lure victims into digital relationships"</sub>

- **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation: develop national action plans and a regional strategy to deal with organized crime, underground banking, money laundering and related criminality in casinos, junkets and SEZs; and revise and strengthen legislation on money laundering, asset forfeiture, casino supervision and management, and SEZs. - **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation: create a regional inter-agency forum, with participation of regulatory bodies, financial intelligence units and law enforcement, to share information and intelligence on the use of casinos for money laundering; and identify unlicensed and unregulated casinos, including online platforms, and prevent them from operating.   <sub>anchor: "A regional inter-agency forum to share information and intelligence on the use of casinos for money laundering is created"</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation: recover, preserve, analyze and share digital forensic evidence; establish a mechanism with social network service providers to monitor job recruitment advertisements; and train authorities on online gambling operations and money laundering methods enabled by sophisticated technologies. - **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation: regulators improve capacity for land-based and online casino management and supervision, particularly in integrating suspicious transaction reporting software and surveillance technologies and enforcing AML measures including enhanced beneficial ownership requirements and KYC and customer due diligence (CDD) policies and procedures, particularly for junkets and associated VIP rooms. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "particularly in the areas of integrating suspicious transaction reporting software and surveillance technologies"</sub>

  <sub>anchor: "Specialized training on money laundering investigations and asset forfeiture, is offered to police, prosecutors, and regulators."</sub>
- **recommended / recommendation**: Recommendation: review and strengthen licensing regimes for money transfer services, making it a criminal offence for a business to engage in related activity without a licence, including cryptocurrency exchanges. (p. 22)[^original]
