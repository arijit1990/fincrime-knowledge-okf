## Regional Overview

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Casinos have been found to function as banks for organized criminal groups operating in the region and globally; in the five lower Mekong countries and the Philippines casinos show extreme and accelerating infiltration by organized crime, and most countries lack a functioning, enforceable casino regulatory management and supervision system. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "casinos which have been found to be functioning as banks for organized criminal groups"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Severe gaps have been observed in transaction monitoring and reporting software integration and utilization by casino operators in countries including Cambodia, Lao PDR and Myanmar. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Severe gaps have also been observed in transaction monitoring and reporting software integration"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Most countries in the region pay virtually no attention to casino junket operators and other VIP tour companies, even though a growing number of East and Southeast Asian authorities report widespread misuse of these businesses for large-scale money laundering and underground banking by transnational organized crime groups. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "pay virtually no attention to casino junket operators and other VIP tour companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: One reason for the lack of DNFBP prioritization in the Mekong is large disparities in foreign direct investment (FDI) and economic development, together with rapid casino-industry growth resulting from tourism and investment-attraction policies; the region includes Singapore, with one of the highest GDPs in the world, and three least developed countries where foreign aid is sometimes known to exceed foreign investment. (p. 43)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "large disparities in foreign direct investment (FDI) and economic development"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Countries with smaller FDI inflows have actively sought FDI, including by granting casino licenses to investors, and countries with less strict standards for vetting FDI are vulnerable to organized crime masking illicit proceeds as genuine investments. ## Casino- and junket-based money laundering

- **descriptive / context**: The establishment of casinos and junkets has surged across East and Southeast Asia and the Pacific over the past decade, forming a critical part of the underground banking and money laundering infrastructure serving transnational organized crime groups. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The establishment of casinos and junkets has surged across East and Southeast Asia and the Pacific"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Money laundering occurs in three phases: placement (funds integrated into the financial system or a legal business), layering (distancing money from its illegal source to make the trail hard for investigators to follow) and integration (money enters the legal economy, appears clean and its origin can no longer be traced). (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fundamentally, money laundering occurs in three phases"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: All three laundering phases are at play in casino- and junket-based methods, which give organized crime groups an end-to-end solution to move massive volumes of money without exposure to the formal financial system. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "All three phases of the money laundering process are at play in casino- and junket-based methods"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Laundering and underground transfers through casinos, junkets and increasingly online casinos use typologies including cash-in cash-out, collusion between gamblers, junket financing or 'offsetting' arrangements, misuse of gambling 'VIP cash' accounts, buying another player's winnings, and mixing gambling and non-gambling laundering methods. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "can be conducted using a variety of typologies including cash-in cash-out"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 66, cash-in cash-out: the simplest and most typical casino laundering method, in which a criminal exchanges money for playing chips and converts them back to cash so the money can be presented as casino winnings; some players split money across several betting accounts to look less suspicious. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A criminal simply exchanges their money for playing chips and then converts them back into cash"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 67, collusion between players (intentional gambling losses): proceeds of crime are brought into physical or online casinos and deliberately lost, for example in poker, to benefit an accomplice in the same game; this lets launderers evade AML detection policies triggered only by successful bets against the casino itself rather than against other players. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "proceeds of crime are brought into either physical or online casinos and deliberately lost"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 68, junket financing: a gambler deposits money into a junket account in one country or stakes other assets, then accesses the credit in another jurisdiction to gamble; the debits and credits offset wins and losses against the original deposit, letting the operator move value quickly and informally below the radar of tax and law enforcement agencies. Junkets may also offer high interest to people who store money with them for offsetting. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "gambler/client deposits money into junket account in one country or stakes other assets"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 69, offsetting arrangements (also called mirror transactions): similar to traditional Hawala networks, they transfer value between jurisdictions through credit and debit relationships between entities in different countries, requiring the facilitator to have fund access in both. Offsetting through casinos, junkets and trade-based arrangements is reported as increasingly used by Asia Pacific money laundering organizations tied to drug production and trafficking, precursor chemical shipments and cybercrime, among other crime types. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "similar to traditional Hawala networks; used as a means of transferring value between jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 70, misuse of gambling accounts between players: buyers and sellers of illegal items use their gambling accounts like bank accounts to pay and receive; once the seller's account is credited the money can be cashed out as a successful gamble, or held in the accounts or wagered at casinos. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "buyers and sellers of illegal items could use their respective gambling accounts as traditional bank accounts"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 71, 'dummy' room transactions and 'incidental' charges: international VIP customers use a credit or debit card at the resort hotel to authorize a funds transfer to their casino/junket; the hotel issues a room charge bill falsely asserting services, the patron pays and receives a voucher, then exchanges it at the casino cage for cash or chips. With 'incidental' charges, a patron who did not stay at the hotel pays an incidental charge by card and the money is made available to the patron. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The hotel issues a room charge bill to the patron, falsely asserting that the hotel had provided"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Casinos and junket operators in East and Southeast Asia and the Pacific have been found to provide 'safekeeping' transactions in which players, including those with clear organized crime links, deposit casino chips with casino treasury divisions and cash out later. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have also been found to provide so-called ‘ safekeeping’ transactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Safekeeping has evolved into 'investment' arrangements with major junket operators in which 'investors' can earn 5 to 7 per cent per month on funds deposited into the junket; the Philippines Security Exchange Commission warned about the trend in 2019. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "‘investors’ can earn between 5 to 7 per cent per month on the funds deposited into the junket"</sub>

## Extensive misuse of the junket industry

- **descriptive / definition**: A junket is an arrangement between a hosting casino and a junket operator to facilitate gambling by an individual or group of high-wealth players for a period through VIP programmes or tours. Junkets are an extension of casino operators and drive VIP gambling by connecting high rollers with casinos; operators can offer incentives and perks to VIP club members. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A junket is an arrangement between a hosting casino and a junket operator to facilitate gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Recent law enforcement action shows some junket operators have served as international bank-like entities offering underground financial services including credit issuance, currency exchange, multi-currency payment and settlement solutions, remittances and extra-legal debt collection, which organized crime has exploited. (p. 44)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "serve as international bank-like entities, providing a variety of underground financial services"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The partner casino often pays some or all of a gambler's travel and accommodation costs, while junket operators typically receive a commission from casinos based on the amount gambled by players in VIP gaming rooms unavailable to mass-market gamblers. A growing number of junket operators also run online and proxy gambling platforms. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "commission from casinos derived from the amount of money gambled by the players within VIP gaming rooms"</sub>

- **descriptive / case example**: Sam Gor, also known as 'The Company', is a multibillion-dollar transnational organized crime syndicate with a dominant role in the Asia Pacific drug trade; money laundering has been essential to it, though little official public information exists on its methods. ## Extensive misuse of the junket industry

- **descriptive / context**: Junket operators began as recruiters of high net worth gamblers but diversified into credit providers for customers wishing to evade capital controls and so became debt collectors; because both lines are illegal in mainland China, they relied on other means, particularly organized crime connections, to collect debts. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "diversified into credit providers to satisfy the need of customers wishing to evade capital controls"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: In the early 2000s forward-thinking junkets diversified into online gambling, first for core clients in Asia and then globally, vastly expanding their market and revenues. (p. 45)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "forward-thinking junkets began to diversify into online gambling"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Junket operators have been relocating operations and players to small-scale casinos in Southeast Asia after regulatory reforms transformed Macau SAR gaming; Macau once had 235 junket promoters but only 18 licensed junket operators remained active as of January 2024.
