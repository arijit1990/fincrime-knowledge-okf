## Alvin Chau Cheok-wa (周焯华) and Suncity junket

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Suncity clients using the settlement system could move large amounts of value between jurisdictions without moving hard currency and with no scrutiny, bypassing the formal financial system. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "large amounts of value from one jurisdiction into another without moving any hard currency and with no scrutiny"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: From 2016 Suncity increasingly turned to online gambling and e-junkets using proprietary 'integrated entertainment platforms' (Easy Bet, Universal e-city, SCM Alliance e-city, SCPP Alliance e-city): in-game points (non-fungible tokens) were credited to Suncity-linked online accounts, gamblers 'recharged' and 'bought code' by converting money from Suncity VIP accounts and reversed it on withdrawal; points could be exchanged for chips, services or cashed out in various currencies. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To reach a broader consumer base, Suncity increasingly turned to online gambling and e-junkets beginning in 2016"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Usernames and passwords on these platforms were linked to the client's original Suncity VIP account, and VIP account credit could be obtained by several methods including cash and various assets at the time of collection. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Usernames and passwords on these platforms would be linked to the client’s original Suncity VIP account"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: According to the initial indictment, Chau and Main Camp associates set up a network of front companies in mainland China, including real estate and property development companies, to launder proceeds under various identities, with assets held and managed by the Suncity asset department; other proceeds were placed, layered and integrated through Suncity VIP room accounts so funds appeared as legitimate Suncity revenues. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "had set up a network of front companies in mainland China including real estate and property development companies"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Using Suncity's regional VIP room network and offsetting, billions of dollars in underground cross-border payments could be transmitted and effectively laundered into jurisdictions where Suncity operated through traditional cash-in cash-out casino laundering. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "would enable billions of dollars in underground crossborder payments to be transmitted and effectively laundered"</sub>

## Confirmed links to organized crime

- **descriptive / case example**: Australia: Alvin Chau, Suncity Group and other junkets were investigated in Australia over illegal bookmaking, drug trafficking and large-scale money laundering at Suncity VIP rooms in Melbourne, Perth and Sydney, culminating in the 2021 parliamentary casino inquiry; a 2018 CCTV image shows Suncity staff handling large cash amounts in the junket's private salon at the Star. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "culminating in the 2021 parliamentary inquiry into casinos"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The inquiry found several junkets, including Suncity and the Iek Junket (which Chau financed), were noncompliant with their junket operator agreements, service desk processes and Australian AML laws, including Suncity staff running an unlicensed cash service desk and helping unknown individuals use CCTV blind spots to avoid cameras. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "were noncompliant with both their junket operator agreements and associated service desk processes"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Cash was repeatedly seen carried in and out in suitcases and backpacks, including by a Suncity limousine driver, during the Star's May 2018 internal investigation of Suncity's Salon 95, titled 'Operation Moneybags'; footnote 23 says Suncity was prohibited from exchanging cash for chips or retaining player cash at its desk, yet was storing large amounts of cash in sports bags on a balcony and staff frequently exchanged chips for cash. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cash was observed being carried in and out in suitcases and backpacks"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Chau, Suncity representatives and key players made numerous multi-million-dollar transfers indicative of laundering between Chau's Suncity junket deposit account and deposit accounts at Crown casinos held by key players on Suncity programmes, other junket operators and unrelated third parties, further substantiating the charges behind his Macau SAR conviction. Footnote 22 notes Chau was the understood financier of the Iek Junket, whose room at The Star's Darling Hotel was branded the Suncity Room for Suncity's exclusive use from 1 July 2017 to 1 September 2019. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "numerous multi-million-dollar transfers indicative of money laundering"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: An unsealed 2022 securities regulator filing stated Australian federal law enforcement found Chau received a cash payment into his Star Sydney casino account from an individual who later pleaded guilty to dealing in proceeds of crime. (p. 82)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Chau to have received payment into his Star Sydney casino account in cash"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The filing cites a Hong Kong Jockey Club report on Suncity noting Chau's 'clear' association with triads including the 14K, and that Australian law enforcement said that during 2013 to 2015 Suncity was believed to be laundering up to AU $2 million per day using various methodologies, with a significant amount of the cash suspected to be drug trafficking proceeds. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Suncity was believed to be laundering up to AU $2 million per day"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In February 2023 Australian authorities (Operation Avarus-Midas) dismantled a money laundering organization linked to Suncity, seizing properties, cryptocurrencies and luxury assets worth over AU $150 million and charging nine suspects including a known business partner of Chau. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "charging nine suspects including a known business partner of Alvin Chau"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The syndicate was designated an Australian Priority Organization Target for acting as an 'unregulated multi-national bank' drawing on cash reserves in multiple countries for criminal clients; the 'shadow banking' organization allegedly moved an estimated AU $10 billion offshore and built a property portfolio of mansions, commercial properties and hundreds of acres near Sydney's second airport. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "unregulated multi-national bank, able to draw on cash reserves held in multiple countries around the world"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The New South Wales casino inquiry in Australia itself treated Alvin Chau as a probable triad associate. It relied on casino due diligence documents that raised extremely serious concerns about the probity of Suncity and its founder, which the report quotes as indicating continuing triad connections and Suncity's facilitation of organized crime. (p. 83)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "identified Chau as a probable triad associate"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: According to authorities the group enabled multiple transnational organized crime groups to launder funds by exploiting casino junkets, daigous and other informal value transfer systems so money circumvented or filtered through legitimate systems. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "exploiting casino junkets as well as daigous"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 32: underground banking often uses the 'Daigou' system, in which cash from crime is paid into accounts of people buying retail goods in one jurisdiction that are in demand in another and then exported for sale, often contravening customs controls; it needs many bank accounts in the target country, often provided by overseas students who may be told they are providing money transmission services for other students but likely know it breaches customs controls in their home country. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This activity requires a large number of bank accounts in the target country"</sub>
- **descriptive / data or feature**: Figure 1 (Australian Federal Police, 2023) describes international controller money laundering networks with four roles. The Controller, often overseas, directs network activities and liaises with organized crime groups wanting to launder money; the model relies on reputation and ability to guarantee value transmission between jurisdictions, and the Controller charges a commission per transaction and profits by negotiating the exchange rate in their favour. The Coordinator manages Collectors and Transmitters, executes the Controller's instructions, may be local or overseas, and is not always present. The Collector acts on the Controller's or Coordinator's direction and collects crime proceeds from organized crime groups, generally via token-based exchanges, then supplies the money to Transmitters or fills the Transmitter role. Transmitters get the proceeds offshore, usually by structured deposits into bank accounts, handing cash to a registered or unregistered remittance service, or to a third party to balance unrelated transactions through informal value transfer systems including Hawala and similar providers. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Figure 1� International controller money laundering networks34"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Beyond Suncity, other documented incidents show extensive misuse of junkets by transnational organized crime in Australia; the Sam Gor drug trafficking network ('The Company') had relationships with several junket operators including Suncity, and a former senior Sam Gor member was known to authorities as a silent partner of the Macau SAR-based Hot Pot junket operating at Crown Casino. (p. 84)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "one former senior member of Sam Gor was known to authorities to have been a silent partner"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Footnote 34: an International Controller Network is a hierarchical professional money laundering network with extensive global reach and varied methodologies, made up of a controller, coordinator, collector and transmitter; records are kept at various levels so the controller can balance transactions, manipulate cash pools and track remittances, and coordinators and collectors keep ledgers or electronic records. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An International Controller Network specializes in laundering the proceeds of crime between jurisdictions"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Footnote 35: when criminals provide cash to the collector, the collector produces a bill with a unique serial number that serves as a kind of receipt; criminals can show it to their boss in the event of a loss, and are assured the collector is the intended recipient since nobody else could hold that note. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the collector produces a bill with a unique serial number"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The casino inquiry found the Hot Pot junket generated hundreds of millions of dollars in turnover for Crown Casino through its operator, Ng Chi Un. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "generated hundreds of millions of dollars in turnover for Crown Casino through its operator, Ng Chi Un"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Suky Lieu, convicted in Australia in June 2015 of drug trafficking and money laundering for attempting to import a commercial quantity of heroin from Viet Nam, was tied to the junket business; phone taps in the prosecution showed a junket was used to wire millions of dollars to Hong Kong SAR on instructions from a senior Sam Gor member. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a junket was used to wire millions of dollars to Hong Kong SAR following instructions"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Lieu's associate and former junket operator Roy Moo, convicted of money laundering in 2013 and imprisoned, continued after being barred from Melbourne Crown Casino to arrange laundering of funds back to Hong Kong for Sam Gor, making several transfers from Crown Casino's Australian bank account to the Bank of China in Hong Kong through a VIP account owned by the Indonesian Madam Ang Junket Group, which employed him; 2012 footage showed him collecting AU $191,000 in cash in a shopping bag and taking it to Crown Melbourne. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Moo made several transfers to the Bank of China in Hong Kong from Crown Casino’s bank account in Australia"</sub>

## Philippines

- **descriptive / case example**: Before Chau's December 2021 arrest, Suncity was among the leading Philippine junket operators, running half a dozen VIP rooms and an integrated casino resort being developed in Entertainment City, Manila, by Suntrust Homes. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "running half a dozen VIP rooms as well as an integrated casino resort"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In 2016 Philippine casinos and junket operators and agents helped launder about US $81 million stolen in a Lazarus Group-attributed cyberattack on Bangladesh Bank's account at the Federal Reserve of New York; filings and Philippine records show Chau received a large part of the funds laundered through the Suncity junket in the Solaire Hotel and Casino, via an exchange of Solaire chips worth 903.7 million pesos (US $20 million) for an equal amount of non-negotiable Suncity junket chips systematically transmitted as VIP rollings over several weeks into casinos outside the Philippines, later confirmed by Solaire's lawyer at Blue Ribbon Committee hearings. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Alvin Chau had been the recipient of a large portion of the stolen funds"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Chau and Suncity are also understood to have helped facilitate sanctions evasion and oil smuggling using a network of falsified vessel identities, ship-to-ship oil transfers and underground banking and laundering schemes through Suncity associates connected to sanctioned entities. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "facilitate sanctions evasion and oil smuggling using a sophisticated network of falsified vessel identities"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: After Suncity's implication in the Philippine senate inquiry and rising pressure in the Philippines and China, in 2019 it sold its Philippine proxy betting company to a fellow junket operator previously arrested for illegal betting in 2014 alongside an alleged senior 14k triad member understood to have strong connections to the Hell's Angels in Australia and Canada, per law enforcement sources. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "in 2019 Suncity sold its Philippine-based proxy betting company to a fellow junket operator"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Although the company and platform were renamed to remove any Suncity association, a Suncity executive testifying in 2022 said Suncity still received 50 per cent of profits and paid staff salaries and other costs; witness testimony indicates the Suncity I.T. team built and maintained a new management system called 'Opsman' for the new owners. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "continued to receive 50 per cent of the business’s profits"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Footnote 45: regional law enforcement told UNODC that in 2019 they discovered many junket operators offering proxy betting through unlicensed live-dealer streaming from their VIP rooms, posing significant money laundering risk. (p. 85)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "a large number of junket operators offering proxy betting services through unlicensed live-dealer streaming services"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: After complaints of alleged financial and investment irregularities against Suncity Group Manila, in 2021 the PAGCOR Board designated a committee to conduct a formal inquiry, and in February 2022 the committee found Suncity and Chau unsuitable to hold any authority and licence to operate a junket business in the Philippines. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "found Suncity and Alvin Chau unsuitable to hold any authority and license to operate a junket business"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Law enforcement said most complainants were heavily connected to offshore online gaming operators in the Philippines, several said they stored large volumes of cash with the junket as a high-return 'investment', and most struggled to explain the source of the funds. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the majority of complainants were heavily connected to offshore online gaming operators based in the Philippines"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Members of drug trafficking groups including Sam Gor are known to frequent casinos with junket operators in the Philippines, described as a significant indication of money laundering; in 2018 authorities dismantled a synthetic drug facility in Malabon City and arrested nine, where precursor shipments were arranged at a Macau SAR VIP room, payment arranged through a junket operator and disbursed in casino chips, and those arrested frequented the Solaire Hotel & Casino, where Suncity VIP rooms operated. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "have been known to frequent casinos with junket operators in the Philippines, a significant indication of money laundering"</sub>

## Cambodia

- **descriptive / case example**: In 2018 Chau expanded Suncity into Cambodia through a business and consulting partnership with Golden Sun Sky Entertainment and CEO Dong Lecheng (both sanctioned under UK Global Human Rights Sanctions in December 2023) to develop and manage a US $360 million beachside integrated resort casino in Sihanoukville. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "establishing a strategic business and consulting partnership with Golden Sun Sky Entertainment and CEO Dong Lecheng"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: That year Suncity also signed a deal for a VIP room at Phnom Penh's Naga World casino, then opened the XiGang International Suncity VIP Club at the Xi-Hu Hotel with K99 Group and K99 Junket, with Chau, Dong Lecheng and Facilitator 1 at the signing; the club had 20 gaming tables in three rooms on the hotel's second floor. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "XiGang International Suncity VIP Club was established on the second floor of the Xi-Hu Hotel"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The integrated resort was developed partly by Property Development Company 1, which has the same business registration address as the Xi-Hu Hotel and was co-founded and co-directed by Facilitator 2, the late brother of Facilitator 1. (p. 86)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Property Development Company 1 which holds the same business registration address as the Xi-Hu Hotel"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Dong Lecheng, known as Heng Tong in Cambodia after naturalization in 2014 (Royal Decree NS/RKT/0214/144, 3 February 2014), is widely cited in Chinese criminal records for illicit activities tied to his Yunnan Jincheng Group; a 2015 Heilongjiang court document refers to a 2006 investigation into Lecheng and partners for establishing Maida Oriental Company in the Maizhayang Special Economic Zone in Kachin State, Myanmar, and profiting from renting and managing illegally advertised gambling halls and facilitating cross-border gambling. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "known as Heng Tong in Cambodia following his naturalization in 2014"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In March 2020 the World Bank issued a notice of uncontested sanctions regarding Lecheng-controlled subsidiary Yunnan Jincheng Construction Engineering for sanctionable procurement practices connected to the Zhejiang Rural Water Supply and Sanitation Project; it noted a pattern of misconduct including fraudulent practices in bids for three separate contracts. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "engaged in fraudulent practices in bids for three separate contracts"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: In June 2023 the Yingshang County Public Security Bureau of Fujian issued a criminal notice after a multi-year investigation into illegal online gambling and telecom fraud operations housed in the Sunshine Bay Hotel and the 'Chinatown' compound area in Sihanoukville; Sunshine Bay was co-founded and co-directed by Facilitator 1, much of Chinatown was developed by Dong Lecheng and Yunnan Jincheng Group, and Sunshine Bay partly shares ownership with the K.B. Hotel, another UK Global Human Rights sanctioned entity in the 'Kaibo' area. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "illegal online gambling and telecom fraud operations housed in the Sunshine Bay Hotel"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: A separate 2009 court record describes how Dong Lecheng's Yunnan Jincheng Group paid bribes to a former deputy director of the Management Committee of the Border Cooperation Zone of Ruili City, China, in order to win a lucrative highway construction contract for its subsidiary, Ruili Jingcheng Road and Bridge Construction Engineering. (p. 87)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "bribed a former deputy director of the Management Committee of the Border Cooperation Zone of Ruili City"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The compound area includes some of the most well-documented, violent and heavily guarded fraud parks in Southeast Asia, a human trafficking, online gambling and cybercrime hub confirmed by regional law enforcement and rescued victims. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "most well-documented,64 violent, and heavily guarded fraud parks in Southeast Asia"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: K.B. Hotel was co-founded and directed by Facilitator 2 and Xu Aimin, a naturalized Cambodian wanted by Chinese authorities for leading an online gambling ring and laundering millions through multiple Hong Kong bank accounts; the pair also co-directed Investment Company 1, a Cambodian VIP Club and official Suncity partner offering VIP gambling credit arrangements, remittance advice and other financial services and listing a Meg-Star.com contact address, and the verdict against Chau repeatedly mentions the Meg-Star junket as a vehicle for laundering money. (p. 88)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "wanted65 by Chinese authorities for leading an online gambling ring and laundering millions of dollars"</sub>
