## 5.2 Baselines & Selected Models for Extension

## 5.3 Metrics

- **descriptive / definition**: F1-min is the F1-score of the minority (illicit) class, measuring the model's ability to recall illicit transactions while maintaining precision. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The F1-score of the minority class to measure the model’s ability to recall illicit transactions while maintaining precision;"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Macro F1 is the average of F1-scores across all classes, giving a balanced assessment of illicit and licit classifications. (p. 11)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The average of the F1-scores across all classes, providing a balanced assessment of both illicit and licit classifications;"</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: PR-AUC is the precision-recall area under the curve, used to assess model robustness under an unknown decision boundary. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Precision-Recall Area Under the Curve to assess the robustness of the model with an unknown decision boundary."</sub>
- **descriptive / method**: The evaluation first compares TeMP-TraG to baselines, then examines aggregation strategies for edge timestamps, and finally explores graph sampling by varying the number of selected first- and second-hop neighbours. (p. 12)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "First, we compare TeMP-TraG to the baselines. Then, we examine the different aggregation strategies for edge timestamps"</sub>

## 6.2 Analysis of Edge Timestamp Aggregation Methods

- **descriptive / method**: Four permutation-invariant aggregation strategies for edge timestamps are compared: sum, mean, min and max. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "using four permutation-invariant aggregation strategies for edge timestamps: sum, mean, min, and max."</sub>
- **descriptive / definition**: Sum emphasises transaction volume, mean balances older and recent transactions, min prioritises older transactions, and max favours more recent interactions. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "_sum_ emphasises transaction volume, _mean_ balances between older and recent transactions, _min_ gives precedence to older transactions, and _max_ favours more recent interactions"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Sum performs worst in four of six cases; in the other two cases mean performs worst. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "_sum_ performs the worst in four out of six cases; in the other two cases, _mean_ performs the worst."</sub>
- **descriptive / limitation**: Sum and mean encapsulate all transactions, so no findings can be derived about a specific single transaction between two nodes. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Both aggregations encapsulate all transactions, making it impossible to derive findings about a specific single transaction between two nodes."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Min and max maintain strong performance across all metrics and datasets, with max achieving the best overall results. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In contrast, _min_ and _max_ maintain strong performance across all metrics and datasets, with _max_ achieving the best overall results."</sub>

## 6.1 Overall Results

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The authors attribute the difference between ETH and IBM to structural differences, stating that ETH has considerably more edges per node pair than IBM, which the excerpt says is shown in Table 1. Table 1 lists the average number of edges per node pair as 17.92 for IBM and 2.53 for ETH, so the table does not match this stated reason. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "This discrepancy can be attributed to the structural differences: ETH has considerably more edges per node pair than IBM"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The authors report that edge aggregation is crucial for handling dense edge structures, and that its benefits diminish when fewer edges exist between a pair of nodes. (p. 13)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Edge aggregation becomes crucial in handling dense edge structures, while its benefits diminish when fewer edges exist"</sub>

## 6.3 Impact of Graph Sampling

- **descriptive / method**: The study varies the number of first- and second-hop neighbours during graph sampling (Section 4.1) and compares TeMP-TraG (GIN with agg) with MEGA-GIN. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "we study how the number of first- and second-hop neighbours during graph sampling (Section 4.1) affects model performance."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: On ETH, performance varies minimally as the number of sampled neighbours increases, indicating robust behaviour. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For ETH, we observe minimal performance variation as the number of sampled neighbours increases, i.e., robust behaviour."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: On IBM, performance initially improves with more neighbours before stabilising; with very few neighbours the gap between MEGA-GNN and TeMP-TraG is lower than 0.5% for all metrics, widening up to 2% with more than 50/50 1-hop/2-hop neighbours. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For very few neighbours, the performance gap between MEGA-GNN and TeMP-TraG is narrow (lower than 0 _._ 5% for all metrics)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: TeMP-TraG shows consistent benefits across graph sampling settings and stays competitive with state-of-the-art models even when only a small number of neighbours is selected. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "maintaining competitive performance with state-of-the-art models even when only a small number of neighbours is selected."</sub>

## 7 Conclusion & Future Works

- **descriptive / context**: TeMP-TraG is introduced as a graph neural network mechanism incorporating temporal dynamics into message passing, with a temporal weighting mechanism for edge features in multigraph embedding. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TeMP-TraG effectively handles edge features for multigraph embedding, incorporating a temporal weighting mechanism in the message-passing neural network."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: TeMP-TraG improves four state-of-the-art graph neural networks by 6.19% on average. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "TeMP-TraG improves four state-of-the-art graph neural networks by 6 _._ 19% on average."</sub>
- **descriptive / recommendation**: Future work includes incorporating geographical information to capture spatio-temporal patterns in financial crime. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "First, incorporating geographical information will enable to capture spatio-temporal patterns in financial crime."</sub>
