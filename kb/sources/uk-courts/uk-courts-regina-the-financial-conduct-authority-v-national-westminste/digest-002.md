## Fowler Oldfield

- **descriptive / finding or result**: From late 2013 numerous NatWest branches began receiving millions of pounds in Fowler Oldfield cash; some branches and cash centres flagged concerns or submitted IMLSRs while others did not, including non-notifiers receiving sums between £12 and 43 million. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "From late 2013, numerous branches started to receive millions in Fowler Oldfield cash."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Some non-notifying branches received cash so bulky that it arrived in black bin bags which tore under the weight, and sums so large the bank's safes were inadequate to store them. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "sums so large that the bank’s safes were inadequate to store them."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Over summer 2014 Washington cash centre staff repeatedly raised concerns with relevant Bank departments about Fowler Oldfield's cash deposits, ultimately raising three IMLSRs on the customer in the relevant period. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Over the summer of 2014 Washington staff repeatedly raised concerns about Fowler Oldfield’s cash deposits"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Washington centre red flags: high volumes of cash, high volumes of Scottish notes (unusual for a business located far from the border), and cash with a prominent musty smell indicating long storage rather than business use. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the cash would at times carry a prominent, musty smell, indicative of long storage, rather than business use."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Washington staff set up a dedicated team to monitor Fowler Oldfield cash deposits and conducted their own enquiries. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Staff at the centre were sufficiently concerned about the activity they set up a dedicated team"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: On 21 July 2014 the NCA submitted a formal section 7 Crime and Courts Act 2013 information request to the Bank about Fowler Oldfield, triggered by the Scottish banknotes; the request was not recorded on the customer's GK file as there was no internal policy requirement, and no IMLSR was raised. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The document was not recorded on Fowler Oldfield’s GK file (there was no internal policy requirement to do so)"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Financial Intelligence Unit concerns about the Relationship Manager's explanations were not recorded on the database used by the analyst deciding whether to report transactions; no EDR was triggered and no SAR was submitted. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "These concerns were not recorded on the relevant database which was used by the analysist"</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: The highly experienced Basingstoke cash centre manager flagged his concerns to a Financial Crime Manager rather than raising an IMLSR, but that manager judged there were sufficient explanations on file; subsequent alerts were closed without contacting the Relationship Manager. (p. 14)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Further alterts from this branch were closed by the investigator without contacting the Relationship Manager."</sub>
- **descriptive / case example**: Fowler Oldfield was listed as an associated party/remitter in one SAR submitted to the NCA in August 2015, concerning a hair extensions/wigs supplier that received about £387k from Fowler Oldfield before funds went to a pub business and then to money transfer businesses. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It was noted that the company had received approximately £387k from Fowler Oldfield"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: An IMLSR at this time asked the Relationship Manager to consider whether an EDR was necessary; his assurance that the activity was fully explained and consistent with the account information was accepted. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "An IMLSR at this time triggered a request for the Relationship Manager to consider whether an EDR was necessary."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: No checking was done, to the extent checking was possible, of the Relationship Manager's inaccurate summary of the relationship with HMRC, and no one challenged his failure to recommend a high-risk rating or to initiate an EDR. (p. 15)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No checking (to the extent checking was possible) took place of his inaccurate summary of the relationship with HMRC."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: No SARs were submitted to the NCA concerning Fowler Oldfield itself until the Bank was notified of the NCA/West Yorkshire Police investigation. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "No SARs were submitted to the NCA concerning Fowler Oldfield itself until the Bank was notified"</sub>

## The investigation and its aftermath

- **descriptive / context**: West Yorkshire Police investigation suspected a large-scale money laundering operation run from Fowler Oldfield from January 2014; NatWest was notified in June 2016, agreed to cooperate from 23 June 2016, exited the customer and informed the FCA of concerns. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "notified NatWest of this in June 2016. The Bank was in communication with WYP"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: After becoming aware of the investigation, the Nominated Office submitted 13 SARs retrospectively reporting conduct back to 2013 and sought consent for account closures. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the Nominated Office submitted 13 SARs which retrospectively reported conduct on the account"</sub>

## Fowler Oldfield

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The March 2016 EDR acknowledged high cash use but described it as common to the sector; the court considered the level of scrutiny of this EDR appears to have been deficient. (p. 16)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The level of scrutiny of this EDR appears to have been deficient."</sub>

## The investigation and its aftermath

- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Bank incurred a small loss on Fowler Oldfield, as fees were more than offset by an informally extended loan outstanding when the company ceased trading. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "The Bank ultimately incurred a small loss from its relationship with Fowler Oldfield"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Internal reviews identified and remediated issues, including making Nominated Office quality assurance more rigorous, escalating repeat non-disclosures, a monthly report ensuring EDRs follow any lowered high-risk rating, and training on challenging relationship manager explanations. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A monthly report was instituted, to ensure that if a customer's high risk rating was lowered"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: An FCA-appointed Skilled Person reviewing the AML Change Programme found demonstrable progress and a clear tone from the top, but flagged remediation of high-risk files as an issue; later it acknowledged progress on most recommendations while noting delays for high-risk files. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "It did however still note issues for improvement - in particular in relation to remediation of “high risk” files."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Group authorised £700m expenditure on AML systems, processes and controls in 2010 to 2015, invested over £700m on financial crime from 2016, and intends to spend over £1 billion between 2021 and 2025. (p. 17)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "During the period 2010 to 2015, the Group authorised the expenditure of £700m"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The Group created a new centralised FinCrime team and grew its Financial Crime and Fraud team to 5,000 members, roughly 8% of NWG's total employees. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "has grown the size of its Financial Crime and Fraud team to 5,000 members"</sub>

## Count 1: Regulation 8(1): Ongoing monitoring

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 8(1) required NatWest to conduct ongoing monitoring of all relevant business relationships, comprising scrutiny of transactions for consistency with expected business activity (Regulation 8(2)(a)) and keeping CDD documents, data or information up to date (Regulation 8(2)(b)). (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regulation 8(1) of Regulations required NatWest to conduct ongoing monitoring of all relevant business relationships."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 8(1) required NatWest to conduct ongoing monitoring of all relevant business relationships, made up of scrutinising transactions for consistency with expected activity and keeping CDD documents, data or information up to date. (p. 18)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Regulation 8(1) of Regulations required NatWest to conduct ongoing monitoring of all relevant business relationships."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The court found NatWest failed to comply with Regulation 8(1) between 7 November 2013 and 23 June 2016 by failing adequately to scrutinise Fowler Oldfield transactions for consistency with expected activity. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NatWest failed to comply with Regulation 8(1) between 7 November 2013 and 23 June 2016"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Relationship Management Team failures included not adequately corroborating or challenging explanations for the shift to large cash deposits, not scrutinising later unusual activity, not initiating reviews after trigger events such as changes in transaction patterns or directorships, and not ensuring the correct risk rating. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Corroborate, scrutinise or critically assess the customer’s explanation for the change in activity"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Cash deposits made through Bank cash centres were erroneously interpreted by the automated monitoring system as cheque deposits throughout the Indictment Period, so they were subject to less stringent cheque rules; the Security Blanket also needed attention. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "cash deposits made directly through Bank cash centres were erroneously interpreted by the system as cheque deposits"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: For most of the relationship there were no cheque-specific monitoring rules, and for June 2014 to September 2015 there were no cash-specific rules, so millions of pounds of cash were neither monitored as cash nor subject to cheque-targeted rules. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "For part of the Indictment Period (June 2014 to September 2015) the rules in place did not include cash-specific rules"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Staff raised 11 IMLSRs and the automated system alerted 10 times on Fowler Oldfield activity; the Bank's investigations between 7 November 2013 and 23 June 2016 were inadequate. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Staff raised 11 IMLSRs and the Bank’s automated transaction monitoring system alerted 10 times"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Alert investigation failings included overreliance on or failure to sufficiently challenge Relationship Manager explanations, and failure to identify that the customer was erroneously rated low or medium risk for most of the relationship. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to identify that the customer was erroneously rated by NatWest as ‘low’ or ‘medium’ risk for most of the relationship;"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: In certain instances, the investigators failed to seek further information from internal and open sources about Fowler Oldfield. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "In certain instances, failure to seek further information from internal and open sources about Fowler Oldfield;"</sub>
- **descriptive / typology or red flag**: Investigators failed to adequately identify and respond to money laundering red flags, including the change in the business model from transfers in with cash out to cash in with transfers out. (p. 19)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to adequately identify, and respond to a series of money laundering ‘red flags’"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Further alert failings included not analysing account behaviour against account-opening information and EDR information, and not considering the cumulative implications of prior investigations when determining each new IMLSR or TM alert. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Failure to consider the cumulative implications of prior investigations"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: NatWest failed to conduct any adequate independent review of account activity, and Fowler Oldfield received no Periodic Reviews during the almost five-year relationship, which would have involved scrutiny of transactions had they been performed correctly. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fowler Oldfield was not subject to any Periodic Reviews during the almost five year relationship period"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Two EDRs (November 2013 and March 2016) with KYC and KYB input were inadequate and missed significant differences between actual transactions and expected activity; additional EDRs were not conducted when multiple policy-required trigger events occurred. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "when multiple events which should, under NatWest’s policy, have triggered further reviews occurred"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: PRs and EDRs should have been the primary means of keeping customer due diligence information up to date, but were not conducted, not conducted frequently enough, or not completed on time. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "PRs and EDRs should have been the primary means of keeping documents, data and the recording of information up to date"</sub>

## Count 2: Regulation 8(3): Risk-sensitive ongoing monitoring

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 8(3) required NatWest to conduct ongoing monitoring on a risk-sensitive basis (per Regulation 7(3)(a)) and to be able to demonstrate to the FCA as supervisor that the extent of its measures was appropriate in view of the risks (per Regulation 7(3)(b)). (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Be able to demonstrate to the FCA (as its supervisory authority) that the extent of its measures were appropriate"</sub>

## Count 1: Regulation 8(1): Ongoing monitoring

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 8(2)(b) required NatWest to keep documents, data or information obtained for customer due diligence purposes up to date. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "To keep documents, data or information obtained for the purposes of customer due diligence up to date (Regulation 8(2)(b))."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: NatWest failed to comply with Regulation 8(1) between 7 November 2013 and 23 June 2016 by failing to keep CDD information up to date, in particular by failing to conduct sufficient formal reviews of activity in line with its own policies. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NatWest also failed to comply with Regulation 8(1) between 7 November 2013 to 23 June 2016"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Bank guidance required consideration of follow-up actions which could include updating the CDD information or risk rating; despite alert investigations on matters relevant to recorded CDD taking place at fairly regular intervals from 2014, no such updates were made. (p. 20)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Bank guidance required consideration of follow up actions which could include updating Fowler Oldfield’s customer due diligence information"</sub>

## Count 2: Regulation 8(3): Risk-sensitive ongoing monitoring

- **descriptive / finding or result**: NatWest failed to comply with Regulation 8(3) between 8 November 2012 and 23 June 2016 by not monitoring Fowler Oldfield on the basis it was high-risk; the court lists weaknesses including erroneous downgrading to low risk in December 2013, medium in April 2014, and return to high only on 3 March 2016. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Fowler Oldfield was erroneously downgraded to low risk in December 2013 before being increased to medium risk in April 2014."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Further Regulation 8(3) weaknesses: no differentiated automated monitoring of high-risk customers until April 2016; no automated rules targeting cash deposits made via BQD; investigations not reflecting high risk; no PRs (which should have been annual); insufficient EDRs; and EDRs not conducted in a risk-sensitive manner. (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "A failure to conduct any PRs (PRs for Fowler Oldfield should have been annual)"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Because ongoing monitoring was not risk-sensitive, NatWest could not demonstrate to the FCA that the extent of its monitoring was appropriate to the risks, a further failure to comply with Regulation 8(3). (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NatWest was unable to demonstrate to the FCA that the extent of its ongoing monitoring was appropriate"</sub>

## Count 3: Regulation 14(1): Enhanced ongoing monitoring

- **mandatory / requirement**: Regulation 14 required NatWest, where a situation by its nature could present a higher risk of money laundering (Regulation 14(1)(b)), to apply enhanced CDD and enhanced ongoing monitoring on a risk-sensitive basis (Regulation 14(2)). (p. 21)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "to apply enhanced customer due diligence and enhanced ongoing monitoring on a risk-sensitive basis"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: NatWest failed to comply with Regulation 14 between 8 November 2012 and 23 June 2016 for Fowler Oldfield, which NatWest accepted was high-risk throughout; it failed to rate the customer high-risk or give enough enhanced scrutiny for over two years, apply differentiated monitoring until April 2016, investigate in a high-risk manner, or conduct PRs and sufficient EDRs at each trigger event. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "NatWest accepts that Fowler Oldfield was, throughout that period, a high-risk customer."</sub>

## Sentencing principles

- **descriptive / context**: Joint sentencing submissions were tendered under the Attorney General's guidelines on plea discussions, but the judge is not bound by any agreement and sentences on her own consideration of the materials. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "as both parties have accepted that as the sentencing Judge I am not bound by any agreement"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Under Regulation 45(1) the maximum penalty for a corporate defendant breaching the Regulations is an unlimited fine; no deliberate flouting or criminal intent was suggested and the offences are strict liability. (p. 22)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "Pursuant to Regulation 45(1) the maximum penalty upon conviction of a corporate defendant"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: There are no specific sentencing guidelines or appellate authorities for offences under the Regulations. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "There are no specific sentencing guidelines for offences under the Regulations"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: The FCA and its predecessor the FSA imposed civil penalties under reg 42 of the Regulations and for AML-related breaches of the FCA's Principles for Businesses, with the most recent NatWest regulatory findings in 2014. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the most recent being in 2014"</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: Under s. 59(1) Sentencing Code the court is obliged to follow any relevant sentencing guideline unless contrary to the interests of justice; two relevant guidelines are the General Guideline on Overarching Principles and the Corporate Guidelines on fraud, bribery and money laundering. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "I am obliged to follow any sentencing guideline which is relevant to a particular case"</sub>
- **expected / expectation**: Under the General Guideline, where no definitive guideline exists the court should consider the statutory maximum, Court of Appeal sentencing judgments for the offence, and definitive guidelines for analogous offences, applying analogues carefully with adjustments. (p. 23)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the court should take account of all of the following (if they apply)"</sub>
- **descriptive / context**: Statutory maxima for natural persons are 14 years imprisonment for substantive money laundering and 2 years for breaches of the Regulations; corporate maxima for both are unlimited fines. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "14 years’ imprisonment for substantive money laundering; 2 years’ imprisonment for breaches of the Regulations."</sub>
- **mandatory / requirement**: The court is required to consider whether to make a compensation order and must give reasons if it does not. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "I am required to consider whether the Court should make a compensation order"</sub>

## The principles applied

- **descriptive / finding or result**: Three offences were sentenced as one lead offence with no separate penalty for the others, with the lead sentence reflecting the totality of the offending. (p. 24)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "I will sentence in relation to one offence, which I will take as the lead offence"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: No compensation order was made because there was no personal injury, loss or damage resulting from the offence. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "because of the absence of personal injury, loss or damage resulting from the offence."</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: A confiscation order under s. 6 Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 was agreed for the amount gained by the Bank from the Fowler Oldfield relationship from 8 November 2012; total fees and charges were £400,818 (£460,047.04 after cost of living adjustment). (p. 25)[^original]

## Harm

- **descriptive / requirement**: The Corporate Guidelines say harm for money laundering will normally be the amount laundered or, alternatively, the likely cost avoided by failing to put in place an effective AML programme if higher. (p. 25)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "the appropriate figure will normally be the amount laundered"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: The court held harm should be measured by funds paid into Fowler Oldfield accounts during the indictment period, rejecting turnover in the business area as unrealistic; FCA and NatWest agreed, at £287,794,887.06. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "They also agree that this figure is £287,794,887.06"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Funds of £66,527,680.87 paid in after 23 June 2016 were excluded from harm, as the Bank had begun cooperating with West Yorkshire Police by then. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "£66,527,680.87 in total"</sub>
- **descriptive / finding or result**: Parties submitted a 40% harm reduction for the difference between regulation breaches and substantive money laundering and for possibly non-criminal funds; the court regarded the second point as nebulous but accepted the first, reducing harm to £172,676,932.23. (p. 26)[^original]  
  <sub>anchor: "I therefore accept the submission that the harm figure should be reduced by 40%"</sub>
